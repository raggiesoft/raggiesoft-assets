**LORE NOTE:**
Released in 1987 as the closing track of *Electric Color* and buried by Apex Records at the very end of the album, this is the band's true origin anthem—and their favorite song on the record for a specific reason: it is the **only** track on the 1987 debut album that perfectly captures the band's true identity. After an entire album of "cold war" compromises between the studio's synth-pop ("Stardust") and the band's rock ("Engine"), *"The Stardust Engine"* was the one moment where Cassidy's shimmering synth pads and bright, chime-like electric-piano synthesizer hook ("Stardust") and Ryan's distorted electric guitars and Tyler's massive gated-reverb drums ("Engine") weren't fighting—they were perfectly blended, just as Ryan, Cassidy, Evan, and Tyler had always intended when they named their band after the cosmos and the rockets that get you there.

Musically, the heart of the song is the **chorus call-and-response** (`01:18–01:22`, `01:33–01:37`, `02:13–02:16`, `03:32–03:35`): right after *"We're burning on the fuel of a brand new light!"* and *"Turning our future on, shining so bright!"*, Cassidy sends up a soaring vocal call—the pilot calling out into the dark—and her bright, chime-like electric-piano synthesizer patch immediately cuts through the rock guitars with the gleaming leitmotif response (*Ba-da-DA-da-DAAA!*), the spacecraft confirming trajectory. That vocal call and synthesizer leitmotif response was Cassidy's deepest musical pride and joy (which is why its deliberate erasure from the chorus of 1990's *Live in Chicago* broke the family's heart, before being triumphantly restored on 1999's *Hard Reset Live* and answered by the entire CPI Marching Forgers trumpet section on 2016's *Live at The Crucible* as CPI's promise to always have Cassidy's back). In a tragic irony for Apex Records, the studio completely ignored the album's closer; critics and chart historians agree that had Apex pushed *"The Stardust Engine"* as the lead single in the summer of 1987, it would have shattered the all-time 1980s chart record (10 weeks) by staying at **#1 for 11 solid weeks**.
