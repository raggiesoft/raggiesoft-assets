**LORE NOTE:**
This track is the "Smoking Gun" that proves Julian Vance (the executive behind the *Friction* disaster) never did his homework. In the intro, Ryan O'Connell—who, like his sister, is autistic and values absolute clarity—explicitly introduces Cassidy as "my sister," publicly disproving the "married couple" assumption years before it destroyed their contract.

Musically, this recording is a source of deep heartbreak and fury for the band. It was the only concession Apex made—allowing them to play their self-titled anthem as the opener of their strict 30-minute support slot. However, the Front-of-House mix is deliberately sabotaged: during the chorus (`01:28–01:31`, `01:38–01:48`, `02:28–02:31`, `02:37–02:46`), you are supposed to hear **Cassidy's soaring vocal call answered immediately by her synthesizer's gleaming brass leitmotif response** (*Ba-da-DA-da-DAAA!*)—the musical heart of the song she and Ryan constructed on Progress Street NE in December 1985, and Cassidy's deepest pride and joy in 1989. On *Live in Chicago*, the house engineer completely buried Cassidy's upper synthesizer tier and bulldozed the chorus pocket with dry, chugging guitars so her synthesizer response is completely missing from the chorus (even causing her to momentarily lose her acoustic orientation in the arena slapback at `02:04–02:11`). Hearing her cherished call-and-response erased from their signature song broke the family's heart—and especially broke Cassidy's heart.
