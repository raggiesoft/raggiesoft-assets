### **1-01-the-stardust-engine-alternate.md**
***


**LORE NOTE:**
This alternate version of "The Stardust Engine" was one of the original demos shopped around to record companies when the band formed in 1986. Apex Records signed them but rejected this version, burying the final studio version as track 13 on 1987's *Electric Color*. The band famously despises Apex for what happened to them during the 1992 label dispute. For this vault release, they officially retitled this early cut "(The Rejected Mix)" instead of naming Apex Records directly, serving as a quiet nod to their lingering resentment.

### **1-04-empty-arenas.md**
***

**LORE NOTE:**
Recorded in late 1990 shortly after Ryan's life-altering bus accident, this solemn, stripped-down acoustic demo (`Empty Arenas (Early Demo)`) reflects the band's raw fear that they would never be able to play live again. Deemed too bleak for the *Friction* sessions, the tape remained locked in the vault until *Lost Sounds* (2007)—though fourteen years after cutting this sketch, the band resurrected the song during the 2004 *Progress Street* sessions and transformed it into a thunderous, full-band 1980s Album-Oriented Rock (AOR) stadium anthem with polished lyrics and a full drum kit.

**LYRICS:**

[Verse 1]

[Chorus]

We used to set this room on fire

In these empty arenas

[Verse 2]
The ticket stubs are swept away
Just ghosts remembering yesterday
I look out at the velvet seats
And listen to my own heartbeat

[Chorus]
Empty arenas, where the echoes go to die
Empty arenas, beneath a painted sky
We used to set this room on fire
Now we're just preaching to the wire
In these empty arenas

[Bridge]

[Chorus]

### **1-09-our-own-frequency.md**
***

**LORE NOTE:**
A brand new track recorded specifically for the 2007 *Lost Sounds* compilation, intentionally designed to simulate the stripped-down, acoustic music Ryan and Cassidy were making together back in 1985 (before their cousins Evan and Tyler joined to form The Stardust Engine). After digging through years of painful, heavily-manufactured Apex Records history, they wanted to close the album by returning to their roots. Written and recorded in a single afternoon with just an acoustic guitar and a piano, "Our Own Frequency" is a triumphant, intimate declaration of their bond as brother and sister. It is the exact kind of acoustic scratch track they always wanted to make, completely free of label interference.
