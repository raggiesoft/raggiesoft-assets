The fluorescent ballast above the mixing desk in Studio A hummed at sixty cycles per second—a cold, relentless B-natural flat that had been drilling into the back of Cassidy O'Connell's skull for four years.

*The walls are cold, the air is thin. The noise outside is closing in.*

She stood with her forehead resting lightly against the cool double-paned glass of the control room window, looking out into the darkened live room of **The Fortress**. Outside the heavy corrugated-steel walls of the warehouse on **Industrial Park Road**, a warm July evening was settling over the southern edge of Blacksburg. Across the two-lane asphalt, behind a chain-link perimeter fence lit by amber high-pressure sodium lamps, the night-shift mechanics at the **Blue Ridge Transit** operations garage were parking the last of the cream-and-maroon **Stalwart** campus shuttle buses after their evening loops around **Commonwealth Green**. Every few minutes, the familiar pneumatic *hiss-clack* of an air brake bleeding down drifted across Industrial Park Road, followed by the distant murmur of impact wrenches in the bus maintenance bays.

That was why Holly had bought this old industrial warehouse at the edge of town in the first place. Out here among the sheet-metal fabrication shops, the limestone quarry sidings, and the Blue Ridge Transit bus yard, there were no residential subdivisions to call the town police when Evan and Tyler pushed a bass rig past a hundred and ten decibels at two in the morning. The alumni of **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute**—the fiercely loyal **Ironheads** who had pooled their own money into the *Forger Nation War Chest* when the band walked out of Los Angeles—had helped them turn this cavernous brick-and-steel shell into a sanctuary.

And yet, tonight, even The Fortress felt like a cell.

"Cass?"

Cassidy didn't have to turn around to recognize the whisper of pneumatic rubber tires rolling across the hardwood floor behind the mixing console. In the dark reflection of the studio glass, she saw her older brother Ryan come to a smooth stop beside her left hip. He was twenty-four now, his dark hair tucked behind his ears, wearing a charcoal flight-crew jacket over a faded CPI Engineering tee. Across his lap rested his cherished cherry-sunburst **Sainte-Claire** solid-body electric guitar, already latched inside a pressurized carbon-fiber flight case.

"You're listening to the ballast transformer again," Ryan said softly.

"I can't unhear it, Ry," Cassidy admitted, her breath fogging a tiny crescent on the soundproof glass. Her eyes drifted to the corner of the lounge table, where a stack of trade magazines and legal briefs from the *Apex Records* litigation sat bound in red tape ready for the archive vault. "We won the verdict. We got our masters back. We built twelve-inch acoustic walls on Industrial Park Road so nobody could touch us. And every time I walk to the grocery store or turn on a radio, we're still the tabloid punchline. The *'wholesome girl'* with the hot chocolate. The *'victim-saints'* who nuked their own careers. Even winning in that courtroom left their fingerprints all over our skin."

Ryan reached out with his right hand and covered hers where it rested on the oak meter bridge of the console. His calloused fingertips were warm and steady.

"That's why we aren't staying in the courtroom, Cass," Ryan said. "And we aren't staying in the basement."

Just then, the heavy steel fire door at the back of the control room swung open. Their eldest sister, Holly O'Connell, stepped in carrying two ruggedized **MagnaPad** flight slates under her arm, followed closely by their twenty-one-year-old twin cousins, Tyler and Evan Wright, who were hauling the last two anodized aluminum road cases of master tape reels and atmospheric sensors between them.

"All surface ties are severed," Holly announced, her voice calm, brisk, and electric with a quiet gravity she had been holding in since the day the *Friction* subpoenas were served. "Power grid transfer to internal geothermal is complete. If anyone from Los Angeles comes knocking on Industrial Park Road tomorrow morning, all they're going to find is a locked rehearsal warehouse and a post-office box. Are we ready to go downstairs?"

Cassidy looked once more at the buzzing fluorescent tube overhead, then reached over to the master wall panel and flipped the studio breaker off.

The sixty-cycle hum died instantly.

"Take us down, Hols," Cassidy said.

## Two Hundred Feet Beneath Industrial Park Road

To anyone driving past the Blue Ridge Transit garage on Industrial Park Road, the southern half of The Fortress appeared to be nothing more than an overgrown grading lot backed against a steep, wooded Appalachian limestone bluff.

What the county tax maps did not show was what lay two hundred feet beneath the warehouse slab.

Millions of years of subterranean groundwater flowing under the New River Valley had hollowed out a colossal karst dolomite cavern directly beneath the industrial park—a natural cathedral of dense, load-bearing Appalachian stone that **Stardust Strategic Assets** and a quiet coalition of CPI aerospace and civil-engineering alumni had spent four years reinforcing with arched titanium-steel ribs.

The five of them rolled and walked out of the back of Studio A into the warehouse's heavy freight bay. Evan punched a six-digit harmonic sequence into the keypad beside the oversized cargo lift. With a deep hydraulic hum, the twelve-by-twelve steel platform unlocked from the concrete floor and began its smooth descent through the bedrock.

For thirty seconds, rough-hewn walls of gray **Ironstone** and dolomite slipped past the safety grating, lit by recessed strips of cool cyan LEDs. Then the rock wall fell away, and Cassidy felt her breath catch in her chest.

The cavern was enormous—easily the size of **The Crucible** stadium bowl on campus, carved out of living mountain stone beneath a vaulted ceiling where camouflaged hydraulic blast doors blended seamlessly into the abandoned limestone quarry bluff above. Arcs of warm amber work-lights illuminated suspended catwalks, liquid-deuterium fuel lines, and acoustic baffling towers that lined the subterranean walls.

And resting in the center of the cavern floor on four massive hydraulic launch cradles was the ***U.S.S. Aethelgard***.

She was a sleek, swept-wing deep-space survey cruiser forged in matte charcoal titanium and brushed silver alloy, with twin acoustic-fusion nacelles faired smoothly into her rear hull and a glowing cyan Continuum collector ring nested beneath her forward nosecone. Along her dorsal spine, painted in crisp, unpretentious lettering beside the Engine Room Records gear emblem, was her registry: **`U.S.S. AETHELGARD • ERR-NCC-1996`**.

"Every single time the elevator clears the rock shelf," Tyler whistled softly, setting his end of the road case down as the lift locked flush with the polished ferro-concrete hangar deck, "I still half-expect to wake up in our dorm room at Pritchard."

"Don't wake up now, Ty," Evan grinned, slapping his twin brother on the shoulder as the elevator gate slid open. "You're flying port helm in twenty minutes."

At the stern of the *Aethelgard*, the wide aft cargo hatch stood open, its zero-lip titanium ramp resting flat against the hangar floor—the exact same threshold that would, seventy-two hours from tonight, open onto the purple shoreline of another star system.

## Boarding & The Multipoint Flight Harness

They crossed the cavernous hangar deck together, the echo of four pairs of boots and the quiet purr of Ryan's high-pressure wheelchair tires rising toward the vaulted limestone roof. Up the wide aft cargo ramp they went, passing the amber-lit starboard telemetry console where Holly immediately docked her MagnaPad to initialize the pre-launch environmental seal.

From the aft cargo bay, a wide, obstacle-free companionway with flush brushed-titanium deck plates led straight forward to the main bridge and cockpit—designed from the very first blueprint with zero stairs, zero raised bulkhead coamings, and thirty-eight-inch clear pocket doors that parted automatically as Ryan's front casters approached.

Inside the forward cockpit, the geometry was built around the five-member family like a custom-tailored recording studio.

Ahead of them stretched a panoramic, octagonal forward viewport framed by overhead status matrices and environmental bus breakers. Flanking the glowing cyan-and-amber center command pedestal were two high-back, contoured charcoal leather flight seats fitted with five-point aviation harnesses: **Port Helm** on the left for Tyler, and **Starboard Astrogation** on the right for Evan. Just aft of the center pedestal, where a traditional captain's chair would have been bolted on an older vessel, the deck plates featured a precision-machined **Four-Point Magnetic & Mechanical Wheelchair Docking Lock** flanked by two swiveling jump-seats for Cassidy and Holly.

Ryan rolled smoothly into the center command position between Tyler and Evan's stations. As his rear wheels settled over the recessed titanium channels, two padded pneumatic clamps rose half an inch from the deck and engaged the frame hubs of his chair with a solid, reassuring *clack-lock*.

"`WHEELCHAIR FRAME LOCK: ENGAGED — RATED 18.0G LATERAL / 24.0G AXIAL`," the center console chimed in a warm, low-glare amber readout.

"Let's get your harness rigged before we seal the outer hatch, Ry," Evan said, stepping over from the starboard seat before strapping himself in.

Because atmospheric breakaway and high-G emergency maneuvers would subject everyone on the flight deck to heavy chest-to-back acceleration—and because a sudden inertial shear in orbit could pitch an unrestrained rider out of a manual seat—Ryan and the CPI mechanical engineering team had designed a custom **Six-Point Quick-Release Flight Harness System** integrated directly into the high-tensile backrest and frame uprights of his flight wheelchair.

Ryan reached over his shoulders first, pulling down the twin padded Kevlar-weave shoulder straps while Evan handed him the left and right pelvic lap belts from the lower frame anchors. Unlike restrictive medical straps—which Ryan had refused to let near his body since leaving the Roanoke rehab ward in 1990—this was pure aerospace hardware: matte-black anodized aluminum cam-lock buckles modeled after sprint-car and orbital shuttle crew restraints, complete with a single center-chest rotary quick-release puck that Ryan could pop open with a quarter-turn of his own wrist whenever he wanted.

*Click. Click. Click. Click.*

Ryan snapped the four outer tangs into the center rotary buckle at his sternum, then pulled the twin cinch tabs snug across his collarbone and hips until his back was locked firmly against the contoured lumbar cushion of his chair. He gave the shoulder harness a practiced two-finger tug. Neither he nor the wheelchair frame moved a millimeter relative to the deck plates.

"Harness green," Ryan reported, resting his forearms comfortably on the wrap-around armrest pads where secondary pitch, trim, and communications toggles were positioned directly beneath his fingertips. "Tether locked at one-point-zero Earth G."

"Aft cargo hatch sealing now," Holly called from the starboard companionway station, tapping the master pneumatic closure switch before taking her seat beside the environmental telemetry bus and buckling her own five-point harness. Behind them, the massive stern ramp lifted from the limestone floor of Silo Alpha and sealed into the hull with a deep, airtight *thump* that cut off the last echo of the underground cavern.

Cassidy buckled her five-point harness in the port-center mission seat right beside Ryan's left wheel. Through the octagonal forward viewport, the dark dolomite wall of the subterranean launch shaft rose two hundred feet toward the hidden quarry doors above Industrial Park Road.

For the first time since 1992, the air inside the cabin didn't smell like courthouse paper, ozone, or fear.

"Holly," Ryan said quietly from the center lock, his eyes fixed on the forward viewport. "Sound the silo clear."
