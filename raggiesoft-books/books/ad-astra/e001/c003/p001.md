For the first four years after Holly O'Connell founded Engine Room Records in a converted brick warehouse off Progress Street NE, her recurring nightmare had always been the same: a room with no doors, a ceiling full of buzzing fluorescent tubes, and a table of men in charcoal suits sliding a pen across mahogany toward her little brother and sister.

She had built the *Aethelgard*—first on paper, then in legal trusts, and finally in the cosmic architecture of their music—for one reason above all others: so that Ryan, Cassidy, Tyler, and Evan would never again have to ask anyone's permission to exist.

Now, standing at the edge of a purple ocean under the golden rings of Vespera while the shipboard chronometer ticked past **`Stardate 49601.8`**, Holly realized that she had spent so many years preparing to defend her family from a hostile world that she hadn't fully prepared for what happens when you land on a gentle one.

## Hailing Frequencies in the Universal Language

Tyler and Evan Wright looked at each other across the nose of Ryan's wheelchair. Without a single word passing between the twins—using that effortless, lifelong shorthand that had anchored every rhythm section they had ever played—they both grinned and turned back toward the *Aethelgard*'s ramp.

"Evan," Ryan called out, already spinning his chair around on the firm basaltic crust and sending himself flying back up the gentle incline of the titanium ramp with four crisp, powerful strokes of his arms. "Unlatch Locker Three on the port bulkhead and hand me the cherry-sunburst solid-body. Tyler, route the cargo bay PA array to the external hull transducers—flat EQ, zero compression, eighty-five decibels max so we don't startle the bay."

"Aye, Captain," Tyler laughed, sprinting up the ramp two steps at a time while Evan popped the padded flight latches on the instrument locker just inside the cargo hatch.

Holly walked up the ramp behind Ryan and Cassidy, resuming her post by the starboard telemetry console—not because she needed to guard the door anymore, but because someone had to run the board when The Stardust Engine opened hailing frequencies with a new world.

Inside the warm, amber-lit threshold of the cargo bay, Ryan clicked his wheel locks into place at the top of the ramp, settled thecherry-sunburst solid-body electric guitar across his lap, and plugged a coiled quarter-inch cable into the shipboard preamp. Beside him, Cassidy clipped a wireless condenser microphone from the console rack and stepped back to the edge of the open hatch, her silhouette framed against the giant golden rings of Vespera.

"Ready on the external transducers, Hols?" Ryan asked, resting his left hand lightly at the second fret while his right thumb hovered over the strings.

Holly tapped a single glowing amber key on her starboard console, switching the *Aethelgard*'s external communications array from standard radio telemetry to full-spectrum acoustic broadcast.

"Hailing frequencies open, Ry," Holly said softly. "120 BPM. Show them who we are."

Ryan didn't play a loud, distorted power chord. Instead, he rolled the volume pot on his guitar back with his pinky finger, engaged the warm analog stereo chorus and dotted-eighth delay that had carried them out of Blacksburg, and struck a single, bell-clear F-sharp major triad—letting the major third ring out into the twilight air of Caelum-IV.

For two beats—*one, two*—the note floated out over the purple sand and the violet water.

On the third beat, the western grove of lavender bio-silica fronds shimmered as a sympathetic harmonic ripple ran from the base of their crystalline stalks all the way to their forty-foot tips, amplifying Ryan's guitar chord into a warm, sustaining cathedral pad.

On the fourth beat, the crimson-and-cyan mycelial veins along the eastern shore pulsed in a synchronous wave of light, and three hundred yards out in the bay, four Vesperan Chorus-Rays breached together beneath the shepherd moons *Lumen* and *Umbra*, answering Ryan's major third with a resonant, low-frequency fifth that locked into the exact pocket of Tyler and Evan's rhythm.

Cassidy raised the microphone to her lips, her eyes looking past the ramp, past the purple waves, and up into the golden rings of Vespera where the stars of an uncharted sector were singing back in a language they finally understood.

They weren't running from *The Cage* anymore. They were home.
