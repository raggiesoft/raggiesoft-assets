The moment Ryan's front pneumatic casters rolled off the beveled titanium lip of the *U.S.S. Aethelgard*'s ramp and touched the surface of Caelum-IV, his hands—and Cassidy's hand resting lightly on his left rear push-handle—told them both everything they needed to know.

Through the anodized aluminum push-rims of his chair, the ground felt like a freshly swept hardwood stage floor laid over solid granite—and in Caelum-IV's natural **`0.88G` (`8.63 m/s²`)** gravity, twelve percent of Earth's familiar downward drag had simply vanished. There was no lateral fishtailing, no sudden resistance trying to pitch his chest forward, and no grinding crunch of loose silica fighting his bearings. Instead, the dark purple basaltic crust yielded by less than a sixteenth of an inch under his rear tires—just enough to leave two clean, parallel tracks in the damp mineral sand—and then returned his kinetic energy with a buoyant, effortless glide.

Right beside his left wheel, Cassidy let out a soft breath of amazement as her red canvas sneakers met the purple sand. On Earth, walking across a beach had always been a nightmare for her cerebral palsy; loose sand shifted unpredictably under her ankles and exaggerated her limp within ten yards. Here, between the firm, slate-smooth basaltic crust, the gentle twelve-percent lift of `0.88G` easing the spastic tension in her calves, and her right hand steadying her balance on Ryan's rubberized wheelchair push-handle like a rolling walker while Ryan propelled them forward with his own hands on the push-rims, she glided down to the water's edge without a single stumble.

Ryan gave the push-rims two long, smooth strokes pitched to match Cassidy's stride, bringing them both to a gentle stop less than three feet from the violet surf.

Behind them, footsteps rang on the metal ramp as Tyler Wright trotted down from the forward flight deck to join Evan and Holly on the shoreline. In the moon's point-eight-eight Earth gravity, every stride had a subtle, unhurried spring to it—close enough to Earth that their balance was second nature, yet light enough that Tyler and Evan practically glided across the purple sand as their boots touched down. For a full ten beats of the planet's quiet pulse, nobody spoke. Tyler and Evan fanned out to Ryan's left toward the rocky outcroppings while Holly stepped up beside Cassidy on the right, letting Ryan and Cassidy share the center of the bay together.

Ryan closed his eyes and tilted his head back slightly, letting his ears map the stereo field of the cove the way he mapped every studio room before touching an amplifier knob.

Back on Earth, outdoor acoustics were almost always chaotic. Wind tore through oak and pine branches in a wash of broadband white noise; surf crashed with jagged, unpredictable transients; distant highway traffic smeared a muddy sixty-to-one-hundred-hertz rumble underneath everything. When Ryan's sensory processing had been pushed past the redline during the worst days of 1992, that wall of unorganized high-frequency hash had felt like sandpaper against his optic nerves.

Caelum-IV had no white noise.

Because the moon was locked in a permanent one-to-one synchronous orbit with Vespera, there were no violent thermal pressure fronts colliding across the ocean—only a steady, laminar tidal breeze drawn inward by the gravitational rhythm of the shepherd moons *Lumen* and *Umbra*. And when that gentle breeze moved through the towering lavender forest framing the left side of the bay, the trees did not rustle.

They *harmonized*.

## The Bio-Silica Synthesizer on the Port Shore

"Listen to the left channel," Ryan said quietly, opening his eyes and nodding toward the western headland fifty yards down the beach. "That's not wind noise. That's a three-two ratio."

Cassidy walked over to stand beside his left wheel, holding her handheld acoustic spectrometer out toward the grove of towering, fern-like structures rising from the purple sand. Each frond stood nearly forty feet tall, curving skyward in graceful, translucent arcs of pale lavender and amethyst. Unlike terrestrial trees built from fibrous cellulose and bark, their stalks and feathery pinnae were spun from flexible **bio-silica**—living, hollow crystal lattices grown from the mineral-rich volcanic soil.

"Look at the FFT waterfall on the scanner, Ry," Cassidy said, angling the glowing cyan display down to wheelchair eye-level so Ryan could read the frequency peaks directly. "The primary stalks are tuned to `92.50 Hz`—a dead-center F-sharp two, matching the *Aethelgard*'s sub-light idle. The secondary fronds above them are resonating at `138.75 Hz` and `185.00 Hz`."

Tyler whistled softly under his breath, squinting up at the shimmering violet stalks. "A root, a pure mathematical fifth, and an octave. No equal-temperament beating. It's a biological analog polysynth pad."

"Why would a forest evolve to hum in fifths?" Evan asked, crouching near the edge of the surf where a translucent ribbon of purple foam curled around the sole of his boot.

"Because there are no violent windstorms or screeching predators on this hemisphere," Holly answered, looking up from the botanical cross-section rendering on her MagnaPad. "On Earth, plants evolved either to rely on insects or to dump billions of pollen grains into turbulent wind currents and hope a few percent land somewhere useful. On Caelum-IV, the breeze is laminar and constant. The bio-silica fronds evolved **acoustic sympathetic resonance**."

She pointed with her stylus toward the tips of the tallest lavender fronds, where tiny, iridescent spores shimmered like suspended gold dust inside the golden-violet ringlight of Vespera.

"When a grove reaches maturity," Holly continued, "the wind passes through the hollow silica flutes and excites a standing wave at a pure fifth above the ground hum. Neighboring fronds half a mile down the coast that share that exact harmonic ratio begin to vibrate in sympathy, opening their micro-valves at the exact same millisecond to receive the spores on the breeze. The forest literally tunes itself to stay alive."

Ryan looked at those towering lavender arches, feeling the warm `92.50 Hz` fundamental vibrating gently through the titanium frame of his chair and up into his ribs. For years, when Cassidy had stood at the vocal mic in their Blacksburg rehearsal room singing the closing lines of Movement IV—*"And the stars are singing / A song I understand"*—they had both thought of it as a metaphor for escaping the noise of an industry that refused to listen.

It wasn't a metaphor anymore. They were sitting inside the chord.

## The Subterranean Handshake on the Starboard Shore

"Look at the ground by Holly's boots," Tyler said suddenly, pointing past Ryan's right wheel toward the eastern edge of the beach where the purple sand met the dark, moss-soft soil of the starboard headland.

Rising along the right side of the bay was a second, completely different architecture of life: a cathedral grove of giant violet **Myco-Towers**. Their broad, umbrella-like mushroom canopies stretched thirty feet across, their undersides ribbed with delicate gills that glowed with a soft, breathing magenta-and-violet bioluminescence against the twilight sky.

Wait—it wasn't just the canopies that were glowing.

Where Holly had stepped off the bare basaltic beach crust onto the edge of the darker soil near the Cargo Hatch's starboard landing strut, a delicate filigree of subterranean root veins had just lit up beneath the surface of the earth. Threads of warm crimson and electric cyan light radiated outward from the imprint of her boot heel, traveling along interlocking geometric nodes like a printed circuit board waking up under smoked glass.

Holly froze automatically—years of range-safety instinct kicking in—and looked down at her feet. "Scanner check, Cass. Did I just step on something fragile?"

Cassidy pivoted to her right, sweeping the spectrometer over the glowing red-and-cyan root network. Instantly, the telemetry readout on both her handheld unit and Holly's MagnaPad chimed with a soft, reassuring emerald indicator:

"You didn't hurt it, Hols," Cassidy said, her voice catching with wonder as she rested her left hand on Ryan's wheelchair push-handle and walked alongside him as Ryan pivoted his chair smoothly across the firm purple sand. Together, brother and sister moved fifteen feet across the beach—Ryan's palms driving the push-rims while Cassidy steadied her limp on his push-handle—until Ryan's front casters and Cassidy's red canvas sneakers rested right at the border where the compacted basalt met the dark mycelial soil.

Carefully, Ryan eased his front wheels two inches over the threshold as Cassidy stepped onto the moss-soft earth beside him.

Instantly, the soil beneath Ryan's casters and Cassidy's sneakers bloomed with a ring of warm crimson light, followed half a beat later by sweeping cyan traces that recognized the exact wheelbase spacing of Ryan's chair and the rhythmic cadence of Cassidy's steps beside it. Unlike the single circular pulse under Holly's boot, the mycelial network beneath Ryan and Cassidy pulsed in an intertwined, symmetrical double-ring—mapping the four contact points of Ryan's manual wheelchair and Cassidy's footsteps steadied against his push-handle as a single, welcome visitor—and sent a ribbon of cyan light racing down the coastline toward the bay.

"It's not an alarm," Ryan said softly, watching the crimson and cyan veins pulse beneath his wheels and Cassidy's sneakers in exact time with the `120.0 BPM` indicator on Holly's pad. "It's a handshake. It's telling the rest of the bay that we just rolled into the room."
