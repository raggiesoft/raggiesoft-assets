## The Delinquent, The Dumpsters, and The Deep Fryer's Due

The memory of the previous afternoon's olfactory assault – a potent souvenir from his first official "date" with the Northwood High dumpsters – clung to Josh Riley like a second, unwelcome skin, impervious to the vigorous scrubbing he’d subjected himself to. As the dismissal bell for Wednesday, their second day of freshman year, echoed through the halls, a stubborn, arrogant thought took root in Josh’s mind: *I am too good for this. I am not spending another afternoon smelling like a skunk’s gym sock that’s been marinated in regret.*

His two companions from the previous day’s "enrichment," Mark (whose last name was lost on Josh) and Ben Carter, looked even more downtrodden than before. They exchanged grim, resigned glances and, like condemned prisoners, began the now-familiar trudge towards the back of the school and their 2:15 PM appointment.

A little ahead of them, already heading with a distinct lack of enthusiasm in the same general direction, was Morgan McAllister. Her own sentence to what she sarcastically dubbed "Advanced Sanitation Studies," thanks to her chronic messiness, was a familiar burden. She still seethed internally at the memory of Josh's lewd proposition and subsequent sexist remarks from the day before.

"You guys coming?" Ben Carter mumbled towards Josh, who was conspicuously *not* following the small trickle of students heading towards various after-school detentions or, in their case, the loading docks.

"Nah," Josh said, trying for an air of nonchalance he didn't feel, his eyes darting towards the main exit. "Got... uh... something else I gotta do. She won't even notice one less freshman." He figured Mrs. DeMarco was just some lunch lady; how much attention could she really pay once the final bell rang and chaos erupted?

He watched Mark and Ben go, then, with a surge of misguided confidence, turned to join the larger throng of students heading for the main exits and the sweet, sweet freedom of the school buses.

The main lobby was a chaotic river of backpacks and shouting teenagers. As Josh tried to merge, looking for all the world like any other student eager to escape, Morgan, who had paused near a group of her own friends also dispersing, noticed his determined path *away* from the direction of the dumpsters. The other students in her group, a couple of juniors who knew Morgan’s "afternoon schedule" well, also spotted him.

One nudged Morgan. "Hey, isn't that the freshman newbie from yesterday? The one who was trying to hit on you by the bins? Looks like he thinks he's making a break for it."

Morgan rolled her eyes, her voice laced with the disdain she now permanently associated with Josh Riley. "Ugh, that idiot. He’s going to get himself so busted." Despite her contempt for him, a small, pragmatic part of her knew that if he caused *more* trouble, it might somehow splash back on everyone currently on Mrs. DeMarco's radar. With a sigh of exasperation, she called out, her voice sharper than intended, "Hey, Riley! Wrong way, moron! The party's out back, remember? You don’t want to be late for your special invitation from Mrs. D!" She even jerked her head pointedly towards the hallway leading to the loading docks, trying to get him to follow.

Her friends snickered. One of the juniors added, a bit more kindly, "Dude, freshman! You sure about that route? Mrs. DeMarco has, like, radar, man. And sonar. And probably a tracking chip in your lunch tater tots. She *always* knows."

Josh barely glanced their way, dismissing them all—Morgan and the warning juniors—with an arrogant little smirk. *Radar? Please. She's just a lunch lady.* He plunged deeper into the crowd, convinced of his cleverness.

He was halfway across the main lobby, weaving through the departing students, already tasting the sweet victory of evasion, when a calm, clear voice cut through the din like a well-honed knife, stopping him dead in his tracks. The chatter around him seemed to dip in volume, a pocket of sudden quiet forming.

"Mr. Riley."

Josh froze. A few students nearby openly stared. Morgan and the juniors who’d tried to warn him exchanged knowing, "told-you-so" glances from across the lobby. Morgan let out an audible groan of "What a tool."

He slowly turned, his heart sinking into his sneakers, which suddenly felt filled with lead. There she was. Mrs. DeMarco, standing near the main office doorway, an almost serene expression on her face, as if she’d been expecting him. She held no list, consulted no notes. She just *knew*. She also gave a brief, almost imperceptible nod towards Morgan, acknowledging her attempt, however disdainful, to redirect the errant freshman.

A wave of hushed whispers rippled through the bunch of students now openly observing the unfolding drama: "Ooh, busted." "DeMarco got him." "He tried to skip!"

"It appears, Mr. Riley," Mrs. DeMarco stated, her voice perfectly even, though it seemed to carry over the entire lobby, "that you've taken a wrong turn. Your colleagues, and indeed Ms. McAllister, are already commencing their duties. Promptly, I might add. Perhaps you require a personal escort to rediscover the path to enlightenment… and our loading docks?"

Josh’s mind raced for an excuse, any excuse, acutely aware of the dozens of eyes now fixed on him. "I... I thought... I wasn't feeling well, Mrs. DeMarco."

"Indeed?" she replied, one perfectly sculpted eyebrow arching slightly. The surrounding students seemed to lean in collectively. "A sudden affliction, then. One that curiously did not manifest until precisely the moment your after-school responsibilities were due to commence. How unfortunate. The fresh air at the back of the school, mingling with the unique terroir of our disposal units, is often quite restorative for such mysterious ailments. Come along."

There was no arguing. Defeated, his brief flicker of rebellion snuffed out, Josh trailed in her wake as she led him, not towards the health office, but directly out the back doors towards his waiting sentence, a silent, ignominious parade of one. Some students snickered; others just shook their heads with a mixture of pity and amusement.

"Rookie mistake," one senior muttered as Josh passed, his voice loud enough for Josh to hear clearly.

Mrs. DeMarco delivered him to the loading docks. Mark, Ben Carter, and a still-disgusted Morgan were already miserably picking up litter. They looked up in surprise, then with a dawning, horrified understanding as Josh was presented before them like a particularly disappointing sacrifice.

"Gentlemen. Ms. McAllister," Mrs. DeMarco addressed the other three, who immediately stood straighter, even Morgan looking slightly less bored. "It seems Mr. Riley here briefly forgot his commitment to our little sanitation seminar. A lapse in memory, perhaps, or a tragically misguided assessment of his obligations to the Northwood High community."

She turned her gaze back to Josh, who was now staring at his shoes, the familiar, potent smell of the dumpsters already reasserting its unwelcome dominion over his senses.

"Mr. Riley," she continued, her voice taking on that new, sterner edge he was beginning to dread, "attempting to evade one's responsibilities is a sign of poor character. It suggests a lack of respect not only for authority but also for the commitments one has made, however reluctantly. Such behavior warrants… a significant re-evaluation and enhancement of the current curriculum."

Josh braced himself. He knew this tone. This wasn't going to be good.

"Therefore," Mrs. DeMarco announced, her expression as placid as if discussing the nutritional benefits of the day's mystery meat, "your participation in standard Trash Duty will, of course, continue as originally scheduled for the remainder of this week. However, to address this new development – your blatant attempt at truancy from your duties – your responsibilities will now also include the thorough cleaning and degreasing of the cafeteria's deep fryer vents and the associated grease traps, commencing tomorrow. This will provide you with a more… shall we say… *immersive* understanding of where cafeteria waste truly originates and the importance of not shirking one's duties."

Mark and Ben Carter visibly recoiled at the mention of grease traps. Morgan actually shuddered, a distant, unpleasant memory perhaps surfacing, or just the sheer imagined horror. Josh felt a wave of genuine nausea.

"Furthermore," Mrs. DeMarco said, her gaze unwavering, "to ensure the lesson regarding the folly of evasion truly takes root and is not merely a fleeting olfactory memory, you will compose an essay. Ten pages, if you please, Mr. Riley. Typed, double-spaced, standard margins. The topic: 'The Inevitability of Consequences and the Folly of Evading Assigned Penance: A Personal Reflection.' It will be due on my desk Monday morning. Any failure to complete this assignment to a satisfactory standard will result in an immediate and indefinite extension of your… practical studies in advanced sanitation."

Josh Riley’s brief flicker of rebellion was not just extinguished; it was ground into a fine, greasy powder. He had not only failed to escape but had managed to triple his misery in front of half the school, and now, his dumpster colleagues. The grease traps. A ten-page essay. He looked at Mrs. DeMarco, who met his gaze with unwavering, unimpressed calm.

"Do we have an understanding, Mr. Riley?"

"Yes, Mrs. DeMarco," he mumbled, the fight completely gone from him, replaced by a crushing wave of despair and the dawning, pungent aroma of his immediate future.

"Excellent." She gave a curt nod. "Then I suggest you begin. Ms. McAllister, Mr. Carter, continue with Dumpster Number One. Mr. \[Mark's last name\], the perimeter. Mr. Riley, the particularly fragrant contents of Dumpster Number Three eagerly await your scholarly attention."

As Josh Riley reluctantly picked up a grabber tool, he was too consumed by his own misery to notice the quiet efficiency that had begun to develop near Dumpster Number One. Morgan McAllister, after her initial disdainful attempt to get Josh to follow her, seemed to have made a pragmatic decision. Josh was clearly a lost cause. Ben Carter, however, looked genuinely miserable and out of his depth; his heart clearly wasn't in the "goon" business.

Josh fumed internally as he saw Morgan and Ben actually *cooperating* on their assigned dumpster. *What, are they an item now or something?* he seethed, his mind immediately supplying crude, sexually suggestive scenarios. *That scrawny Carter and "Morgue" McAllister? Figures she’d go for a loser like him. Probably making plans for a hot date behind the recycling bins when DeMarco’s not looking.* He almost opened his mouth to shout something suitably degrading, but the memory of Mrs. DeMarco's icy pronouncements about his *current* punishment, the looming grease traps, and that ten-page essay on *consequences* was just enough to make him bite back the vile remarks. He didn't dare say anything with Mrs. DeMarco still overseeing their initial efforts from the loading dock.

Instead, Morgan, with her years of experience, directed Ben Carter with a sigh. "Alright, Carter," she said, her voice still holding a general disdain for the situation but lacking the sharp contempt she reserved for Josh. "You look like you might actually know how to tie a knot in a trash bag without crying about it. Let's just get Dumpster Number One sorted so we can breathe air that hasn't been pre-digested by the entire freshman class, okay?"

Ben, surprised to be addressed by his last name with something other than outright scorn, just nodded dumbly, a small spark of relief in his eyes. An unlikely partnership formed. Morgan would point; Ben would retrieve. He’d hold a bag open; she’d efficiently sweep debris into it.

"No, not like that, genius," Morgan might say, though without real heat, if Ben fumbled a bag. "Angle it, or you'll have last Tuesday's tater tots all over your shoes. Again."

Ben would flush but correct his technique. He was, indeed, starting to understand that trash duty, especially under the ever-watchful eye of Mrs. DeMarco and amidst the potent aromas, was definitely not for him. The lingering smell on his clothes was a constant, unpleasant reminder. He glanced over at Josh, who was now actually *kicking* Dumpster Number Three and muttering to himself. *Nope,* Ben thought, *this life isn't for me. Definitely not worth whatever "cool points" Josh thinks he's scoring.*

By the time Mrs. DeMarco finally called an end to their labors for the day, Dumpster Number One, under Morgan and Ben's combined efforts, looked surprisingly tidy. "Adequate work, Ms. McAllister, Mr. Carter," Mrs. DeMarco commented, her gaze lingering perhaps a fraction longer on Ben's diligent effort. "Mr. Riley," she then addressed Josh, who looked even more disheveled and smelled considerably worse, "your progress with Dumpster Number Three, and your upcoming deep fryer vent detailing, will require significantly more… focus… tomorrow. Dismissed, all of you. Be prompt for your next session."

As they walked away, Ben Carter found himself falling into step beside Morgan, a safe distance from Josh, who was already muttering about the unfairness of it all.

"Thanks," Ben mumbled to Morgan, not quite meeting her eye. "For, you know, showing me how to actually do that."

Morgan shrugged, a flicker of something almost like… not quite a smile, but less of a sneer, on her face. "Whatever. Just don't be a slob at lunch tomorrow, okay? And maybe rethink your choice of 'friends.' Some of us are trying to graduate from this aromatic apprenticeship, not make it a permanent career."

Ben nodded quickly. "Yeah. Right. I hear you." He made a mental note: sit far away from Josh Riley at lunch. And maybe even try to use the trash can correctly for once. The thought of avoiding this fate again was a powerful motivator. He didn't want to become a fixture out here like Riley clearly was.

The unlikely duo of the messy senior girl and the reluctant freshman goon parted ways, both perhaps contemplating paths to a less fragrant after-school existence. Josh Riley, however, was already plotting his revenge on the unfairness of the universe, the grease traps, and the ten-page essay, blissfully unaware that his "apprenticeship" was already inspiring different choices in others – just not in himself. His high school career was off to a truly spectacular, and very public, start.

## A Humble Request and a Changing Tide

The acrid memory of grease traps and the lingering phantom smell of Dumpster Number Three were still fresh in Josh Riley's tormented senses. He was, for the moment, actually attempting to complete his ten-page essay on "The Inevitability of Consequences" in the school library, a picture of sullen martyrdom. His usual goon, Mark, was wisely giving him a wide berth.

Meanwhile, an entirely different scene was unfolding near Mrs. DeMarco’s small, tidy office just off the main cafeteria.

Morgan McAllister and Ben Carter stood there a little awkwardly, the usual lunchtime cacophony a dull roar behind them. They’d had another "talk" during their shared sanitation duties the previous afternoon – mostly about the sheer unpleasantness of the task, the lingering aroma on their clothes, and a mutual, unspoken agreement that Josh Riley was, in fact, a complete tool. Ben had found himself surprisingly comfortable talking to Morgan, or at least working alongside her in companionable, non-harassing silence. She was no-nonsense, and after her initial disdain, she'd even shown him a more efficient way to bag leaky trash without getting it on his sneakers. He was beginning to genuinely appreciate her straightforwardness, a stark contrast to Josh's manipulative bluster. He realized he really *didn't* want to be like Josh.

Morgan, for her part, found Ben’s quiet diligence a welcome change from Josh's predatory idiocy. Working with Ben made the "aromatic apprenticeship" slightly less soul-destroying. He didn't leer, he didn't make crude remarks, he just... worked. It had made her think. Maybe, just maybe, there was a way out of her "Morgue McAllister, Queen of the Mess" reputation.

Taking a deep breath, Morgan knocked lightly on Mrs. DeMarco’s open office door. Ben stood a step behind her, looking nervous but resolute.

Mrs. DeMarco looked up from a stack of order forms, her expression neutral but her eyes sharp. "Ms. McAllister. Mr. Carter. An unexpected pleasure. To what do I owe this visit? I trust you’re not here to volunteer for additional grease trap detailing – Mr. Riley has that particular honor well in hand for the foreseeable future."

Morgan flushed slightly but stood her ground. "No, Mrs. DeMarco. Actually… um… we wanted to ask you something. Seriously."

Ben Carter found his voice, surprising himself. "Yeah, Mrs. DeMarco. Some… advice, maybe?"

Mrs. DeMarco leaned back in her chair, a flicker of something unreadable – perhaps intrigue – in her eyes. "Advice? This is a new development. Please, continue."

Morgan took the lead. "Well, Mrs. DeMarco, you know I’m… usually out back with the bins. Because I’m messy. I get it." She swallowed, then rushed on. "But I really, *really* don’t want to be out there anymore. Especially if… well, if *certain people* are going to be permanent fixtures." The unspoken reference to Josh Riley hung in the air. "So, I was wondering… could you maybe give me some tips? On how to… you know… not be a slob at lunch? How to actually clean up my area so it meets… your standards?" She looked genuinely earnest, a hint of desperation in her plea.

Mrs. DeMarco regarded Morgan thoughtfully for a moment. Then, she turned her gaze to Ben. "And you, Mr. Carter? What 'life tips' do you seek? I trust you're not looking for pointers on how to better assist Mr. Riley in his… unique brand of social interaction?"

Ben paled slightly at the memory of Josh's lunchtime antics, then straightened up. "No, ma'am. Definitely not. Actually, that's kind of it. I… I don't want to be like him. I don't want to be a goon, or a wannabe bully. This whole trash duty thing… it’s really not for me, Mrs. DeMarco. And Morgan here," he gestured towards her, a newfound respect in his tone, "she was showing me how to actually do the work yesterday without… well, without making it worse. She’s not like Riley at all, even if she is messy. She made me realize… I don’t want that title."

He took a breath. "So, I guess my question is… how does a guy stop being seen as just… Josh Riley’s dumb goon?"

A long silence filled the small office. Mrs. DeMarco’s expression was still unreadable, but her sharp gaze seemed to soften almost imperceptibly as she looked from Ben’s sincere, anxious face to Morgan’s determined one. She’d seen countless students in her tenure – the defiant, the clueless, the truly malicious. But this… this was different. A genuine request for guidance from two students on the cusp of becoming "habitual offenders" themselves.

Finally, Mrs. DeMarco spoke, her voice surprisingly gentle, though still firm.

"Ms. McAllister," she began, "the path to a tidier lunchtime existence is paved with conscious effort. It begins with awareness. Notice what you bring to the table. Notice what you leave behind. Use the provided receptacles – they are not merely decorative. A damp napkin to wipe spills immediately is often more effective than hoping they will magically evaporate. And a simple scan of your area before you depart… it takes perhaps ten seconds. I am happy to provide you with a laminated checklist of common 'Points of Post-Meal Neglect' if you believe it would be beneficial." A ghost of a smile touched her lips. "Consider it… preventative maintenance."

Morgan actually looked relieved. "A checklist? Yes, please, Mrs. DeMarco. That would… actually help. A lot."

Mrs. DeMarco nodded, then turned to Ben. "Mr. Carter. Detaching oneself from negative associations requires consistent demonstration of positive behavior. You are not defined by Mr. Riley’s poor choices unless you choose to continue emulating them. Choose your companions more wisely. Speak with respect. Act with integrity. Offer assistance to those who need it, rather than ridicule. These are not complex edicts, Mr. Carter, but simple principles of decent human conduct. They are, however, principles Mr. Riley seems pathologically incapable of grasping."

She paused. "Your actions today, Mr. Carter – your diligence at the dumpsters yesterday, and your choice to seek advice rather than follow Mr. Riley down his current aromatic path – speak well of your potential. As do yours, Ms. McAllister, in your desire for self-improvement, however recently acquired."

Morgan looked at Ben, a small, almost shy smile now. Maybe this kid Carter wasn't so bad after all. He seemed to genuinely get it.

Then Morgan, with a sudden boldness that surprised even herself, gestured vaguely towards the cafeteria. "So, Mrs. DeMarco… about the 'title' of Habitual Offender… if Ben and I are, you know, trying to reform… This new kid?" she jerked her thumb in the general direction of where Josh Riley was likely still wrestling with his essay. "He can have my title. With my compliments. I’m officially abdicating."

Ben Carter grinned, a genuine, relieved grin. "Yeah, what she said! He can be King of the Dumpsters all by himself."

For the first time, Mrs. DeMarco allowed a full, if fleeting, smile. "Your abdication is noted, Ms. McAllister. And Mr. Carter, your desire for a different path is commendable. The cafeteria, and indeed the loading dock, always benefits from fewer… 'repeat customers.' Now, if you'll excuse me, I have lunch orders to verify." She gave them a look that was both a dismissal and, perhaps, a sliver of approval. "I trust I will see a marked improvement in both your lunchtime deportment and your post-meal clean-up efforts?"

"Yes, Mrs. DeMarco!" they both said, almost in unison, a wave of unexpected hope washing over them.

As they left her office, stepping back into the lunchtime roar, both Morgan and Ben felt a little lighter. Josh Riley’s sexually suggestive thoughts about them working together, had he voiced them, would have seemed even more ridiculous now. Their "teamwork" by the dumpsters hadn't been about secret trysts; it had been about shared misery leading to an unexpected, shared desire for something better. Josh could have the dumpsters, the grease traps, and the infamy. Morgan McAllister and Ben Carter were aiming for a future that smelled a whole lot sweeter.
