## The Video Call Interruption

The late afternoon sun of Fall 2003 cast long shadows across Lisa Court as Vera’s Holt Automotive sedan pulled up in front of the Miller house. This was it – her first visit to Alex’s home.

Emily Miller opened the door with a warm, welcoming smile. "Vera, dear, come in! Alex has been looking forward to you coming over." James Miller looked up from his armchair, offering a polite nod.

"Thank you so much for having me, Mrs. Miller," Vera said, her voice exuding charm as she presented Emily with a small potted chrysanthemum.

Alex watched as Vera followed his mother towards the kitchen, already making herself agreeable. He knew his scheduled video call with Chloé was coming up soon. A few minutes later, as Vera and Emily chatted, Alex navigated his wheelchair to the desk where his computer was set up. He opened the video call application, his fingers moving with familiar speed.

The living room was soon filled with the cheerful, familiar sound of Chloé Mason’s voice. Their conversation was light and easy, a cherished ritual that bridged the miles.

"...so then Professor Abernathy actually tried to explain quantum entanglement using sock puppets!" Chloé was saying from her dorm room, laughing. "It was… memorable!"

Alex grinned, typing quickly on his AAC: HE DID NOT! SOCK PUPPETS? THAT IS AMAZING.

Vera, who had been ostensibly helping Emily, appeared in the living room doorway. "Alex," her voice cut in sharply. "Are you still on the phone with *her*? We were supposed to watch that movie I brought over."

Alex startled, his smile faltering. JUST FINISHING UP, VERA, he typed.

Chloé, on screen, picked up on the sudden tension. "Hey Alex, no worries if you need to go! We can talk later, okay?"

"No, it's fine," Vera said, her voice dripping with false sweetness as she positioned herself in the webcam's view. "I just think it's a little rude, Alex, to ignore your *actual* girlfriend for some long-distance high school buddy."

Alex felt a flush of embarrassment. Instinctively, hoping to create a small space of comfort, he switched his AAC to its Québécois French voice: CHLOÉ, MA BELLE AMIE, VERA EST UN PEU… TENDUE AUJOURD'HUI. (Chloé, my dear friend, Vera is a little… tense today.)

Chloé, understanding perfectly, immediately responded in kind. "*Pas de problème, Alex, mon cher. Prends ton temps.*" (No problem, Alex, my dear. Take your time.)

Vera, hearing the sudden switch to a language she didn’t understand, looked furious. "What the hell? What are you two even saying? Alex, speak English! This is rude!"

At this point, Emily and Sophie Mason, who had been working together in the kitchen, intervened. Emily walked into the living room, a warm, unruffled smile on her face. "*Chère Chloé, bonjour! Comment vas-tu à Lynnwood?*" she said, her own learned Québécois surprisingly warm and natural. (Dear Chloé, hello! How are you in Lynnwood?)

Sophie followed, wiping her hands on an apron. "*On est en train de préparer une surprise pour le souper, ma belle! Une vraie poutine!*" (We're making a surprise for dinner, my sweet! Real poutine!)

Vera stared, completely bewildered. They all spoke French?

Chloé, on screen, let out a gasp of pure, ecstatic delight. "*Pas possible! Maman, vous avez utilisé les bons fromages en grains? Ceux qu'on a rapportés de Stanstead?*" (No way! Mom, did you use the good cheese curds? The ones we brought back from Stanstead?)

"*Bien sûr!*" Sophie confirmed proudly. (Of course!)

James Miller then stepped forward, also addressing Chloé in comfortable Québécois French. "Chloé, *ma fille*, it's good to see your face. Alex, son, please, don't let Vera interrupt your call. You continue chatting with Chloé. Take all the time you need. We don't care if it's in Québécois French or American English – though, frankly, Sophie and Thomas have taught us so well, we rather enjoy hearing the French!" He winked.

Vera stared at the Millers, then at Alex, her face a mask of pure indignation and suspicion. This "Chloé" wasn't just some distant friend; she was deeply enmeshed with Alex's entire family, to the point of a shared private language and secret, special food. The feeling of being an outsider, of being actively excluded, was palpable.

"What is going on?" Vera demanded in American English, her voice shrill. "Since when does everyone in this house speak French except me?"

James Miller turned to Vera, his expression now quite stern. "Vera," he said, "Alex is allowed to speak with his best friend Chloé, who is like family to us, in whatever language they are both comfortable with. It is not 'rude'; it is their connection, and we encourage it."

Emily then addressed Vera, her polite smile not quite reaching her eyes. "Vera, why don't you come with James and me into the other room? You can tell us about that movie you brought. Alex and Chloé will join us when they're done." Her tone was pleasant, but it was a clear, non-negotiable directive.

Vera looked from James's unyielding expression to Emily's calm but firm one. She saw no allies here. They weren't falling for her victim act. With a huff and a venomous glare at Chloé's smiling image on the screen, Vera stalked out of the room, James and Emily following.

Alex let out a shaky breath, the tension slowly ebbing. His parents… they had seen. They had stepped in. They had defended him. He turned back to the computer screen, where Chloé was waiting, her expression a mixture of concern, relief, and wry amusement.

MERCI D'AVOIR ATTENDU! he typed, his relief palpable. MES PARENTS SONT LES MEILLEURS! VERA EST… TRÈS DIFFICILE AUJOURD'HUI. (THANKS FOR WAITING! MY PARENTS ARE THE BEST! VERA IS… VERY DIFFICULT TODAY.)

Chloé’s laughter, warm and genuine, filled the speakers again. "*Je vois ça, Alex! Tes parents sont magnifiques! Alors, la poutine...*" (I see that, Alex! Your parents are magnificent! So, about that poutine...)

Their conversation resumed, Alex feeling a profound sense of gratitude. Vera had made a scene, but in doing so, she had only revealed her own unpleasant nature further and solidified the Millers' understanding that Chloé was, indeed, a far more positive and connected presence in their son's life. The lines had been drawn, and Alex's loving parents had made it unequivocally clear on which side they stood.

## A Dinner of Dissent

An hour later, the five of them were gathered around the Millers' dining table. In the center of the table, on a small stand, the MagnaByte laptop was open, Chloé's smiling face a warm, digital presence. She had her own small meal, a simple salad, ready to eat alongside them from her dorm room three thousand miles away.

The main event arrived with a flourish. Sophie and Emily emerged from the kitchen, carrying a massive platter. It was a beautiful, authentic poutine—a mountain of golden-brown french fries, dotted with fresh, squeaky cheese curds, all smothered in a rich, dark gravy.

POUTINE! Alex's Quantum Communicator XT blared, its synthesized voice full of pure, unadulterated joy.

Thomas Mason's face lit up. "*Ah, magnifique!*" he declared.

Vera, however, just stared at the platter. She remembered Chloé's excited question from the video call: "*Did you use the good cheese curds? The ones we brought back from Stanstead?*" This wasn't just dinner; this was *Chloé food*. It was a taste of Québec, a symbol of the deep, shared history that connected this entire family, a history she was not a part of.

Emily, a gracious hostess, served everyone a generous portion. When she got to Vera, she offered the serving spoon with a polite smile. "Vera, dear? You have to try some. It's a family favorite."

Vera's own smile was a mask of polite, theatrical regret. "Oh, it looks... wonderful," she said, her voice dripping with a false sincerity. "But I couldn't possibly. All that grease and gravy... my stomach is a little sensitive. I think I'll just have a small salad, if that's okay."

The refusal, as polite as it was, landed on the table with the force of a thrown gauntlet. Alex, who was already halfway through his first, delicious bite, stopped, a flicker of confusion on his face. The four parents and Chloé, on the screen, all exchanged a single, knowing look. They saw the move for what it was: a quiet, passive-aggressive act of protest.

Vera watched, her own internal monologue a bitter, venomous stream. *Look at him. Shoveling in that disgusting, foreign food. He loves it. He loves her food. He loves her.* She forced herself to maintain her polite facade. The memory of the late-night rescue, of Megan Carter's cold, dismissive fury, was still fresh in her mind. She would not make another tactical error. She would be the perfect, polite guest, even as she seethed on the inside.

The family, for their part, continued their meal, their conversation warm and bilingual. The parents' quiet, loving attempt to drive Vera away with a taste of their shared culture had not worked, not exactly. She was still here. But her refusal to partake in their family's comfort food had drawn a new, clearer line in the sand. It was a quiet, delicious, and deeply symbolic battle, and Vera, with her polite refusal and her bowl of plain lettuce, had just decisively lost.

## The Slow Encroachment

The weeks following the "Video Call Interruption" saw a subtle but decisive shift. Vera, having been so thoroughly shut down by the Millers' united, bilingual front, changed her tactics. She stopped trying to confront Alex's connection to Chloé at his house and instead began to pull him more and more into her own world.

"It's just easier at my place in Portsmouth, sweetie," she'd say, her voice full of a sweet reason that was hard to argue with. "My mom's out most of the time, so we have real privacy. We don't have to worry about... interruptions."

Alex, still smarting from the awkwardness of the video call and desperate to avoid any more conflict, found himself agreeing. He began spending more and more time at her apartment. At first, it was just after their shared class. Then, it was entire afternoons. Soon, he was staying for dinner, and then, more and more frequently, he was spending the entire weekend there.

His academic work at CCHR began to suffer. An assignment he should have spent Saturday working on was forgotten in the haze of a movie marathon with Vera. A study session was cut short because Vera wanted to go out. He was still passing, but the sharp, focused edge of his intellect was being dulled by the constant distraction.

The sexual intimacy, which had at first felt thrilling and new, was becoming the central, almost transactional, part of their time together. If he seemed distant or sad, thinking of Chloé, Vera's solution was always physical. "Don't be sad, Alex," she'd murmur, her hands already on him. "Let me make you feel better. I can give you everything you need, right here."

He was beginning to feel a deep, gnawing misery, a sense of being trapped. But he was terrified. He still had to see her in their Student Development class every week, and the thought of facing her anger after a breakup was daunting. But the deeper fear, the one that kept him paralyzed, was the thought of being alone again. This miserable connection felt better than no connection at all.

One evening, as they were lying in her bed, Vera brought up a new idea, her voice casual. "You know, Alex, this driving back and forth is getting to be a pain. And my mom is thinking of moving soon. I was thinking... maybe we should look into getting our own place. And you know, CCHR has that big campus right here in Portsmouth. We could take all our classes together next semester. Wouldn't that be great?"

She then added, her voice taking on a dismissive, almost pitying tone, "And then you wouldn't have to waste so much time on the phone with that mousy little friend of yours. Why do you even still talk to her so much, anyway? Shouldn't I be enough for you?"

The words—"mousy," "waste so much time"—were a direct, venomous assault on the most important person in his life. A flicker of his old, protective anger flared, but it was quickly extinguished by the cold wave of fear. He was in her bed, in her apartment, miles from his own home. He was trapped. And in that moment, he knew, with a sickening certainty, that he was in very, very deep trouble.

## An Unstable Connection

Vera's campaign of isolation was subtle but relentless. The cherished nightly video calls with Chloé, once a sacred, protected ritual, became a new battleground.

If Alex was at Vera's apartment, she would invent reasons why the call couldn't happen. "Oh, sweetie, the internet is acting up tonight," she'd say with a dismissive wave of her hand. Or, "I was really hoping we could just have a night to ourselves, Alex. No distractions."

When he was at his own home, she would often "coincidentally" call his cell phone right in the middle of his video call with Chloé, creating a jarring interruption he couldn't ignore. The calls that did happen were often strained, with Vera hovering just off-camera, her presence a silent, judgmental weight that made it impossible for Alex to speak freely.

From her dorm room in Lynnwood, Chloé saw it all. She saw the new, constant tension in Alex's shoulders. She saw the way his eyes would dart nervously off-screen. She saw how their easy, flowing conversations had become stilted and guarded. She knew, with a certainty that made her heart ache with a cold, helpless anger, that Vera was deliberately sabotaging their connection. But she refused to give up. She kept calling, kept her tone light and cheerful, kept being the steady, reliable presence she had always been, a quiet, digital promise that she was still there, waiting for him.

Alex, for his part, clung to these fractured connections like a lifeline. Each call, however strained, was a reminder of the safety and unconditional love that was waiting for him. He was miserable, he was scared, but he had a plan. He just had to hold on until Christmas break. When Chloé came home, when they were all together, he would find the courage. He would ask his parents, and Chloé, to help him. He would finally, finally, end things with Vera. He just had to survive until then.

## A Lifeline from Lynnwood

The constant interruptions and the growing strain of Vera's presence were taking their toll on Alex. He missed the easy, uninterrupted conversations with Chloé with an ache that was a constant, physical presence. He knew he needed to end things with Vera, but the thought of the confrontation, of her anger, and of the subsequent, crushing loneliness, was paralyzing.

One afternoon, he worked up the courage for a small act of rebellion. He told Vera he needed to stay home on Lisa Court to work on a major project for his programming class, a plausible excuse that she reluctantly accepted. The moment he was alone in his own living room, the sense of peace was so profound it almost made him dizzy. He looked at the clock. It was time for his call with Chloé.

When her face appeared on the screen, bright and beloved, he felt a wave of relief so intense it was almost painful. For the first time in weeks, there was no Vera hovering, no threat of a sudden interruption.

"Hey, Alex!" she said, her smile warm but her eyes full of a quiet concern. "It's so good to see you. Really see you. How are you?"

He typed, his fingers flying, the words a torrent of pent-up frustration and fear. I HATE THIS, CHLOÉ. I HATE IT. VERA... SHE'S... TOO MUCH. I DON'T KNOW WHAT TO DO.

Chloé’s expression softened with a deep, aching empathy. She had been waiting for this, hoping he would finally feel safe enough to tell her the truth. "I know, Alex," she said softly. "I can see it. She's trying to cut you off from everyone."

She took a deep breath, then a new, determined light entered her eyes. "Alex," she began, "I have something to show you. Something I've been working on. I was waiting for the right time, but I think the right time is now."

She shared her screen. A moment later, a familiar webpage appeared: the official registration portal for the Community College of Hampton Roads. And on the screen was her official class schedule for the Spring 2004 semester.

Alex stared, his heart stopping for a beat. English Composition. Calculus I. General Biology with Lab. All at the Virginia Beach campus.

CHLOÉ... WHAT IS THIS? he typed, his fingers trembling.

"It's my new plan," she said, her voice full of a quiet, fierce conviction. "My dream school isn't a place, Alex. It's you. I can't do this for two years. I'm coming home." She pointed to the screen. "I've already been accepted. I've already registered. And I picked these classes because I know you'll need to take them too. Here," she said, bringing up a text document, "are the Class ID numbers. If you want... we can have the same schedule. Just like in high school."

Alex stared at the screen, at the tangible, undeniable proof of her plan, of her unwavering commitment to him. It was a lifeline, a rescue mission, a future he hadn't dared to hope for, delivered through a video screen. The fear that had been paralyzing him, the fear of being alone, was instantly obliterated by the fierce, powerful truth of her love. He wasn't going to be alone. Chloé was coming home.

***

## Vera's \

The video call with Chloé left Alex with a profound, secret hope. For the first time in months, he had a concrete, tangible end date for his misery: Christmas break. The knowledge that Chloé was coming home for good was a small, bright flame he carefully guarded in the cold landscape of his life with Vera.

He became a better actor. He was more placid, more agreeable. He was counting down the days. Vera, completely unaware of the real reason, misinterpreted his newfound calm. She saw it as proof that *her* influence was finally working, that she was successfully "rescuing" him from the poison of his loneliness and the lingering influence of his "mousy" friend.

This perceived success fed her own toxic need. She didn't just want him; she believed she *needed* him to be whole. Her mission to "fix" his life, to make him hers completely, intensified. The sexual encounters, which had become a frequent, transactional part of their relationship, now became an almost daily occurrence. For Vera, it was a tool of control, a physical reaffirmation of her ownership. For Alex, it was a miserable, hollow act he endured to keep the peace, to avoid an argument, to simply get through one more day until Chloé came home.

He was miserable, but Vera saw a happier, more compliant boyfriend. She didn't know his quiet smiles were because he was thinking of Chloé's CCHR schedule. She didn't know his calm was the patience of a prisoner waiting for a liberation he knew was coming. She saw a victory, and decided it was time for the final, grand gesture of her "rescue."

## A Secret Rebellion

A few days after his life-changing video call with Chloé, Vera was in a particularly "helpful" mood. "Alex, sweetie," she said, sitting beside him as he was on the CCHR website, "I was thinking. We should really get our spring schedules aligned. It'll be so much easier if we're on the same campus."

She leaned over, pointing to the screen. "Look, the Portsmouth campus has a great English 101 section, and a history class we could take together. It's so much closer to my place. Let's sign you up for these."

Alex felt a familiar wave of dread, but it was now mixed with a new, secret resolve. He knew he couldn't fight her openly, not yet. He had to play along. He let her guide him through the registration, selecting the classes at the Portsmouth campus that she wanted. To placate her, he even printed out the "official" schedule and showed it to her.

"See?" Vera said, beaming, taking the piece of paper as a trophy. "So much better. We're going to have a wonderful semester together."

Later that evening, after Vera had left, Alex wheeled himself back to his computer. His heart was pounding, but his purpose was clear. He logged back into the CCHR portal. With swift, deliberate keystrokes, he dropped every single one of the Portsmouth classes Vera had chosen for him.

Then, he pulled up the text document Chloé had sent him, the one with the Class ID numbers. One by one, he carefully entered the numbers, registering for the exact same English, Calculus, and Biology classes at the Virginia Beach campus that she was enrolled in. When he was done, he had a new schedule. A real schedule. *Their* schedule.

With a final, furtive glance towards the door, he opened his email, attached a screenshot of his new, correct schedule, and sent it to Chloé with a simple, powerful subject line: "See you in class."

Vera was none the wiser. Alex was still trapped for now, but he had just executed his first, silent, and deeply satisfying act of rebellion. He wasn't just waiting to be rescued anymore. He was actively planning his own liberation.
