## The Relic

A sharp, mechanical chirp from the hauler’s primary console broke the silence of the cabin.

Wyatt blinked the sleep from his eyes, his taped hand still locked firmly with Sarah’s across the center console. The green *TRANSIT SAFE* light on the dashboard flipped to a blinking amber. Proximity alert. They had arrived.

He gently squeezed Sarah’s hand. "Hey. We're here."

She stirred, letting out a soft breath as she opened her eyes. She blinked at the amber light, then turned her head to look out the thick, scratch-resistant viewport.

Floating in the endless, freezing void of deep space was Outpost Delta-Niner.

It didn't look like the sleek, silver corporate hubs featured in the Apex Defense Solutions recruitment vids. It looked like a deep-sea oil rig that had been violently ripped from the ocean and hurled into the stars. It was modular, blocky, and completely asymmetrical—a cluster of heavy steel cylinders and pressurized shipping containers bolted around a central hub. Thick, exposed bundles of cabling wrapped around the hull like the exposed veins of a massive machine.

Above the primary module, a massive, physical parabolic radar dish slowly, mechanically rotated, hunting for telemetry signals in the dark. The hull's ablative armor was scorched, pockmarked by decades of micrometeorite impacts, and painted in a faded, oxidized hazard-yellow.

"It looks like it's going to fall apart," Sarah whispered, her eyes wide as she took in the rusted, archaic beast of a station.

"It's not," Wyatt told her, a slow, genuine grin spreading across his face. He recognized this kind of engineering from his days on the deep-sea rigs. "It's pure, heavy-duty analog. No delicate quantum processors or fragile glass touchscreens. It’s built out of cast iron and copper wire. If it breaks, you don't need a software patch; you just hit it with a wrench. She'll take care of us."

The cargo hauler's automated nav-computer took over, guiding the ship toward the protruding airlock.

There was no smooth, magnetic docking sequence. The hauler slammed into the docking ring with a violent, metallic *CLANG* that rattled Wyatt’s teeth. Heavy physical clamps engaged outside the hull with a series of deafening, hydraulic thuds.

The console flashed green. *PRESSURIZATION COMPLETE.*

Wyatt hit the heavy release switch on his harness, the thick straps retracting into the seat. He unbuckled Sarah, stepping out of the cockpit and heading into the narrow cargo hold to grab their canvas duffel bags.

He keyed the airlock. The heavy steel door groaned, protesting the movement before sliding open with a loud hiss of equalized pressure.

Wyatt stepped through the threshold onto Outpost Delta-Niner, Sarah right right behind him.

The station woke up to greet them. Caged fluorescent tubes embedded in the low ceiling flickered, buzzing like angry hornets before casting the narrow corridor in a warm, yellowish light. The air scrubbers kicked on with a deep, bass-heavy rumble. The air didn't smell like the ozone and blood of Sector 4; it smelled like warm dust, heated copper wire, and, faintly, decades-old stale coffee.

"I'm going to find the quarters," Sarah said, dropping her heavy duffel bag onto the grated floor. She immediately reached for the collar of her scavenged S.H.O.P. Marine armor. "But first, I am getting out of this straitjacket."

Wyatt leaned against the heavy bulkhead, watching with a soft, appreciative smile as she unzipped the stiff, black utility shirt. She shed the heavy armor right there in the corridor, leaving the oversized uniform pooled on the floor. She unzipped her duffel, pulling out the crisp, bright fabric of her slate-blue Helios Field Safety Technician uniform.

She pulled the breathable shirt over her head, adjusting the familiar collar with a massive sigh of relief. She smoothed out the cargo pants, her posture instantly relaxing. She wasn't playing the part of a dead-eyed grunt anymore. She was just Sarah.

"Better?" Wyatt asked, his voice a low rumble in the quiet station.

"So much better," she breathed out, walking over and wrapping her arms around his waist, burying her face in his chest. "Find the command center. I'll go find us a bed that's wider than thirty inches."

Wyatt kissed the top of her head, completely savoring the absolute privacy of the moment, before letting her go.

He carried his duffel down the primary corridor, following the faded yellow painted lines on the floor until he reached a set of heavy double doors marked *OPERATIONS*. He pushed them open.

The command center was a technological graveyard. There were no sleek holographic tables. The consoles were angled steel banks covered in massive, illuminated square buttons in primary reds, yellows, and whites. Heavy metal toggle switches lined the communication boards.

Wyatt walked up to the primary terminal. Built directly into the steel housing was a massive, glass-tubed CRT monitor. He reached out and flipped the heavy main power switch. It engaged with a loud, incredibly satisfying *CLACK*.

The CRT monitor warmed up with a high-pitched whine. Static washed over the curved glass before resolving into a solid, glowing amber interface. Visible scanlines pulsed down the screen.

Suddenly, the heavy tape reels in the server bank behind the console began to spin wildly. The station's dot-matrix printer, bolted to the side of the desk, violently jerked to life. *Chk-chk-chk-chk.* Wyatt frowned, stepping back as the printer fed a sheet of continuous, perforated paper out of the slot. On the amber CRT screen, a single, glowing cursor blinked, overriding the standard Apex boot sequence.

Lines of blocky, green text began to cascade down the monitor.

**ENCRYPTED OVERRIDE: EXECUTIVE LEVEL ZERO.** **TO: CONTRACTOR COLTON, W. / CONTRACTOR HAYES, S.** **IF YOU ARE READING THIS, THE NTTR CONTAINMENT HAS FAILED.**

Wyatt’s blood ran cold. He leaned closer to the curved glass, his eyes scanning the amber text.

**THE AUTOMATED HR ALGORITHM DID NOT DEPLOY YOU TO THIS OUTPOST. I DID. THE CAMERA IN FILTRATION BREAK-ROOM 4-C WAS NEVER BROKEN. IT WAS AN ISOLATED ANALOG FEED. I WATCHED YOU.** **IN A FACILITY BUILT TO STRIP AWAY EVERY OUNCE OF HUMANITY, YOU TWO FOUND A WAY TO HOLD ONTO IT. I COULD NOT LET THAT BURN WITH THE REST OF THEM. I DELETED YOUR BUNK ASSIGNMENTS TO KEEP YOU OFF THE PRIMARY GRID. I FORCED THE SYSTEM TO ROUTE YOU HERE.**

Wyatt stared at the screen, the breath catching in his throat. It hadn't been luck. It hadn't been a glitch. As the executive hierarchy of Apex panicked and abandoned the facility, some high-level architect—someone who had the keys to the entire kingdom—had sat in a locked room, watched them fall in love, and decided that their survival was the only thing worth saving.

The final block of text typed out slowly, the amber glow reflecting in Wyatt's eyes.

**OUTPOST DELTA-NINER IS ENTIRELY OFF THE NETWORK. NO CAMERAS. NO OVERSEERS. THE MASTER ACCESS CODES ARE ATTACHED TO THIS FILE. THE STATION IS YOURS.** **SURVIVE. AND TAKE CARE OF EACH OTHER.** **- THE ARCHITECT.**

The screen flashed once, locking the master codes into the terminal's physical memory banks, and then returned to a standard, blinking command prompt.

Wyatt stood in the quiet hum of the command center, the weight of what had just happened settling over him. They hadn't just escaped. They had been deliberately, miraculously spared.

"Wyatt?"

He turned around. Sarah was standing in the doorway, her slate-blue Helios uniform looking bright and vibrant against the rusted, industrial walls of the station. She had a soft, brilliant smile on her face.

"I found the captain's quarters," she said softly. "It has a real door. And a real bed."

Wyatt looked from the amber glow of the terminal back to the woman he loved. He reached out, flipping the heavy master-lock toggle switch on the console. The station gave a low, affirming mechanical thud as the blast shields slid into place over the exterior airlocks.

They were locked in. They were completely safe.

"Lead the way," he told her.

## The Captain's Quarters

Wyatt turned away from the glowing amber CRT monitor and the heavy silence of the command center. He followed Sarah down the narrow, curved corridor of Outpost Delta 9.

As they walked, Wyatt ran his taped hand along the heavy, brushed-steel bulkheads. You could tell just by looking at the architecture that when this station was first launched into the dark, it wasn't a forgotten relic; it was the absolute bleeding edge of corporate technology. Decades ago, Delta 9 had been the crown jewel of Apex's frontier expansion. It was built with pride, designed to be a state-of-the-art, impenetrable fortress at the edge of the known territories.

Sarah stopped at the end of the corridor in front of a heavy, pressurized door marked *QUARTERS - COMMANDER*. She didn't have to scan a biometric tracker or wait for an algorithm to grant her permission. She just reached out, gripped the heavy mechanical latch, and pulled it down with a satisfying metallic *clank*.

The door slid open with a pneumatic hiss. Wyatt stepped into the room behind her, and for a moment, they both just stopped and stared.

It was a massive upgrade from anything Apex Defense Solutions offered its modern workforce. The room was spacious, lined with retro-futuristic wood-grain laminate and heavy, carpeted flooring that completely muted the metallic hum of the station. Built into the wall was a personal viewport, currently shielded by a heavy, manual blast shutter.

But the centerpiece of the room was the bed.

It was a massive, genuine queen-sized mattress set into a recessed, padded alcove. It was flanked by analog control panels with heavy yellow and white toggle switches for the room's lighting and temperature. It was made up with thick, real fabric blankets, not the stiff, scratchy material of a corporate deployment bag.

Wyatt let out a low, disbelieving breath. This room was built for a high-ranking pioneer, a Captain or a Director who demanded luxury while overseeing the edge of civilization. It was explicitly, undeniably *not* designed for a minimum-wage S.H.O.P. Marine and a Helios Field Safety Technician.

And it certainly wasn't designed for two terrified grunts who had fallen hopelessly in love in forty-eight hours.

Sarah walked over to the edge of the mattress. In her bright, slate-blue Helios uniform, she looked entirely out of place in the dark, heavy-duty military aesthetics of the station, but Wyatt thought she had never looked more perfect.

She pressed her hand into the mattress. It actually gave way, soft and supportive. She looked back at Wyatt, her eyes shining with a mix of exhaustion and sheer, unadulterated joy.

"It's a real bed," she whispered, a breathless laugh escaping her lips. "Wyatt, we don't have to sleep on a cracked vinyl sofa. We don't have to share thirty inches of a Twin XL."

Wyatt dropped his canvas duffel bag onto the carpeted floor. He unclipped the heavy Mark-IV flashlight from his belt, setting it down on the wood-grain nightstand. The crushing, claustrophobic survival instincts that had kept his muscles coiled tight for four days finally, completely unspooled.

He closed the distance between them, stepping right into her space. He reached out, his hands resting on her waist.

"It's all ours," he told her, his voice a quiet, deeply grounding rumble. "No shift klaxons. No armed guards. Just us."

Sarah didn't hesitate. she threw her arms around his neck, pulling him down into a slow, incredibly tender kiss. There was no desperate, adrenaline-fueled panic behind it this time. They weren't kissing like the world was ending; they were kissing like their lives were finally beginning.

When they finally broke apart, they didn't bother unpacking. They kicked off their heavy boots and climbed right into the center of the queen-sized bed.

For the first time since they had met, they actually had the physical space to stretch out. They could have slept on completely opposite sides of the mattress without touching.

But as Wyatt lay back against the thick pillows, Sarah immediately closed the gap. The luxury of space didn't matter. She crawled right against his side, resting her head on his chest, her arm wrapping tightly around his torso. Wyatt wrapped his broad arm around her shoulders, holding her securely against him, his chin resting on the top of her head.

They had sixty inches of state-of-the-art, retro-futuristic comfort, and they were still sleeping exactly the way they had on that cramped, vinyl sofa. They were completely, undeniably anchored to each other.

Wyatt reached out, flipping the heavy analog switch on the nightstand. The amber lights of the cabin clicked off, plunging the room into a quiet, peaceful darkness. He held the woman he loved, listening to the steady, mechanical hum of Outpost Delta 9, and finally let himself sleep.
