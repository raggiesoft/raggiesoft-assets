## The Algorithm's Gift

The heavy VCR clicked loudly, automatically rewinding the final tape as the credits rolled off the massive CRT television. The screen faded to a soft, static blue, casting a quiet, flickering glow across the spacious Captain's Quarters.

Outside the hull, the absolute blackout of the FTL slipstream held strong. There were no incoming telemetry pings, no emergency broadcasts, and no corporate overseers. They were completely severed from the universe, locked safely in their own private dimension.

Sarah shifted on the massive mattress, turning her body fully toward Wyatt. She rested her hand flat against the center of his chest, feeling the slow, steady rhythm of his heart.

She looked up at him, the blue light catching the soft, unguarded affection in her eyes. She leaned in, pressing her lips to his in a soft, tender kiss. It started as a question—a quiet, vulnerable invitation in the perfect silence of the room.

Wyatt didn't hesitate to answer. He brought his taped hand up to cup her jaw, tilting her head slightly as he parted her lips. His tongue met hers, deepening the kiss with a sudden, sweeping heat that sent a jolt of pure electricity straight down her spine.

The lazy comfort of the afternoon movie marathon instantly evaporated, replaced by a fierce, consuming passion. Sarah gasped softly into his mouth, her hands gripping his shoulders as she pulled herself flush against him.

As the kiss broke for a ragged breath, Wyatt's hands slid down to her waist, his eyes dark and entirely focused on her.

"Wait," Sarah whispered, her voice a breathless rasp. She rested her forehead against his, a warm, completely secure smile on her face as she brushed a stray lock of hair from his eyes. She wanted to remove every single boundary between them. "Just to be completely sure... you remember what I told you in the break room, right? About the clinic?"

Wyatt’s thumbs traced the curve of her hips. "The Omni-Cycle injection."

"Six months," she confirmed, her eyes shining with absolute trust. "I got it the day before I boarded my transport. There's nothing to worry about, Wyatt. Nothing holding us back."

Wyatt let out a low, shuddering breath, completely undone by the absolute certainty in her voice. He pulled her down, capturing her lips again, entirely surrendering to the woman he loved.

Sometime later, the heavy wool blankets were pushed down to their waists. Sarah lay with her head resting on Wyatt’s chest, their legs tangled together in the center of the massive bed.

The intense, fiery passion had settled into a state of profound, absolute peace. Wyatt’s arm was wrapped tightly around her, holding her securely against him as if he were trying to physically anchor her to his soul.

"It's crazy," Sarah murmured, her fingers idly tracing the faded scars on his forearm.

"What is?" Wyatt asked, his voice a low, gravelly rumble that vibrated against her cheek.

"Us," she said softly, looking up at him. "Four days ago, I didn't even know you existed. When the drills started failing, I thought we were going to die in the dark. I thought I was never going to have a future. And now..."

She let the sentence trail off, tightening her arms around his torso in a fierce, desperate hug. She squeezed her eyes shut, entirely overwhelmed by the sheer scale of what they had found in the middle of a nightmare.

Wyatt hugged her back just as tightly, burying his face in her hair. He knew exactly what she meant. They had fallen in love with terrifying, reckless speed, forged in the intense pressure cooker of the NTTR. But the speed didn't make it fragile; it made it unbreakable. They had seen each other at their absolute worst, completely stripped of pretense, and they had chosen each other anyway.

"We aren't going to die tomorrow, Sarah," Wyatt promised, his voice thick with emotion. "We have the rest of our lives."

Eventually, they reluctantly slipped out of the warmth of the bed and headed into the Captain's private ensuite. The luxury of the executive bathroom was a far cry from the rusted pipes of Sector 4. They showered together under a heavy stream of genuinely hot water, washing away the lingering tension of their escape.

They dried off and pulled on fresh, clean civilian clothes—Sarah in her soft denim jeans and a new t-shirt, Wyatt in his canvas pants—but they completely ignored their boots and socks. Walking barefoot on the plush carpets and grated floor plates had become their shared ritual, a physical declaration that the ship was entirely theirs.

"I want to check on the garden," Sarah said, threading her fingers through his as they stepped out of the quarters.

They walked hand-in-hand down the quiet corridors to the lower ring, the heavy iron bulkhead door sliding open to reveal the Hydroponics Bay.

The wall of humid, earthy heat welcomed them. Under the vibrant pink and purple UV lights, the deep-space crops were absolutely thriving. The broad leaves of the soybean plants looked visibly greener, and a new cluster of bright red strawberries had already begun to form near the base of the metal troughs.

"Aria," Sarah called out to the ambient microphone array built into the ceiling. "Can I get a diagnostic on the bay?"

*"Certainly, Sarah,"* Aria’s pleasant voice echoed warmly through the humid room. *"The hydroponics systems are operating at peak efficiency. The secondary water pump you repaired is functioning flawlessly. The nutrient ebb-and-flow cycles, localized humidity, and UV yields are all perfectly within optimal parameters."*

"Thank you, Aria," Sarah smiled.

*"You are very welcome. Please let me know if you require any adjustments to the climate zones."*

Wyatt walked over to the stack of rusted supply crates, pulling one out into the center aisle. He sat down, and Sarah immediately backed up between his knees, leaning her weight against his chest. Wyatt wrapped his arms securely around her waist, resting his chin on her shoulder.

They just sat there in the warm, pink-hued light, entirely soothed by the rhythmic, rushing sound of the water cycling through the roots of the plants. It was a space dedicated entirely to life, hurtling safely through the dead of space.

Sarah rested her hands over his, a quiet, awestruck laugh suddenly escaping her lips.

"What's so funny?" Wyatt murmured, kissing the side of her neck.

"I was just thinking about the HR algorithm at the NTTR," Sarah said, shaking her head in sheer disbelief. "The Desert Star flight attendant told me all the Helios transfers were slated for Shift 3. If that glitch hadn't happened—if I hadn't been accidentally dumped into Shift 2—I would have walked up to that checkpoint and been partnered with some random, terrifying mercenary."

Wyatt tightened his arms around her, a cold chill briefly touching his spine at the thought of her walking that perimeter with anyone else.

"I don't even want to think about what would have happened to me down there without you," Sarah whispered, leaning her head back against his shoulder. "That mix-up... it literally saved my life. It gave me you."

Wyatt looked out over the thriving garden, holding the woman he loved perfectly safe in his arms.

"Best clerical error in corporate history," Wyatt agreed softly.

## The Blank Slate

For the next six days, *The Nomad* became a universe unto itself.

The profound, unbroken isolation of the spacetime fold provided something neither Wyatt nor Sarah had experienced since the day they signed their corporate contracts: time to breathe. There were no shift klaxons. There were no quotas.

But the trauma of the Nevada Test and Training Range didn't just vanish overnight. It manifested in a quiet, undeniable physical tether between them. Wherever Wyatt went, Sarah went. If Wyatt was deep in the engine room checking a pressure valve, Sarah was sitting on a rusted crate five feet away with her notebook. If Sarah was in the Hydroponics Bay pruning the tomato vines, Wyatt was sitting in the aisle, tinkering with a piece of salvaged tech. They never let each other out of their line of sight. The fear of forced separation was still too fresh, too deeply ingrained by the horrors of the subterranean bunker.

On the second day of transit, they tackled the ship's maintenance.

Wyatt had fully expected to spend his week fighting failing gaskets and rusted pneumatics, but as he pulled the heavy access panels off the primary life-support junctions in the lower ring, he found a mechanic's paradise.

"The rusted exterior is just camouflage," Wyatt said, his voice echoing slightly in the narrow access shaft. He wiped a perfectly clean, freshly greased gear with his thumb. "This paranoid billionaire spared absolutely no expense. He kept every primary system in pristine condition. There's not a stripped bolt or a frayed wire anywhere in the primary grid."

Sarah handed him a heavy iron wrench from the red toolbox, her bare feet silent on the grated floor plates. "So she's not going to fall apart on us?"

"Not a chance," Wyatt grinned, tightening a perfectly machined locking nut. "She’s built like a tank. The only damage is a few cosmetic dents and scratches on the outer hull."

Sarah leaned against the bulkhead, watching him work. She reached up, absentmindedly tracing the faded *HAYES* nametape on the black utility shirt she had finally discarded, opting today for one of the soft cotton t-shirts from the executive stash.

"Wyatt," Sarah said thoughtfully. "When the NTTR collapsed... all the corporate servers went down with it, right?"

"Everything in Sector 1 is buried under millions of tons of bedrock," Wyatt confirmed, sliding out from under the manifold. "Why?"

"I was just thinking about the HR algorithm," she murmured. "Our intake files, our biometric logs, our contracts... they're gone. We don't have datapads anymore. We don't have physical ID cards or passports. The copies on Earth are ash, and the originals are lightyears away on our home planets."

Wyatt set the wrench down, the profound reality of her words settling over him.

"We have absolutely no documentation proving who we are," Sarah whispered, a slow, wondrous smile breaking across her face.

"We're blank slates," Wyatt agreed, stepping closer and wrapping his arms around her waist. He pulled her flush against him in the narrow corridor. "Apex doesn't own our names anymore. Nobody does. We get to decide exactly who we are from now on."

By the fourth day of the jump, the quiet domesticity had entirely healed their exhausted nerves. The heavy, dead-eyed S.H.O.P. Marine masks had been completely discarded.

That evening, they found themselves in the 2044-era tech deck, standing inside the heavily soundproofed analog music studio.

The acoustic foam lining the walls made the room feel incredibly intimate. Wyatt walked over to the rack of perfectly preserved instruments and lifted a heavy, solid-body electric guitar from its stand. He plugged the thick copper cable into a massive, vintage amplifier stack in the corner.

He flipped the power toggle. The amp hummed with a low, potent analog hiss.

Across the room, Sarah was sitting on a padded stool in front of a sprawling, physical drum machine. She was tapping the heavy rubber pads, her eyes lighting up as each one produced a crisp, synthesized snare or a deep, booming bass kick.

Wyatt adjusted the volume dial on his guitar and struck a heavy, distorted power chord.

The sound blasted through the soundproofed room, a rich, aggressive wall of noise that vibrated right through the soles of their bare feet.

Sarah actually jumped, laughing out loud at the sheer volume of it.

"Too loud?" Wyatt yelled over the ringing feedback, a massive grin on his face.

"No!" Sarah shouted back, her eyes shining with pure, unadulterated joy. "Play it louder!"

For the last month, they had lived in absolute terror of making too much noise. They had whispered in maintenance closets and walked on eggshells to avoid drawing the wrath of a S.H.O.P. Foreman—the miserable, low-level managers who treated the grunts just as horribly as the executives did. They had lived in fear of waking up the subterranean monsters beneath the bedrock.

But here, wrapped in the spacetime fold, they were untouchable. No S.H.O.P. Foreman was going to bang on the bulkhead door and write them up. No anomaly was going to hear them through the vacuum of space.

Sarah laid down a clumsy, chaotic, but incredibly enthusiastic beat on the drum machine. Wyatt followed her lead, shredding a messy, heavily distorted rock riff that he hadn't played since he was a teenager. They weren't musical geniuses; they were just two kids who had survived the end of the world, reveling in the absolute freedom of making as much noise as humanly possible.

On the fifth day, they had their first official date night.

Sarah had spent the afternoon in the Hydroponics Bay, carefully harvesting a basket of fresh, deep-space greens, crisp dwarf-carrots, and a handful of earthy mushrooms.

Wyatt had taken on the galley. He raided the billionaire's vacuum-sealed executive freezer and pulled out two thick, premium cuts of actual beef—an absolute impossibility for anyone below the Apex Board of Directors.

He pan-seared the steaks in the heavy iron skillet, filling the narrow brushed-steel kitchen with a rich, savory aroma that completely banished the lingering memory of gray nutrient paste.

They ate at the small, square dining table securely bolted to the grated floor. They were both barefoot, dressed in soft civilian clothes, sharing a bottle of reconstituted red wine Wyatt had found tucked behind the dry goods.

They clinked their heavy ceramic mugs together, toasting to the empty universe outside their window. They laughed, they ate, and they treated the massive dreadnought like it was a luxury apartment. The ghosts of Nevada were finally, truly behind them.

## The Backlog

The 144-hour mark arrived exactly as Aria had predicted.

Wyatt and Sarah stood barefoot in the Operations center, Wyatt's hands resting lightly on the heavy mechanical throttle levers.

<aac>"Slipstream telemetry indicates we have reached the coordinates for System 88-Tango,"</aac> Aria’s synthesized voice echoed smoothly from the bulkhead speakers. <aac>"Ready for manual disengagement, Wyatt."</aac>

Wyatt looked at Sarah. She nodded, her hand resting warmly on his shoulder.

He pulled the heavy iron levers backward, pulling them out of the red zone and snapping them into the neutral position.

The deep, sub-audible vibration of the localized spacetime folding engine smoothly spun down into silence. Outside the massive viewport, the swirling, kaleidoscopic tunnel of blue and purple light collapsed in on itself with a silent, brilliant flash.

Normal space rushed back in to meet them.

The viewport was suddenly filled with the breathtaking sight of Exoplanet 4. It was a massive, vibrant jewel suspended in the dark—swirling white clouds over deep, sapphire oceans and vast, sprawling continents of rich green and brown. It was pristine. It was completely untouched.

Sarah gasped, stepping closer to the thick glass. "Wyatt, look at it. It's beautiful."

Wyatt smiled, looking at the virgin world below them. "That's our new home."

But before they could even celebrate, the physics of normal space violently reasserted themselves.

The electromagnetic block of the FTL slipstream instantly vanished. The station's massive parabolic dish was suddenly exposed to the open void, and six days' worth of delayed, light-speed radio waves from the Sol System finally caught up to them.

The Operations center exploded into absolute chaos.

The heavy, grated bulkhead speakers didn't just hiss; they screamed. A terrifying, overlapping cacophony of panicked military chatter, automated distress beacons, and the grating, dual-tone blare of the Emergency Broadcast System flooded the room all at once.

On the command console, the heavy amber CRT monitor flared violently, lines of frantic, unencrypted data cascading down the screen.

And then, the heavy dot-matrix printer bolted to the side of the desk violently shrieked to life.

*ZZZT-ZZZT-ZZZT-ZZZT!*

It began churning out continuous-feed paper at maximum speed, the pins striking the ribbon so hard it sounded like a machine gun. It was a physical backlog of horror, printing out every single apocalyptic broadcast they had missed while they were safely hidden in the fold.

Wyatt and Sarah froze, the serene beauty of their new planet completely overshadowed by the agonizing, mechanical screams of the world they had left behind.

## The Backlog

The heavy dot-matrix printer screamed, the mechanical pins striking the ribbon with the frantic, deafening rhythm of a machine gun. *ZZZT-ZZZT-ZZZT-ZZZT!*

Wyatt and Sarah stood frozen in the Operations center, the breathtaking view of their pristine new world suddenly overshadowed by the ghosts of the one they had left behind. The electromagnetic block of the FTL slipstream was gone, and six days of delayed, light-speed radio waves from the Sol System were violently slamming into the station’s massive parabolic dish all at once.

Wyatt broke from his shock. He stepped up to the console, grabbing the long, continuous-feed paper spilling out of the printer and onto the grated floor.

Sarah stepped up beside him, her hands trembling as she braced herself against the edge of the 1970s terminal.

The first page was dated just two days after they had entered the slipstream.

**EBS TRANSCRIPT: INTERCEPT DELTA NINER (BROADCAST 006)** **DATE RECEIVED (DELAYED):** Thursday, October 27, 2044 **SOURCE:** United States Northern Command / FEMA **TARGETING:** Eastern Seaboard / Mid-Atlantic Region **THREAT LEVEL:** Extinction Level Event (East Coast Breach / VEI-8 Eruption) **FORMAT:** Synthesized Text-to-Speech

\<EAS DUAL-TONE ACTIVATION BEEP\>

"THE FOLLOWING MESSAGE IS TRANSMITTED AT THE REQUEST OF THE UNITED STATES NORTHERN COMMAND.

THE SUBTERRANEAN ANOMALY HAS BYPASSED THE CONTINENTAL DIVIDE AND REACHED THE EASTERN SEABOARD. THE ORGANIC MASS HAS COLLIDED WITH A HIGHLY PRESSURIZED MAGMA CHAMBER LOCATED BENEATH MIDDLETOWN, VIRGINIA.

THIS COLLISION HAS TRIGGERED A CATASTROPHIC VEI-8 SUPERVOLCANIC ERUPTION. THE TOWN OF MIDDLETOWN AND THE SURROUNDING SHENANDOAH VALLEY HAVE BEEN VAPORIZED. MOLE HILL, A SECONDARY VOLCANIC PLUG, HAS ALSO ERUPTED.

WASHINGTON D.C. AND THE SURROUNDING NATIONAL CAPITAL REGION ARE CURRENTLY BEING BURIED UNDER TOXIC VOLCANIC ASH. CONTINUITY OF GOVERNMENT PROTOCOLS WERE SUCCESSFULLY INITIATED PRIOR TO THE ERUPTION. THE MOUNT WEATHER EMERGENCY OPERATIONS CENTER WAS EVACUATED PRIOR TO THE BREACH.

DO NOT ATTEMPT TO RESCUE CASUALTIES IN THE SHENANDOAH VALLEY. MAY GOD BE WITH YOU."

\<TRANSMISSION TERMINATED\>

Sarah let out a sharp, ragged breath, her hand covering her mouth. "It crossed the whole country. It set off another volcano..."

"It hit the magma chamber under Virginia," Wyatt said, his voice a hollow rasp as he stared at the blocky text. The continent had been cracked wide open.

But the printer wasn't done. It kept churning, feeding another massive block of text into Wyatt's hands. The timestamp on this one was from just twenty-four hours ago.

**GLOBAL BROADCAST: INTERCEPT DELTA NINER (BROADCAST 007)** **DATE RECEIVED (DELAYED):** Monday, October 31, 2044 **SOURCE:** United Nations Security Council / Global Defense Coalition **TARGETING:** All Surviving Global Frequencies **THREAT LEVEL:** Planetary Collapse (Global Evacuation Initiated) **FORMAT:** Unencrypted Human Voice Transmission (Multiple Languages)

"This is a general broadcast on behalf of the surviving members of the United Nations Security Council and the Global Defense Coalition.

The subterranean anomaly that originated in the North American continent has fractured the Earth's tectonic plates. Seismic shockwaves and subsequent supervolcanic eruptions have rendered the Earth's atmosphere toxic and structurally unstable.

The planet is no longer viable.

To all remaining civilian populations: Major world powers have successfully preserved their continuity of government. A coordinated, mass off-world evacuation of what is left of Earth is currently underway. International atmospheric transports are departing from all surviving launch sites. We are directing all orbital traffic to designated staging flotillas beyond the Lunar orbit.

If you are receiving this broadcast, look to the sky. Help is launching. Humanity will survive this, but we must leave our home behind. May God bless us all."

\<HEAVY STATIC / SIGNAL LOST\>

The printer finally gave a last, violent *clack* and went silent. The heavy amber CRT monitor returned to its steady, blinking command prompt. The bulkhead speakers faded back into the low, ambient hiss of deep space.

Wyatt lowered the perforated paper, his hands shaking slightly.

He looked at Sarah. Tears were silently tracking down her cheeks. Neither of them had been born on Earth; they had both grown up on fading off-world colonies, and they had only known the planet as a terrifying corporate meat-grinder. But it was still the cradle of humanity. The staggering loss of the homeworld, the original oceans, and the ancient continents—it was an irreplaceable wound to the entire galaxy. Earth was dead.

But beneath the profound, crushing grief, a different emotion was taking root.

"They survived," Sarah whispered, her voice cracking as she looked up at Wyatt. She tapped the final paragraph of the printout. "Wyatt, the people on Earth... they're evacuating. They made it off."

Wyatt looked at the paper, then back up to the massive viewport. Suspended in the dark was the brilliant, vibrant jewel of Exoplanet 4—a completely untouched world of swirling white clouds, sapphire oceans, and rich green continents.

"The cradle is gone," Wyatt said, his voice steadying, thick with a complex mixture of sorrow and immense relief. "But our people aren't."

He reached out, pulling Sarah firmly against his chest. He held her tight, anchoring her as the final tether to their old lives was severed. The billions fleeing Earth would scatter into the stars, joining the vast, existing network of frontier colonies and off-world settlements. Humanity would survive the loss of its home.

And millions of lightyears away, completely off the corporate grid, Wyatt and Sarah had already found their sanctuary.

"We let the universe go its way," Wyatt murmured into her hair, looking out at the virgin planet waiting for them below. "And we go ours."
