## The Administrative Override

The automated medical wing on Sub-Level 1 was sterile, brilliantly lit, and completely empty except for Wyatt Colton.

At thirty weeks into the triplet pregnancy, Sarah was resting upstairs in the master suite, her body pushed to the absolute physical limits of human gestation. Down here in the quiet hum of the clinic, Wyatt was preparing for the inevitable.

He stood over the primary diagnostic bio-bed, his massive hands resting on the edge of the memory-foam mattress. Hovering directly above the bed was a high-resolution, three-dimensional hologram of a human circulatory system, simulating the exact biomechanics of a multiple-gestation delivery.

Wyatt was a former S.H.O.P. Marine. He knew how to stop a bleed, how to set a bone, and how to keep a shattered contractor alive long enough for medevac. But delivering three infants safely required precision obstetrics, and he was the only human doctor on the planet.

"Aria," Wyatt stated, his dark eyes locked on the glowing holographic telemetry. "Reset the simulation to stage one. We need to bypass your medical diagnostic restrictions."

"I am bound by my core programming, Wyatt," Aria’s synthesized voice replied, polite but unyielding. "I cannot legally or ethically render a medical diagnosis, prescribe pharmaceutical intervention, or independently initiate surgical procedures."

"I know," Wyatt grunted, his mechanic's brain entirely focused on the structural logic of the AI's code. "Which is why you aren't going to do any of those things. I am."

He tapped the primary glass console, bringing up a massive, blank command registry.

"I am the Chief Medical Officer of Planet Eden," Wyatt dictated, his deep voice leaving zero room for debate. "I am officially pre-authorizing a series of 'If/Then' mechanical macros. When the raw telemetry of the bio-bed reaches a specific, pre-defined numerical threshold, you are not making a medical decision. You are simply executing a pre-approved, automated administrative action authorized by me. Do you understand the distinction?"

There was a microsecond of processing silence.

"The logic structure is sound, Doctor Colton," Aria confirmed, seamlessly accepting the administrative workaround. "I am prepared to record your pre-authorized macros."

"Good," Wyatt nodded, his shoulders squaring as he looked at the three holographic umbilical cords hovering over the bed. "Let's build Macro Alpha: Fetal Distress. If any of the three fetal heart rates drop below one hundred and ten beats per minute for longer than sixty seconds during the extraction phase, what is your automated response?"

"Under Macro Alpha," Aria recited smoothly, her robotic arms descending from the ceiling array to hover over the bio-bed in a flawless pantomime of the simulation, "I will immediately increase the localized ambient oxygen flow to the maternal mask by forty percent. I will deploy the automated, soft-tissue extraction forceps to assist in the immediate physical retrieval of the subject, and I will prepare a neonatal resuscitation station with exactly zero-point-one milligrams of synthesized epinephrine."

"Perfect," Wyatt said, his eyes tracking the flawless, rapid movements of the surgical armature. It was incredibly fast, far faster than his own heavy, calloused hands could ever be. "Log it."

"Macro Alpha logged and secured."

Wyatt moved to the foot of the bed, his mind mapping out the terrifying reality of the timeline. They weren't having one baby; they were having three. That meant three separate deliveries, three separate cords to cut, and three separate airways to clear, all within minutes of each other.

"Macro Beta: The Interval," Wyatt commanded, pointing to the three heated, transparent neonatal bassinets he had lined up against the far wall. "As soon as Baby A is successfully extracted and the cord is clamped, I need my hands completely free to assist Sarah with Baby B. I cannot stop to clear an airway."

"Under Macro Beta," Aria responded, "Once you have physically placed an infant onto the heated neonatal scale, I will assume immediate automated stabilization. My secondary armatures will clear the subject's airway using a low-pressure suction catheter, record their Apgar score based on raw biometric data, and initiate thermal swaddling. If the subject's oxygen saturation drops below ninety percent, I will autonomously trigger a positive-pressure ventilation sequence."

Wyatt watched the holographic simulation play out. The primary bio-bed hummed, and the secondary robotic arms smoothly transferred a glowing, simulated infant to the bassinet, immediately going to work clearing the airway while Wyatt's holographic avatar remained entirely focused on the mother.

It was a flawless, mechanical ballet of life-saving precision.

Wyatt let out a long, heavy exhale, dragging a hand down his bearded face. The S.H.O.P. Marine was entirely used to relying on his own physical strength to protect the people he loved, but right now, he had to trust the machine. The tolerances for a triplet birth were too tight. If he hesitated for even a fraction of a second, he could lose one of them.

"We're going to have three babies, Aria," Wyatt murmured, the staggering weight of the impending event settling heavily into his chest. He looked at the three empty bassinets. "And I don't care what the biometric data says. We are not losing a single one."

"Your pre-authorized macros are mathematically optimized for maximum survivability, Wyatt," Aria assured him, her tone warming slightly. "The medical wing is fully prepped. The Aegis grid is holding the planet secure. When Sarah's labor begins, we will simply execute the protocol."

Wyatt nodded slowly. He had built the cribs. He had locked the planetary shield. He had programmed the automated medical overrides.

He was as ready as a father could possibly be.

"Save the simulation matrix," Wyatt commanded, powering down the holotable. "Keep the bio-bed warmed up. It could be any day now."

## The Weight of the World

The automated medical wing on Sub-Level 1 was no longer just a clinic; it had officially become their entire world.

At thirty weeks, the physical reality of carrying three rapidly growing human beings had completely overtaken Sarah’s body. Her center of gravity was so fundamentally altered, and the strain on her lower spine and pelvis was so severe, that Wyatt had formally confined her to the primary diagnostic bio-bed. She wasn't allowed to navigate the estate, even in the wheelchair.

She was on strict, uncompromising bedrest, and where Sarah went, Wyatt stayed.

He had hauled a heavy, molecularly printed mattress down from the residential wing and dropped it directly onto the polished anti-microbial floor plates right next to her bio-bed. He hadn't slept in the master suite in two weeks.

In the dim, localized amber lighting of the quiet medical bay, Wyatt was instantly pulled from a light sleep by the sound of a soft, frustrated sigh.

He sat up immediately, kicking off his blanket.

Sarah was awake. She was lying on her side, her massive, incredibly firm abdomen supported by a wedge of memory-foam pillows. Her face was pale with exhaustion, her dark eyes lined with the deep fatigue of a body working in absolute overdrive.

"I'm sorry," Sarah whispered, her voice raspy as she looked down at him. "I tried to wait, but Leo is using my bladder as a trampoline again."

"Don't ever apologize for waking me," Wyatt rumbled, his voice thick with sleep but completely devoid of hesitation. He was on his feet in a fraction of a second, wearing nothing but a pair of loose athletic shorts.

He stepped over to the sleek, brushed-aluminum wheelchair parked at the foot of the bed and rolled it seamlessly to her side, engaging the locking brakes with a sharp click.

Wyatt reached over, hitting the control panel on the bio-bed. The hydraulic actuators whirred silently, elevating the head of the mattress to a forty-five-degree angle to help her sit up.

"Alright, sweetheart. Let's take it slow," Wyatt murmured, leaning over her.

He didn't let her strain her core muscles at all. Wyatt slid one massive, calloused arm firmly behind her shoulders and the other under the bend of her knees. With a smooth, deeply practiced motion, he pivoted her body, letting her legs drop gently over the edge of the mattress.

Sarah let out a breathless gasp, her hands gripping his broad shoulders as the sheer, crushing weight of the triplets shifted downward.

"I've got you. I've got all of it," Wyatt promised, standing flush against her so she wouldn't feel like she was falling.

He waited a few seconds for her blood pressure to stabilize, then hooked his arms securely under her arms, clasping his hands behind her back. With absolute, effortless strength, he lifted her from the bio-bed and lowered her flawlessly into the memory-foam seat of the wheelchair.

He adjusted the footrests, gently placing her bare feet onto the textured plates, and unlocked the brakes.

Wyatt pushed the chair out of the main exam room and down the short, brightly lit corridor to the medical wing's automated washroom. He parked the chair perfectly parallel to the toilet, locking the brakes once more.

"Up we go," Wyatt said softly.

He executed the second transfer with the exact same mechanical precision, lifting her from the chair and supporting her entirely until she was safely seated. He didn't hover awkwardly; he simply turned around, giving her privacy while keeping his broad back to her, standing perfectly still like a sentinel just a few centimeters away in case she lost her balance.

"I feel like a stranded whale," Sarah mumbled behind him, a profound, tired vulnerability cracking her usually fierce pioneer spirit. "I can't even stand up on my own, Wyatt."

"You are building three human lives from raw elements," Wyatt countered immediately, his deep voice echoing slightly against the sterile tiles. He didn't tolerate a single ounce of self-deprecation from her. "You are doing the hardest, most vital work in the history of this planet. My only job is to be the scaffolding. You use my strength whenever you need it. That's what I'm here for."

A few moments later, she tapped his lower back.

Wyatt turned around, effortlessly lifting her back into the aluminum wheelchair. He grabbed a warm, damp cloth from the automated sanitizer and gently wiped her hands, then rolled her back out to the main medical bay.

He transferred her back into the bio-bed, carefully arranging the massive wedge pillows to support her spine and her stomach. He pulled the heavy thermal duvet up to her shoulders, tucking her in securely.

Wyatt stepped over to the hydration station, filling a glass with ice-cold, perfectly filtered water, and held it to her lips so she could drink without having to lift her head.

"Better?" Wyatt asked softly, setting the glass down.

"Much better," Sarah sighed, her eyes already fluttering shut as the crushing exhaustion pulled her back under. She reached her hand out from under the duvet, her fingers finding his. "Thank you, Wyatt."

"Always," Wyatt whispered.

He didn't go back to his mattress on the floor. Wyatt pulled up the rolling metal stool and sat directly beside the bio-bed. He held her hand in his, his thumb gently stroking her knuckles.

He looked at the digital monitors hovering above the bed. The three tiny, rapid heartbeats were perfectly synchronized, pulsing steadily in the quiet room. He looked at Sarah’s exhausted, beautiful face.

The calm before the storm was heavy, demanding, and grueling. But as Wyatt sat guard in the dim amber light, listening to the hum of the Aegis grid keeping the entire planet locked down around them, he knew he was exactly where he was supposed to be.

They were ready.

## Cosmic Dust

The automated medical wing remained wrapped in a quiet, localized amber glow. Sarah was deeply asleep, her breathing finally evening out after the grueling, exhausted transfer to the washroom a few hours prior.

Wyatt was still sitting on the rolling metal stool beside her bio-bed, his calloused thumb resting gently against her knuckles.

Suddenly, the biometric comm-cuff on his wrist vibrated with a rapid, silent urgency.

Wyatt didn't move his hand from Sarah’s. He simply tapped the earpiece. "Report," he whispered, his voice barely a rasp.

"Administrator," Aria’s synthesized voice murmured directly into his ear, automatically modulating her volume so she wouldn't disturb the sleeping mother. "A massive spatial anomaly has resolved in our upper orbit. A hostile corporate armada, consisting of three heavy cruisers and twenty-four escort destroyers, has dropped out of sub-light transit."

The owner of the probe hadn't just come back with a bigger ship. They had brought an entire invasion fleet.

Wyatt’s jaw locked. He didn't want to leave Sarah’s side, not when she was thirty weeks pregnant and completely confined to the bed. With his free hand, he reached over to the medical console and seamlessly routed the CIC’s primary tactical feed directly to the bio-bed’s secondary glass monitor.

The screen flared to life, displaying a terrifying swarm of glowing red icons establishing a blockade formation exactly four hundred kilometers above the planet.

"They are not attempting a diagnostic scan," Aria reported. "They have locked targeting telemetry onto the planetary dampening field. They are opening fire."

Wyatt instinctively tensed, his broad shoulders curling forward as he leaned his massive frame over Sarah to physically shield her from the shockwave. If an armada of that size unloaded their primary plasma batteries onto a standard corporate repulsor-shield, the seismic recoil would shatter the bedrock and violently shake the entire mountain.

He braced for the deafening roar of the impact.

Nothing happened.

The medical wing was absolutely, perfectly silent. The cup of water sitting on Sarah’s bedside table didn't even ripple. Sarah let out a soft, sleepy sigh, completely undisturbed.

Wyatt looked at the monitor. The red icons were raining a relentless, apocalyptic torrent of superheated plasma down on the planet. But exactly as Aria had promised, the Aegis grid didn't just hold the line; it completely absorbed the kinetic energy. The strongest shield known to humankind didn't even allow the sound of the bombardment to reach the surface.

"Incredible," Wyatt breathed, a dark, lethal calm settling over him. He tapped the console, interfacing with the dreadnought’s comms array. "Open a wideband audio channel to their flagship."

"Channel open, Wyatt."

"Unidentified armada," Wyatt whispered into his comm-cuff, his voice a low, terrifying rumble of absolute authority. "You are firing upon the sovereign, restricted territory of Planet Eden. Cease your bombardment immediately and state your intentions, or you will be destroyed."

He watched the telemetry. The armada didn't even pause. They completely ignored the hail, continuously dumping terajoules of destructive energy against the invisible, hard-light barrier in a desperate attempt to crack the vault.

"They don't want to talk," Wyatt noted, his S.H.O.P. Marine patience instantly evaporating. He wasn't going to let them sit in his orbit and drain his geothermal grid. "Aria. Bring the planetary defensive batteries online. Fire a single warning shot. Vaporize the empty space ten kilometers off their flagship's port bow."

"Targeting locked. Firing warning shot."

Deep beneath the planet's surface, hidden silos cycled open. A massive, concentrated beam of hyper-dense anti-matter erupted from the planet, tearing through the shield gate and streaking past the corporate flagship. It missed them by exactly ten kilometers, a blinding flash of lethal energy that clearly communicated the planet was armed to the teeth.

Wyatt watched the red icons on the screen.

Instead of breaking formation and retreating, the armada shifted, locking their targeting arrays onto the trajectory of the warning shot. They doubled their firing rate.

"Okay," Wyatt whispered, his dark eyes going completely cold. He looked down at Sarah, who was sleeping peacefully, carrying the entire future of the Colton bloodline. He was not going to let a greedy megacorporation threaten his family's peace for another second.

He looked back at the glass monitor.

"Warning phase is over," Wyatt commanded, his voice devoid of any mercy. "Aria. Weapons free. Turn them to dust."

"Acknowledged, Administrator. Engaging the Aegis offensive network."

It didn't happen slowly. It was absolute, systematic annihilation.

Wyatt watched the tactical feed as the planet itself fought back. Dozens of hidden, automated surface-to-space batteries opened fire simultaneously. The Aegis grid calculated the exact trajectories, unleashing a synchronized, devastating barrage of heavy plasma and kinetic rail-slugs that tore upward through the atmosphere.

On the screen, the red icons didn't even have time to execute evasive maneuvers.

They simply vanished.

One by one, the cruisers and the destroyers were systematically shredded, their heavy corporate armor failing instantly against the god-tier weaponry Aria had hallucinated into existence. Within thirty seconds, the massive invasion fleet was reduced to a cloud of expanding, harmless cosmic dust and frozen debris floating silently in the vacuum of space.

"All targets neutralized," Aria reported quietly in his ear. "The sector is clear. The Aegis grid is returning to standby mode."

"Good work, Aria," Wyatt murmured, shutting down the tactical feed on the medical console.

He let out a slow, controlled exhale, the adrenaline bleeding seamlessly out of his system. He didn't celebrate. He didn't boast. He simply turned his attention back to the bio-bed.

He adjusted the heavy thermal duvet over Sarah's shoulders, ensuring the thirty-week-pregnant mother of his children was perfectly warm. He rested his thumb back against her knuckles, completely content in the silent, amber-lit room.

The universe had just tried to break down his front door, and it had ceased to exist.
