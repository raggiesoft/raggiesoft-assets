## 5:30 PM — The First Assist

By the time dinner finished, the emotional and physical toll of the long, chaotic day had caught up with Jordan. His shoulders slumped, and he quietly admitted through his text-to-speech software that he was exhausted and just wanted to head back to the quiet of the dorm.

The group moved as a unit back to Bennett Hall. As they reached the second floor, Mark paused outside the suite doors. With polite respect for their shared space, he looked at Jordan, Aubrie, and Madison.

"Mind if we all hang out in 214 for a bit?"

Jordan gave a welcoming nod, tapping his OEA-encoded Poly Pass to the reader so the latch unlocked and the motorized opener swung the door wide open on its ten-second timer before closing and re-locking behind the group.

The seven of them filed into the cramped room. Mark, Hannah, Lauren, and Zoë found spots on the floor and the desk chairs, while Jordan wheeled himself toward his bed.

A sudden, urgent pressure hit Jordan. A sudden flush of embarrassment creeping up his neck, he looked toward the connecting bathroom door. He hurriedly pulled his heavy laptop onto his lap, popping the lid open to type out a request for assistance.

He got as far as typing <aac>I need to use—</aac> before the screen flickered, froze, and went completely black. The ruined battery had died yet again.

Jordan froze, panic and mortification flooding his face. He gestured helplessly toward the dead machine and then pointed toward the bathroom door, completely unable to voice his urgent need.

Aubrie, whose eyes had barely left him since they sat down at dinner, read his distressed body language instantly. A sudden, protective flutter sparked in her chest as she realized he was trapped without his voice.

"I'll help!" Aubrie blurted out.

The words left her mouth a fraction of a second too quickly. She scrambled to her feet, her cheeks instantly blooming into a deep, vivid shade of crimson.

A beat of total silence fell over the room. Madison's eyebrows shot up. Lauren suppressed a smile, and Mark let out a quiet, amused chuckle.

Aubrie, refusing to make eye contact with anyone in the room, kept her gaze fixed firmly on the floor as she hurriedly grasped the handles of Jordan's wheelchair. "Let's go," she muttered, quickly steering him into the connecting ADA-compliant bathroom and shutting the door behind them.

Inside the small room, Aubrie took a deep breath, forcing her blush to recede as she shifted into a mode of practical, judgment-free care.

But as she turned to face him, Jordan was completely frozen, his knuckles white as he gripped his armrests. He had never, in his entire life, allowed anyone other than his mother to assist him with this. He was bracing himself for what he had always known: a sudden, dictatorial loss of agency where his body was handled without warning, dignity, or permission. Because he had never been taught the actual mechanics of his own care, he had absolutely no idea how to guide Aubrie, and without his laptop, he couldn't even attempt to explain his needs.

Aubrie, despite her racing pulse and the undeniable butterflies swirling in her stomach, saw the sheer, paralyzing terror in his eyes. She realized instantly that neither of them had a script for this.

"Okay, Jordan," Aubrie whispered softly. She knelt down so she was perfectly at eye-level, refusing to loom over him. "I've never done this before, and I know you can't speak right now. So we're going to figure this out together. Just nod or shake your head for me, okay?"

He swallowed hard, his breathing shallow, and gave a small, shaky nod.

Instead of just reaching for his waistband, she pointed to it. "Do you want me to unbutton your jeans first?" He nodded. She did it slowly, deliberately, ensuring he was comfortable with every single movement. "Okay. Since your legs can't bear weight, do you want to hold onto the grab bar while I lift and swing you over?" She pantomimed the motions, giving him the power to choose.

Once he was finished and safely back in his chair at the roll-under sink, Aubrie ran the faucet until the water was gently warm, wet a soft hand towel with soap, and reached out to help wash his hands and forearms.

The instant the cloth neared his skin, Jordan flinched violently, his shoulders hunching up toward his ears and his eyes squeezing shut as he braced for the harsh, painful scouring and the inevitable shout he had known at every bath for eighteen years.

Aubrie froze immediately, pulling her hand back an inch. Her hazel eyes dropped to his forearms and the edge of his collar, where faint, blotchy patches of raw redness from his mother's rough scrubbing at the motel two nights ago were still visible against his pale skin. Her chest tightened with a sudden, fierce ache as she realized *why* he had flinched. He didn't even know what a gentle touch felt like.

"Hey... Jordan, open your eyes," Aubrie whispered softly, holding the warm cloth still in the air where he could see it. "I'm not going to hurt you. Ever. Feel the water first, okay?"

She waited until he slowly opened his eyes, then let his fingertips brush the soft terrycloth so he could feel that it wasn't scalding. Only when he gave a tiny nod did she gently press the warm cloth to his wrists and forearms—using slow, feather-light strokes without a single ounce of force or impatience, before patting his skin dry with a clean towel.

It was a painstakingly careful process, but she assisted him with a tender, clinical respect that completely bypassed the awkwardness of the situation. For Jordan, sitting there with his heart pounding in his throat, it was an earth-shattering revelation. He hadn't known kindness like this existed until he met Aubrie Kelly. She was asking for his consent. She was treating his body with a gentleness and dignity his mother had never once shown him, and Aubrie found that she cared far more about protecting his dignity than she did about her own embarrassment.

When the door clicked open a few minutes later and Aubrie wheeled Jordan back into the main room, she was met with a wall of knowing, playful looks.

Madison was leaning against the top bunk, a wide, unmistakable smirk on her face. "Wow, Aubrie. You were certainly eager to volunteer for bathroom duty".

"It's called being a helpful suitemate, Madison," Aubrie said defensively, her face immediately turning bright red all over again. She busied herself with straightening the blanket on Jordan's bed, acting as if it were the most engrossing task in the world.

Zoë adjusted her glasses, her tone perfectly deadpan. "The swiftness of your response time was statistically notable," she observed, her crisp London accent underscoring the dry remark.

Hannah just smiled warmly, watching Aubrie fret over the blanket. The entire room had put two and two together. Aubrie Kelly was falling head over heels in love with the quiet boy in the wheelchair, and even if she wasn't quite ready to admit it out loud yet, her blush had already given her away.

## 5:45 PM — The Spare Hardware

As the laughter from Aubrie's embarrassed flush began to settle, Zoë turned her analytical gaze toward Jordan's dead, bulky machine.

"The data indicates your current hardware is entirely insufficient for continuous use," Zoë observed, her crisp British consonants cutting through the room's chatter. "A dead battery poses a critical communication failure. Fortunately, I have a logical solution. My family resides just down the road in Christiansburg. I have a spare, fully functional laptop at home."

She pulled out her mPhone. "I will ring my father and have him bring it round."

Jordan's body instantly locked up. His eyes went wide, and his chest tightened with a sudden, suffocating panic. A gift. She was offering him a gift. In Aubrie Sinclair’s household, there was no such thing as a free offering. Everything had a string attached; everything was a transaction, usually paid for with guilt, shame, or severe punishment. He frantically shook his head, waving his hands to stop her, but lacking his text-to-speech software, he was trapped in his own silence.

"It's no trouble at all," Zoë added, misinterpreting his panic as mere politeness as she held the phone to her ear. "Dad? Yes, it's Zoë. Could you do me a massive favour..."

For the next twenty minutes, Jordan sat in a state of mounting dread. It wasn't just the fear of the gift that paralyzed him; it was the realization of *who* was bringing it. A father. He was an eighteen-year-old male sitting in a college dorm suite assigned to five young women, and a protective "girl dad" was on his way. He knew the stereotypes. He expected rage. He expected to be yelled at, accused, and immediately thrown out of the room.

At 6:05 PM, Zoë's phone buzzed. "Right, he's in the lobby," she announced, stepping to the wall to press the square **PUSH TO OPEN** handicap button so the door unlocked and swung open. "I'll just pop down in the lift and escort him up."

Ten seconds after she stepped into the corridor, the motorized arm pulled the heavy wooden door shut until the lock clicked into place, and Jordan's breathing grew shallow. Aubrie, noticing his sheer terror, gently rested her hand on his arm. "It's okay, Jordan," she whispered softly.

A few minutes later, the exterior reader chirped as Zoë tapped her Poly Pass to unlock the latch and pushed the door open. Zoë walked in, followed by a tall, broad-shouldered man with kind eyes and a sturdy, protective presence. This was Daniel Brooks.

"Dad, as I was explaining downstairs, there has been a massive administrative error," Zoë began, gesturing around the cramped space. "Housing mistakenly assigned a male to our suite due to a naming confusion."

Daniel stepped into the room. He carried a sleek, silver laptop case under one arm. He stopped, his eyes sweeping over the crowded space. He saw Mark sitting respectfully on the floor near Hannah. He saw his daughter’s new roommates. And then, his gaze landed on Jordan. He saw the boy in the wheelchair, practically vibrating with fear, his knuckles white as he gripped his armrests, bracing for the inevitable explosion of paternal anger.

Daniel's brow furrowed slightly in confusion. He slowly turned his head to look back at the heavy wooden door they had just walked through. His eyes traced the brass fixtures embedded in the solid wood. He saw the standard peephole at standing height. Then, his gaze dropped lower, resting on the second peephole drilled perfectly at wheelchair level.

The pieces clicked together instantly in Daniel's mind. The university hadn't just put a boy in a girls' suite; they had put a boy who desperately needed an accessible room into the *only* accessible room available on the floor.

Daniel looked back at the five young women, taking in their relaxed posture and the protective, caring way Aubrie was sitting next to Jordan's chair. They weren't uncomfortable. They weren't threatened. They had already accepted this frightened young man as their own.

The tension in Daniel's broad shoulders melted away, replaced by a profound, warming wave of empathy. His heart ached for the sheer terror radiating from the boy.

Daniel bypassed the girls and walked directly over to Jordan. He didn't loom over him; he knelt down so they were eye-to-eye, offering a warm, incredibly gentle smile.

"You must be Jordan," Daniel said, his voice a deep, reassuring rumble that carried the same distinct London accent as his daughter, but with absolutely zero anger. "Zoë tells me your computer gave up the ghost. I'm Daniel."

He gently placed the laptop case on Jordan's desk, unzipped it, and popped the matte-black lid open. Even at a glance, the machine was built like an absolute tank—an enterprise-grade business notebook with a rigid magnesium-alloy frame and a crisp, tactile keyboard. It booted almost instantaneously.

"It's a Pinnacle Vanguard Pro," Zoë explained, stepping up beside her father. "Originally engineered by Atlas before Pinnacle bought their hardware division—built solid to last for years. Dad and I just snapped a brand-new, fresh extended-capacity battery pack into the back before he drove over, so it will easily hold a charge all day. And I wiped Quantum OS off it ages ago; it currently runs a custom distribution of Aura Glade, which is visually identical to what you are used to, but requires a fraction of the processing power."

She tapped the trackpad, bringing up a clean, minimalist text box. "I also pre-installed a program called *EchoType*. It synthesizes speech locally, meaning you won't experience latency issues. And don't worry about network connectivity; the wireless card driver on this model was notoriously stubborn, so I manually compiled a patch in the terminal to force the hardware handshake a while back. The connection is entirely stable."

"It's yours to use for as long as you need it, son," Daniel finished for her, setting the power adapter and the sealed box from the fresh battery gently on the desk. "No strings attached."

Jordan stared at the man, his mind struggling to process the interaction. There was no shouting. There was no accusation. There was just a father looking at him not as an intruder, but with the same unconditional kindness his daughter had shown. In that simple, quiet moment in Room 214, a tiny seed was planted in Jordan's chest—the fragile, miraculous hope that maybe, just maybe, he was finally safe.

## 6:15 PM — The New Voice

Zoë set the sleek Pinnacle Vanguard Pro down on Jordan's desk, right next to his old, battered machine.

"Right, let's get you properly set up," Zoë said, her fingers flying across the keyboard as she opened a dark terminal window. "I am creating a dedicated user account for you. And I'm adding you to the sudoers file, granting you full sudo rights. You have root access. It is completely your machine now."

Jordan watched in awe. In his mother's house, he had never been given administrator privileges to anything, let alone his own life.

Zoë produced a small USB stick from her pocket. "Let's transfer your data over. Documents, photos, music, whatever you need." She popped the lid of the old laptop, waking the sluggish Quantum OS 7 from its slumber. After a moment of navigating the file system, her analytical brow furrowed. She looked at Jordan, puzzled. "Jordan, the directories are completely empty. There is absolutely no data on this drive."

Jordan looked down, a hot flush of shame warming his cheeks. He instinctively gripped the armrests of his wheelchair. His mother had never allowed him to save anything personal. He had no photos, no music, no life of his own to transfer. The empty hard drive was a stark, embarrassing reflection of his isolated existence.

Daniel Brooks noticed the immediate return of Jordan's terror. He saw the boy shrink into himself, clearly bracing for an imposing "girl dad" to reprimand him or view him with suspicion. Understanding exactly what the boy needed, Daniel stepped forward slowly, keeping his movements deliberate and unthreatening.

He gently closed the lid of the old, heavy laptop with a soft click.

"Well," Daniel said, his voice a warm, reassuring rumble that carried no judgment whatsoever, "that just means you get to start with a completely clean slate. Let's put this old relic up here on the dresser, shall we? That way, you'll have plenty of room in your backpack for the new one."

Daniel carefully lifted the heavy machine and placed it out of the way on top of the wooden dresser, treating the boy not as a threat, but with a careful, fatherly respect. It was a physical act of packing away his old life, deliberately demonstrating to Jordan that he did not need to be terrified of him.

With the old machine gone, Jordan reached out and carefully pulled the new Vanguard Pro onto his lap. It was significantly lighter and cooler than his old machine. Since it was going to be his primary communication device, he needed to know how it felt to type on it while sitting in his chair. It rested perfectly across his legs.

He logged into his new account. The Aura Glade interface was clean, lightning-fast, and reassuringly familiar. Zoë leaned over to guide him through his new text-to-speech software.

"This is *EchoType*," Zoë explained. "You have several vocal output profiles to choose from."

Jordan scrolled through the list. On his old laptop, his mother had strictly enforced the use of "Quantum David," a flat, grating, and deeply robotic male voice, insisting that anything else was "inappropriate" for him to use. Now, sitting in a room entirely free from her gaze, he felt a quiet spark of rebellion.

He bypassed the male options entirely. He scrolled down and paused on a female voice profile named "Sydney." It possessed a bright, clear Australian accent. He could not explain exactly why he liked it—he had never been to Australia, nor did he know much about it—but he liked the way it sounded in his head. And for the first time in his life, that simple preference was all that mattered. Choosing a female voice felt like a profound act of defiance.

He selected the profile, typed a quick sentence into the text box while the machine rested securely on his lap, and hit the execute key.

<aac>Testing. This is my new voice,</aac> the crisp, female, Australian-accented voice announced to the crowded room.

Jordan instantly tensed, bracing his shoulders. He waited for someone to tell him it was wrong, or weird, or unacceptable for a guy to use a female voice.

Instead, Aubrie’s face lit up with a brilliant smile. Madison let out a cheerful, "I love it!" Lauren and Hannah beamed at him. Mark offered an approving thumbs-up, and Daniel gave a warm, encouraging nod. Even Zoë gave a small smile of logical approval.

The entire room—Aubrie, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Mark, Madison, and Daniel—happily and seamlessly accepted his choice without a single question or judgment. A wave of profound empowerment washed over Jordan as he rested his hands on the keyboard. He finally had a voice, and it was entirely his own.
