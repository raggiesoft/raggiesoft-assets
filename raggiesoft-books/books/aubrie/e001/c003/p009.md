## 7:30 AM — Morning Divergence

The sharp, mechanical buzz of a digital alarm clock shattered the quiet of Room 214.

Aubrie’s hand shot out from under the blankets, blindly slapping the snooze button on the nightstand to kill the noise. As the room settled back into a heavy silence, Jordan opened his eyes. He was greeted by the steady, reassuring rhythm of Aubrie’s breathing. She was still curled up beside him in the narrow bed, her arm draped protectively across his waist. The nightmare-fueled dread that usually accompanied his mornings was entirely absent, replaced by a profound, grounding safety.

Above them, the springs of the top bunk creaked. Madison swung her legs over the side and climbed down the wooden ladder, her hair sleep-tousled but her energy already shifting into practical motion.

"Alright, suite family," Madison whispered brightly, kneeling beside the dresser to pull out a stack of clean clothes. "First day of classes. Let's get moving."

Aubrie stirred, rubbing the sleep from her eyes. She offered Jordan a soft, lingering smile before untangling herself from the blankets. Without a word of complaint, the two women fell into a seamless, coordinated routine. They gathered Jordan’s clean clothes, a stack of towels, and their own shower caddies, then helped him transfer into his wheelchair.

They bypassed the cramped connecting bathroom and headed down the quiet hallway to the spacious accessible shower room. Once the heavy wooden door clicked shut and locked behind them, the radical, judgment-free logic of their shared living arrangement took over.

There was no hesitation and no performance of modesty. They helped Jordan navigate the difficult process of toileting with clinical, unwavering respect. Then, guided by pure pragmatism, the two women utilized the facilities themselves. They were already there, the morning clock was ticking, and they had already crossed the ultimate threshold of vulnerability with one another. It was a shared, undeniable reality: they all had physical needs, and there was absolutely no reason to hide them.

When it was time to shower, the dynamic shifted from simple caretaking to something deeper. The accessible room featured only a single large, roll-in shower stall. With the clock ticking toward their morning lectures, Aubrie turned on the warm water, letting the stall fill with steam. She looked at Jordan, her cheeks flushing slightly, but her eyes held a steady, undeniable affection.

Instead of reaching in from the outside to assist him, Aubrie stepped fully into the water with him.

It was a quiet, monumental shift. As the warm water washed over them, she carefully washed his hair, her touches lingering, infused with a tender, budding romance that went far beyond mere assistance. Jordan closed his eyes, his heart hammering against his ribs. He had never been touched with such gentleness, never been looked at as someone worthy of romantic intimacy. Having Aubrie pressed close to him in the warm steam was a revelation of absolute trust.

A few minutes later, Aubrie finished washing her own hair. She glanced through the steam at Madison, who was patiently waiting her turn near the sinks. Aubrie realized the math of the morning commute was getting tight, but more importantly, she remembered the profound pact they had made the afternoon prior. Madison fancied him, too.

Aubrie reached for a towel and stepped out of the water, offering Madison a warm, encouraging smile as she handed over the bottle of shampoo.

"I'm all rinsed off, and we're on a massive time crunch," Aubrie said smoothly, her voice completely devoid of jealousy. "Do you want to step in and finish helping him while you wash up?"

Madison’s blue eyes widened in mild surprise for a fraction of a second before softening into a brilliant, deeply grateful smile. "Yeah," Madison said softly, appreciating the beautiful lack of territorial boundaries. "I'd love to."

Madison stepped into the warm stall, taking over the shower. The energy shifted from Aubrie's quiet, romantic intensity to Madison's bright, affectionate warmth. As Madison washed her own hair and expertly helped Jordan finish his shower, she chatted easily with him, her hands gentle and caring. For Jordan, sitting in the warm steam, the experience was earth-shattering. He was being handed back and forth not as a burdensome chore, but as someone cherished. He was experiencing the absolute, undeniable proof of their Constellation.

Once they were all clean, dried, and dressed, they returned to Room 214. Madison immediately opened the connecting bathroom door and knocked lightly on the door to Room 216.

"Are you three decent?" Madison called out.

"We are," Zoë’s crisp British voice replied.

Hannah and Lauren stepped through the bathroom, fully dressed and buzzing with first-day energy. Lauren practically bounced on her heels, eager to go meet Tom before his early lecture, while Hannah gave a warm, contented smile, already anticipating her breakfast with Mark.

"Have a great first day, J," Hannah said, leaning down to wrap her arms securely around Jordan’s shoulders in a tight, sisterly hug.

Lauren followed suit, giving him a cheerful squeeze. "See you guys this afternoon!"

As Hannah and Lauren disappeared out the suite door to meet their boyfriends, Jordan watched them go. He realized, with a wave of calm clarity, that he didn't feel abandoned. He wasn't being left behind. He was simply watching his family expand their perimeter.

"Right then," Zoë said, adjusting her glasses as she walked into Room 214, her backpack slung over one shoulder. "Shall we proceed to the dining hall? I require a proper cup of tea before I can process any academic syllabi, though I am fully bracing myself for the American atrocity of lukewarm water and paper-flavored tea."

The four of them made the trek across the rolling campus to Kincaid Hall. The cafeteria was already a chaotic sea of freshmen scrambling to eat before their morning lectures.

While Aubrie and Madison guided Jordan toward the food stations, Zoë broke off toward the beverage line with low expectations. She anticipated finding a standard metal urn dispensing tepid water. Instead, she stopped dead in her tracks.

Mounted directly into the stainless-steel counter was a dedicated, high-pressure boiling water tap. A small digital readout on the dispenser proudly displayed **100°C**. Beside it sat a neatly organized rack of premium, robust black tea bags.

"Can I help you find anything, hon?" a cafeteria worker asked, noticing Zoë staring at the tap.

"The water," Zoë said, pointing to the readout. "It is actually boiling. Not simmering. Boiling."

The worker laughed, wiping down the counter. "Oh, yeah. CPI has a massive British exchange program. Five years ago, they nearly started a riot over the coffee urns. The university finally ripped out the old machines and installed proper boiling taps in every dining hall on campus just to keep the peace."

A rare, thoroughly impressed smile spread across Zoë's face. "Outstanding."

She prepared her tea exactly to her specifications—steeping the bag in the boiling water for precisely four minutes before adding a splash of milk—and rejoined the group with a newfound respect for the university's logistics.

She found them in the serving area, joining the mass of students staring up at the brightly illuminated breakfast menus. Jordan wheeled forward, immediately hunching his shoulders. He squinted intensely at the glowing boards, his eyes narrowing into slits as he tried to force the blurry, floating shapes into legible words.

Before Aubrie could even take a step forward, Zoë moved seamlessly to his side, cradling her perfectly brewed tea in one hand.

Zoë gently wrapped her free arm around Jordan's shoulders, pulling him into a secure, grounding side-hug. Leaning down, she cupped her hand around his right ear, expertly blocking out the overwhelming wall of cafeteria noise.

"The font scaling on these displays is highly illogical," Zoë murmured directly into his ear, her crisp London accent cutting through the static with perfect clarity. "Let us review your options. Station one offers scrambled eggs, bacon, and toast. Station two is a waffle bar. Which data set appeals to you this morning?"

Jordan relaxed instantly, leaning into Zoë's supportive embrace. He quickly pulled his Vanguard Pro onto his lap, his fingers flying across the keys.

<aac>Waffles, please,</aac> the Sydney voice of EchoType responded.

Standing a few feet away, Aubrie watched the interaction unfold. Her heart swelled. In any other dorm, in any other social circle, a girl watching another woman wrap her arm around the boy she was falling in love with would spark instant, bitter jealousy.

Aubrie felt nothing but pure, radiant joy. The pact was working. Jordan was being anchored, protected, and loved from multiple sides, exactly as they had designed.

They gathered their food and settled at a quiet table near the back of the hall. As Madison and Zoë debated the most efficient walking routes to the engineering buildings, Aubrie turned her full attention to Jordan.

"Okay, Astronomy 101," Aubrie began, her eyes sparkling with excitement. She knew his mother had forced him into the class to watch him fail, so she immediately set to work dismantling the trap.

"Don't worry about the math right now," Aubrie explained, leaning in close so he could hear her clearly. "The math is just the language we use to describe the system. Focus on the rules. A star is basically a giant balancing act. Gravity wants to crush it inward, and nuclear fusion wants to blow it apart. As long as those two forces are perfectly equal, the star survives. It’s a closed loop, Jordan. It’s entirely predictable."

Jordan watched her, captivated. His mother’s world was a chaotic, unpredictable minefield of shifting rules and sudden punishments. But here, Aubrie was offering him a universe governed by absolute, unbreakable logic.

He typed on his keyboard, hitting the execute key.

<aac>So... if you know the mass, you know exactly how it will die?</aac> the Australian voice asked.

"Exactly!" Aubrie beamed, practically vibrating with enthusiasm. "The rules never lie. If it's small, it cools into a white dwarf. If it's massive, it goes supernova. The universe doesn't play tricks, Jordan. We just have to learn how to read the patterns."

Jordan felt a slow, genuine smile spread across his face. For the first time in his life, he wasn't dreading the classroom. He was looking forward to it.

## 8:50 AM — Into the Stars

Outside the glass doors of Kincaid Hall, the sprawling campus was alive with a nervous, electric energy. Thousands of freshmen were crisscrossing the paved walkways, hunting for their first lecture halls. The suite family naturally began to splinter toward their respective academic buildings.

"Alright, duty calls," Madison sighed, adjusting the strap of her heavy backpack. She stepped forward and wrapped her arms around Jordan’s shoulders in a warm, lingering hug. "Have a great morning, J. See you this afternoon."

Zoë was next. She gave him a brisk but entirely sincere squeeze. "Do try to survive the chaos. We will reconvene at the suite this evening to analyze the day's data."

Jordan smiled, tapping his fingers against the armrest of his chair in a happy, staccato rhythm. As Madison and Zoë merged into the river of students heading toward the engineering quad, Aubrie stepped up beside him.

"Ready?" she asked, her eyes bright.

Jordan nodded, taking a deep breath.

Their 9:00 AM Astronomy 101 class was located in one of the older, brutalist concrete structures of the Science Complex. As they navigated through the heavy double doors and into the massive, tiered lecture hall, the architectural reality of the aging building immediately became apparent. The room was a steep amphitheater. A long flight of carpeted stairs led down to a stage at the very bottom, meaning the only wheelchair-accessible seating was a single row of desks bolted to the floor at the very top, in the absolute back of the room.

They settled into two seats near the exit. The room was already buzzing with the chatter of two hundred freshmen, and models of planets hung suspended from the high ceiling.

Jordan stared down at the massive projection screen at the front of the hall, his hands gripping the armrests of his chair. This was it. His mother’s weapon. The insurmountable mountain she had deliberately chosen for him, perfectly designed to ensure his academic failure.

He pulled his Vanguard Pro onto his lap, his fingers trembling slightly as he opened a blank document and typed. He angled the screen so Aubrie could read it without him having to trigger the Australian text-to-speech voice in the quiet room.

<aac>This is a lot,</aac> he typed, the cursor blinking rapidly.

"It's okay," Aubrie beamed, leaning in close so her shoulder brushed against his. Her excitement was a physical shield against his fear. "It's the real deal. No more high school physics. We're in this together, remember? I've got you. I'm not going to let you fail."

At exactly 9:00 AM, Dr. Alistair walked onto the stage far below them and turned on his microphone. A comfortable silence fell over the room as he brought up his first slide.

For Aubrie, the lecture hall instantly became a sanctuary—a place where the universe operated on clear, understandable laws. But for Jordan, the nightmare was just beginning.

From the very back row, the projection screen was nothing but a glowing, blurry white rectangle. The text on Dr. Alistair's slides smeared together into illegible gray smudges. Worse, the acoustics of the cavernous room were terrible. The professor’s voice echoed off the concrete walls, washing over Jordan in a muffled, distorted hum. He leaned forward, instinctively cupping his hand behind his ear, squinting so hard his eyes watered.

Panic spiked in his chest. *This was it.* His mother was right. He couldn't even perceive the class, let alone pass it.

Aubrie, hyper-observant as always, noticed his rigid posture immediately. She looked at his squinting eyes, then down at the way he was cupping his ear. The reality of his untreated sensory deficits became glaringly obvious.

She reached under the desk and gently, discreetly, took his hand.

Jordan flinched slightly at the unexpected contact, but her grip was firm, warm, and entirely certain. She laced her fingers through his and squeezed. With her free hand, she pulled her notebook directly between them and began writing in large, clear, block letters.

*I will take all the notes today. Do not panic.*

Jordan exhaled a shaky breath, staring at her neat handwriting. She quickly scribbled another line.

*You have that 1:00 PM meeting with Ms. Higgins at Educational Accessibility today. This is exactly what that meeting is for. Tell her you need front-row seating, a note-taker, and whatever else helps you see and hear. They HAVE to fix it.*

Jordan looked at her. In his mother's world, a barrier like this was a final judgment—proof that he was broken. In Aubrie's world, it was just an administrative error waiting for a logistical solution.

He squeezed her hand back, the terror in his chest slowly beginning to recede.

For the next fifty minutes, Dr. Alistair spoke about stellar nurseries and hydrostatic equilibrium. Jordan didn't understand the complex words, and he couldn't see the diagrams, but he stopped trying to force it. Instead, he just held Aubrie's hand and watched her. She was fascinated, hanging on every muffled word, her free hand flying across the notebook as she translated the muffled echoes of the professor into crisp, legible facts.

She was his safe person, and she loved this. Her excitement made the terrifying subject feel manageable. His mother's weapon suddenly seemed entirely defused. He had someone to climb the mountain with.

When Dr. Alistair finally dismissed the class, the spell was broken. The lecture hall erupted back into noise as students began packing their bags. Jordan let out a breath he felt like he'd been holding for nearly an hour. He had survived.

He looked at Aubrie as she finally let go of his hand to pack up her notebook.

"See?" she whispered directly into his ear, her smile bright. "Not so bad. And once Ms. Higgins gets your accommodations sorted, it'll be a breeze."

<aac>Not so bad,</aac> Jordan typed on his screen, offering her a small, genuine smile.

As they moved out into the crowded hallway, the mass of students jostled past them. Jordan tensed, his anxiety flaring at the claustrophobic press of bodies, but Aubrie seamlessly stepped in front of his wheelchair, acting as a small, sturdy buffer against the crowd.

"I guess we have to go our separate ways for our mid-morning classes," Aubrie said, her own expression dropping a little at the thought of leaving his side.

Jordan nodded, his heart sinking a fraction.

"Well," she said softly. And then, right there in the crowded hallway, she leaned down and wrapped her arms around his neck. This hug was different from the desperate, grounding embraces of the weekend. It was warm, lingering, and entirely voluntary—a reassuring physical promise that she wasn't disappearing.

He held onto her for a moment, absorbing her steadying presence. As she pulled back, her cheeks were slightly pink.

"Are you free for lunch?" she asked.

Jordan quickly pulled up his master schedule on his Vanguard Pro, though he already knew the answer. His heart gave a happy jolt.

<aac>Yes. At noon,</aac> EchoType replied in its crisp Australian cadence.

"Perfect," she beamed. "Meet me at the dining hall? Kincaid Commons?"

<aac>Yeah. Okay. Lunch.</aac>

"It's a date," Aubrie said brightly. Then her eyes went wide, realizing what she had just said. A deep, furious blush rushed all the way up to her hairline. "I mean... not a... you know. A literal date. Just... a lunch... with a time. I'll just see you then!"

She spun around, completely flustered, and hurried off toward the math building.

Jordan sat in the hallway, watching her go, a massive, uncontainable smile spreading across his face. He still had to face his mid-morning class entirely alone, a prospect that was undeniably intimidating. But as he maneuvered his wheelchair toward the elevators, his mind was remarkably at ease.

He had a schedule. He had a system. He knew he would see her on Mondays. Wednesdays, and Fridays. He knew he would see her in the dorm tonight. And now, he knew he would see her in just a couple of hours.

## 10:15 AM — The Ascent

"It's a date," Aubrie had said brightly in the crowded hallway of the Science Complex. Then her eyes had gone wide as she realized the weight of her own words. "I mean... not a... you know. A literal date. Just... a lunch... with a time. I'll just see you then!"

Flustered and blushing furiously, she had spun around to hurry off toward the math building. But after taking only two steps, she stopped. She turned back, marched right up to his wheelchair, leaned down, and quickly pressed her lips against his cheek.

It was a fleeting, lightning-fast peck, gone before Jordan could even register what was happening. She pulled away, her face burning an even deeper shade of crimson, and bolted down the corridor without looking back.

Jordan sat perfectly still in the middle of the rushing stream of freshmen. He slowly raised his hand, his fingertips resting against the skin of his cheek. The spot felt impossibly warm, practically glowing against the cool, air-conditioned air of the building.

It was his first kiss. From anyone. Ever.

His heart hammered a chaotic, frantic rhythm against his ribs. He felt entirely confused, completely overwhelmed, and wildly, desperately hopeful for noon to arrive.

But as the hallway began to empty out, reality slowly settled back in. For the very first time since arriving at CPI, the safety net of his suite family was entirely gone. Aubrie, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, and Hannah were all scattered across the massive campus. He was alone.

He pulled up his class schedule on his Vanguard Pro. *10:30 AM – HIST 101: Western Civilization I.* It was located in Carter Hall, an older brick building situated near the upper quad.

Jordan took a deep breath, gripped the hand rims of his wheelchair, and pushed through the heavy glass doors back out into the humid Virginia morning.

The topography of the campus was notoriously unforgiving. CPI had been built into a series of rolling, aggressive hills, and the path to Carter Hall was a steep, winding ribbon of concrete. Jordan started up the first major incline, his arms straining with the effort. Without Aubrie or Lauren walking behind him to help push, the true, brutal weight of his own momentum settled squarely onto his shoulders.

Halfway up the first ridge, his triceps began to burn with a dull, throbbing ache. The wheelchair slowed to a crawl. He grit his teeth, forcing the wheels forward inch by inch, but gravity was pulling him backward just as relentlessly.

"Hey, man," a voice called out.

Jordan flinched, instinctively bracing himself for an insult or a sharp reprimand for blocking the walkway.

Instead, a tall guy wearing a faded CPI lacrosse hoodie stepped up behind him. "This hill is a beast. You want a hand?"

Jordan blinked, surprised by the easy, casual tone. He gave a short, grateful nod.

Without making a spectacle of it, the lacrosse player grabbed the push handles and easily rolled Jordan the rest of the way up the ridge, bringing them to the flat plateau near the library.

"Have a good one," the guy smiled, giving Jordan a quick pat on the shoulder before jogging off to his own class.

Ten minutes later, as Jordan approached the final, sweeping incline leading up to Carter Hall, his arms were practically shaking with exhaustion. The burn was intense, and his progress ground to a halt.

Once again, the universe intervened. A girl carrying a massive stack of art portfolios noticed him struggling. She didn't ask; she simply shifted her heavy folders to one arm, stepped behind his chair with the other, and leaned her weight against the handles to help him crest the final slope.

"They really should put an elevator on the outside of this hill," she joked, breathing heavily as they reached the top. She smiled warmly at him. "See you around."

As Jordan rolled toward the entrance of Carter Hall, a dark, familiar shadow crept into the back of his mind. *His mother's voice.*

He had needed help twice in the span of fifteen minutes just to get to a basic freshman lecture. His mother’s harsh words echoed in his head, sharp and undeniable: *You are a burden. You are fundamentally incapable. You don't belong here.*

For a terrifying moment, the anxiety threatened to swallow him whole. Maybe she was right. Maybe he should have just stayed home in Hampton Roads, hidden away in his bedroom where the ground was flat and the expectations were zero. If he couldn't even push himself to class, how was he supposed to survive four years here?

But then, he thought about the morning.

He thought about Madison stepping into the shower stall, taking the shampoo bottle from Aubrie with a bright, unquestioning smile. He thought about Zoë shielding him from the overwhelming noise of the cafeteria, gently reading the menu directly into his ear. He thought about Mark’s firm, respectful handshake.

And he thought about his cheek, which still felt impossibly warm.

They had seen every limitation he had. They had seen his absolute most vulnerable moments. And none of them had looked at him like he was a burden. They had looked at him like he was family. The random students on the pathway hadn't looked at him with pity, either—they had just seen a fellow student fighting a steep hill, and they had stepped in to help.

Jordan pushed open the door to Carter Hall and wheeled himself into the air-conditioned lobby. His arms ached, his chest was heaving, and he still had to figure out how he was going to hear the history professor.

But as he navigated toward the classroom door, a quiet, defiant thought blossomed in his mind.

*Maybe,* he thought to himself, *I'll just get used to the hills.*
