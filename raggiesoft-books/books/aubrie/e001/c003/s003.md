Outside the single window, the heavy gray clouds that had hung low over Blacksburg all morning had bruised to a deep, threatening charcoal. Before he ventured out to explore the campus and before the sky opened up, he decided to set up his most important tool first: his laptop. He wheeled himself over to his desk, placed the heavy machine on it, and opened the lid. The battery icon blinked red, and a moment later, the screen went completely black. Dead.

He sighed, a silent puff of air, and squinted around the room for an outlet. He spotted one tucked low on the cinderblock wall underneath his wooden desk, but recessed behind the desk legs where it was awkwardly out of reach from his wheelchair.

Unzipping the backpack resting on the floor behind his chair, he pulled out the laptop's black power cable, intending to lean in and plug it in himself. He maneuvered his chair closer to the desk, stretching with the cord in hand, but he couldn't angle his frame far enough beneath the desk to push the prongs into the recessed wall socket. He grunted with frustration, a small but significant reminder that even in an "accessible" room, the world wasn't always designed with him in mind.

Out in the corridor, a polite, rhythmic knock sounded against the heavy wooden door, followed by a clear voice calling through the oak: *"Residence Life! Isabel, second-floor RA—doing early check-in rounds!"*

With his focus locked on the recessed socket under the desk—and completely unaware that he had lived his entire life with undiagnosed hearing loss—Aubrey didn't catch the muffled knock or her announcement through the thick solid-core fire door at all.

Following standard CPI Residence Life protocol when an early-arrival resident who had already checked in at the front desk didn't respond, Isabel paused five seconds, knocked a second time more firmly, and called out again out of concern for his safety: *"Residence Life! Aubrey, I'm going to tap my Poly Pass and key in to check on you!"*

Still hearing nothing from inside, Isabel tapped her RA Master Poly Pass against the exterior reader. With a soft electronic beep and the mechanical whir of the overhead opener, the latch released and the heavy oak door swung inward into Room 214 on its ten-second timer.

Catching the sudden movement and the bright hallway light spilling across the carpet out of the corner of his eye, a startled Aubrey turned in his wheelchair with the unplugged power cable still in his hand.

Standing on the threshold was a petite young woman on the shorter end—around five-foot-two, with straight jet-black hair past her shoulders, warm dark eyes behind stylish rectangular glasses, and a maroon CPI Housing & Residence Life Student Staff polo with her nametag and duty lanyard. Seeing that he hadn't heard her knocking—and immediately spotting his dark laptop on the desk, the unplugged power cable in his hand, and the awkward outlet tucked underneath the desk—she offered a warm, unhurried smile and a friendly wave so she wouldn't startle him.

"Hey there—sorry if I surprised you! I knocked twice, just wanted to make sure you were alright. Need a hand with that?"

Aubrey flinched slightly and instinctively cupped a hand behind his ear to catch her muffled, friendly voice. "I'm Isabel Dawson, one of the two RAs for this floor," she continued, stepping inside as the ten-second timer expired and the door eased shut behind her. "My room is 215, offset just across the hall—just doing my rounds for early check-ins."

Aubrey felt a flush of embarrassment at being caught struggling. With his laptop completely dead, he was entirely voiceless. Unable to speak, he simply gave a grateful nod and gestured to the power cable in his hand and the recessed outlet underneath the desk.

Isabel saw the problem instantly. "Oh yeah, that's a terrible spot for an outlet under the desk. No problem at all. Let's make this easier."

He reached back into his backpack on the floor and handed her his power strip. Ducking under the desk, she plugged the power strip into the wall outlet, ran the cord neatly up onto the desktop, and pulled a strip of double-sided tape from her pocket to affix the power strip securely to the top corner of the desk. "There," she said with a smile, raising her volume slightly after noticing his cupped ear. "Now you can plug your laptop and anything else right into the top of your desk."

Aubrey immediately plugged in his adapter and waited for the slow Quantum OS 7 system to boot up. Once the desktop appeared, he opened *SimpleSpeak*, hunching uncomfortably close to the monitor and squinting intensely to make out the keys as he typed. He hit the execute key.

<aac>Wow. Thanks. Thank you,</aac> the robotic, digitized voice announced to the room. Aubrey looked up, offering a genuinely grateful smile.

Isabel didn't bat an eye at the synthesized voice. "Don't mention it. Do you need a hand with anything else? Getting your bed set up?"

Aubrey hesitated, then nodded. He turned back to the screen and typed again.

<aac>Actually, yeah. The pillows and blankets are kind of a pain.</aac>

For the next ten minutes, Isabel helped him peel open the heavy packing tape on the cardboard box from the mail room and make his bed, placing his sheets, pillows, and blankets exactly how he liked them. She unpacked his canvas gym bag, putting his folded clothes neatly away in the dresser drawers, and set his clean towels and toiletries bag right on the edge of the roll-under sink counter where he could easily reach them. She was efficient, friendly, and didn't make him feel like a burden for a single second.

"There you go," she said finally. "All set. Also, just so you know, the accessible roll-in shower room for our floor is just a few doors down the hall toward the central atrium." Walking back to the door, she pressed the square **PUSH TO OPEN** handicap button on the wall to unlock and swing the door open, then paused at the threshold and gestured diagonally across the staggered hallway toward her own door. "Seriously, Aubrey, my room is right across the hall in 215. If you need any help with anything at all, you just knock on my door, or find our other floor RA, Henry Walsh, over in the other wing in 201, or our Hall Director, Kelsey. That's what we're here for."

Aubrey leaned close to the keyboard and typed a final message before the door's ten-second timer could close it between them.

<aac>I will. Thank you, Isabel. Do you know who my two roommates are? Or when they will arrive?</aac>

Isabel caught the edge of the door lightly with one hand as the timer expired, glancing down at her clipboard with an apologetic look. "I'm sorry, I only have the Friday early check-in list on my clipboard right now," she explained. "Since Bennett Hall is co-ed by suite now—each connecting suite is all guys or all girls, even if the next suite over is the opposite gender—Central Housing is still finishing the master room roster and sending their student runners over this afternoon to put up the door cards for tomorrow. Main move-in starts tomorrow morning, so you can expect the other guys in your suite to start arriving tomorrow at the soonest. Looks like you've got the whole place to yourself for tonight!"

As she stepped out into the corridor and let the heavy door swing shut and lock automatically behind her, Aubrey nodded, a wave of both relief and nervousness washing over him. One night of peace before meeting the strangers who would become his new roommates.
