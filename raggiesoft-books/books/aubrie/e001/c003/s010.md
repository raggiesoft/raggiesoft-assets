## 10:30 AM — Western Civilization

The classroom in Carter Hall was much smaller and older than the massive science amphitheater, smelling faintly of lemon polish and old paper. Jordan wheeled himself into a spot at the end of the front row just as the professor—a tall, older man with a neatly trimmed beard—walked in and set his leather briefcase on the desk.

"Welcome to History 101," the professor announced, turning to the chalkboard to write his name. "I am Dr. Gable."

From his spot in the front row, Jordan immediately leaned forward. The white chalk on the dusty green board was completely unreadable, and Dr. face had been turned toward the wall when he spoke, muffling the sound. Jordan instinctively cupped his hand behind his ear and squinted, a familiar spike of anxiety hitting his chest.

Dr. Gable turned around. His eyes swept the front row, immediately landing on Jordan’s strained posture.

The professor didn't sigh. He didn't ask Jordan what was wrong, and he didn't draw the attention of the entire room to the boy in the wheelchair. Instead, he simply set the piece of white chalk down. He reached into his desk drawer, pulled out a thick, heavy-duty black marker, and walked over to the whiteboard mounted on the adjacent wall.

When Dr. Gable spoke again, he took two steps closer to Jordan's side of the room. He squared his shoulders, faced the class directly, and projected his voice with crisp, booming clarity that easily cut through Jordan's hearing deficit.

"Let's try the whiteboard instead," Dr. Gable boomed pleasantly. "Now, before we dive into Mesopotamia, I prefer to run this class as a discussion rather than a straight lecture. To make that work, I want everyone paired up. Can I get a volunteer to partner with our gentleman in the front row and help ensure he catches all the notes?"

A chair scraped against the linoleum a few rows back.

"I can do it, Professor."

Jordan’s head snapped up. The voice cut through the air with a distinct, undeniable cadence. He recognized the accent immediately—it was the exact same Sydney inflection that his EchoType software used.

A girl with dark hair pulled into a messy ponytail walked down the aisle and casually dropped her backpack into the empty desk right next to his wheelchair.

"Hey, mate," she smiled, extending her hand. "I'm Sarah."

Jordan blinked, slightly taken aback by her easy, radiant confidence. He shook her hand, finding her grip firm and friendly.

"I live over in Bennett Hall," Sarah continued, settling into her chair and pulling out a notebook. "Up on the fifth floor. You're the bloke from the second floor, right? The famous suite?"

Jordan tensed slightly, bracing himself for the inevitable invasive questions or the weird, pitying looks he usually got when people figured out his living situation.

But Sarah just smiled, completely unbothered. "Good on ya. I heard you've got the biggest bathroom in the building down there. Absolute luxury compared to the nightmare we're dealing with on five."

She made absolutely no big deal out of the fact that he was living with five women. To her, it was just another quirky piece of campus logistics. Her total lack of judgment made Jordan’s shoulders drop two inches in relief.

Jordan pulled open his Vanguard Pro. Because he didn't want to use his EchoType software and disrupt the quiet classroom with its digitized voice, he opted for a completely different approach. He opened a sleek, dark-themed terminal window and launched NeoEdit—a streamlined, text-based command-line interface that Zoë had pre-installed for him. It was simple, completely distraction-free, and most importantly, it allowed him to scale the neon-green text up massively against the stark black background so he could actually read it.

<aac>I'm Jordan,</aac> he typed, angling the screen so Sarah could see the large letters. <aac>Thank you for helping me.</aac>

"No worries at all, Jordan," Sarah whispered, her Australian accent warm and perfectly clear. "Let's smash this class."

The next hour was a revelation.

Dr. Gable was a masterful professor. He kept his voice loud, always making sure he was facing the front row when he spoke, and wrote his key points in massive, bold letters on the whiteboard. When the class shifted into a discussion about the agricultural revolution, Sarah proved to be the ultimate partner. She casually slid her notebook over so Jordan could read her meticulously organized bullet points.

When Dr. Gable posed a question to their side of the room, Jordan’s fingers flew across his keyboard.

<aac>The shift to agriculture created static borders, which required the invention of standing armies to defend them,</aac> Jordan typed into his terminal.

Sarah glanced at his screen, didn't miss a single beat, and raised her hand. "Jordan points out that the shift to agriculture created static borders," she announced clearly to the room, seamlessly acting as his voice. "Which required the invention of standing armies to defend them."

"Excellent point, Jordan," Dr. Gable nodded approvingly.

Jordan sat back in his chair, completely stunned. It was working. He was actively participating in a college-level discussion. He wasn't a burden, he wasn't a disruption, and he wasn't failing.

When the class finally ended, Jordan felt a rush of genuine, unadulterated triumph.

"Brilliant work today," Sarah smiled, packing her notebooks into her bag. She pulled out a pen and scribbled a string of numbers onto a scrap of paper, handing it to him. "Here’s my mobile number. Text me if you need the notes for whatever reason, or if you just want to go over the reading before Friday."

Jordan typed a quick <aac>Thank you</aac> into his terminal, taking the slip of paper with a massive smile.

"Catch you later, Jordan," Sarah waved, heading for the door.

As Jordan rolled out of Carter Hall and back into the bright Virginia sunshine, the brutal, rolling hills of the CPI campus didn't look nearly as terrifying as they had an hour ago. He had survived his first solo trek. He had found a professor who actually cared. He had made a new friend.

And most importantly, it was almost noon. It was time to go meet Aubrie.

## 12:00 PM — The Broken Elevator

As Dr. Gable formally dismissed the class, Sarah slung her backpack over her shoulder and looked down at Jordan.

"Where are you heading next?" she asked easily.

Jordan quickly typed into his terminal. <aac>Kincaid Commons. Lunch.</aac>

"Perfect, that’s exactly on the way to my next lecture," Sarah smiled. "I’m heading to the building right across from the lower delivery bays. You want a lift? These hills are absolute murder before noon."

Jordan hesitated for a fraction of a second before nodding. Sarah seamlessly stepped behind his wheelchair, grabbed the push handles, and steered him out of Carter Hall.

As they navigated the winding concrete paths downward, Jordan realized that his new friend was surprisingly strong. She handled the steep, downward grades with ease, holding the chair steady so gravity didn't rip it out of her control, chatting happily about the history syllabus the entire time. It was a massive relief not to have to fight the terrain, but more importantly, he was struck by how normal it felt. Sarah didn't act like she was doing a noble, charitable deed; she just saw a friend who needed to go in the same direction.

When they reached the bottom of the hill, they arrived at the rear, ground-level entrance of Kincaid Commons. The massive brick building was built directly into the incline, meaning the main entrance was a full story above them on the upper quad.

"Right, this is my stop," Sarah said, bringing the chair to a halt on the concrete patio just outside the delivery bays. She walked around to the front, offering a warm, polite smile and a quick wave. "Brilliant working with you today, Jordan. See you on Wednesday."

"Thank you," Jordan mouthed silently, offering a grateful wave back.

As Sarah jogged across the street toward her next class, a wave of anticipation washed over him. It was noon. He was finally going to see Aubrie.

He wheeled himself toward the glass doors of the lower level, his stomach tight with a mix of hunger and nervous excitement. But as he rolled into the dimly lit lower lobby, he found nothing but a series of loading docks and staff offices. There was no clear path to the main dining area above. He was completely lost.

His mPhone buzzed in his pocket. He pulled it out to see a text from Aubrie.

**Aubrie:** <sms>Hey! I’m here. Just inside the main entrance, by the stairs. Waiting for you! :)</sms>

Jordan’s heart sank. He was at the delivery entrance on the exact opposite side—and elevation—of the building. He quickly texted her back, his thumbs fumbling slightly on the screen.

**Jordan:** <sms>I think I'm at a different door. The lower level? I'm looking for the elevator but I don't see one.</sms>

He wheeled back and forth on the concrete patio, feeling a sudden, sharp jolt of panic. A cafeteria worker, pushing a large metal cart of supplies, noticed him spinning his wheels in the empty corridor.

"You looking for the way in, honey?" she asked kindly.

Jordan nodded quickly. He held up his mPhone, pointing to the word <aac>elevator</aac> on his screen.

The worker sighed, her face immediately falling into an expression of deep sympathy. "Ah, yeah, that's because the main public one is broken. The motor burned out this morning." She keyed her radio. "Hang on, let me get my manager. He can take you up the freight elevator."

A moment later, a man in a polo shirt with a manager's name tag pushed through a set of swinging metal doors. "Hi there," he said, assessing the situation. "Sorry about our elevator. Come with me, let's get you the scenic route."

He badged them through a heavy steel door marked *Staff Only.*

Jordan followed him, instantly crossing the threshold into a maze of loud, steamy industrial kitchens. The sensory assault was immediate and overwhelming. The clanging of massive metal pots, the aggressive hiss of industrial dishwashers, and the muffled, chaotic shouting of the line cooks hit him all at once. Because of his untreated hearing loss, the noise wasn't crisp; it was a terrifying, distorted wall of vibration and pressure. The heat of the ovens felt suffocating.

He hunched his shoulders, keeping his eyes glued to the manager's polo shirt, praying for it to end. The manager led him to a large, battered metal freight elevator and swiped a key against the panel. The heavy grated doors slammed shut, and they rode up one floor.

When the doors opened again, Jordan found himself emerging from an unmarked employee door directly into the bright, chaotic main dining area.

He saw her immediately.

Aubrie was standing right where she said she'd be, looking anxiously toward the front entrance. Her hair was catching the light from the massive glass windows, and she was shifting her weight from foot to foot, clearly worried about him.

Her face lit up the second she spotted him rolling out of the kitchen. But as she took a step forward, her brilliant smile faltered. She saw his expression.

Jordan stopped the wheelchair. The anxiety from the broken elevator, the claustrophobic sensory overload of the steamy kitchen, and the sheer frustration of being lost had all collided inside his chest. But underneath it all was a deeper, more profound terror.

Looking at Aubrie—radiant, safe, and waiting for him—a dark, invasive thought seized his mind. *What if this isn't real?*

The brief, terrifying moment of being lost, of being unable to find her, had felt entirely too much like his old life. He was suddenly terrified that this was all a beautifully constructed dream, a cruel trick of his own mind, and that he was going to wake up at any second, trapped back in his mother's suburban house in Virginia Beach, forever at her mercy.

Aubrie saw the raw, unmistakable fright in his eyes. She didn't know the full logistical cause of his panic, only that the boy she was falling for was terrified.

She didn't hesitate. She rushed to him, closing the distance in seconds. Right there in the middle of the crowded dining hall, entirely uncaring of who was watching, she threw her arms around him and pulled him into a desperate, grounding hug. She buried her face in his neck, her arms serving as a firm, undeniable anchor against the overwhelming tide of his panic.

"Hey," she whispered into his ear, her voice fierce and absolute. "You're okay. You found me. You're safe."

She felt him trembling against her, his hands gripping the back of her shirt like it was a lifeline. Her heart ached for him. She realized something was deeply scaring him, something far heavier than just a broken elevator. She wished, with a sudden, powerful longing, that she could just skip her afternoon classes entirely and stay with him, acting as his shield for the rest of the day.

But she couldn't. They both had to keep moving forward.

The hug in the middle of the chaotic dining hall had grounded him. Jordan let out a shaky breath as Aubrie finally pulled back just enough to look into his eyes, though she kept a firm, reassuring hand on his shoulder.

She offered him a soft, affectionate smile, leaned in, and pressed a quick, gentle kiss to his cheek.

Jordan blinked, his breath hitching in his chest. It was only the second kiss of his entire life—the second one *today*—and the sudden, undeniable warmth of it chased away the last lingering chill of his panic.

"Come on," Aubrie said, her voice bright, deliberately ignoring the stares of a few passing upperclassmen. "Let's go get lunch. Together."

## 12:15 PM — The Taste of Freedom

Aubrie stepped behind his wheelchair and pushed him toward the long, winding serving lines. The noise and smell of the food were overwhelming, a chaotic mix of sizzling grease, baking bread, and a hundred overlapping conversations.

"Hey," Aubrie said, noticing him looking nervously at the stack of plastic trays. "It's a massive pain to try and balance a tray on your lap while rolling. Let me just grab yours for you. What do you want?"

Jordan looked up at the massive, illuminated menus above the stations. Pizza. Pasta. A deli. A grill. He froze. He was completely, utterly overwhelmed by the choices. His entire life, food had been a simple, non-negotiable command. His mother made something, and he ate it. If he didn't like what Aubrie Sinclair was serving, he didn't eat. He was never asked what he wanted.

"Jordan?" Aubrie asked gently, seeing him staring blankly at the glowing boards. "You okay?"

Then, he spotted it. At the grill station, a student in a maroon apron was flipping burgers on a flat top. A basket of fresh, golden french fries was resting right next to the fryer. His mother never made burgers, viewing them as greasy garbage, and fries were a rare, frozen-bag-in-the-oven affair that always came with a lecture about health.

He pulled out his mPhone, his thumbs moving quickly. He turned the screen toward her.

<aac>Can I... can I have that? A cheeseburger? And... fries?</aac>

"A burger? Heck yeah. Good choice," Aubrie said, not blinking an eye. She had absolutely no idea she had just enthusiastically approved two items his mother would have outright denied him. "What do you want on it?"

He was stunned. He typed a single word: <aac>Everything.</aac>

"You got it. One cheeseburger with everything, and a side of fries," she called to the worker over the glass partition. "What about a drink?"

He pointed to the soda fountains, another heavily restricted battleground he'd always lost at home. He typed: <aac>A soda?</aac>

"Perfect." Aubrie grabbed a plastic cup, filled it with cola, and placed it on the tray next to the paper boat of fries and the foil-wrapped burger. She then quickly grabbed her own lunch—a large salad and a bottle of water.

They arrived at the register. Jordan, suddenly realizing he had to actually purchase the food, began to panic. He fumbled awkwardly at his pocket, his fine motor skills failing him under the pressure as he tried to dig out his Poly Pass. He expected Aubrie to sigh in frustration or simply shove him aside and pay for it herself.

Instead, Aubrie set her tray down on the counter. She stepped to his side, gently placing her hand over his trembling fingers to steady them.

"Hey. Take your time. You've got this," she murmured softly. She helped him guide his wallet out of his pocket and slide the plastic Poly Pass free.

Jordan took a deep breath, reached up, and swiped his own card through the reader. The digital register chirped merrily, displaying a deduction from his tax-free **Forge Funds** balance. He had done it. He had chosen his own meal, and he had paid for it himself. It was a tiny, mundane transaction for anyone else, but for Jordan, it was a massive, intoxicating hit of pure independence.

Aubrie swiped her own card, balanced the two trays, and steered them toward a quiet table near the back windows overlooking the quad.

Once they were seated, Jordan unwrapped his burger. He took a bite, and the taste of salt, melted cheese, and absolute freedom flooded his senses. It was the best thing he had ever eaten.

Aubrie watched him with a soft smile, stabbing a cherry tomato with her plastic fork. "So? How was your mid-morning class? You survived the solo trek across the hills?"

Jordan’s face lit up. He swallowed, quickly wiping his hands on a napkin, and pulled his Vanguard Pro onto the table. He launched his sleek NeoEdit terminal, eager to tell her everything. He scaled the bright green text up so she could read it easily across the table.

<aac>The hills were brutal. But a couple of people actually helped push me. And the class was amazing. History 101.</aac>

"Yeah?" Aubrie leaned forward, genuinely invested. "Did the professor use the microphone? Could you hear anything?"

Jordan’s fingers flew across the keys.

<aac>I couldn't hear him at first, and he was writing on a dusty chalkboard. But he noticed me squinting. He stopped, switched to a whiteboard with heavy black markers, and moved closer so I could hear him. He didn't even make a big deal out of it.</aac>

"Jordan, that’s incredible!" Aubrie beamed, practically vibrating with second-hand excitement. "See? Some of the professors here actually get it."

<aac>But it gets better,</aac> Jordan typed, a massive grin on his face. <aac>He asked for a volunteer to partner with me. A girl sat next to me. She’s from Australia. Her name is Sarah.</aac>

Aubrie raised an eyebrow, smiling. "An Australian study buddy? That’s awesome."

<aac>She was great,</aac> Jordan continued. <aac>She lives on the fifth floor of Bennett Hall. I told her I was in the ADA suite, and she already knew about it. She didn't think it was weird at all. She took all my notes, and when I typed answers into my terminal, she read them out loud to the class for me. I actually participated.</aac>

Aubrie’s heart swelled. She reached across the table and lightly tapped the edge of his laptop. "Look at you," she said, her voice thick with pride. "Day one, and you're already leading classroom discussions and making friends on the fifth floor."

Jordan looked down at his keyboard, his cheeks flushing pink, but he couldn't stop smiling. The morning that had started with a terrifying astronomy lecture and a panic attack in the kitchen had completely turned around. He was capable. He belonged here.

Aubrie checked the clock on the dining hall wall. It was 12:45 PM.

"Eat up, genius," she said gently, packing up her empty salad container. "You've got a one o'clock appointment with Ms. Higgins at Educational Accessibility. It's time to make sure every single professor treats you like Dr. Gable did."
