## 9:45 AM — The Mega-Meetup

The massive, stadium-style lecture hall in the College of Health Sciences was already buzzing with the low, chaotic energy of three hundred freshmen. The room was steeply tiered, built to accommodate massive general education requirements, with a wide, flat accessible row running straight across the middle tier.

Aubrie pushed Jordan’s Vanguard Pro through the double doors, followed closely by Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, and Mark. They claimed a massive block of seating right in the center of the accessible row. Jordan parked his wheelchair at the end of the long desk, plugging his headphones into the FM receiver and setting up his new MagnaByte mPad next to his laptop.

The fact that all seven of them were in this exact section was a massive, statistically absurd coincidence. But the fact that Jordan was in the class at all was a masterstroke of administrative negligence by his mother.

Like all undergraduates at CPI, Jordan was required to complete two credits of Health or Physical Education to graduate. Over the summer, Aubrie Sinclair had logged into his student portal to build his schedule. She had seen "HLTH 201" fulfilled the requirement, checked the box, and moved on, completely failing to read the actual course title or description.

If she had bothered to look, she would have realized she had just enrolled her heavily sheltered, medically isolated son in *Dimensions of Human Sexuality*.

For eighteen years, Aubrie Sinclair had religiously signed the opt-out forms for every single public school Family Life and sexual education module. Now, thanks to her own careless rushing, the university was about to give him a comprehensive, college-level education on the exact subject she had spent his entire life trying to hide from him.

"Jordan!"

A bright, distinctly Australian voice cut through the chatter. Jordan looked up from his terminal. Sarah Braithwaite was bounding down the tiered stairs, a massive smile on her face. Two other girls were trailing closely behind her.

Jordan’s face lit up. He quickly typed into his NeoEdit terminal, the neon-green text glowing against the sleek, dark glass of his new Dark Aero window manager.

<aac>Sarah! You're in this class too?</aac>

"Gen-ed requirement," Sarah grinned, dropping her backpack onto the desk next to Lauren. "I saw you guys roll in. These are my roommates from the fifth floor. Jessica, and Chloe."

Jessica, who shared Sarah's accent but carried herself with a much more grounded, no-nonsense energy, offered a friendly wave. "Nice to finally meet the famous Room 214."

Chloe smiled warmly, claiming a seat next to Madison. "Sarah hasn't stopped talking about how much you saved her in Western Civ yesterday."

Sarah laughed, sliding into the seat between Jessica and Lauren. She turned to Lauren, offering her hand. "I'm Sarah, by the way. I don't think we formally met in the lobby on Saturday."

"Lauren," she replied, shaking Sarah's hand with a bright, genuine smile. "I'm in 216. It is so nice to meet you. We were just saying how incredibly lucky Jordan was to get you as a note-taker on the very first day."

"Oh, please, he's the one who got me Priority Registration," Sarah said easily as she pulled out her notebook. "He's an absolute lifesaver. How's the whole suite dynamic going? Is it as chaotic as everyone thinks?"

"Honestly? It's amazing," Lauren admitted, her smile softening as she looked down the row at Jordan, who was currently showing Jessica how his FM receiver worked. "It just... works. We all really look out for each other."

They fell into an easy, comfortable conversation. Sitting side-by-side in the crowded lecture hall, the girls bonded instantly over the shared anomaly of Jordan’s living situation and the chaotic reality of freshman year. Neither of them brought up their boyfriends. In the bright, bustling energy of the morning, surrounded by friends, the dark, uneasy shadow of Lauren's phone call with Tom the night before felt temporarily pushed away.

At exactly 10:00 AM, the lights in the auditorium dimmed slightly. A tall, fiercely confident professor with a wireless microphone clipped to her lapel strode to the center of the stage. She didn't look like a stuffy academic; she exuded a vibrant, no-nonsense energy that instantly commanded the room.

"Welcome to Health Sciences 201: Dimensions of Human Sexuality," she announced, her voice echoing clearly through the massive room. Instantly, the same audio pumped directly into Jordan's headphones, crisp and perfectly isolated from the background noise. "I am Dr. Evans."

The projector flared to life, casting the syllabus onto the massive screen at the front of the room. A split second later, the exact same document appeared in high resolution on Jordan's silver mPad.

"Over the next fifteen weeks, we are going to have frank, mature, and clinical discussions about human biology," Dr. Evans continued, clicking to the first slide. "We will cover male and female anatomical structures, the physiological mechanics of the reproductive system, physical response cycles, and sexual health. And let me be clear right now: I expect you to know the proper medical terminology for the exam. But I also know what you all call these parts on the weekend."

A ripple of nervous laughter moved through the auditorium.

"I am not afraid to use the street slang in this room," she stated flatly, pacing the stage. "If we are talking about a penis or a vagina, we will use the clinical terms, but I will also make sure you understand the slang you're going to hear out in the real world. There is zero shame in this classroom. Anatomy is anatomy."

Lauren and Sarah exchanged a highly amused glance. Mark let out a quiet chuckle. Aubrie and Madison pulled out their notebooks, immediately respecting the professor's unapologetic vibe.

"A few ground rules," Dr. Evans said, leaning against the front podium. "First: my office is in Room 412. There is a massive glass bowl of condoms sitting right on the edge of my desk. They are free. You do not need to ask me for them. You do not need to make eye contact with me. Just walk in, take what you need, and walk out. Second: this class covers heavy, deeply personal material. If any of you need to have a private conversation with me—whether you have questions about birth control, if you need resources for an abortion, or if you simply grew up incredibly sheltered and need extra help understanding the basics without the whole class staring at you—email me. We will meet one-on-one outside of my regular office hours. Total privacy. No judgment, ever."

The room grew quiet, a genuine wave of respect washing over the three hundred students.

"Finally," Dr. Evans said, clapping her hands together once. "Before we dive into chapter one, I have a standard request from Educational Accessibility. We have a student in this section who requires a peer note-taker. The university will compensate you with Priority Course Registration. If you have decent handwriting and want the perk, come see me at the podium after class."

Down in the accessible row, all nine of Jordan's friends—Aubrie, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Mark, Sarah, Jessica, and Chloe—simultaneously turned their heads to look at him, grinning. Jordan couldn't help but smile, his cheeks flushing slightly at his own private army of note-takers.

But as Dr. Evans clicked to the next slide, launching into the introductory material, Jordan's smile quickly faded.

He was staring down at his mPad. The screen displayed a list of the upcoming textbook chapters. He read the clinical terminology, his eyes scanning the incredibly detailed anatomical diagrams that served as the header for the document. Dr. Evans fired off a few of the slang terms alongside the medical ones, but it didn't help.

A cold, heavy knot of pure panic began to form in his stomach.

He didn't know what any of these words meant. He didn't understand the diagrams. He didn't even know the slang. His mother hadn't just opted him out of the classes; she had actively shamed the concept of the human body to the point where he had learned to completely ignore his own physical existence. While the rest of the three hundred students in the room were comfortably absorbing the lecture, Jordan was staring at a completely alien language.

He looked down at his own hands, resting on the armrests of his wheelchair, a sudden, overwhelming wave of inadequacy washing over him. He wasn't just behind. He was entirely, fundamentally lost.

## 10:50 AM — Office Hours

At ten-fifty, the massive lecture hall erupted into the chaotic sounds of three hundred freshmen packing up their bags. As the crowd funneled up the tiered stairs toward the exits, Jordan’s group stayed exactly where they were.

Jordan carefully closed the lid of his Vanguard Pro laptop, sliding it into his backpack alongside his new mPad and FM receiver. Once the room had mostly cleared out, Aubrie unlocked the brakes on his wheelchair, and the massive ten-person entourage made its way down to the front of the stage.

Dr. Evans was at the podium, clicking out of her presentation. She looked up, her eyebrows raised in mild surprise as an absolute wall of students approached her.

"Well," Dr. Evans said, leaning against the podium with an amused smile. "I usually have to beg for a single peer note-taker. I don't think I've ever had an entire platoon volunteer on the first day."

Sarah laughed, stepping forward. "We're a package deal, Professor."

"And which one of you is the student registered with Educational Accessibility?" Dr. Evans asked, looking over the group.

Jordan raised his hand slightly, offering a shy, polite smile.

Dr. Evans looked at Jordan, then back at the nine people standing defensively and affectionately around his wheelchair. "Let me guess. You guys all live on the same floor?"

"Same suite, actually," Madison grinned. "Well, five of us. Plus a boyfriend, and three friends from the fifth floor. Have you heard the rumor about the housing clerical error in Bennett Hall?"

Dr. Evans’ eyes widened in sudden realization. She let out a sharp, genuine laugh. "Wait. You're the Suite 214 kid? The one who got dropped into a room full of women?"

Jordan nodded, pulling his laptop back out of his bag for a second just to type a quick response. He turned the screen so she could read the glowing green text.

<aac>I am. They decided to keep me.</aac>

"I can see that," Dr. Evans smiled, her tone softening with genuine warmth. "Alright, tell you what. My office hours start in ten minutes. Why don't you all follow me up to Room 412, and we'll get everyone's student ID numbers written down. If you're all willing to share the note-taking load for Jordan, I am more than happy to authorize Priority Registration for the whole group."

A collective cheer went up from the freshmen.

They followed Dr. Evans out of the auditorium and took the massive freight elevator up to the fourth floor. Room 412 was a standard, slightly cramped faculty office, filled with towering bookshelves and the smell of old paper. Sitting right on the edge of her desk, exactly as promised, was a massive, crystal-clear glass bowl overflowing with brightly colored foil condom wrappers.

It was a tight squeeze getting ten students into the office, but they made it work, shuffling around Jordan's wheelchair. Dr. Evans pulled out a legal pad and passed it around, letting Mark, Sarah, Jessica, Chloe, Hannah, Lauren, Zoë, Madison, and Aubrie write down their names and CPI ID numbers.

While the pad made its rounds, Jordan opened his Vanguard Pro again. His fingers hovered over the keyboard. The panic he had felt during the lecture was still sitting heavy in his chest, but Dr. Evans’ announcement at the beginning of class—her promise of total privacy and zero judgment—gave him the courage he needed.

He took a deep breath and began to type.

When the legal pad finally made its way back to Dr. Evans, Jordan pushed his wheelchair forward slightly, turning his laptop screen toward her desk.

Dr. Evans adjusted her glasses and read the bright green text.

<aac>Professor Evans, you said to let you know if we grew up extremely sheltered. My mother opted me out of every Family Life and health class since the fourth grade. She told me the human body was shameful. I don't know what any of the diagrams on the syllabus mean. I don't know any of the slang. I don't know how any of this works. I am completely lost.</aac>

The easy, amused energy in the office instantly vanished. Dr. Evans read the paragraph twice. When she looked up at Jordan, her eyes were filled with profound, quiet compassion.

Around him, the suite fell completely silent. Aubrie stepped a little closer, resting her hand gently on the back of his wheelchair in a silent show of absolute support. Lauren felt a sharp pang of empathy in her chest, the contrast between Jordan's deeply vulnerable honesty and Tom's toxic paranoia striking her all over again.

"Jordan," Dr. Evans said softly, her voice entirely devoid of pity, replaced instead with a fierce, protective academic respect. "Thank you for telling me that. I know that took a lot of courage."

She turned to her own desktop computer, her fingers flying across the keyboard.

"You are not the first student to sit in that chair with this exact problem," she told him firmly. "When parents completely isolate their children from basic biology, it does real damage. But you are here now, and we are going to fix it. There is absolutely nothing shameful about the human body, and you are not going to fail this class."

She hit print, and the machine in the corner of her office whirred to life. She grabbed the pages and handed them to Jordan. It was a list of highly reputable, clinical web resources—university health sites and basic anatomical guides designed for young adults.

"Start here," Dr. Evans instructed gently. "Look over these sites at your own pace. They explain the absolute basics. Read them over the next few days. If you are still feeling lost, you email me, and we will sit down one-on-one, and I will explain whatever you need. Okay?"

Jordan looked down at the printed URLs, a massive wave of relief washing over him. He looked back up, nodded emphatically, and typed: <aac>Thank you.</aac>

Aubrie leaned over Jordan's shoulder, looking at the printed pages. She saw the URLs for the 2D diagrams and clinical explanations. It was a great start, but she knew Jordan. She knew how his brain worked, and she knew how deeply ingrained his mother's psychological conditioning was. Staring at flat, sterile drawings on a computer screen wasn't going to break eighteen years of trauma.

Aubrie slowly looked up, catching Madison's eye across the cramped office.

Madison, who was looking at the exact same printout, raised an eyebrow. An unspoken, radical, and intensely practical idea passed between the two girls. They had built an ecosystem of absolute trust in Room 214. They had already broken down so many physical barriers just by sharing a bathroom and a sleeping space.

Aubrie looked back down at Jordan, squeezing his shoulder.

"Don't worry, J," Aubrie said softly, a warm, protective promise in her voice. "We'll go over the printouts with you. We'll figure out a way to help you study tonight when we get back to the dorm. I promise."

Dr. Evans smiled, completely unaware of the unconventional lengths the girls of Suite 214 were willing to go to for their friend. "You've got a great support system here, Jordan. I'll see you all on Thursday."

## 12:15 PM — The Mega-Group Lunch

By the time the massive ten-person entourage made it to Kincaid Commons, the lunchtime rush was in full swing. It took some serious maneuvering, but Mark and Sarah managed to push three rectangular tables together near the back windows, creating enough space for everyone, including a spot at the head of the table for Jordan’s wheelchair.

The energy was loud, chaotic, and incredibly warm. They traded stories about their bizarre first-day professors, the dreadful dining hall pizza, and the absolute absurdity of their shared health class.

Sitting across from each other, Lauren and Sarah naturally gravitated toward a side conversation. Despite growing up worlds apart—Lauren from Canada and Sarah from the bustling coast of Sydney—they hit it off instantly. Sarah was drawn to Lauren's gentle, steady kindness, while Lauren found Sarah's bold, unapologetic confidence incredibly refreshing.

"I still can't believe you managed to get a job at the campus coffee shop as a freshman," Lauren said, taking a sip of her water. "I tried to apply, and they practically laughed me out of the student union."

"You just have to be incredibly persistent," Sarah grinned, stealing a fry from Jessica's plate. "Or just have an accent. I swear the manager only hired me because he thinks Americans like ordering lattes from Australians."

As they laughed, Lauren’s mPhone, resting face-up on the table, buzzed loudly against the laminate wood.

The screen lit up with a new text message. The contact name simply read: **My MVP 🥍**

Lauren’s smile instantly faltered. Her stomach gave that same, uneasy flutter she had felt on the top bunk the night before. She didn't open the message; she just quickly reached out and flipped the phone face-down.

Directly across the table, Sarah’s phone also vibrated. Sarah glanced down at her screen. The contact name read: **Lax Toss 🙄**

Sarah let out a quiet, frustrated sigh, rolling her eyes as she hit the lock button to silence it and shoved the phone into her backpack. She looked up and caught Lauren's eye.

"Boy drama?" Sarah asked sympathetically, nodding toward Lauren’s face-down phone.

"Something like that," Lauren deflected with a tight, forced smile. "Just... a lot of texts today."

"I hear you. Mine's being incredibly aggro this week," Sarah commiserated, waving a hand dismissively. "It's exhausting. But anyway, what's your major? You seem like an English girl to me."

Lauren visibly relaxed, grateful for the pivot. "Nailed it. English literature."

They smoothly transitioned back into the safety of academic small talk, neither of them mentioning a name, entirely unaware that the same aggressively insecure lacrosse player was currently sitting in his room on the other side of campus, glaring at his phone and wondering why neither of his girlfriends was answering him.

By a quarter to one, the dynamic of the table began to shift as the reality of afternoon classes set in.

"Alright, team," Mark announced, standing up and grabbing his tray. "I have a two-hour chemistry lab on the other side of the quad, so I need to run."

"Same," Hannah said, standing up to join him. "And Zoë and I have a seminar at one."

Sarah checked her watch and groaned. "Jessica and Chloe and I have a mandatory freshman orientation module in ten minutes. We have to bolt." Sarah slung her backpack over her shoulder, turning to Lauren. "It was so great meeting you. We all need to get together for dinner tonight. Same tables?"

"Absolutely," Lauren smiled. "See you tonight."

Within a minute, the chaotic energy of the mega-group dispersed, leaving only Jordan, Aubrie, Madison, and Lauren sitting at the long table.

With the loud buffer of the group gone, the silence felt a little heavier. Lauren looked down at her dark phone. She knew there were at least five unread messages waiting for her, and she felt physically sick at the thought of reading them.

Aubrie leaned forward, her elbows on the table, studying Lauren’s face. The protective instincts she usually reserved for Jordan immediately extended across the table.

"Lauren," Aubrie said softly, cutting right to the chase. "Are you okay? You've been looking at your phone like it's a bomb all through lunch."

Lauren let out a shaky breath, tracing the edge of her phone case. The absolute trust that defined Suite 214/216 made it impossible to lie to them.

"It's my boyfriend," Lauren admitted quietly, her voice barely carrying over the dining hall chatter. "We were on the phone last night while you guys were showing Jordan the Dark Aero desktop. I... I told him about Jordan."

Jordan, who had his silver mPad resting on his lap, looked up, his eyes wide with quiet concern.

"He didn't take it well," Lauren continued, her voice thick with unshed frustration. "He didn't just think it was weird. He immediately jumped to the worst, most disgusting conclusion possible. He accused Jordan of playing a pity card to try and sleep with all of us. He called our suite a 'messed-up thing'."

Madison's jaw dropped. "Are you kidding me?"

"I wish I was," Lauren whispered, blinking back a sudden wave of tears. "And now he's blowing up my phone because I haven't answered him since nine-thirty this morning. He's demanding to know where I am and if I'm with 'the wheelchair guy'. He's just so... angry. And controlling. And I'm just sitting here realizing that I'm not happy. I haven't been happy for a while."

She looked up at the three of them, terrified that she was bringing too much drama into their peaceful dynamic.

Instead of judgment, Jordan reached across the table. He didn't type anything. He just placed his hand gently over hers, a quiet, physical anchor of solidarity.

"Lauren," Madison said fiercely, reaching out to squeeze her other arm. "He does not get to talk to you like that, and he certainly doesn't get to talk about our family like that. Mark told you last night, and we are telling you right now: we have your back."

"You don't have to put up with him just because you're used to him," Aubrie added, her voice steady and fiercely protective. "Whatever you decide to do, we are right here."

Lauren looked at the three of them. She had known them for barely four days, but the safety she felt in this exact moment was more profound than anything she had felt in months of dating Tom.

"Thank you," Lauren breathed, a genuine, tearful smile breaking through the anxiety.

Aubrie stood up and walked around the table, pulling Lauren out of her chair. Madison immediately joined them, wrapping her arms around Lauren's shoulders. Jordan locked the wheels of his chair and leaned forward, wrapping his arms securely around Lauren's waist to complete the circle.

It was a tight, solid, profoundly grounding group hug. For Jordan, physical touch was no longer something to flinch away from; it was how his family communicated safety.

"Okay," Lauren whispered, wiping her eyes as they finally pulled back. "I have a two o'clock literature lecture. I'll be okay. I promise."

"We'll see you back at the suite before dinner," Aubrie smiled, unlocking Jordan's brakes. "You're safe with us, Lauren. Always."
