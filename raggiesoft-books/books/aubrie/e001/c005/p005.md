## September 8, 2014 • 6:30 PM — The Suite Arrangement Consent Form

Monday, September 8 marked the end of the two-week post-census trial window that **Dr. Alan Mercer** had established on Move-In Day.

For the six residents of Suite 214/216, those sixteen days had flown by in a whirlwind of firsts: their first full week of college lectures, their first bus trip together down to Christiansburg to pick up Jordan's prescription eyeglasses and digital behind-the-ear hearing aids, and their first Monday night watching ***AWE Monday Night WAR*** together in the first-floor lobby lounge. When **Isabel Dawson** and **Henry Walsh** had checked in privately with each of the six suitemates over the past week to remind them of Dr. Mercer's confidential "no-fault opt-out" guarantee, all six of them had given the exact same answer: nobody wanted to leave.

At 6:30 that Monday evening, just after the suite had returned from dinner at Kincaid Commons and settled into Room 214 to go over their Tuesday reading assignments, a familiar, cheerful knock sounded on the hallway door.

Hannah stepped over and pressed the square stainless-steel **PUSH TO OPEN** handicap button on the interior wall to unlock and swing the heavy oak door open. Standing on the threshold was their Hall Director, **Kelsey Brennan**, holding a brushed-aluminum clipboard and a blue ballpoint pen, with **Isabel Dawson** (Room 215) and **Henry Walsh** (Room 201) smiling warmly right behind her in the hallway before the ten-second dormitory hold-open timer counted down.

"Hi, everyone—happy Monday," Kelsey said, stepping inside while Isabel and Henry lingered just inside the open doorway with matching grins. "Sorry to interrupt your evening study hour, but since five o'clock today officially marked the university's **Post-Census Room-Change Freeze**—and the end of your fourteen-day provisional agreement from Move-In Day—I wanted to bring you the news from my afternoon meeting with **Dr. Alan Mercer** in the central **Office of Housing and Residence Life**."

Around the small cinderblock room, five pairs of eyes—and Jordan's behind the crystal-clear polycarbonate lenses of his new glasses—locked onto Kelsey at once.

Sitting in his wheelchair beside his desk with his Pinnacle Vanguard Pro open on his lap, Jordan realized with a quiet start just how different this moment felt compared to sixteen days ago.

On Move-In Saturday (August 23), when Kelsey and Dr. Mercer had walked through that exact doorway with a clipboard, anything past arm's length had been a blurry smear of light and shadow, spoken voices across the room had sounded like a muffled underwater hum unless he cupped his hand behind his ear, and the mere thought of an adult authority figure asking for his opinion had made his hands shake with eighteen years of conditioned terror.

Tonight, through his new glasses, he could read the tiny gold lettering on Kelsey's CPI Residence Life lapel pin from eight feet away. Through his new digital behind-the-ear hearing aids, every syllable of Kelsey's warm voice came through in crisp, effortless high definition. And with **Aubrie** sitting right on the edge of the lower bunk beside his wheel, **Madison** perched on the desk chair, and **Zoë**, **Lauren**, and **Hannah** standing shoulder-to-shoulder by the connecting doorway, Jordan didn't shrink back an inch.

"First of all," Kelsey said, her eyes crinkling with warmth as she looked around the circle, "in the spirit of total transparency: when David Thorne ran the final post-census numbers at noon today after two weeks of campus-wide add/drop and no-shows, thirty-six beds finally opened up on campus—which Central Housing is using first to rescue the poor freshmen who have been trapped out in the **Radford** overflow hotels dealing with connecting buses to the McConnell Transit Center, the once-hourly **Radford Connector**, and the packed-to-the-brim CPI and chartered shuttle buses every morning, while **The Hotel at Poly** and the **Christiansburg** hotels are still full. Technically, five of those post-census vacancies were scattered female beds across West Campus in Bradshaw Hall and Thornton Hall."

Madison's jaw set instantly, and Lauren took half a step closer to Jordan's chair as if ready to barricade the door with her own body. "Kelsey, we already told Izzy and Henry—"

"Hold on, Madison—let me finish," Kelsey laughed, holding up a reassuring hand while Isabel grinned from the doorway. "Because when Dr. Mercer sat down with me and Ms. Higgins from Educational Accessibility at two o'clock today, he read the fourteen-day evaluation reports that **Izzy** and **Henry** wrote about your suite."

Jordan glanced over at Isabel and Henry by the door. Isabel gave him a warm wink, and Henry offered a proud thumbs-up.

"Izzy and Henry told Dr. Mercer the truth about what the six of you have built in here over the last two weeks," Kelsey continued, her voice softening with genuine pride. "They reported that while standard single-gender suites on this floor have already needed roommate mediations over thermostats and noise, Suite 214/216 has operated with zero complaints, total mutual respect, and clockwork coordination. They reported how the five of you—along with your friends on Floors 3 and 5—have stood by Jordan as his peer note-takers, campus navigators, and family. And Dr. Mercer said that on Move-In Day, none of us had a crystal ball—he wanted the six of you to prove in the real world that this arrangement could work. Today, he said that breaking up the strongest, most supportive suite in Bennett Hall just to fill five scattered beds across campus would be a disservice to all six of you."

A collective cheer erupted across Room 214. Madison pumped both fists in the air, Lauren threw her arms around Hannah and Zoë's shoulders, and Aubrie reached over to squeeze Jordan's hand with a radiant grin that made Jordan's chest swell with warmth.

Kelsey held up the brushed-aluminum clipboard, which held a single crisp sheet of heavyweight cream bond paper stamped at the top with the embossed seal of **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute**.

"Because your suite's co-ed configuration began as an emergency exception, the fourteen-day temporary form expires tonight," Kelsey explained, handing the clipboard and blue ballpoint pen to Hannah. "To lock your suite in permanently through May 2015, Dr. Mercer has already pre-signed the administrative approval block at the bottom of this page. All he needs for the university's permanent audit vault is one final, written confirmation signed by all six residents."

Across the top of the heavyweight bond paper, in bold serif type that Jordan could read effortlessly from his chair without squinting, was the heading:

**OFFICE OF HOUSING AND RESIDENCE LIFE — 2014–2015 SUITE ARRANGEMENT CONSENT FORM (FORM HRL-2014-COED-PERM)**

Beneath the header, a concise paragraph stated in clear institutional language that having completed the fourteen-day post-census evaluation period with distinction, all six eighteen-year-old adult residents of **Suite 214/216 in Eleanor Bennett Commonwealth Hall** were fully aware of the co-ed nature of their living assignment, that each resident consented to the arrangement of their own free will without coercion, and that all six requested that the assignment be made **permanent for the entirety of the 2014–2015 academic year**.

"This is for your protection just as much as the university's," Kelsey added gently. "Once all six of your signatures are on this bond paper, nobody—inside or outside the administration—can ever claim your living arrangement was forced or temporary."

To Jordan, looking at the heavy cream bond paper as it passed from hand to hand around the room, it felt less like a housing form and more like a constitutional treaty. It was the permanent legal charter of their chosen family.

One by one, the five women read the paragraph and signed their full names in blue ink beside their printed student ID numbers and bed codes:

- **Madison Elizabeth Scott** (Room 214, Bed C)
- **Zoë Wren Brooks** (Room 216, Bed A)
- **Hannah Ashlee Parker** (Room 216, Bed C)
- **Lauren Nicole Barnes** (Room 216, Bed B)
- **Aubrie Jean Kelly** (Room 214, Bed B)

Aubrie handed the clipboard and blue pen to Jordan last.

Unlike Move-In Day, when his fingers had trembled with fear that having a voice would get him punished, Jordan's hand was completely steady. Resting the brushed-aluminum clipboard against the flat matte-black lid of his Pinnacle Vanguard Pro, Jordan looked clearly through his glasses at the top signature line (Room 214, Bed A) and signed his name in bold, clean blue ink:

**Jordan Fuller (Aubrey Jordan Fuller)**

Then he flipped open his laptop lid, typed a single line into EchoType, and hit play so his chosen "Sydney" voice spoke for the whole room and for his two RAs standing by the door:

<aac>Thank you, Kelsey, Izzy, and Henry. And thank you to my suitemates for keeping me.</aac>

"Always, J," Aubrie murmured softly, resting her hand over his shoulder. "You're stuck with us for the whole year now."

"Thank you all," Kelsey said warmly, taking the clipboard back and inspecting the six signatures with a satisfied nod. "This goes straight into Dr. Mercer's locked vault across the firewall first thing tomorrow morning. Have a wonderful week, Suite 214/216."

## September 19, 2014 • 3:30 PM — Transforming Jordan into a Forger

By the third Friday of September, the first crisp, cool breeze of Appalachian autumn had arrived in Blacksburg, and the energy across campus was electric. Homecoming Week was just days away, and the walkways around **Commonwealth Green** were already a vibrant sea of Foundry Maroon and Molten Orange.

After their Friday afternoon classes wrapped up, the entire nine-person crew—Jordan, Aubrie, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Mark, Sarah, and Jessica—walked over to **The Foundry**, the **CPI Campus Bookstore** at the edge of the academic district, to pick up supplies.

The moment Jordan rolled through the automatic glass doors of the rotunda-shaped building, he was hit by a wave of pure, unfiltered collegiate spirit. Through the crisp lenses of his new glasses, the store looked like a cathedral dedicated to an identity he desperately wanted to belong to. Towering racks overflowed with heavy-cotton **CPI Forgers** hoodies, burnt-orange jerseys, pennants, and embroidered baseball caps.

"Okay, I officially need this for the Homecoming game," Madison announced, holding up a fitted maroon t-shirt with metallic gold and orange *FORGERS* lettering across the chest.

"Solid choice," Mark grinned, trying on a burnt-orange CPI cap with the classic anvil-and-hammer crest. "Gotta represent in The Anvil."

Jordan wheeled himself quietly down the center aisle, reaching out to brush his fingers against the thick, fleece-lined sleeve of a heavy maroon CPI Forgers hoodie. He had never owned a single piece of school-spirit clothing in his life. Back in Virginia Beach, his mother had only ever bought him the cheapest, plainest clothes from discount racks, insisting that anything with a logo was a "frivolous waste of money" on someone who didn't participate in school activities. He imagined pulling this maroon hoodie over his shoulders and rolling into **The Crucible** next Saturday alongside sixty-five thousand screaming fans—not as an outsider looking in, but as a Forger.

Then he flipped over the cardboard price tag hanging from the cuff.

Fifty-four dollars.

Jordan's stomach dropped. His mother had left him with barely enough cash in his checking account to cover his lab fees and required textbooks, and she monitored every bank transaction online like a hawk. He let the soft maroon sleeve slip from his fingers, locked his jaw, and quietly began to turn his wheelchair away from the rack.

Aubrie, who had been watching him from the end of the aisle, stepped gently in front of his chair.

"You're not getting it, J?" she asked softly.

Jordan's cheeks flushed warm. He looked down at his Vanguard Pro resting on his lap and typed a quiet line into NeoEdit, angling the screen just toward her so he wouldn't embarrass himself in front of the whole store.

<aac>I can't afford it. My mother only left enough in my account for books, and she checks the statements.</aac>

Over by the t-shirt wall, the playful chatter among Madison, Mark, Hannah, Lauren, Zoë, Sarah, and Jessica stopped instantly. Without a word, all seven of them walked over, closing ranks around Jordan's chair as their expressions shifted from carefree to fiercely protective.

"Oh, no, mate, that is not how this works," Sarah said firmly, crossing her arms.

"Dude, Homecoming Week starts Monday," Mark added, stepping up beside Aubrie. "You can't roll into The Crucible without Forger gear. It's practically in the CPI student constitution."

Jordan shook his head, his fingers typing rapidly on his terminal before he triggered EchoType.

<aac>I can't pay you guys back.</aac>

"This isn't a loan, Jordan," Hannah said, her warm, steady "big sister" voice carrying absolute authority. "This is your initiation. You're part of our family now, which means you're part of this school, too. All eight of us are pitching in—and if your mother checks your bank statement, she won't see a single cent missing."

Before Jordan could type another protest, his chosen family sprang into coordinated action.

"We're getting the heavyweight maroon hoodie," Aubrie declared, pulling a size medium off the rack and laying it across his lap.

"And this heather-grey **CPI FORGERS** crew-neck t-shirt for everyday wear, plus the *'Beat VDU!'* shirt for Saturday," Madison insisted, snatching both from the display table and placing them on top of the hoodie.

"And the cap to top it off," Mark finished with a grin, setting the burnt-orange CPI anvil-crest baseball cap right on top of the pile.

Jordan looked down at the stack of maroon, heather-grey, and orange fabric resting on his knees, then up through his glasses at the eight smiling, expectant faces surrounding him—from six-foot-three Jessica beaming down at him to Lauren and Zoë nodding in quiet solidarity. They weren't pitying him. They were claiming him.

A sharp, overwhelming prickle of emotion stung behind his eyes—not a meltdown of fear, but a surge of pure, unadulterated gratitude. His fingers tapped two simple words into his terminal:

<aac>Thank you.</aac>

Ten minutes later, after the group split the tab at the register, Aubrie and Madison helped Jordan slip the new maroon CPI Forgers hoodie over his head right outside **The Foundry**. It was thick, fleece-warm, and smelled like new cotton.

When the nine of them rolled back into **Suite 214/216** in **Bennett Hall** twenty minutes later, Jordan wheeled into the connecting jack-and-jill accessible bathroom to wash his hands and paused in front of the tilted mirror mounted above the roll-under sink.

Through the crisp polycarbonate lenses of his new glasses, he stared at his reflection. With his new glasses, his subtle behind-the-ear hearing aids, and the heavyweight maroon **CPI Forgers** hoodie draped over his shoulders, he almost looked like a real college freshman—except for the patchy, uneven eighteen-year-old facial fuzz along his jaw and upper lip.

In eighteen years in Virginia Beach, Jordan had **never once been clean-shaven**. His mother had flatly refused to let him near a safety razor, insisting that his Cerebral Palsy fine-motor spasticity would cause him to slice his own face open, and she had never bothered to teach him how to groom himself. Whenever she had wanted him to look "presentable" for company, she had simply cornered him in the bathroom and scraped a cheap, dry electric hair trimmer roughly across his chin without guards or lotion, leaving his skin raw and stinging while complaining about having to groom a grown teenager.

Unconsciously, Jordan lifted his right hand and touched the patchy stubble on his chin, his shoulders sagging half an inch.

Standing in the open doorway of Room 214, **Mark** caught the look in the mirror immediately.

"Hey, J," Mark said casually, leaning against the doorframe with a warm, easy grin. "Since we're officially transforming you into a full-fledged CPI Forger before Homecoming Week... you want a hand getting a real clean shave?"

Jordan blinked, looking up at Mark in the mirror. Turning to his Vanguard Pro on his lap tray, he typed honestly:

<aac>I've never actually had a clean shave before. My mother never let me near a razor because of my hands.</aac>

Mark's expression softened with quiet, brotherly understanding, though he kept his voice upbeat and completely matter-of-fact so Jordan wouldn't feel self-conscious for a second.

"Well, we're fixing that right now, brother," Mark said, grabbing his dopp kit from his backpack in Room 214 and stepping into the bathroom. "Every guy deserves to know what a proper hot-towel shave feels like. And tomorrow when we're out, we'll pick you up an ergonomic rotary foil shaver with a wide rubber grip so you can do touch-ups yourself whenever you want without ever worrying about a blade."

Mark ran the tap until the water was steaming hot, soaked a clean white hand towel, wrung it out, and gently draped the warm cloth across the lower half of Jordan's face for thirty seconds to soften the bristles. Jordan closed his eyes behind his glasses, letting out a long, slow breath at the soothing warmth.

Then Mark lathered up a palmful of rich, cedar-scented shaving cream, spread it evenly across Jordan's jaw, cheeks, and upper lip, and held up a fresh, multi-blade safety razor.

"Alright, man—just tilt your chin up about ten degrees and relax your jaw," Mark coached calmly, resting his left hand steadily on Jordan's shoulder as a grounding anchor so Jordan knew every movement before it happened. "Short, smooth strokes with the grain. I've got the angle completely locked."

With unhurried, practiced precision, Mark drew the razor cleanly down Jordan's cheek, rinsing the blade under the warm faucet between each gentle pass. Nobody rushed him. Nobody scraped his skin dry or sighed about how long it took. Standing by the open doorway of Room 214, **Aubrie Jean** and **Hannah** watched with quiet, misty-eyed smiles as Mark chatted easily with Jordan about Saturday's football matchup, treating the ritual like the most natural brotherly hangout in the world.

Two minutes later, Mark wiped away the last trace of foam with a warm, damp washcloth and patted a splash of soothing, alcohol-free balm onto Jordan's skin.

"There you go," Mark grinned, stepping back and clapping Jordan warmly on the shoulder. "Take a look, Mr. Fuller."

Jordan looked into the lowered mirror above the sink.

Staring back at him was a clean-shaven nineteen-year-old young man in dark-framed rectangular glasses and a maroon **CPI Forgers** hoodie, his jawline smooth and clear for the very first time in his life. As he ran his fingertips lightly across his smooth chin, a huge, luminous smile broke across his face. He didn't look like the neglected boy locked in a back room in Virginia Beach anymore. Inside and out, he looked like a CPI Forger.

## September 19, 2014 • 7:30 PM — One Hundred and Five Percent

Friday evening in Suite 214/216 had begun as a quiet, triumphant celebration.

It was the tail end of CPI's beginning-of-semester **Health and Safety Week**—the mandatory campus-wide compliance week held near the start and end of every semester. All week long, Environmental Health & Safety (EHS) had been running its annual once-a-year daytime fire drills across every academic building on campus. By sheer luck of the schedule, every single academic-building drill that week had gone off while Jordan was somewhere else: Walton Hall had been evacuated on Tuesday morning twenty minutes before his afternoon Astrophysics lecture; Colonial Hall's alarm had sounded on Wednesday while he was across campus eating lunch at Kincaid Hall; and Commonwealth Hall had run its drill on Thursday afternoon right after his Calculus section let out.

In Bennett Hall, the only sign of Health and Safety Week that Suite 214/216 had noticed was a bright yellow Residence Life checklist pinned to the corkboard outside **Isabel Dawson**'s door in Room 215 reminding residents not to daisy-chain extension cords, overload surge protectors, or hang laundry from the sprinkler pipes. Yesterday afternoon, **Zoë** had dutifully inspected every power strip in both rooms, declared Suite 214/216 one hundred percent compliant with the Virginia Statewide Fire Prevention Code, and checked the item off her mental list.

None of them gave Health and Safety Week a second thought tonight—because tonight, Suite 214/216 had something far bigger to celebrate.

At seven-thirty that evening, **Dr. Alistair** had posted the grades on Canvas for their first college **ASTR 101: Introduction to Astronomy & Astrophysics** exam.

Sitting in his wheelchair at his desk in his brand-new maroon **CPI Forgers** hoodie, Jordan had clicked open the gradebook tab on his Pinnacle Vanguard Pro while **Aubrie Jean** leaned over his shoulder—and for three full seconds, Jordan had simply stared at the screen through his new glasses in stunned disbelief.

He hadn't just passed his first college exam.

He had scored a perfect **100%**, plus all five bonus points on Dr. Alistair's brutal orbital-mechanics extra-credit derivation, for a total score of **105%**.

"A hundred and five percent!!" Aubrie Jean had shrieked with pure joy, throwing both arms around Jordan's shoulders and kissing his cheek while **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Hannah**, and **Lauren** crowded around his desk to read the glowing numbers for themselves.

"Look at Dr. Alistair's comment in the rubric margin!" Zoë had pointed out, her analytical gray eyes shining with pride. *"'Flawless Keplerian transfer derivation—cleanest vector notation in the lecture hall.'"*

For a young man who had spent eighteen years being told by his mother that his mind was broken and that going to college was a pointless fantasy, seeing **105 / 100** stamped across a university astrophysics gradebook felt like watching the sun rise for the first time.

By a quarter to eight, the initial burst of cheering had settled into a warm, peaceful Friday night glow. Sitting at his desk in his fleece-lined Forgers hoodie, Jordan was happily reviewing his next chapter of astrophysics notes while basking in the quiet pride of his suitemates. Across Room 214, Aubrie Jean was curled up on the lower bunk in her reading glasses, reading under a soft fleece blanket, Madison was lounging on the top bunk scrolling through her mPhone, and Zoë, Hannah, and Lauren were chatting casually through the open connecting bathroom doorway in Room 216 as the digital clock on Jordan's desk ticked toward 8:00 PM.
