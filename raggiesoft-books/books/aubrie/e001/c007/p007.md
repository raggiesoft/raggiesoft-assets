## October 21, 2014 • 2:30 PM — The Local From Radford Takes Command

Tuesday afternoon was the final day of **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute**’s four-day **Fall Break** before classes resumed on Wednesday morning—and for **Jordan**, it was Day Four of his new life after telling his mother *"I'm staying here"* outside Bennett Hall.

At `14:30`, **Mark Anderson** walked into Room 214 wearing his maroon CPI hoodie with a rolled-up towel slung over his shoulder and a look of mock local indignation on his face.

Having grown up right down the river in **Radford** and spent every summer of his childhood attending youth engineering and sports camps on the CPI campus, Mark knew every back road, bus route, and trail in Montgomery County by heart.

"Okay, hold everything," Mark announced, looking around the suite at **Jordan**, **Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Isabel**. "You guys have been living in the New River Valley for two full months, the leaves are at absolute peak October color, and none of you have ever been on the **Huckleberry Trail** OR inside the **Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center**?"

"I know the trailhead starts over by the **Pendleton Library** lawn," Hannah said, looking up from her sketchbook, "but how far does it go?"

"Six miles of paved, car-free rail-trail all the way from the library lawn to **Christiansburg**," Mark grinned. "Built right over the bed of the old nineteenth-century **Huckleberry Train** that used to chug between **Cambria Depot** and campus until 1958. And right at the Christiansburg end off **Mill Lane** and **North Franklin Street** is the **Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center**—where the **CPI Forgers** swim team races, AND where they have a ninety-five-point-five-degree warm water therapy pool called **The Cove**."

At the words *ninety-five-point-five-degree warm water therapy pool*, Jordan’s eyes lit up behind his rectangular polycarbonate glasses. Ever since the October mountain chill had settled over Blacksburg, the cerebral palsy spasticity in his calves and hamstrings had been noticeably tighter in the mornings.

Tap-tap-tap went Jordan’s fingers on the keyboard of his `Vanguard Pro`:

**"Do they let Blacksburg students use the therapy pool? How much is a membership?"**

"Because it’s owned and operated by the **Town of Christiansburg**, Town of Christiansburg residents get resident rates, and those of us living in **Blacksburg** or **Radford** pay the **Non-Resident Monthly Membership**," Mark explained. "Thirty bucks a month for unlimited thirty-day access—or ten bucks for a day pass—and it has zero-depth ramps and private accessible family changing rooms. Grab your swimsuits and your hoodies. We’re taking **Blue Ridge Transit Route 1** and **Route 2** down to the Aquatic Center first while our muscles are warm, and then we’re walking and rolling the **Huckleberry Trail** back to campus at sunset!"

## 3:15 PM — *The Cove* (`95.5°F`) at the Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center

Forty minutes later, after flashing their **Poly Passes** on **Blue Ridge Transit** down to Christiansburg and signing up for their **`$30.00/month` Town of Christiansburg Non-Resident** barcode keytags at the front desk (`$30.00` loaded neatly into `Fin#` under `Recreation & Physical Therapy`), the ten of them stepped into the sixty-four-thousand-square-foot natatorium of the **Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center**.

Jordan had never seen an indoor pool complex like it in his life. To their left stretched the massive **50-Meter Olympic Competition Pool** (*The Forger Natatorium*) beneath a ten-meter concrete diving tower and a thousand-seat spectator gallery; straight ahead was the **Leisure Pool** with its zero-depth beach entry and two-story triple-loop yellow water slide; and tucked into a warm, sunlit alcove to the right was **The Cove**.

After changing inside one of the private, wheelchair-accessible **Family Changing Suites** (equipped with a roll-in shower, grab bars, and a fold-down padded transfer bench) with **Aubrie Jean**’s help, Jordan transferred into one of the facility’s waterproof PVC deck wheelchairs and rolled straight down the gentle, non-slip zero-depth ramp into **The Cove**.

The instant the **`95.5°F`** water enveloped his legs and hips, Jordan let out a long, shaky breath of pure relief.

On land, cerebral palsy meant gravity was a constant negotiation—a low-level static of involuntary muscle tone in his legs and lower back. In `95.5°F` water, gravity simply ceased to exist. As Aubrie Jean stepped into the warm water beside him and wrapped her arms around his waist to float with him near the hydrotherapy jets—joined by Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Mark, Sarah, Jessica, and Isabel—Jordan’s tight hamstrings and calves unknotted completely, floating weightless and warm while Zoë logged the thermal metrics with a contented sigh:

## 5:30 PM — The Huckleberry Trail & The Unwritten Rule of Blacksburg

By `17:00`, thoroughly warmed from the `95.5°F` pool, showered, and dressed in their warm fleece layers and maroon-and-orange **CPI** hoodies, the group decided to walk and roll the northern half of the **Huckleberry Trail** together from the **Pendleton Library** lawn out past the agricultural pastures and back at dusk.

As they rolled south from the **Pendleton Library** trailhead along the ten-foot-wide paved asphalt path, Jordan fell in love with the trail immediately.

Unlike a city street, there were no cars, no curbs, no diesel exhaust, and no steep cross-slopes—just smooth, level black asphalt winding along the gentle grade where the old **Huckleberry Train** had rolled for half a century, bordered by weathered wooden split-rail fences, grazing cattle in rolling green pastures, and a blazing autumn canopy of crimson sugar maples and burnt-orange oaks overhead.

At **`5:30 PM`**, with the valley dressed in its autumn dress as the air turned crisp (`48°F`) and woodsmoke curled in a silver wisp from a farmhouse chimney across the pasture, Mark glanced fifty yards down the paved path toward Christiansburg—and stopped talking mid-sentence.

Coming northbound up the Huckleberry Trail straight toward them were five figures moving together at an easy, unhurried pace: a woman in her mid-forties with glasses and a maroon CPI jacket pushing a man in a matte-black titanium manual wheelchair, while another woman and two tall identical twin men walked side by side right alongside them through the golden leaves.

Madison’s breath caught in her throat. She whispered so softly only their group could hear:

*"Wait... is that—"*

"Shh—yep," Mark whispered back with a proud local’s grin, keeping his voice low and his stride completely natural. "That’s **Ryan, Cassidy, and Holly O'Connell**, and their twin cousins **Evan and Tyler Wright**. **The Stardust Engine**. They live right here in Blacksburg on **Progress Street**, and they swim down at the Aquatic Center all the time. Remember the rule of the Huckleberry Trail: out here, they aren’t rock stars. They’re our neighbors. Don't stare, don't pull out a phone, and don't ask for an autograph—just give 'em a neighbor's wave."

Behind his rectangular glasses, **Jordan** blinked in quiet curiosity. Having grown up in near-total isolation in Virginia Beach where his mother banned secular and popular music, Jordan had **no clue** who **The Stardust Engine** was; he had never heard their songs, and the band name meant nothing to him.

Instead, what caught Jordan’s eyes—and warmed his chest—was something much simpler: the sight of another man in a manual wheelchair out here on the six-mile mountain path, rolling through the autumn leaves with his sister pushing his chair and his family walking alongside him while their neighbors treated them with effortless, unbothered respect.

As the distance between them closed between the split-rail pasture fences, Ryan O'Connell looked directly across the asphalt at Jordan’s manual wheelchair, noticed Jordan’s maroon CPI hoodie and his gloved hands on his handrims, and broke into a warm, genuine smile.

Jordan smiled back and raised his right hand from his handrim in a quiet, respectful neighbor’s wave.

Ryan tapped two fingers to his temple in a fellow wheelchair user’s salute. "Evening, guys! Beautiful night for a roll!"

"Evening!" Mark nodded warmly with a smile, while Aubrie Jean, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Sarah, Jessica, and Isabel waved back as Cassidy, Holly, Evan, and Tyler smiled and offered a friendly hello, the two groups passing each other smoothly along the leaf-strewn path.

A minute later, as Jordan and his friends turned around to head back north toward **Pendleton Library** at the end of their loop, the sun dipped low behind **Price Mountain’s crest**—and up ahead against the darkening purple sky, all four massive **Ironstone** floodlight towers of **The Crucible** flickered on in the night, painting the valley in electric light.

With his muscles still loose and warm from the `95.5°F` therapy pool and the crisp mountain breeze cooling his face, Jordan reached up from his left handrim and took Aubrie Jean’s hand as they rolled back toward campus together under the autumn stars.
