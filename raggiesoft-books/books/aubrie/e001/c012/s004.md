Back inside the quiet warmth of Suite 214/216 after the all-clear was given, the lingering adrenaline from the fire alarm was only slowly beginning to ebb away. Jordan was still visibly shaken up from the sheer terror of the evacuation; even wrapped in a soft fleece blanket in his wheelchair, dried tear tracks still marked his cheeks beneath his glasses, a few fresh tears still welled at the corners of his eyes, and his chest hitched every few seconds with the quiet, involuntary sniffles of hard crying that was trying to subside. All six suitemates were huddled together in Room 214, cradling steaming mugs of hot chocolate with mini-marshmallows that Madison had insisted on warming up to help him and Aubrie settle.

Jordan had turned his hearing aids back down to their comfortable indoor profile now that the hallways were silent, his Vanguard Pro open on his lap as he took a shaky sip from his mug and let out another soft sniffle.

"I still cannot believe they subject legal adults to unannounced 100-decibel acoustic assaults at eight o'clock on a Friday night," Lauren said, shaking her head as she blew across the top of her mug. "It's genuinely barbaric."

"What matters is that our emergency response protocol functioned with zero casualties," Zoë observed from the foot of the bunk, her gray eyes resting warmly on Jordan and Aubrie. "Your sensory and cognitive freeze responses were completely understandable, but your immediate willingness to trust our manual override was the decisive variable."

"And it's a good thing we all have each other," Madison added softly, glancing around the circle of blankets. "Especially so nobody has to be alone... you know. After a crash like that."

A quiet, meaningful stillness settled over the room. Everyone knew what Madison was referring to—the nights Aubrie had held Jordan after his move-in meltdown and his 2:00 AM nightmare, and the night Jordan had held Lauren after Tom's betrayal had shattered her world.

As always, it was Aubrie who gave gentle, fearless voice to the thought forming in everyone's mind.

"It shouldn't only take an emergency or a heartbreak for us to have that," Aubrie said softly, looking first at Jordan with a tender smile before turning her gaze to Madison, Zoë, Lauren, and Hannah. "That feeling of waking up safe... of knowing you aren't alone in the dark... all of us deserve that. And after eighteen years of nightmares, Jordan deserves that every single night."

Lauren nodded wholeheartedly, hugging her knees to her chest. "That Tuesday night after everything with Tom came out... sleeping next to Jordan was the only reason I didn't fall apart in the dark. Just feeling someone kind breathing beside me fixed something in my head."

"Then we should formalize the protocol," Zoë said, her pragmatic, systematic mind immediately seeking structure so no one would ever feel uncertain or hesitant. "Establishing a clear, explicit nightly arrangement ensures equity and eliminates any potential for social ambiguity."

"It doesn't have to be a laminated spreadsheet on the bathroom door, Zo," Madison laughed gently, her cheeks warming slightly. "But yeah. If we're all really doing this—no jealousy, no weirdness—then it should be an open invitation. Whenever one of us needs comfort, or just wants to be close to him at night, we rotate."

Every pair of eyes in the room turned toward Jordan. They were offering him their hearts, their trust, and their nights, but none of them would take a single step unless it was truly what *he* wanted.

Jordan set his hot cocoa mug on his desk and placed his hands on the keyboard of his Vanguard Pro. Looking around at the women who had rescued him from the storm of his old life, he typed carefully into EchoType and pressed execute:

<aac>I want that more than anything. Knowing one of you is right there beside me is the only reason the nightmares stopped. I trust all of you completely.</aac>

Hannah smiled warmly from her spot near the connecting door, taking a sip of her cocoa. "You guys already know I think what you have is the most beautiful, healing thing in the world, and my daytime hugs are on call twenty-four-seven," she said with gentle affection. "And you know that at night, Mark and I have our own bed and our own relationship in 216."

"Of course," Aubrie said immediately, reaching out to squeeze Hannah's hand. "You and Mark are our bedrock, Hannah. And for the rest of us—me, Madison, Zoë, and Lauren..." Aubrie's eyes brightened with an inclusive thought. "And for Sarah, too, whenever she stays down here with us and needs a safe harbor. The invitation is open to all five of us."

Jordan sat quietly in his wheelchair for a moment, letting out another small, lingering sniffle from the fire drill as his mind traced the constellation of people who made him feel safe in this building. Less than an hour ago, when he had been screaming and clinging to his desk in blind terror, **Isabel Dawson** had knelt down on the linoleum right in front of his wheelchair, shown him her clipboard, and rescued him from the panic. Outside on the lawn, he had hugged her and added her to his inner list of **Safe People**.

In Jordan's sheltered, earnest mind—having never navigated college social structures or staff hierarchies before—the equation felt completely simple: *Safe People who protect me are the people who can hold me at night.* And honestly, after how gentle and warm Isabel had been when he hugged her on Washington Street, he wanted Isabel on that list too.

His fingers returned to the keyboard of his Vanguard Pro. Completely sincere, he typed out a question and pressed execute:

<aac>What about Isabel? After how she helped me during the fire drill tonight, I added her to my list of Safe People. Can we add Isabel to the list of people who are allowed into my bed with me too? I would really like her to be part of it.</aac>

For a fraction of a second, the warm, easy atmosphere in Room 214 froze solid.

Aubrie, Madison, Lauren, Zoë, and Hannah all exchanged a quick, wide-eyed glance. There wasn't a single trace of jealousy in the room—none of them begrudged his affection for their RA for even a heartbeat. Instead, every single one of them felt a sudden spike of protective alarm for Jordan's fragile emotional state.

He was still visibly shaken up from the fire drill, his cheeks still streaked with dried tears, his nervous system raw and depleted after a massive panic response. And now they were going to have to tell him *no* about someone he had just trusted with his whole heart.

Silently, operating on pure instinct, the five women prepared themselves for Jordan to go into a second meltdown. Aubrie shifted half an inch closer to his wheelchair on the edge of Bed B; Lauren and Madison subtly set their hot chocolate mugs down on the floor so their hands would be free to hold and ground him if the rejection triggered a sensory crash; Hannah and Zoë softened their posture, bracing for the storm.

"Jordan, sweetheart," Aubrie began, her voice impossibly gentle and warm as she reached over to rest a steady hand on his forearm. "First of all, I love that you added Isabel to your list of Safe People. She completely earned that tonight, and she cares about you so much."

"She really does," Lauren agreed softly, leaning forward with warm, reassuring eyes. "What she did for you during the drill was incredible. You can trust her with anything, J."

"However," Zoë stepped in, keeping her voice deliberately calm, measured, and gentle so it wouldn't sound like a reprimand, "while Isabel is unequivocally one of your Safe People, she cannot be included in the Nightly Bed Pact."

Aubrie held her breath, her thumb rubbing a slow, soothing circle against Jordan's wrist while Madison and Lauren tensed slightly, ready to wrap their arms around him the second his breathing hitched.

"It's not because she doesn't care about you, Jordan, and it's not because you asked anything wrong," Aubrie explained softly, looking straight into his green eyes behind his glasses. "It's because of what a Resident Advisor is. Us five—and Mark, Hannah, and Sarah—we're all freshmen. We're your peers and your equals. But Isabel is a junior employed by the university. As our RA, she's in an official position of authority and power over everyone who lives on this floor."

"Precisely," Zoë added gently. "University housing regulations establish a strict institutional boundary between Residence Life staff and the students under their supervision. Because Isabel holds a position of power—enforcing rules, conducting safety sweeps, and filing official reports—sharing a bed with a resident on her floor would be considered highly inappropriate by the university. Even with the purest intentions, it would send the wrong message to the rest of the floor regarding favoritism and staff boundaries, and if anyone ever found out, the university would fire her immediately and take away her room across the hall."

"We want to keep Isabel safe too," Madison said tenderly, offering him a soft, sympathetic smile. "And keeping her safe as our RA means she stays our protector right across the hall in 215, while the bed pact stays just the five of us."

The five suitemates went quiet, every muscle poised for the emotional crash they were sure was coming.

**Except the meltdown never came.**

Jordan didn't tense up. His breathing didn't accelerate, his hands didn't ball into fists, and no fresh tears spilled over his lashes. Instead, he sat very still in his wheelchair, his head tilting slightly as his sharp, analytical mind processed the architecture of what they had just explained.

For eighteen years in Virginia Beach, Jordan had lived under someone who wielded absolute, unchecked power over him without a single boundary or rule to protect him. Now that Aubrie and Zoë had mapped out the social and institutional mechanics of a Resident Advisor, the logic clicked into place in his mind with crystal clarity. Isabel wasn't just an older friend who lived in Room 215; she held a position of authority and power over Floor 2. If a staff member in a position of power climbed into a resident's bed, it would cross a line that existed to protect everyone—it would be highly inappropriate, it would send the completely wrong message, and worst of all, it could cost Isabel the job she was so good at.

Jordan gave a slow, steady nod. Reaching up with the corner of his blanket to wipe away the last lingering sniffle from the fire drill, he placed his fingers calmly back on the keyboard of his Vanguard Pro and typed:

<aac>I understand. Because Isabel is our Resident Advisor, she is in a position of power, so being in the bed pact would be inappropriate and would send the wrong message. And I never want to do anything that would get Isabel in trouble. She can stay on my Safe People list as my RA across the hall, and the bed pact is just for us.</aac>

Around the room, five simultaneously held breaths released in a wave of pure, astonished relief.

Madison's shoulders dropped two inches as a radiant, proud smile broke across her face, and Lauren let out a soft, watery laugh, reaching over to squeeze Jordan's knee. Aubrie's eyes shone with quiet pride as she leaned in and pressed a warm kiss to his cheek.

"You are amazing, Jordan," Aubrie whispered.

There it was: the **Nightly Pact**, spoken aloud and agreed upon without a single trace of fear, possessiveness, or misunderstanding. Mark and Hannah had their devoted partnership, Isabel and Henry stood watch as his trusted RAs across the hall, and around Jordan stood a five-star constellation—Aubrie, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, and Sarah—bound together by an intuitive, zero-jealousy promise that none of them would ever have to face the dark alone again.

Zoë nodded once, a rare, radiant smile softening her face. "The parameters are clear, ethically sound, and mutually ratified."

Madison picked her mug back up off the rug and raised it in a toast. "Best. Suite. Ever."
