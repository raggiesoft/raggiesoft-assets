Even inside the human ring of his friends, the pounding bass of the halftime show and the press of the stadium concourse were pushing Jordan closer and closer to the edge. His rocking hadn't stopped, and his eyes remained wide and unfocused behind his glasses, trapped in the suffocating conviction that his mother's world was closing in on him.

"Okay, we're done. We're getting him out of here," Mark said, his voice a low, decisive command. Nobody hesitated for a second. The game no longer existed; their sole priority was extracting Jordan back to Bennett Hall.

The Group of 8 re-formed their protective diamond for movement.

"Mark, you and I take point," Hannah directed, her protective instincts locking into gear. "Clear a lane through the tunnel."

Zoë stepped briskly behind Jordan's wheelchair, gripping the push-handles with firm, steady hands. "I have propulsion and navigation."

Aubrie and Madison flanked the left and right sides of Jordan's chair, keeping one hand resting firmly on each of his shoulders to provide continuous, grounding deep-pressure input as they rolled, while Lauren and Sarah guarded the rear to buffer him from the jostling halftime crowd.

Moving as a single, disciplined unit, they cut through the sea of maroon and orange against the tide of fans heading for the concession lines. Whenever the concourse narrowed into a bottleneck near the exit ramps, Mark used his broad shoulders to part the crowd with polite, unyielding authority.

"Excuse us—wheelchair coming through, give us a lane, please," Mark repeated steadily, opening a clean corridor all the way out the main gates.

The moment they cleared the outer turnstiles of The Crucible, the deafening roar of the stadium dropped away into the crisp autumn air. Nobody spoke during the brisk roll back up the campus pathways. When they reached the lower quad-side entrance of "The Old Lady on the Hill," Jordan's trembling hand barely managed to tap his OEA-encoded Poly Pass against the exterior reader; the familiar mechanical whirr of Bennett Hall's accessible door opener swinging wide for ten seconds sounded like a collective sigh of relief.

They rode the elevator up to the second floor. As the elevator doors slid open, **Isabel Dawson** and **Henry Walsh** were standing near the water fountain down the hall chatting with two residents from Room 210.

One look at Jordan's pale face, his violent rocking torso, and the tight, protective formation around his chair stopped both RAs mid-sentence. Having seen Jordan cling to his desk screaming in terror during last Friday night's fire drill, Isabel and Henry instantly recognized that he was on the verge of a massive crisis.

Without saying a word to draw attention to Jordan, Henry casually stepped across the width of the hallway and gently steered the two chatting residents down toward the first-floor stairwell and lounge area to clear the corridor completely, then jogged quietly back to join Isabel. Ahead of the group, Isabel hurried to Room 214, tapped her RA master Poly Pass—which, like Jordan's OEA card, was encoded to trigger the accessible suite's automatic door opener—and held the doorway clear as the heavy wooden door unlocked and swung inward on its ten-second dormitory dwell timer.

"Bring him right in—the hall is clear," Isabel murmured softly as Zoë wheeled Jordan straight across the threshold.

"Isabel, Henry—please come in with us," Lauren whispered urgently over her shoulder as the rest of the Group of 8 filed inside. "Whatever made him panic during the fire drill last Friday... it's happening again."

Isabel and Henry didn't hesitate for a second. Both RAs stepped into Room 214 right behind Mark and Sarah just as the ten-second timer expired and the motorized arm pulled the heavy wooden door shut until the electronic lock clicked automatically into place.

The football game was still raging a few blocks away, but inside their cinderblock sanctuary, all ten of them were finally sealed off from the world.
