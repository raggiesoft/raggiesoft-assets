The walk from Bennett Hall across campus to the transit stops along **Commonwealth Mall** outside **Kincaid Hall** was a sight to behold. All nine friends moved as a single, cohesive organism—a bustling caravan of rolling suitcases, overstuffed duffel bags, and cheerful pre-holiday chatter, with Jordan’s wheelchair right at the protected center of their formation.

They reached the crowded sidewalk along **Commonwealth Mall** across the street from **Kincaid Hall** at four-thirty-five. Unlike Christiansburg's sawtooth transfer islands or Radford's numbered stalls at McConnell Transit Center, Commonwealth Mall had **no bus bays at all**—just a long, unmarked stretch of granite curb beneath **The Keystone Bridge** where buses from every transit agency in the valley simply lined up one behind the other in a loud, disorganized free-for-all.

All around them on the brick sidewalk, dozens of CPI students with rolling suitcases and holiday duffels were grumbling under their breath and craning their necks toward the intersection at **Main Street (Business 460)**.

"Man, I cannot wait until they finally build a real transit center on this campus," an upperclassman in a Forgers beanie muttered nearby, shifting his heavy backpack as a sixty-foot **Blue Ridge Transit** articulated bus and a twenty-nine-foot **Radford Transit** bus idled bumper-to-bumper at the head of the curb. "You literally never know which bus is going to turn off Business 460 next or where in the conga line it's gonna stop."

According to the timetable on Zoë's mPhone, the next southbound **Blue Ridge Transit Route 1** local bus down Business 460 was due in about ten minutes. Just as the nine friends were settling in along the sidewalk and watching the Main Street corner like everyone else, however, a completely different vehicle swung wide off **Business 460** onto **Commonwealth Mall** and glided to the curb right behind the departing local buses with a deep, turbocharged diesel purr.

It wasn't a standard low-floor city transit bus. It was a towering, forty-five-foot **Sainte-Claire 45H3** over-the-road highway motorcoach painted in gleaming maroon and silver livery, its high-visibility amber LED destination sign above the panoramic windshield cycling cleanly between two crisp displays (CBURG TC standing for Christiansburg Transit Center, and EXP for Express):

<led>MAROON EXPRESS</led>
<led>BLUEWATER EXP via CBURG TC</led>

With a sharp pneumatic hiss, the forward plug door swung outward, and a clear, automated female voice immediately began announcing the coach’s route on the exterior ADA speaker loop:

<pa>Bluewater Transit Maroon Express to Bluewater Transit Center via Christiansburg Transit Center</pa>

**Madison**’s eyes went wide, and she practically bounced on the balls of her sneakers.

"GUYS! LOOK!" Madison whisper-yelled, pointing excitedly at the gleaming highway coach that had just turned off Business 460 right in front of them. "It's the **Maroon Express**! It's the real **BT**!"

Seeing a couple of quizzical looks from the non-Southwest Virginia members of the group, Madison beamed with hometown pride and explained rapidly: "That's **Bluewater Transit**—my hometown bus! Between here and the **Christiansburg Transit Center at Falling Branch**, both the ***Maroon Express*** and Roanoke Valley Transit's ***Roanoke Express*** make the exact same three stops—**CPI**, **New River Valley Mall**, and **Christiansburg Transit Center**—before branching off on I-81 toward Bluewater or Roanoke! Even though each one runs hourly, the two buses are timed thirty minutes apart so an express coach comes by every thirty minutes! Instead of stopping at every single stoplight down Business 460 for forty-five minutes, it just makes one quick stop at the Christiansburg mall and takes the US-460 Bypass straight to Falling Branch! Come on, we have to take it!"

Boarding the high-deck highway coach was a slightly more involved mechanical production than rolling up the low-floor ramp of a campus shuttle. The friendly Bluewater Transit operator stepped out in a crisp uniform jacket, unlocked the mid-coach wheelchair lift bay doors, and operated the hydraulic platform with practiced, unhurried skill. The slow, whirring lift always drew a little more attention from passing students than Jordan preferred, but surrounded by Mark, Aubrie, and his friends cheering him on, the self-consciousness evaporated in seconds.

Once inside the elevated cabin—and after all nine of them had simply let the driver see their **Poly Passes** so their ride was free—Jordan transferred smoothly into a plush, high-backed reclining coach seat with reading lamps and upholstered armrests, while the driver and Mark folded his wheelchair and secured it safely inside the climate-controlled luggage bay below.

**Aubrie Jean** immediately slid into the window seat right beside him. The moment the coach pulled away from the bumper-to-bumper curb line on Commonwealth Mall, Jordan leaned gratefully against her side, and Aubrie wrapped her arm warmly around his shoulders, the two of them cuddling close as the big highway cruiser merged onto the four-lane US-460 Bypass. Across the aisle and in the rows directly behind them, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Mark**, **Sarah**, and **Jessica** claimed an entire section of the coach, filling the quiet cabin with happy, infectious holiday energy.

As the rolling pastures and autumn ridges of Montgomery County blurred past the tinted windows at sixty-five miles an hour, Zoë immediately pulled out her mPhone and fired off two texts—one to **Jordan Comms** so Isabel, Henry, and Kelsey knew they were airborne on the *Maroon Express*, and one to her parents letting them know their arrival time at the Christiansburg Transit Center had just been cut in half.

Ten minutes later, the forty-five-foot coach glided smoothly off the bypass into the **New River Valley Mall Transit Center** for its single intermediate stop, pausing briefly to let a handful of holiday shoppers step off before merging straight back onto the **US-460 Bypass** toward the direct **BUSES ONLY flyover ramp** into Falling Branch.

As the coach climbed back up to highway speed, Zoë turned around in her seat to brief the team.

"Okay, everyone, regarding house logistics," Zoë began in her crisp, organized cadence. "My parents have cleared out the entire finished basement living area for the nine of us. We have four queen-sized air mattresses plus the wide pull-out sectional sofa, so we will have plenty of sleeping space. And just as a preparatory heads-up: all six of my older brothers and sisters are home for the holiday, so prepare yourselves for a significant volume of domestic chaos."

Madison grinned from across the aisle. "Awesome! Okay, so Hannah and Mark are obviously claiming one queen mattress—"

"I really hope your mom is making her sweet potato casserole with the pecan crust!" Lauren chimed in dreamily.

"And since my parents are fifteen minutes away in Radford and Jessica's are right down I-81 in Roanoke, we'll still get almost the entire five days together at base camp!" Sarah added happily.

They were so absorbed in their enthusiastic planning—debating movie queues, mapping out Thursday's quick local family visits for Sarah, Jessica, Mark, and Hannah before reconvening for the afternoon feast, and trading stories as the coach glided down the BUSES ONLY ramp from the US-460 Bypass—that the automated chime and PA announcement overhead caught all nine of them by surprise:

<pa>NEXT STOP: CHRISTIANSBURG TRANSIT CENTER — FALLING BRANCH.</pa>

They looked at one another across the aisle and burst out laughing. Madison’s hometown highway express had been so fast that they had barely made a dent in their five-day itinerary.

Disembarking at the **Christiansburg Transit Center** at Falling Branch was just as smooth as boarding. Standing together under the concrete transit canopy as the *Maroon Express* rumbled back toward the interstate on-ramp for Bluewater, the nine friends breathed in the crisp, pine-scented late-afternoon air.

The entrance to Zoë’s neighborhood was just one short block away, at the corner of **U.S. Route 11** and **Simmons Road SE**. From there, it was only a few quiet, tree-lined blocks to the cul-de-sac of **Geneva Street**—where a warm golden porch light was already shining at the end of the lane to welcome them home.
