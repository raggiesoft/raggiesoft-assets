## 9:15 AM — Room 214

As the heavy wooden door of Room 214 clicked shut behind his mother's brisk footsteps, Aubrey Jordan Fuller found himself truly alone for the first time in his new dorm room. The quiet of the empty space was a muffled, heavy silence—a sudden, breathtaking relief after the suffocating night at the motel and the rushed chaos of unloading the car. He took a long, steadying breath and began to take a proper inventory of the small space—*his* space.

The room was tiny, clearly designed for two people but now cramped to fit three due to the university's housing crisis. Because the sky outside the single window was thick with low, slate-gray storm clouds, the interior was shadowed in a dim morning gloom. He flipped the main switch by the entry, and a large fluorescent light recessed into the drop ceiling flickered on, bathing the painted cinderblock walls in a harsh, sterile glow. He immediately squinted; the abrupt, glaring light caused the distant corners of the room to blur into a smear, sending a familiar, dull ache throbbing behind his eyes.

He rolled forward and looked back at the heavy, dark wood door he'd just entered. Like every locked residential door on campus, tapping an authorized CPI Poly Pass to the exterior reader unlocked the latch so someone could push the door open—except that for students registered with the Office of Educational Accessibility, the Poly Pass was encoded to also trigger the motorized automatic door opener, which normally stayed open for five seconds on standard campus doors, but was programmed for a full ten seconds on the heavier dormitory doors before automatically closing and re-locking. On the inside wall, tucked over by the doorframe on the right right beside the wall light switch so a resident could exit to the hallway, he saw the corresponding handicap actuator button: a square brushed stainless-steel push plate set in a black frame, stamped with the blue wheelchair symbol above the bold block words **PUSH TO OPEN**. High on the cinderblock wall above the switch and actuator sat a red **Simplex** fire alarm horn-and-strobe annunciator paired with a white ceiling smoke detector. Pressing the **PUSH TO OPEN** button from inside the room both unlocked the electronic latch and swung the door inward for ten seconds before it automatically closed and locked again—a setup shared by both Room 214 and its connecting suite partner, Room 216. Rolling a few inches closer and narrowing his eyes to focus, he could just make out the brass fixtures embedded in the solid wood of the door: two distinct peepholes, one drilled at standard standing height, and a second one positioned right at his eye level in the chair.

To his immediate left was a closed door, which he knew led to the shared toilet room. He wheeled over and opened it, confirming what he'd been told at check-in: a small, functional room containing only a toilet, fitted with sturdy metal grab bars mounted securely along the walls beside it. Across the small tiled space, he saw the second door on the opposite wall—the one that connected to Room 216.

Back in the main room, he noted the sink and mirror area next to the toilet door. He rolled his wheelchair closer, a small smile touching his lips as he saw the open space underneath. It was a roll-under sink, intentionally designed without a cabinet so he could pull his knees right up under the basin.

He wheeled toward the single window set in the center of the far wall—where the overcast sky outside was growing steadily darker over the campus—and turned his chair around to take in the full layout of the room.

To his right, looking back toward the wooden entry door, was a standalone, single Twin XL bed with a solid wood frame—Bed A, his assigned spot. His zipped canvas gym bag and the large, unopened cardboard parcel box from the first-floor mail room sat on the floor right where his mother had left them. A matching wooden desk was wedged tightly between the headboard and the front wall of the room.

To his left, occupying the opposite cinderblock wall, was a sturdy set of wooden bunk beds.

Both bed setups currently held nothing but bare, clinical mattresses, waiting for the pillows, sheets, and blankets still taped inside his mailed cardboard box.

He counted the rest of the furniture. Two desks, two dressers, and two nightstands. That confirmed the room's original two-person design. Until he had rolled through the door ten minutes ago, he had assumed he would only be sharing Room 214 with one other guy, with two guys on the other side of the bathroom in Room 216. Instead, the university had squeezed a bunk bed in against the wall—meaning his two future roommates in 214, whoever those guys were, would have to share a single desk and dresser between them.

This was it. College.

## 1:00 PM — The Awkward Outlet

Outside the single window, the heavy gray clouds that had hung low over Blacksburg all morning had bruised to a deep, threatening charcoal. Before he ventured out to explore the campus and before the sky opened up, he decided to set up his most important tool first: his laptop. He wheeled himself over to his desk, placed the heavy machine on it, and opened the lid. The battery icon blinked red, and a moment later, the screen went completely black. Dead.

He sighed, a silent puff of air, and squinted around the room for an outlet. He spotted one tucked low on the cinderblock wall underneath his wooden desk, but recessed behind the desk legs where it was awkwardly out of reach from his wheelchair.

Unzipping the backpack resting on the floor behind his chair, he pulled out the laptop's black power cable, intending to lean in and plug it in himself. He maneuvered his chair closer to the desk, stretching with the cord in hand, but he couldn't angle his frame far enough beneath the desk to push the prongs into the recessed wall socket. He grunted with frustration, a small but significant reminder that even in an "accessible" room, the world wasn't always designed with him in mind.

Suddenly, he felt a subtle shift in the air pressure and a cool draft brush across the back of his neck as the heavy dorm door swung inward behind him, spilling a wedge of bright hallway light across the carpet.

Startled, Aubrey turned around in his wheelchair with the unplugged power cable still in his hand.

Standing in the open doorway was a petite young woman on the shorter end—around five-foot-two, with straight jet-black hair past her shoulders, warm brown eyes behind rectangular glasses, and a maroon CPI Housing & Residence Life Student Staff polo with her nametag and duty lanyard. Spotting his dark laptop on the desk, the unplugged power cable in his hand, and the awkward outlet tucked underneath the desk, she offered a warm, unhurried smile and a friendly wave.

"Hey there—sorry if I surprised you! Need a hand with that?"

Aubrey flinched slightly and instinctively cupped a hand behind his ear to catch her muffled, friendly voice. "I'm Isabel Dawson, one of the two RAs for this floor," she continued, stepping inside as the ten-second timer expired and the door eased shut behind her. "My room is 215, offset just across the hall—just doing my rounds for early check-ins."

Aubrey felt a flush of embarrassment at being caught struggling. With his laptop completely dead, he was entirely voiceless. Unable to speak, he simply gave a grateful nod and gestured to the power cable in his hand and the recessed outlet underneath the desk.

Isabel saw the problem instantly. "Oh yeah, that's a terrible spot for an outlet under the desk. No problem at all. Let's make this easier."

He reached back into his backpack on the floor and handed her his power strip. Ducking under the desk, she plugged the power strip into the wall outlet, ran the cord neatly up onto the desktop, and pulled a strip of double-sided tape from her pocket to affix the power strip securely to the top corner of the desk. "There," she said with a smile, raising her volume slightly after noticing his cupped ear. "Now you can plug your laptop and anything else right into the top of your desk."

Aubrey immediately plugged in his adapter and waited for the slow Quantum OS 7 system to boot up. Once the desktop appeared, he opened *SimpleSpeak*, hunching uncomfortably close to the monitor and squinting intensely to make out the keys as he typed. He hit the execute key.

<aac>Wow. Thanks. Thank you,</aac> the robotic, digitized voice announced to the room. Aubrey looked up, offering a genuinely grateful smile.

Isabel didn't bat an eye at the synthesized voice. "Don't mention it. Do you need a hand with anything else? Getting your bed set up?"

Aubrey hesitated, then nodded. He turned back to the screen and typed again.

<aac>Actually, yeah. The pillows and blankets are kind of a pain.</aac>

For the next ten minutes, Isabel helped him peel open the heavy packing tape on the cardboard box from the mail room and make his bed, placing his sheets, pillows, and blankets exactly how he liked them. She unpacked his canvas gym bag, putting his folded clothes neatly away in the dresser drawers, and set his clean towels and toiletries bag right on the edge of the roll-under sink counter where he could easily reach them. She was efficient, friendly, and didn't make him feel like a burden for a single second.

"There you go," she said finally. "All set. Also, just so you know, the accessible roll-in shower room for our floor is just a few doors down the hall toward the central atrium." Walking back to the door, she pressed the square **PUSH TO OPEN** handicap button on the wall to unlock and swing the door open, then paused at the threshold and gestured diagonally across the staggered hallway toward her own door. "Seriously, Aubrey, my room is right across the hall in 215. If you need any help with anything at all, you just knock on my door, or find our other floor RA, Henry Walsh, over in the other wing in 201, or our Hall Director, Kelsey. That's what we're here for."

Aubrey leaned close to the keyboard and typed a final message before the door's ten-second timer could close it between them.

<aac>I will. Thank you, Isabel. Do you know who my two roommates are? Or when they will arrive?</aac>

Isabel caught the edge of the door lightly with one hand as the timer expired, glancing down at her clipboard with an apologetic look. "I'm sorry, I only have the Friday early check-in list on my clipboard right now," she explained. "Since Bennett Hall is co-ed by suite now—each connecting suite is all guys or all girls, even if the next suite over is the opposite gender—Central Housing is still finishing the master room roster and sending their student runners over this afternoon to put up the door cards for tomorrow. Main move-in starts tomorrow morning, so you can expect the other guys in your suite to start arriving tomorrow at the soonest. Looks like you've got the whole place to yourself for tonight!"

As she stepped out into the corridor and let the heavy door swing shut and lock automatically behind her, Aubrey nodded, a wave of both relief and nervousness washing over him. One night of peace before meeting the strangers who would become his new roommates.
