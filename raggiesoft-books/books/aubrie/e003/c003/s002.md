At the top of the internal basement stairs, **Daniel Brooks** had heard what **Jordan's** AAC tablet said about **Firelight's** 1987 synthesizer score—and a slow, delighted grin spread across his face.

"Hold everything right there," Daniel called down in his crisp London accent, descending the stairs two at a time with **Laura** right behind him. "Did I just hear that my son has never been properly introduced to 1980s music?!"

In his right hand, Daniel was holding up a clear plastic cassette tape with **`THE BROOKS MIX`** written across the spine label in his neat block lettering.

"We've got to chase away the gloom," Daniel declared, crossing to the component stereo rack in the corner of the basement. "You liked *The Gauntlet*, Jordan? You liked that sound? Well, I'm about to spin your head around. You haven't heard the best stuff yet."

**Mark** jumped up from the sectional sofa, grinning from ear to ear as Daniel popped open the dual cassette deck, slid the clear plastic cassette into the transport with a loud, mechanical *click*, and pressed **`PLAY`**.

For a brief second, there was the warm, analog hiss of magnetic tape.

Then a massive, echoing, gated-snare drum fill exploded through the floor-standing speakers, followed by an irresistibly bright, bouncing synth-pop bassline and a shimmering keyboard hook.

Sitting between **Aubrie Jean** and **Madison** on the sectional sofa, Jordan felt the bass pulse vibrate right through the cushion and into his own thumb on the armrest.

For eighteen years in Virginia Beach, his mother had locked popular culture out of the house, keeping his world quiet, cold, and rigid. Now, the Brooks basement turned into a neon explosion in the night—no heavy dread, no suffocating cage, just bright major chords, fast tempos, and unabashed 1980s joy.

Track after track on **`The Brooks Mix`** introduced Jordan to an entire musical universe he had never been allowed to hear:

* There was a synth-pop anthem about a girl in a diamond dress, followed immediately by a driving stadium-rocker about a runaway express with guitar solos soaring to the rafters and vocals that climbed impossibly high.
* By the door at the foot of the stairs, **Daniel** was unabashedly playing air guitar with his eyes closed while **Laura** laughed and clapped along on the beat.
* Out on the carpet in front of the coffee table, **Zoë** and **Madison** pulled **Lauren**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Hannah** up off the cushions to dance across the floor.
* And on the sofa beside **Aubrie Jean**, Jordan found himself tapping his foot against his footrest and nodding his head in time with the gated drums—falling head-over-heels in love with the genre and the music of the 1980s.

When **Track Four** kicked in with a driving bass sequencer and a steady drum-machine pulse, Jordan looked at the digital timer on the stereo deck and typed out a laughing note on his **mPad**:

<aac>Track four is six minutes long, and it has a three-minute saxophone solo! I don't know why, but I love it!</aac>

"Because in the eighties, son," Daniel laughed over a screaming, heavily processed electric guitar solo that followed the saxophone breakdown, "more was *always* more! Turn it up!"

As the final track of the cassette arpeggiated wildly through a long, classic 1980s fade-out and the tape transport stopped with a crisp mechanical *click*, Jordan was beaming from ear to ear, his chest warm and his eyes bright behind his glasses.

Yet as the cassette deck clicked silent, **Mark** and **Daniel** exchanged a quiet, knowing look across the stereo rack.

"That mixtape was the appetizer, J," Mark said softly, walking over to the walnut vinyl crate beside the high-end turntable. "Now that you know the language of the eighties... there is one record you *have* to hear."
