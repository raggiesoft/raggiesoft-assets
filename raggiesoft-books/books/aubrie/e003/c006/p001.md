## March 16, 2015 • 1:30 PM — Three Hundred and Fifty Pounds of Freedom

On Monday afternoon, March sixteenth—as the nine students returned from a peaceful, laughter-filled Spring Break in Bluewater to learn from **Daniel Brooks** that Aubrie Sinclair's private investigator had checked out of his Route 460 motel and gone back to Tidewater empty-handed—the hydraulic lift-gate on the back of the **Blue Ridge Complex Rehab & Mobility** sprinter van hummed down onto the concrete apron of the Brooks driveway at **414 Geneva Street**.

**Marcus Vance** backed the flagship machine out into the March sunlight, while his assistant wheeled out Jordan's matching fourteen-pound **`Vanguard AeroLite-Ti`** custom brushed-titanium folding manual chair for tight indoor spaces and car trunks.

Even knowing the specifications he had personally picked out and signed for in January—and knowing that **Daniel Brooks** had paid for both custom machines in cash out-of-pocket so Jordan would never have to spend another day in the clunky, ill-fitting manual hospital wheelchair his mother had sent him to CPI with—seeing his custom **Vanguard Titan-X350** in person took Jordan's breath away.

Three hundred and fifty pounds of matte-black, aircraft-grade aluminum and steel sat planted on six independent suspension wheels. The high-backed contoured biomechanical seat was upholstered in breathable charcoal ballistic mesh over the custom fluid-cell memory cushion. Mounted on the swing-away right armrest sat the sleek proportional R-Net color LCD joystick module, alongside the ruggedized articulating bracket for his **Pinnacle Vanguard Pro** workstation, integrated amber-and-white LED path-lights tucked into the front matte-black fenders, and a heavy-duty backpack rack welded seamlessly behind the headrest.

"Here she is, Mr. Fuller," Marcus said proudly, locking the electromagnetic parking brakes with a solid *click* and flipping up the left armrest flush against the backrest. "Custom-built and calibrated to your exact seating geometry, right alongside your fourteen-pound *AeroLite-Ti* titanium manual chair. Want to transfer out of that old hospital chair for the very last time?"

Jordan grinned. Rolling the old manual chair forward an inch and back half an inch to trail the front casters, he parked at a crisp **forty-five-degree angle**—the exact **Forty-Five-Degree Transfer** he had just taught Madison's fourteen-year-old brother **Bob** down in Bluewater on Wednesday—tucked his elbows in, leaned his head and chest forward over his knees, and pivoted smoothly across the two-inch corner onto the contoured seat of the *Titan-X350*.

Marcus smiled with a nod of veteran respect. "Cleanest forty-five-degree pivot transfer in Montgomery County, Mr. Fuller."

<aac>Angles and leverage beat brute force every time,</aac> Jordan's AAC speaker replied proudly as **Madison** beamed beside him, thinking of her little squirt practicing that exact pivot down in Bluewater. Then Jordan looked back at the empty, squeaking old hospital wheelchair from Virginia Beach and tapped a second line:

<aac>Mr. Vance, can you haul that old hospital chair away in your van for scrap recycling? I never want to see my mother's chair again.</aac>

"Nothing would give me greater pleasure, Mr. Fuller," Marcus grinned, folding up the worn institutional relic, tossing it into the back of the sprinter van for scrap, and slamming the rear doors shut to a round of cheers from the entire driveway.

The difference inside the new chair was instantaneous. Instead of feeling perched on a lightweight manual sling frame that tipped backward on steep curb cuts and demanded upper-body force for every foot of forward motion, Jordan felt anchored inside a cockpit built like a vault.

Marcus folded the left armrest down and tapped the power button on the right-hand module. The high-contrast color LCD screen booted up with five speed profiles (1: Indoor Dorm, 2: Lecture Hall, 3: Sidewalk Cruise, 4: Hill Climb Torque, 5: Full Campus), battery telemetry (100% — 22.0 MI RANGE), and power tilt-in-space controls.

Jordan rested his right fingertips lightly over the soft-touch ball of the joystick and nudged it two millimeters forward.

With a whisper-quiet, futuristic turbine hum from the dual four-pole center-drive motors, the three-hundred-and-fifty-pound chair glided forward across the driveway as smoothly as if it were riding on magnetic rails. Jordan eased the stick left, then pulled it slightly back, spinning a complete, effortless 360-degree turn inside the footprint of a single sidewalk square before tapping the rocker switch to ignite the bright white LED fender lamps.

Around the driveway, **Caleb**, **Owen**, **Lucas**, and **Jacob** burst into applause, while **Daniel** stood with his arm around **Laura**, beaming with pride.

Looking up at Daniel, Mark, and the women of his Constellation with a fierce glint in his green eyes, Jordan tapped his AAC screen:

<aac>There is only one real test for this chair. Take me up to Bennett Hall. I want to climb the exterior hill from the first-floor inner quad up to Washington Street.</aac>

## March 16, 2015 • 2:15 PM — Conquering the Bennett Hall Hillside

Thirty minutes later back on campus in **Blacksburg**, Daniel parked the accessible ramp-equipped transit van—which he had leased for the spring and summer so Jordan could roll straight inside without transferring—along **Washington Street SW** outside the second-floor entrance of **Bennett Hall**.

After rolling down the exterior hillside walkway to the **first-floor inner quad doors** at the bottom of the slope, Jordan spun his chair around at the base of the hill.

It was the exact twelve-degree paved exterior incline between the first-floor inner quad level and the second-floor **Washington Street SW** entrance where, all through Fall Semester and the first two months of Spring, Jordan had either had to take the interior elevator ("Riding the Hill") or rely on Mark, Lauren, or Aubrie Jean bracing the rear push-handles of his manual chair to keep him from rolling backward.

He looked up the long, steep grade rising alongside the Ironstone and brick facade of Bennett Hall toward **Washington Street SW**. Up at the top of the slope outside the second-floor Washington Street SW doors, **Isabel Dawson** and **Garrett Reynolds** happened to be stepping out for their afternoon building walk—and both of them froze on the upper walkway, staring down the hill in amazement as they saw Jordan sitting in the matte-black power chair at the bottom of the ridge.

"Want us to walk behind you or beside you, Jordan?" **Mark** asked, his hands empty at his sides.

<aac>Beside me,</aac> Jordan's AAC speaker rang out clearly across the pavement. <aac>Nobody ever pushes from behind again.</aac>

Jordan tapped the mode button on his right-hand joystick up to **PROFILE 4: HILL CLIMB TORQUE** and pushed the joystick forward with two fingers.

The dual high-torque motors engaged with a deep, authoritative purr.

Without a single wobble, without a single dip in speed, and without Jordan having to strain a muscle in his shoulders, the three-hundred-and-fifty-pound matte-black *Titan-X350* surged straight up the twelve-degree exterior hillside walkway from the inner quad to **Washington Street SW** at a brisk four miles per hour.

**Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Mark**, **Sarah**, and **Jessica** actually had to lengthen their strides just to keep pace beside his wheels as he climbed the hill effortlessly in the bright afternoon sun.

When Jordan crested the top of the ridge onto the level **Washington Street SW** sidewalk outside the second-floor entrance of Bennett Hall, he spun the chair around to face the campus quad stretching out below them.

Outside the second-floor Washington Street SW doors, **Garrett** grinned and applauded, while **Isabel** pressed both hands over her mouth, her dark eyes shining with pure, unvarnished joy at seeing him back on the hill, whole and unstoppable.

"Three hundred and fifty pounds of freedom," Aubrie Jean whispered, leaning down to kiss his cheek as the wind ruffled his hair at the crest of the ridge.

## March 17, 2015 • 9:00 AM — Maiden Voyage of the S.S. Freedom and the Kuiper Belt

On Tuesday morning, March seventeenth—the first morning of classes after Spring Break—**Jordan Fuller** rolled out through the upper-terrace doors of **Bennett Hall** into the bright spring sunshine.

Before they had gone to sleep the night before, **Mark** and **Hannah** had presented Jordan with a matching pair of laser-engraved matte-silver nameplates that Mark had machined in the **Joule Hall** engineering student shop—one mounted neatly on the rear backrest bracket of Jordan's three-hundred-and-fifty-pound *Vanguard Titan-X350* power wheelchair, and its twin mounted on the brushed-titanium crossbar of his fourteen-pound *Vanguard AeroLite-Ti* tight-space manual wheelchair (christened in honor of both the astronomer Galileo Galilei from `ASTR 204` and *Star Trek*'s famous auxiliary shuttlecraft):

**S.S. FREEDOM — CAPT. J. FULLER**
**SHUTTLECRAFT GALILEO — AUX. CRAFT TO S.S. FREEDOM**

As the nine of them set out across the wide paved paths of **Commonwealth Green** toward their morning lecture in **Archer Hall**, Jordan realized that something fundamental in the way he experienced the campus had changed overnight.

All through Fall Semester and the first half of Spring, rolling across campus in a manual chair had meant constantly calculating every curb lip, every uneven slab of concrete, and every uphill grade—always relying on Mark, Lauren, or Aubrie Jean to take the handles whenever the slope steepened.

Today, the air over **Commonwealth Green** smelled like damp earth, budding maple trees, and freshly cut grass—and Jordan was driving his own ship.

He nudged the joystick of the *S.S. Freedom* up to Profile 3: Sidewalk Cruise, feeling the warm spring breeze against his face as he rolled right down the center of the path—his left hand free to hold **Aubrie Jean's** hand as they moved side-by-side!—while **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Mark**, **Sarah**, and **Jessica** walked alongside him without anyone having to stand behind his back.

When they arrived at **Archer Hall** at 9:30 AM for **HIST 115: History of Ancient Rome**, **Dr. Marcus Gable** paused by the lectern as Jordan glided effortlessly up the ramped aisle into the top-tier accessible bay.

"Welcome back from the frontier, Mr. Fuller, Ms. Kelly, Ms. Scott," Dr. Gable said warmly, his sharp eyes taking in the matte-black power wheelchair with an approving nod. "Ms. Higgins told me the three of you executed a textbook strategic withdrawal before the break—and it appears the cavalry has upgraded its armor."

Jordan grinned and tapped his **Pinnacle Vanguard Pro** keyboard:

<aac>Thank you for letting us study remotely before the break, Dr. Gable. And yes, sir—the testudo is motorized now.</aac>

The entire back row chuckled, and Dr. Gable flashed a rare, proud smile before launching into the morning's lecture on Julius Caesar's bridging of the Rhine.

## March 17, 2015 • 2:00 PM — Pluto, Charon, and the Kuiper Belt

That afternoon in **Kepler Hall**, after **Dr. Alistair** had similarly welcomed Jordan and Aubrie Jean back to **PHYS 102** and handed back their latest exam (99/100 for Jordan, 98/100 for Aubrie Jean), the two of them rolled upstairs into the darkened planetarium dome for **ASTR 204: Observational Astrophysics**.

Today's lecture topic was the outer frontier of the solar system: the **Kuiper Belt** and the scattered disk of icy planetesimals orbiting out beyond Neptune in the deep, cold twilight forty astronomical units from the Sun.

As the star projector cast the faint, glittering ring of trans-Neptunian worlds—**Pluto and Charon** locked in their mutual gravitational embrace, along with Haumea, Makemake, and Eris—across the curved dome overhead, Aubrie Jean leaned her shoulder gently against Jordan's left armrest and laced her fingers with his.

"Look at Pluto and Charon up there," Aubrie Jean whispered softly while Dr. Alistair explained how the binary pair orbited a shared center of gravity outside either body—tidally locked so that they forever faced each other across the dark. "Out at the edge of the cold where nobody thought a warm core could survive, and they never let go of each other."

Jordan looked up at the twin points of light wheeling slowly across the planetarium dome, then looked at Aubrie Jean beside him.

With a grin, Jordan typed quietly on his laptop screen for her:

<aac>And in sixty-seven days, we're taking our own orbit around this planet.</aac>

## March 18, 2015 • 12:30 PM — The Food Court Affirmation and the Night of the Pact

At twelve-thirty on Wednesday afternoon, all nine members of **Suite 214/216** met up inside the bustling, multi-level hillside dining hall of **Kincaid Commons**.

At the start of Fall Semester, a crowded campus dining hall at the noon rush—five hundred students talking at once, stainless-steel trays clattering, espresso machines hissing, and lines snaking around the Ironstone pillars—would have spiked Jordan's sensory meter past eighty within three minutes and required Mark and Lauren to form a physical wedge around his manual chair while someone else ordered his food for him.

Today, as the nine of them claimed a long corner table by the tall arched windows overlooking the quad, **Mark** started to ask out of habit, "Want me to grab your grilled chicken wrap from the counter while you park, Jordan?"

Jordan smiled and shook his head.

He tapped his armrest tablet with a bright grin:

<aac>I've got it today, Mark. Meet you guys back at the table in five.</aac>

Guiding the *S.S. Freedom* with effortless precision through the bustling lunch crowd—using the twenty-one-inch mid-wheel turning radius to pivot cleanly around backpack-wearing students in the queue—Jordan rolled up to the counter by himself. When the student cashier looked down and smiled, Jordan triggered his order preset on his phone, flashed his **Poly Pass** dining card at the accessible reader, hooked the warm takeout bag onto his armrest carry-loop, and navigated smoothly back to the corner table without a single spike in his pulse or a single tremor in his hands.

When he pulled into his spot at the head of the table, all eight of his suitemates looked at him with quiet, beaming pride.

Grinning as he unwrapped his sandwich, Jordan tapped a quick key:

<aac>What?</aac>

"Nothing," **Hannah** said softly, leaning over to kiss his cheek. "Just watching a guy who owns his whole life order lunch."

## March 18, 2015 • 9:30 PM — Night of the Constellation Pact

That evening back in **Bennett Hall**, after homework was finished and **Mark** and **Hannah** had retired into **Room 216** for the night, the connecting bathroom door clicked shut and the **six women of the Constellation Pact**—**Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Sarah**, and **Jessica**—gathered with **Jordan** inside **Room 214**.

All week in Bluewater, sleeping side-by-side on the air mattresses in Madison's ground-floor childhood bedroom had been cozy and wonderful, yet out of respect for David, Diane, Bob, and Emily under the same roof, they had kept their nights strictly PG-13 under their pajamas and quilts.

Tonight, back inside their private second-floor sanctuary with the deadbolt turned and the warm amber fairy lights glowing along the cinderblock wall above **Bed A** and the two-high bunk (**Beds B and C**), the seven of them shed every layer of fabric and hesitation.

Using his practiced **Forty-Five-Degree Transfer**—the same smooth corner pivot he had passed down to Madison's little brother **Bob** in Bluewater—Jordan parked the *S.S. Freedom* at a clean forty-five-degree angle beside his nineteen-inch standalone Twin XL **Bed A**, leaned forward over his knees, and pivoted smoothly across the corner gap straight into the warm, bare arms of his Constellation.

Even within the narrow footprint of a 1960s dormitory room, there was no rush, no hierarchy, and not a shadow of jealousy anywhere in the space—only six women who loved him fiercely and loved one another as sisters, rotating effortlessly between Jordan's **Bed A** and the bunks across the narrow aisle as they took turns holding him, kissing him, and sharing a slow, breathtaking night of mutual passion and skin-to-skin tenderness where every touch affirmed how far he had come from the boy who used to flinch at his own reflection.

When they finally drifted off to sleep after midnight—with **Sarah** and **Jessica** curled bare and warm against Jordan's left and right sides on **Bed A** for their long-promised overnight turn, **Aubrie Jean** and **Madison** reaching across the narrow aisle from the lower bunk (**Bed B**) to hold his hands, and **Zoë** and **Lauren** nestled up on the top bunk (**Bed C**)—Jordan rested his palm lightly over the center of his chest, remembering the sacred **Heart-Hum** name (*The Key*) that **Bob** and **Emily** had given him in Bluewater, and drifted into a deep, peaceful sleep.
