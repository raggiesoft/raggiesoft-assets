## May 15, 2015 • 11:00 AM — Stripping Room 214

By eleven o'clock on Friday morning, May fifteenth—the final day of Spring Semester move-out at **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute**, and the final day **Eleanor Bennett Commonwealth Hall ("The Old Lady")** would ever house undergraduate students before being permanently closed for demolition—**Suite 214/216** had been returned to the bare, echoing white cinderblock and polished linoleum it had been when Jordan first rolled across its threshold nine months earlier.

Final exams were officially in the books.

Yesterday afternoon, when the Registrar in **Preston Hall** posted spring grades, Jordan had stared at his transcript screen in quiet wonder: five As across **PHYS 102**, **ASTR 204**, **GEOL 101**, **MATH 112**, and **HIST 115**—locking in a cumulative **4.00 freshman-year GPA** right alongside **Aubrie Jean’s** matching 4.00.

Now, with only **eight days** remaining until all eighteen members of the family departed on **Saturday, May twenty-third** for **Jordan's World Trek**, the nine residents of Suite 214/216 were finishing the last step of freshman year: stripping Room 214 and Room 216 for move-out.

With **Mark**, **Lauren**, and **Daniel** rolling the last of the storage totes and rolled-up foam mattress toppers out to the central elevator—ready to be transferred to the Christiansburg house where their new off-campus quarters and the nine-foot-by-nine-foot Alaskan King bed frame were waiting for sophomore year—Jordan sat at the controls of his matte-black *S.S. Freedom* near the open doorway between Room 214 and the suite bathroom, wearing the heather-grey **CPI FORGERS** crew-neck t-shirt he had bought for himself at **The Foundry** (the CPI campus bookstore).

He packed the last of his desk books—including Arthur MacAllister's *Civitas*, his **ASTR 204** Kuiper Belt star charts, and the **Electric Color teal-and-maroon CAPTAIN OF THE HILL wooden clothespin** Isabel had brought him from the **CACUHO** conference—into his backpack.

He looked around the empty room where the string lights had hung above the headboard all winter and spring.

"Hard to believe freshman year is actually over," **Aubrie Jean** said softly, stepping up beside his chair and resting her hand on his shoulder as they looked at the bare university-issue Twin XL bed frames—Jordan's standalone nineteen-inch **Bed A** and the two-high bunk frame (**Beds B and C**) along the opposite wall—stripped clean for the final housing inspection. "Nine months ago, we walked into the Old Lady just praying we could keep you safe through Labor Day."

"And instead," **Madison** said, joining them with the swept dustpan in her hand and a warm smile on her lips, "we built a whole life inside these walls."

Out in the second-floor hallway, a polite knock sounded against the open oak door.

**Kelsey Brennan**, **Isabel Dawson**, and **Garrett Reynolds** stood at the threshold wearing their official **CPI Housing & Residence Life** staff uniforms—Kelsey in her full-time Professional Staff two-tone Chicago Maroon polo with Burnt Orange side panels, and Isabel and Garrett in their Student Staff Chicago Maroon polos with crisp white side panels and Burnt Orange shoulder trim, all tucked neatly into belted khaki pants—holding the official yellow **Room Condition Report (RCR)** clipboards and clearance envelopes.

"Ready for the last checkout inspection in the history of the Old Lady?" Kelsey asked warmly, her eyes misty as she looked around the spotless, swept floors of Room 216 and Room 214. "Let me sign off Mark, Hannah, Sarah, and Jessica's rooms first, and then we'll take the final cart down to Daniel's trailer."

***

## 11:20 AM — A Very Long Hug in the Second-Floor Hallway

Within five minutes, eight of the nine yellow **Room Condition Reports (RCRs)** were signed, stamped, and filed into their completed checkout folders, deactivating their Bennett Hall door-access permissions on the central **Poly Pass** server while leaving their university ID cards active for the rest of their college careers.

**Daniel**, **Mark**, **Hannah**, **Lauren**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Sarah**, and **Jessica** rolled the last four loaded utility carts out through the **second-floor Washington Street SW entrance** to load the trailer waiting at the curb. **Aubrie Jean** paused at the doorway, gave Jordan's shoulder a gentle, affectionate squeeze, and walked down the hall toward the Washington Street SW doors with the others so Jordan could finish the final Room 214 checkout himself.

At the doorway of Suite 214, **Garrett Reynolds** reached down and shook Jordan's hand with a firm, warm grip.

"It has been an absolute honor being your RA this year, Jordan," Garrett said sincerely. "Watching you climb the Bennett Hall hill from the inner quad up to Washington Street in that matte-black tank and watching how the nine of you stood together all year—that's the kind of thing I'm going to remember long after I graduate. Have the trip of a lifetime across the world this summer, man."

Jordan tapped his armrest tablet warmly:

<aac>Thank you for everything, Garrett. For keeping our wing locked down tight after that investigator showed up outside Bennett Hall, for fighting for our suite at CACUHO, and for keeping the second floor steady.</aac>

Garrett glanced once at **Isabel Dawson**, who was standing beside the open door of **Room 215** holding the very last yellow RCR clipboard—Jordan's—and then glanced at **Kelsey Brennan**.

"Hey, Kelsey," Garrett said smoothly, picking up the heavy file box of completed first-floor and second-floor RCR checkout folders from the hallway table, "let's run these RCR archive boxes down to the main office while Isabel finishes the final signature on 214."

"Good idea," Kelsey said, flashing Jordan a warm smile before heading toward the stairwell with Garrett. "Have an incredible summer abroad, Jordan! We'll see you in August!"

The heavy stairwell door clicked shut at the end of the corridor.

Suddenly, the sun-drenched second-floor hallway of **Bennett Hall** was completely quiet. Dust motes floated in the afternoon light pouring through the tall casement window at the end of the hall.

Isabel stood alone in front of Jordan's chair with the two-part carbonless checkout form and a black pen in her hands.

"One final line at the bottom of the Room Condition Report, Mr. Fuller," Isabel said softly, trying to use her official Resident Advisor voice one last time, though her dark eyes were already bright with emotion behind her glasses as she held out the clipboard. "Zero damages, room left in immaculate condition, Room 214-A cleared and Poly Pass residential access scheduled for five o'clock deactivation."

Jordan took the pen and signed *Jordan Fuller* on the resident checkout line.

Isabel tore off the yellow resident receipt copy, handed it to Jordan, and tucked the white Housing original into the clipboard folder.

Looking up at the Resident Advisor who had welcomed him into **Bennett Hall** nine months ago, knelt beside his desk during the September fire drill, welcomed his found family to Charlottesville over Thanksgiving, and stood guard over their floor all year, Jordan smiled warmly and tapped a lighthearted message onto his armrest tablet screen:

<aac>As of right now, my lease for the academic year is closed. Which means you aren't my Resident Advisor anymore.</aac>

Isabel froze.

Her breath caught audibly in her throat, and a deep, rosy blush rose up her neck into her cheeks. She looked down at the yellow RCR clipboard in her hand, and then looked back at Jordan—her lips parting slightly as her right hand came up to touch the collar of her shirt, as if she were suddenly flustered by a rush of emotion she wasn't ready to put into words.

Then Isabel glanced down at the white embroidered **CPI HOUSING & RESIDENCE LIFE — RESIDENT ADVISOR** lettering on the left chest of the two-tone maroon-and-white Student Staff athletic polo shirt she still had tucked into her belted khaki pants, looked at the black electronic **Poly Pass** card reader glowing green directly underneath the **ROOM 214** sign on the cinderblock wall where she was still officially on duty until five o'clock, and looked back at Jordan, who was flying across the oceans in eight days to see the world.

She let out a shaky breath, a bright tear slipping down her flushed cheek as a radiant, tender smile broke through at the exact same time.

"No," Isabel whispered, her voice thick with emotion. "As of eleven-twenty-five on May fifteenth... I'm not your RA anymore."

Jordan tapped the button on his right armrest to flip up the left arm of his chair and opened his arms wide.

Isabel didn't hesitate for a heartbeat.

She stepped right into the open space beside his frame, dropped to her knees on the clean hallway floor so they were at exact eye level, and wrapped both of her arms tightly around Jordan's neck—pulling him into **a very long, deep, breathless goodbye hug**.

Jordan wrapped his strong arms around her waist and shoulders, holding her close against his chest, feeling her shoulders tremble slightly as she cried softly against him—overwhelmed by the end of their year in the Old Lady and by how much she was going to miss him while he was away all summer.

Isabel buried her face against the crook of his neck, her dark hair brushing his smooth, clean-shaven jaw as warm tears dampened the collar of his shirt while she held onto him for nearly a full minute in the quiet hallway.

"I am so proud of you, Jordan Fuller," Isabel whispered fiercely through her tears, her hands gripping the back of his heather-grey **CPI Forgers** shirt as if she couldn't bear to let go yet. "You walked into this building last August terrified of the world, and you turned into the bravest, kindest, strongest man I have ever known. I am going to miss having you right across the hall so much."

Resting his cheek against her hair and holding her with his right arm, Jordan tapped his tablet screen with his left thumb so the soft voice spoke just for her:

<aac>Thank you for never giving up on me when I was still afraid of my own shadow, Isabel.</aac>

When they finally drew back just enough to look into each other's eyes, Isabel reached up and brushed her thumb gently across his cheekbone—lingering there for three warm, unmistakable seconds before smiling through her tears.

"Go see the whole wide world this summer, Jordan," she whispered. "Every ocean, every mountain, every Roman ruin you read about in Dr. Gable's class. And when you come back to Blacksburg in August—when the Old Lady is gone and I'm settled into my new place over at **Allegheny Commons Apartment 101**... come find me."

<aac>I will. Count on it,</aac> Jordan's tablet promised softly as he squeezed her hand.

## 11:35 AM — The Eleventh Child: Ready for the World

Ten minutes later, Jordan rolled down the second-floor corridor of **Bennett Hall** and straight out the grade-level **Washington Street SW entrance** into the golden May sunshine.

Out at the **Washington Street SW curb**, the entire family was waiting for him beside Daniel's ramp-equipped transit van and Mark's SUV.

There stood **Daniel** and **Laura Brooks**, smiling in the warm spring breeze.
There stood **Mark** and **Hannah**, leaning arm-in-arm against the open side door of the van.
And lined up along the sunny sidewalk at the base of the van's power ramp stood the **six women of the Constellation Pact**: **Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Sarah**, and **Jessica**.

**Zoë** held up a thick, zippered leather travel folio that had arrived at the Christiansburg house that morning.

"Final pre-departure checklist item complete!" Zoë called out, her eyes shining in the May sunlight as she fanned out the **sixteen crisp navy-blue United States Passports** and **sixteen RFID-chipped U.S.–Canada NEXUS Trusted Traveler cards** for the sixteen U.S. citizens, right alongside **Sarah and Jessica's two Australian ePassports (Series P)** with their signed Form I-20 endorsements and Canadian eTA clearances, plus the laminated GlobalTrekker Platinum Direct international insurance binder. "Eighteen travelers. Fully credentialed. Eight days until Wheel-Up for the World Trek!"

Around the van, everyone cheered.

Jordan paused at the top of the transit van's deployed ramp on **Washington Street SW**, his right hand resting lightly on the joystick of the three-hundred-and-fifty-pound matte-black *S.S. Freedom*.

He looked back up once at the warm Ironstone and brick facade of **Bennett Hall**—the "Old Lady" on the hill that had sheltered them through their entire freshman year before her final summer.

Up on the second floor, standing by the open casement window of **Room 215**, **Isabel Dawson** raised her hand in a quiet, radiant wave. Jordan smiled and raised his left hand back to her.

Then he looked down at the six women of his Constellation waiting for him at the bottom of the ramp, at Mark and Hannah, and at Daniel and Laura holding open the door of the van that was about to carry all eighteen of them across the borders of the earth.

Nine months ago, he had been the unwanted kid in Virginia Beach—the boy locked in a back bedroom, told he was too broken to ever live a real life.

Today, with a 4.00 freshman year behind him, a passport in his pocket, and a Gauntlet of Hearts standing beside his wheels, **Jordan Fuller—the Eleventh Child—nudged the joystick forward and rolled out to meet the world.**
