## 11:30 AM — The Outback Lift

After a final round of snacks and a highly coordinated trip to the Premier Lounge's accessible restrooms, Daniel Brooks gathered the group. It was time.

As they walked out of the lounge and merged into the bustling concourse heading toward their departure gate, the sheer gravity of the moment finally settled squarely on Jordan’s shoulders. This was it. When the wheels of this mainline jet touched down again, he wasn't just going to be in another state. He was going to be in a completely different country. He was going to Canada.

They reached the gate just as First Class boarding was called. Jordan steered his massive 350-pound power chair down the sloped jet bridge, Aubrie walking faithfully by his side.

At the end of the bridge, parked right next to the aircraft door, a flight attendant was waiting with a standard-issue airport aisle chair. It was a narrow, rigid, heavily strapped contraption designed to wheel non-ambulatory passengers down the tight aircraft aisles.

Jordan took one look at the aisle chair and immediately stopped his power chair. His chest tightened. He remembered the cramped, restrictive, and deeply uncomfortable experience of being strapped into a similar chair on the previous flight from ROA to IAD. He absolutely hated it. He looked at the flight attendant and vehemently shook his head no.

Before Aubrie could even reach for her phone to try and negotiate a solution, a shadow fell over Jordan's chair.

Jessica Gallagher stepped forward from the line of boarding passengers. Jordan had known Jessica for a while now, and she had been smoothly integrating herself into their protective pact, but as she stepped directly in front of him, he truly *looked* at her for the first time.

Standing at an imposing 6'3", Jessica possessed a heavy-muscled, natural bodybuilder physique honed by a lifetime of physical labor. She was a towering, immovable force.

"Don't stress it," Jessica said gently. She leaned down, wrapped her strong arms securely around Jordan's back and beneath his knees, and effortlessly scooped him out of his heavy power chair.

As Jessica stood up straight, holding him securely against her chest, Jordan let out a small gasp. Cradled in her arms, he suddenly realized just how incredibly high off the ground he was. He was practically looking down at the flight attendants.

"You don't like the strapping chair, yeah?" Jessica asked, looking down at him.

Jordan gave a firm, grateful nod.

"No problem, mate!" Jessica smiled, her voice carrying a thick, unmistakable drawl from the deep Australian Outback. "I'll just carry you to your seat myself."

Jessica turned to the stunned flight attendants standing by the aircraft door. "He doesn't like the aisle chair," she stated, her tone radiating the grounded, no-nonsense authority she had developed growing up in the Northern Territory. "I'm carrying him to his seat. And if he needs to be repositioned during the flight, or if he needs to use the lavatory, I'll be carrying him then, too. I'll carry him off the plane when we land in Toronto."

The flight attendants, slightly intimidated by her towering height and sheer physical strength, quickly nodded and stepped aside.

As Jessica carried Jordan smoothly through the First Class cabin, Mark caught up to them in the aisle. He quickly assessed the logistics of the situation.

"Hey, Jessica," Mark called out, tapping her on the shoulder. "Hannah and I are assigned to the seats directly behind Jordan and Aubrie. But if you're on lifting duty for the flight, it makes way more sense for you to be right behind him."

"We can totally swap," Hannah agreed, smiling warmly from the aisle. "You and Sarah can take our seats behind them, and Mark and I will take your row."

"Brilliant," Sarah Braithwaite beamed, sliding into the row behind Aubrie’s assigned aisle seat. "Works perfectly for us."

Jessica carefully lowered Jordan into his wide First Class window seat, ensuring he was comfortable before taking the seat directly behind him, right next to Sarah. The impromptu seat swap meant that the two Australians were now sitting side-by-side—Sarah, with her bold energy from Liverpool, New South Wales, and Jessica, with her unshakeable pragmatism from a remote roadhouse in Petermann, Northern Territory.

Aubrie slid into the seat next to Jordan, immediately reaching out to take his hand. She looked over the back of her seat at Jessica.

"Thank you, Jessica," Aubrie whispered sincerely.

"Anytime," Jessica smiled, settling into her seat. "We take care of our own."

Jordan looked out the window as the massive jet engines began to whine. He didn't feel like a burden. He felt completely supported by a dynamic, fiercely protective family that adapted to his needs in real-time. He squeezed Aubrie's hand, feeling the rumble of the aircraft, absolutely ready to cross the border.

## 12:30 PM — The Price of Freedom

As the mainline jet reached cruising altitude, the First Class cabin settled into a comfortable, luxurious hum. The flight attendants began their meal service, rolling the carts down the wide aisle of the two-by-two cabin configuration.

When the cart reached their rows, the lead flight attendant offered a warm smile, but she didn't bother asking for drink orders. Daniel Brooks, managing the complex logistics of their eighteen-person roster, had already handled it. He had documented directly with the airline well in advance that the entire family—even the adults over twenty-one—would be declining alcohol. The flight attendants simply handed out premium hot meals, bottles of sparkling water, and fresh juices, allowing the group to eat in total peace.

Jordan sat in his window seat, his tray table lowered to hold his meal. Aubrie was sitting right beside him, happily digging into her lunch. Directly behind him, he could hear the quiet, easy conversation between Sarah and Jessica, the two Australians seamlessly blending into the protective perimeter of the pact.

Jordan took a bite of his food, but his attention was entirely captured by the view out the window. The vast, sprawling blue expanse of Lake Ontario was coming into view below them.

They were crossing the border. He was leaving the United States.

A sudden, profound realization settled over him. For eighteen years, his mother, Aubrie Sinclair, had been the absolute dictator of his small, silent universe. She had weaponized his medical vulnerabilities, isolating him and convincing him that he was nothing but a helpless burden.

As the jet hurtled toward a foreign country, Jordan searched his chest for any lingering sense of guilt or sadness about leaving her behind. He found absolutely none. The toxic, suffocating trauma bond had been entirely broken by the radical, unselfish love of the women sitting around him.

He thought back to a quiet, pivotal conversation he had experienced just a few days prior in Christiansburg.

Daniel and Laura Brooks had pulled him aside in their massive home. Daniel had sat down across from him, his deep London accent completely devoid of pity, and laid out a new reality. Because Jordan had graduated first in his high school class—and because his mother's household lived below the Federal Poverty Level on government benefits—CPI had enticed him with a full-ride package that stacked a university academic merit scholarship on top of federal Pell and state need-based grants. Aubrie Sinclair had gladly signed that first freshman FAFSA, since it cost her nothing.

But renewing those federal and state need-based grants for sophomore year would have required filing a new FAFSA—and getting Aubrie Sinclair's signature. Instead, Daniel had calmly explained that while Jordan's hard-earned academic merit scholarship would renew automatically on his Dean's List GPA alone without a FAFSA, Daniel and Laura were paying the remaining balance directly to the CPI Bursar's Office in cash for the rest of his college career.

The gravity of that gift was staggering. It wasn't just about the money; it was about the legal leverage. Because Daniel was covering the entire grant difference directly with the Bursar's Office, Jordan would never again need to file a FAFSA. He would never have to find out whether Aubrie Sinclair would have signed it or held it hostage—because he would never need her signature on a financial aid document again.

The final, bureaucratic chain she held over his life had been permanently severed.

Jordan looked away from the window and glanced around the cabin. He was flying in a First Class seat, surrounded by a massive, eighteen-person chosen family who protected him without hesitation. He had his custom-built Vanguard Pro safely stowed beneath the seat in front of him, giving him a voice that was entirely his own.

He was completely, undeniably free. And the best part of it all was that back in Virginia, Aubrie Sinclair was none the wiser. She had absolutely no idea that her prisoner was gone forever.

"We're starting our descent, J," Aubrie whispered next to him, her dark eyes shining with excitement as she squeezed his hand.

Jordan smiled, his heart soaring lighter than the aircraft itself. Next stop: YYZ.

## 2:00 PM — The Commonwealth Question

When the mainline jet finally arrived at the gate at Toronto Pearson International Airport, the First Class cabin began to empty out. True to her word, Jessica Gallagher stood up from her seat in the third row, her imposing 6'3" frame easily navigating the cabin aisle.

"Alright, mate, let's get you off this plane," Jessica smiled, her thick Outback accent a warm, familiar comfort. She leaned down, wrapped her strong, heavily muscled arms around Jordan, and effortlessly deadlifted him out of his window seat.

She carried him out the aircraft door and onto the jetbridge to wait for the baggage handlers to bring up his massive 350-pound power chair. As they stood there in the cool air of the enclosed bridge, Jordan rested his head against Jessica's shoulder. Just like at the TSA checkpoint in Roanoke, a profound realization washed over him. He wasn't just being carried; he was being held. The secure, unyielding strength of Jessica's grip didn't feel clinical or mechanical. It felt exactly like a massive, protective hug.

He instinctively laced his arms around her neck, leaning into the contact and letting out a soft, contented exhale.

Jessica, whose survival instincts were honed by a lifetime in the deep Petermann bush, was incredibly observant. She felt his posture shift, recognizing the exact moment the transfer turned into an embrace. A bright, genuine smile broke across her face. Completely unbothered by the weight, she gladly leaned into it, wrapping her arms just a fraction tighter around him and rocking him gently side-to-side until the heavy-duty power chair was finally rolled up the ramp.

She carefully lowered him into the seat, making sure he was perfectly comfortable before grabbing the handles of her backpack.

"Welcome to Canada, everyone," Lauren beamed, her brown eyes shining as the group of eighteen funneled out of the jetbridge and into the sprawling, glass-lined international arrivals hall of YYZ.

As they approached the massive Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) checkpoint, the logistical reality of their passports came into play.

"Okay, this is where we split for a bit," Daniel Brooks announced. He pointed toward a bank of automated machines. "Everyone with a Nexus card, follow Lauren and me. Sarah, Jessica, you two head for the international ePassport gates over there."

"Got it. We'll see you on the other side," Sarah Braithwaite said, flashing a quick smile before she and Jessica peeled off toward the foreign national queues.

As Jordan rolled his chair toward the Nexus lane alongside Aubrie, he pulled out his mPhone. His logical, systems-based brain was already processing the geopolitical data. He typed a quick question and angled the screen toward Lauren, who was walking just ahead of him holding her dark blue Canadian passport.

<aac>Canada and Australia are both Commonwealth nations with the same monarch,</aac> Jordan typed. <aac>Do Australians not get an expedited customs track here? Why do they have to wait in the long line?</aac>

Lauren smiled, looking down at his screen. "That is an incredibly smart question, J. They are both in the Commonwealth, and that actually does give them a perk—they get to use the electronic ePassport gates instead of talking to an agent manually, which saves them a ton of time. But Nexus is a very specific bilateral security treaty strictly between the United States and Canada. Commonwealth status doesn't grant you Nexus clearance."

Jordan nodded, filing the logistical data away as they approached the front of the Nexus line.

They stepped up to the CBSA podium after scanning their cards at the kiosks. Daniel expertly placed the stack of blue United States passports on the desk for the Brooks family, ensuring they legally matched their Nexus profiles instead of causing a system error with their British passports. Lauren stepped up to the front, placing her Canadian passport and Nexus card on top of the pile.

The uniformed border agent looked at Lauren's passport and offered the standard, federally mandated bilingual greeting required at all Canadian ports of entry.

*"Bonjour. Hello."*

Lauren didn't miss a single beat. Having spent significant time in Québec, she possessed a fierce fluency that went far beyond high school language classes. She immediately answered the agent in rapid, flawless, colloquial Québécois French.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Hello! We are coming home for a visit, and these are my friends from the United States,">Bonjour! Nous rentrons au pays pour une visite, et voici mes amis des États-Unis,</lang>" Lauren replied smoothly, her voice bright and polite as she gestured to the massive American contingent standing behind her.

The CBSA agent paused, his eyebrows shooting up in genuine surprise. Hearing an authentic Québécois accent in the middle of an Anglophone Toronto terminal was rare enough, but hearing it from a girl whose passport listed her birthplace as Gander, Newfoundland—a province famous for its radically distinct English dialect—was a massive, delightful curveball.

The agent's formal, bureaucratic posture completely melted into a warm, disarmed smile. He quickly stamped the necessary paperwork. *"Bienvenue chez vous. Welcome home."*

As they rolled past the podium and officially cleared customs, Lauren looked down at Jordan's stunned expression and laughed.

"I told him we are coming home for a visit, and that you guys are my friends from the States," Lauren translated for the group, her face glowing with excitement. "Come on. Let's head up to the Northern Crown Lounge in the domestic departure terminal. We've got a lounge to raid while we wait for the Aussies to clear the international gates!"

Surrounded by his chosen family and officially on foreign soil for the very first time in his life, Jordan grabbed his joystick and eagerly followed her lead.

## 2:45 PM — The ePassport Bottleneck

The Maple Leaf Air Northern Crown Lounge in the domestic terminal was a quiet, sprawling sanctuary of polished wood and comfortable armchairs. The sixteen members of the Nexus group had easily secured a large corner section, indulging in the complimentary buffet and waiting for the final two members of their party.

Jordan sat in his heavy-duty power chair by the window, his Vanguard Pro resting securely on his tray table. He tapped his fingers rhythmically against the edge of the plastic, a subtle self-soothing stim. He was watching the clock on his dark-themed NeoEdit terminal. It had been nearly forty-five minutes since they had separated at the customs hall.

His highly structured, logical brain did not like unexpected delays. The longer they waited, the more a low, buzzing annoyance began to build in the back of his mind.

Finally, the frosted glass doors of the lounge slid open. Sarah Braithwaite and Jessica Gallagher walked in, both looking distinctly exasperated.

"There they are!" Madison called out, waving them over to the corner.

Jessica dropped her heavy backpack onto an empty chair with a weary sigh, her towering 6'3" frame sinking into the seat next to it. Sarah, radiating her usual bold, unapologetic energy, grabbed a bottle of water from the cooler and collapsed into the chair next to Lauren.

"Sorry we took so long, mates," Sarah groaned, twisting the cap off her water. "The automated ePassport gates were an absolute disaster."

Jordan frowned, his fingers moving quickly over his keyboard. <aac>Did the system reject your Australian passports?</aac> the bright green text read on his angled screen.

"No, our passports were fine," Jessica grumbled, her thick Outback drawl thick with annoyance. "It was the bloke standing directly in front of us in the queue."

"It was infuriating," Sarah elaborated, leaning forward. "This guy had absolutely no business being in the ePassport lane. He didn't have a chipped passport, he couldn't get the machine to scan his documents, and instead of just stepping aside, he tried to force it. He ended up completely locking down the kiosk and triggering a system error for our entire row."

"We were stuck standing there for twenty minutes just waiting for a CBSA agent to come override the machine," Jessica added, crossing her muscular arms. "They ended up taking the guy out of the line and marching him through a set of security doors, probably for additional questioning. But by the time they reset our gate, the queue was a mile long."

Jordan stared at the screen of his terminal. His initial annoyance at the delay flared up again, completely directed at the sheer inefficiency and entitlement of the stranger who had ignored the rules and disrupted the system. But as he looked at Sarah and Jessica—who had sprinted through the terminal just to get back to the family—the irritation vanished.

<aac>I am annoyed that people refuse to read instructional signage,</aac> Jordan typed, offering the two Australian women a warm, reassuring smile. <aac>But I am glad you are both okay, and I am glad you are back.</aac>

"Thanks, J," Sarah smiled, her frustration melting away instantly. "We wouldn't miss this flight for the world."

Daniel Brooks checked his watch and stood up, clapping his hands together once.

"Well, the timing is actually perfect," Daniel announced, looking around the lounge. "Our departure gate for Gander is just down the concourse, and First Class boarding begins in ten minutes. Let's gather our things."

The group sprang into action, grabbing their carry-on bags and finishing their drinks. Aubrie took her familiar place at Jordan's right side, while Lauren stepped up to his left.

As Jordan steered his power chair out of the lounge and into the bustling domestic terminal, Lauren gently rested her hand on his shoulder.

"This is it, J," Lauren said softly, her brown eyes shining with an intense, fierce pride. "Our next stop is YQX. My hometown."

Jordan looked up at her, feeling a profound wave of anticipation. He had spent his entire life trapped in a world where strangers were a threat, and the unknown was something to be feared. But he remembered the stories she had told him in the van that morning—about the thirty-eight grounded airplanes, the stranded passengers, and the town that had opened its doors without a second thought.

He wasn't flying into a hostile void anymore. He was flying directly into the heart of Operation Yellow Ribbon.

Lauren smiled, interlacing her fingers with his as they approached the gate. It was time to finally show Jordan what true hospitality in the face of the unknown was all about.
