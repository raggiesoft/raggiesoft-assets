## June 8, 2015 • 3:00 PM — Carnaby Street and the Hollow Square

From Piccadilly, Jordan guided the family aboard a low-floor red double-decker bus up the sweeping curve of Regent Street, disembarking via the hydraulic ramp at Beak Street and rolling east into the heart of **Soho**.

One block in, the roar of London traffic vanished behind them as they passed beneath a famous wrought-iron welcome arch spanning a vibrant, completely pedestrianized thoroughfare paved in level stone flags:

**`WELCOME TO CARNABY STREET`**

For more than half a century, this narrow fourteen-street enclave in the City of Westminster had been the beating heart of British music and fashion culture. Independent record shops, Mod tailors, bespoke guitar luthiers, and brightly painted three- and four-story Georgian and Victorian shopfronts lined both sides of the car-free walkway.

Halfway up the street, near the corner of Foubert's Place, Daniel and Laura Brooks slowed to a stop outside a narrow four-story brick building. On the ground floor sat a classic Soho vinyl record store with wooden crate bins set out beneath its awning; above the shop rose three stories of residential flats with tall white sash windows.

"Look up at the third-floor window on the left, Jordan," Daniel said quietly, slipping his arm around Laura's waist as Zoë stepped up beside Jordan's wheelchair. "That was our flat before my firm transferred us to Osaka and then Virginia. That's the exact window where Zoë learned to read systems architecture manuals at three years old—and even before we moved into that flat, when Laura and I were first dating in London in the early eighties, we spent every Saturday afternoon crate-digging in this record shop right beneath us."

"And where my processor first determined that Soho street volume without double-glazed windows is a structural design flaw," Zoë added dryly, though her gray eyes were warm with childhood affection as she looked up at the brickwork.

Right at that moment, inside the ground-floor vinyl shop beneath their old flat, the clerk dropped the needle onto a newly remastered 180-gram LP spinning on the front turntable.

Pouring out through the open shop doors into the warm afternoon air of Carnaby Street came a brooding, solitary drum-machine pulse, quickly joined by a deep, resonant analog synth bassline—the unmistakable opening bars of [**"The Hollow Square"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/fractured-prisms/1983-carnaby-street?track=1-01-the-hollow-square), the lead track from **[Fractured Prisms](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/fractured-prisms)**' landmark 1983 album, [***Carnaby Street***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/fractured-prisms/1983-carnaby-street).

Jordan's hands stilled over his keyboard as Rhys Manning's voice rose over the street:

> *"Four empty spaces in the room*<br>
> *A silent echo in the gloom*<br>
> *We crossed the water to find a shore*<br>
> *But left behind what came before..."*

"You know why Rhys and Claire Manning named that album after this street, don't you, J?" Daniel asked softly as the massive gated snare drum kicked in on the chorus.

Jordan tilted his head, looking from the spinning vinyl turntable inside the shop window back up to Daniel and Laura.

"Back in the winter of 1981," Daniel explained, his voice low and respectful beneath the soaring progressive-pop synthesizers, "before they were called **Fractured Prisms**, there were six of them—siblings and cousins in the **Manning Family Orchestra** from Romford. Before the four touring members—Gwen, Alistair, Julian, and Simon—drove north up the M6 for a winter show across the Scottish border, all six of them spent their last afternoon in London right here in this exact record shop on **Carnaby Street**, laughing and digging through the import crates together."

Laura Brooks reached out, touching the wooden frame of the record shop doorway with a quiet, wistful smile.

"Daniel and I were actually inside this shop that afternoon," Laura said softly, making Jordan, Aubrie, and Madison turn to look at her in astonishment. "We were nineteen years old, sheltering from the December drizzle and flipping through the progressive-rock bins in the back corner. There were six young musicians in knit scarves crowded around the import counter debating synthesizer patches and concert flute arrangements. I remember a quiet, observant young woman sitting in her manual wheelchair right next to me by the seven-inch singles rack—covering her ears slightly when the shop speakers crackled—while her younger brother stood right beside her chair like a bodyguard. Because of her wheelchair, she couldn't quite reach a sleeve on the upper rack, so I handed it down to her. She gave me the shyest, most polite little smile, and her brother thanked me in a light Romford accent."

Laura's eyes glistened as she looked back inside the warm, record-lined room.

"We had no idea who they were at the time," Laura whispered. "Not until October 1983, when ***Carnaby Street*** came out and we saw the photograph of **Rhys and Claire Manning** and read the liner notes dedicating the album to that final afternoon in Soho. We had stood two feet away from all six of them on the very last day their family was whole."

Beside Jordan, Aubrie and Lauren listened silently as the second verse echoed off the Soho brick:

> *"The cables hum, the circuits glow*<br>
> *To mask the quiet down below*<br>
> *We sequence notes to fill the void*<br>
> *A symphony we re-employed*<br>
> *Just two remain to hold the line*<br>
> *And reconstruct the grand design..."*

"Twenty-four hours after we saw them in this shop," Daniel continued quietly, "Gwen, Alistair, Julian, and Simon hit a blinding white-out of freezing fog and black ice on the M6 over Shap Fell in Cumbria. All four of them were killed instantly. The only reason **Claire** and **Rhys** survived was because they were hundreds of miles south, safely at home in Romford. Claire has lived with Cerebral Palsy and needed a wheelchair since birth, and she is autistic—so the grueling, unpredictable winter tour up the M6 wasn't compatible with her needs. And where Claire stayed, Rhys stayed."

Jordan's breath caught in his chest. He had heard *"The Hollow Square"* on the radio before, but he had never realized that **Claire Manning** had lived with Cerebral Palsy from birth, used a wheelchair her entire life, and was autistic—just like him—or that her wheelchair and sensory needs were the exact reason she and her brother were still alive.

"Everyone in the British press thought the music died on Shap Fell," Zoë picked up softly, resting her hand on the cool magnesium-alloy edge of Jordan's **Pinnacle Vanguard Pro**. "Instead, after Claire left a terrible marriage in Romford in 1982, she and Rhys packed the surviving instruments of their deceased family and fled across the Atlantic to their father's old ranch house in Williamsport, Maryland. Rhys refused to hire session musicians to stand in the four empty spaces of their family—the *Hollow Square*. Instead, he filled the house with analog synthesizers, sequencers, and drum machines to engineer a predictable, accessible sanctuary where Claire's mind could safely operate, while Claire taught herself to play Gwen's wooden flute from her wheelchair. And when they released that debut album in 1983, they named it ***Carnaby Street***—after the last place in London where all six of them had been together."

As the soaring melodic guitar solo broke over the driving drum-machine beat and built into the final chorus—

> *"Standing in the hollow square!*<br>
> *Reaching out, but no one's there!*<br>
> *We forge the armour, paint the sky*<br>
> *To keep the fading embers high!"*

—Jordan looked down at his own glowing `NeoEdit` terminal, mounted to the frame of his power chair on the stones of Carnaby Street.

*The cables hum, the circuits glow.*

Just like Rhys and Claire Manning had used cables, circuits, and sequenced notes to build a fortress against trauma when the world tried to break them, Zoë had handed Jordan an Aura Glade laptop and a custom kernel patch in Bennett Hall so he could build his voice—and six extraordinary women, a brother like Mark, a sister like Hannah, and the entire Brooks family had closed ranks around his wheelchair so his square would never be hollow again.

Jordan typed slowly, letting his `"Sydney"` voice blend softly with the fading synth bass of the outro:

<aac>They used circuits and cables to build a sanctuary across the ocean,</aac> Jordan said quietly, looking up at Zoë, Aubrie, and Daniel. <aac>Just like you did for me.</aac>

## June 9, 2015 • 1:15 PM — Houghton Street and the Transatlantic Reunion Call

At twelve-fifty on Tuesday afternoon, Jordan steered the group away from the stairs-only entrance of **Holborn Underground Station** and guided them aboard the low-floor red **Route 91** bus down **Kingsway**, stepping off via the hydraulic ramp at **Aldwych** just two hundred yards from the **London School of Economics and Commerce (`LSEC`)**.

Unlike the sprawling, Neo-Gothic **Ironstone** academic buildings ringing **Commonwealth Green** at **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute** back in Blacksburg, `LSEC` was a dense, vertical urban campus woven directly into the historic streets and legal inns of court between Kingsway and the Royal Courts of Justice. Even so, the university had fitted its pedestrianized streets and modern academic buildings with flush dropped curbs, automatic glass doors, and wide platform lifts.

As Jordan rolled onto the smooth paving stones of **Houghton Street** outside the **Old Building**, the heavy double doors swung open and a wave of international university students poured out from their final summer-term seminar—led by **Henry Walsh** with his leather messenger bag slung over his shoulder.

"Right on time, Chief Navigator!" Henry grinned, spotting Jordan's power chair immediately and walking over to bump fists with Jordan and Mark. "Did you guys take the red double-decker bus down Kingsway?"

<aac>Route 91 to Stop D at Aldwych,</aac> Jordan typed proudly. <aac>Zero stairs, zero escalators, and a four-minute roll down Houghton Street.</aac>

"Wait until we get back to Blacksburg in August," Henry laughed, shaking his head as he led Jordan, Aubrie, Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Sarah, Jessica, Hannah, and Mark over to a wide, accessible timber picnic table in the sunlit **Shaw Library / Student Union courtyard** while Daniel and the older Brooks siblings grabbed takeaway teas and sandwiches from the campus café. "Between Blue Ridge Transit, Reykjavik, and the London bus grid, you're going to know more about public transit routing than half the Civil Engineering faculty at CPI."

Sitting across from Jordan at the courtyard table, Henry leaned forward on his elbows, his expression turning warm and earnest.

"Seriously, though, Jordan—look at you," Henry said quietly, taking in the clean-shaven jawline Mark had helped Jordan maintain since Fall semester, the effortless way Jordan sat upright in his power chair, and the two international transit smart cards gleaming in the plastic sleeve clipped to his laptop mount. "I still remember walking the second-floor corridor of Bennett Hall on Move-In Saturday back in August when Zoë stepped out of Room 214, tapped my shoulder, and told me we had a 'significant logistical error' because Housing had put a guy in a female suite. Back then, you were so terrified someone was going to yell at you that you wouldn't even look up from your lap. And now you're navigating Central London with a crew of eighteen people."

<aac>You and Isabel and Kelsey didn't kick me out,</aac> Jordan typed softly, his fingers steady on the keys of his **Pinnacle Vanguard Pro**. <aac>If you had separated us on that Saturday morning, I never would have made it to today. Where did Housing reassign you for senior year now that Bennett Hall is closed for good and slated for demolition?</aac>

"They moved me and **Isabel** right over to **Allegheny Commons** for senior year!" Henry smiled, a mischievous glint entering his eyes as he leaned in a little closer. "It's still wild to me that the Old Lady actually closed her doors for good in May while I was over here in London, and that she's slated for the wrecking ball. With **Garrett** graduating in May and Bennett Hall shutting down, **Kelsey** got reassigned to oversee the **Allegheny Commons Apartments**—and I actually just got Kelsey's finalized staff placement email this morning. Not only am I in Allegheny Commons, she assigned me as the Male Senior RA in **Apartment 115**, on the *exact same floor* as Isabel in **Apartment 101**! And Isabel doesn't even know yet!"

"Wait—you haven't told Isabel you're her floor co-RA again?!" Madison gasped, her eyes lighting up with instant delight.

"The email only hit my inbox two hours ago!" Henry laughed. "I was saving it—though since you guys are moving into your off-campus sanctuary house on Geneva Street in Christiansburg for sophomore year, our floor is still going to be a whole lot quieter without Suite 214/216."

"Speaking of Isabel," Aubrie said with a bright, mischievous smile, glancing at her watch. "It's one-forty-five in the afternoon here in London—which means it's **`8:45 AM`** on Tuesday morning in Charlottesville. We promised her a video call from `LSEC`—and if Henry ratted her out for the Heathrow ambush, it's only fair that we break the Allegheny Commons news to her right now!"

"Do it!" Henry grinned, sliding his bench seat right up beside Jordan's right armrest while Aubrie leaned in on Jordan's left and Madison, Zoë, Lauren, Sarah, Jessica, Hannah, and Mark crowded into the wide-angle frame behind them.

Jordan unlocked his `mPad`, angled it on the secondary mount so both his face and Henry's were squarely centered in front of the `LSEC` red-brick crest on the courtyard wall, and tapped **Isabel Dawson**'s contact.

The call connected on the second ring.

In Charlottesville, Virginia, Isabel was sitting at her parents' sunny kitchen table in a soft maroon CPI t-shirt, holding a ceramic mug of morning coffee with her dark-framed glasses perched on her nose.

For half a second, she saw Jordan and Aubrie—and then her eyes registered **Henry Walsh** grinning right next to Jordan's wheelchair in the middle of London.

*"HENRY!!"* Isabel squealed so loudly she nearly sloshed her coffee across the kitchen table, clapping her free hand over her mouth as her face lit up like a sunrise. *"Oh my gosh! Henry! Jordan! You're actually together at LSEC right now!!"*

"Good morning from Houghton Street, Dawson!" Henry called out cheerfully, waving both hands at the screen. "Look who navigated the London bus grid all the way to Holborn without hitting a single flight of stairs!"

*"I knew he would!"* Isabel beamed, her warm brown eyes locking onto Jordan through the camera lens with unmistakable pride. *"Jordan, how is London treating you? Did you get your second transit card?"*

Jordan held up his clear plastic sleeve so the sky-blue **`Pearl Card`** gleamed right below his yellow-and-blue **`NordicPass`**.

<aac>Smart Card Number Two: London Transport Authority Pearl Card,</aac> Jordan announced via his `"Sydney"` voice. <aac>And I also found out who leaked our Sunday flight itinerary from Reykjavik so Henry could ambush us at the Heathrow Terminal 2 customs barrier.</aac>

Beside Jordan, Henry burst out laughing, pointing straight at the camera. "Oh, I ratted you out immediately, Izzy! You should have seen Jordan's face when he rolled out of the Green Channel at Heathrow and saw my sign!"

On the `mPad` screen, Isabel's cheeks turned the exact shade of a ripe tomato. She set her coffee mug down, tucked a strand of straight jet-black hair behind her ear, and tried—and completely failed—to look innocent.

*"I—well, Henry is our friend and co-RA, and I thought it would be a nice welcome to England!"* Isabel stammered, her fingers fluttering up to her collarbone as she blushed from ear to ear. *"Besides, since I couldn't be there in London myself, somebody had to make sure the old Bennett Hall Second Floor crew was represented at the airport!"*

Beside Jordan, Aubrie exchanged a tiny, fond, knowing glance with Madison and Lauren, her heart warming at just how hopelessly transparent their former RA was—even if Jordan himself simply smiled back at the screen, completely touched by her thoughtfulness.

<aac>It was the best surprise I have ever had at an airport, Isabel,</aac> Jordan said through his speaker, making Isabel's smile soften into pure, glowing affection. Then a playful grin tugged at the corner of Jordan's mouth as he glanced at Aubrie and Madison and typed one more sentence: <aac>Also, since Henry ratted you out for Heathrow, we thought you should know that Kelsey assigned Henry to Apartment 115 at Allegheny Commons. He is the Male RA on the exact same floor as you again this year.</aac>

On the `mPad` screen, Isabel's jaw dropped open.

*"WAIT—WHAT?!"* Isabel gasped, her eyes darting from Jordan to Henry. *"You're on the FIRST FLOOR of Allegheny Commons with me?!"*

"Surprise, Room 101!" Henry grinned shamelessly, giving her a two-finger salute. "Just got Kelsey's placement email this morning! Looks like you're stuck with me as your floor co-RA for Senior Year too!"

Isabel narrowed her eyes behind her glasses, folding her arms across her maroon CPI shirt as she put on her sternest, most theatrical mock-glare—even though the corners of her mouth were twitching so hard she could barely keep from laughing.

*"Henry Thomas Walsh! You knew for two whole hours that you were on my floor at Allegheny Commons and you didn't even text me?!"* Isabel huffed, pretending to be deeply offended while her eyes sparkled with delight. *"I had to find out from Jordan and the girls across the Atlantic Ocean?! Unbelievable! You are officially on first-week duty roster probation in August!"*

"Hey, consider us completely even for the Heathrow ambush, Dawson!" Henry laughed as the entire courtyard table erupted into cheers.

<aac>On Thursday morning we are taking the high-speed tilting train from London Euston up to Liverpool,</aac> Jordan typed happily once the laughter settled, <aac>and on Saturday we fly to Tokyo.</aac>

*"Hold onto Henry for one more big hug from me before you leave London, okay—even if he IS in the doghouse for not texting me!"* Isabel said warmly, her pretend scowl melting right back into a radiant smile as she waved with both hands. *"Have the best time in Liverpool, guys, and text me the second you touch down in Japan!"*

## June 9, 2015 • 4:00 PM — The Thames Riverbus, the Millennium Wheel, and Babbage's Difference Engine

After wrapping up their transatlantic video call with **Isabel** from the courtyard of the **London School of Economics and Commerce (`LSEC`)**, **Henry Walsh** slung his canvas bookbag over his shoulder and officially clocked in as their afternoon London companion.

"My seminar readings are done until tomorrow morning," Henry grinned, walking alongside **Mark** and **Jordan** as they rolled south down Savoy Street toward the **Victoria Embankment**. "And since the Chief Navigator already mastered the red double-decker buses yesterday, what do your **`LTA`** maps say about taking the river?"

Jordan's eyes lit up behind his glasses. Pulling up the **London River Services** diagram on his **Pinnacle Vanguard Pro**, he guided the nineteen-person group onto the floating pontoon of **Embankment Pier**.

Tapping their sky-blue **`Pearl Cards`** at the pier reader, they boarded a sleek, high-speed **Thames Riverbus Clipper** catamaran. Unlike the nineteenth-century Underground tunnels beneath the city, every pier and twin-hulled vessel on the Thames Riverbus grid was 100% step-free: the deck crew laid a wide aluminum brow ramp across the gunwale, and Jordan rolled smoothly into the panoramic forward cabin as the boat surged out onto the tidal **River Thames**.

Five minutes later, the catamaran glided across the water to **Waterloo Pier** directly beneath the towering, 443-foot cantilevered white steel rim of **The Thames Millennium Wheel**.

As Jordan rolled up the wide stone riverwalk toward the boarding platform, he tipped his head back and stared up at the thirty-two sealed, ovoid glass passenger capsules rotating slowly above the Thames—and then looked directly across the river at the golden Gothic clock face of **The Great Westminster Clock Tower** (*`St. Stephen's Bell`*).

A sudden, vivid memory flashed across Jordan's mind.

<aac>Wait a second,</aac> Jordan typed into `NeoEdit`, pausing his chair beside **Aubrie**, **Madison**, **Daniel**, and **Laura**. <aac>Five months ago on New Year's Eve in your living room on Geneva Street, this exact wheel and that clock tower across the river were on the television screen when the fireworks launched at midnight—the night I learned what fireworks were.</aac>

Laura Brooks smiled with deep maternal warmth, resting her hand gently on Jordan's shoulder. "And on December 31st, you were watching it on a television screen from four thousand miles away, love. Today you're going right up to the top of it."

When Capsule 14 glided through the boarding deck, the station attendants slowed the wheel's rotation and deployed a flush metal bridge plate. Jordan drove his 350-pound power chair straight into the center of the spacious, air-conditioned glass pod alongside **Henry**, **Daniel**, **Laura**, **Aubrie**, **Zoë**, **Madison**, **Lauren**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, **Hannah**, **Mark**, and the **Brooks** siblings.

Over the next thirty minutes, as their glass capsule rose four hundred and forty-three feet into the clear June afternoon sky, the entire vast geometry of **London** unfolded in a 360-degree panorama below Jordan's wheels: the gleaming meanders of the Thames, the white dome of St. Paul's Cathedral to the east, the green royal parks stretching past **St. James's Sovereign Palace** to the west, and the intricate rooftop courtyards of **Westminster Parliament Palace** directly below.

From the South Bank, Jordan routed the group aboard a low-floor red **Route 148** and **Route 70** bus link west to Exhibition Road in **South Kensington**, rolling through the step-free glass atrium of **The South Kensington Museum of Science & Computing**.

While Caleb, Lucas, and Owen headed downstairs to inspect the Apollo 10 command module and the historic *Stephenson's Rocket* steam locomotive, **Zoë** and **Daniel** led **Jordan**, **Aubrie**, **Madison**, and **Henry** up the wide passenger lift to the third-floor ***Information Age & Computing Gallery***.

Standing inside a massive glass display case at the center of the hall was a gleaming, five-ton marvel of bronze gears, steel shafts, and precision-milled figure wheels: **Charles Babbage's *Difference Engine No. 2***, built to Babbage's original `1847` mechanical blueprints—accurate to thirty-one digits of polynomial calculation and sitting directly across the gallery from **Alan Turing's `1950` *Pilot ACE*** early electronic stored-program computer.

For fifteen minutes, Jordan and Zoë were completely transfixed.

"Look at the carry-lever tolerances on the bronze figure wheels, J," Zoë murmured in hushed reverence, pointing to the mechanical carry mechanism and the analytical notes written by **Ada Lovelace** displayed in the adjacent case. "In 1843, before electricity or vacuum tubes even existed, Ada Lovelace looked at Babbage's mechanical looms and realized that if you could encode mathematical rules into physical state transitions, a machine could compose music, process symbolic logic, and execute deterministic algorithms without human error."

Jordan looked from the nineteenth-century bronze gears of Babbage's engine down to the glowing, neon-green **`NeoEdit`** terminal running on his **Pinnacle Vanguard Pro**—the modern descendant of those exact bronze shafts and vacuum tubes, giving a non-verbal nineteen-year-old from Virginia a clear, unbreakable voice across the globe.

<aac>Every person in this gallery built a machine to think and speak in new ways,</aac> Jordan typed softly as Aubrie squeezed his hand. <aac>I feel like I am looking at my own family tree.</aac>
