## 4:15 PM — Two Different Branches on College Avenue

Friday afternoon, February 12, 2016 was crisp and cold across the 2,100-foot plateau of Blacksburg. A winter storm two days earlier had blanketed the New River Valley in four inches of snow; by Friday afternoon, the brick walkways and asphalt lanes were dry and salted, while patches of mostly melted white snow still clung to the shaded grass along **College Avenue** and **Commonwealth Mall**.

With their Friday afternoon classes finished for the week, **Jordan** guided his **power wheelchair** (*S.S. Freedom*) smoothly along the wide brick sidewalk alongside **Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Sarah**, **Jessica Gallagher**, **Mark**, **Hannah**, and **Isabel**, heading past **The Hotel at Poly** and **Kincaid Student Center** (`290 College Ave.`) toward the curbside bus lineup on **Commonwealth Mall** to catch their bus back to the **Christiansburg Transit Center** and `414 Geneva Street`.

As they passed the wide brick circular drive of **The Hotel at Poly**—the university conference hotel directly adjacent to campus—the sidewalk was suddenly buzzing with fifteen-passenger university vans, rolling suitcases, painted fabric banners, and dozens of bundled-up college students wearing lanyards and colorful winter parkas loaded with painted wooden clothespins.

Across the street at the entrance to **Kincaid Student Center**, a massive maroon-and-orange welcome banner billowed between two stone columns:

> **WELCOME TO COMMONWEALTH POLYTECHNIC INSTITUTE!**<br>
> **SAACURC 2016 REGIONAL STUDENT LEADERSHIP CONFERENCE**<br>
> ***South Atlantic Affiliate of College and University Residence Halls***<br>
> **February 12–14, 2016 • Kincaid Student Center (`290 College Ave.`)**

"Whoa, wait a second," **Madison** laughed, glancing from the banner outside Kincaid Student Center back toward the vans unloading at **The Hotel at Poly**. "Didn't we already see those decorated conference clothespins last spring when Isabel, Kelsey, and Garrett went up to **Shenandoah Valley University** in **Harrisonburg** for `CACUHO`? Why isn't Isabel working the check-in desk today?"

"Because `CACUHO` and `SAACURC` are two completely different branches of the housing world, run by two completely separate entities—and they don't intermingle at all," **Isabel** explained with a warm smile, adjusting her maroon scarf against the February breeze. "`CACUHO`—the *Commonwealth Association of College and University Housing Officers* that **Shenandoah Valley University** hosted up in **Harrisonburg** last February—is the professional Housing and Residence Life organization for university housing staff and hired RAs. **`SAACURC`**, on the other hand, is strictly the student-governed **Residence Hall Association (`RHA`)** branch, where **every single RHA position is elected by on-campus residents**."

"Affirmative," **Zoë** added, her eyes scanning the arrival logistics outside **The Hotel at Poly**. "Unlike RA staff who are employed by the university, `RHA` executive officers and hall council representatives are democratically elected by the students living in the dorms. The visiting delegations from across the region arrive tonight—Friday night—to check into **The Hotel at Poly**, unless their campus is close enough for a short daily commute like **Radford University** fifteen minutes away. Then the conference sessions run all day Saturday and Sunday morning inside Kincaid Student Center, wrapping up Sunday afternoon with a closing lunch and awards ceremony—where, per the universal conference conflict-of-interest ground rule, the host school (`CPI`) is never eligible to win any awards—before the delegations drive back to their home campuses."

<aac>"And since we all live off campus at 414 Geneva Street in Christiansburg now,"</aac> Jordan tapped on his `mPad` with an amused grin as he steered *S.S. Freedom* around a cluster of suitcases on the sidewalk, <aac>"none of us are on-campus residents eligible for RHA or SAACURC. We are officially off-campus commuters heading home for Friday night dinner."</aac>

"And thank goodness for that," **Mark** chuckled, wrapping an arm around **Hannah**’s shoulders. "Hearing about those thirty-five-foot Gamble Aria shuttle buses in Harrisonburg last spring was plenty of conference excitement for me!"

## 4:25 PM — Passing Ships at the Commonwealth Mall Curb

As the ten of them rounded the corner from **The Hotel at Poly** onto the wide sidewalk of **Commonwealth Mall** beneath the limestone arch of **The Keystone Bridge** across from **Kincaid Hall**, another visiting delegation was crossing in passing from the hotel's accessible ground-floor wing toward Kincaid Student Center to pick up their Friday evening registration packets.

Even in the sea of collegiate colors along the sidewalk, their winter gear stood out immediately: deep, rich **`Chesapeake Teal`** (`#005F6A`) and bright **`Harbor Gold`** (`#D4AF37`), embroidered with the distinctive emblem of an eighteenth-century Chesapeake naval Admiral and a golden fouled anchor—the delegation from **Virginia Dominion University (`VDU`)** in Norfolk, accompanied by their **VDU RHA Advisor** and four student delegates carrying a rolled `VDU ADMIRALS RHA` banner.

At the center of the VDU group rolled a young man of about twenty-one—the exact same `5'7"` frame as the young woman walking right beside him if he were standing—with short dark brown hair and rectangular glasses, bundled up in a heavy `Chesapeake Teal` and `Harbor Gold` **VDU Admirals** winter parka over an embroidered **`VDU RHA • DIRECTOR OF OPERATIONS`** badge. He was propelling a blue-upholstered manual wheelchair with the careful, deliberate upper-body cadence of someone who was not normally a wheelchair user, but was temporarily using the chair while recovering from a planned surgery. Walking right beside his right wheel in effortless synchronization was a young woman of the exact same `5'7"` height with warm brown hair and a matching `Chesapeake Teal` winter coat and `Harbor Gold` scarf over her **`VDU RHA • SECRETARY`** sweater, dozens of painted wooden clothespins clipped along the strap of her messenger bag.

Just as both groups passed the **Commonwealth Mall** crosswalk—where there were **no dedicated bus bays**, and buses from every transit agency in the New River Valley simply pulled up and lined up one behind the other along the curb—two buses rumbled into view beneath **The Keystone Bridge**.

Pulling up first at the front of the curb line was a thirty-foot low-floor **Radford Transit** bus (`Unit 104`)—operating the hourly ***Radford Connector*** route that exits CPI from **Commonwealth Mall**, turns right onto **Stanger Street**, left onto **Prices Fork Road**, right onto **Peppers Ferry Road**, left onto **U.S. Route 11**, crosses the **New River Bridge** into **Radford**, and turns left onto **West Main Street** to terminate at **McConnell Transit Center** (providing once-hourly local service for **Prices Fork**, **Belview**, **Centerville**, and **Fairlawn**, where rural stop spacing lets it move like an express bus). Its amber LED destination sign cycled between:

```
RADFORD CONNECTOR
```

and:

```
TO MCCONNELL TRANSIT CTR via PRICES FORK & FAIRLAWN
```

And gliding smoothly into line right behind the thirty-foot *Radford Connector* came a sixty-foot low-floor articulated **Blue Ridge Transit (`BRT`)** coach manufactured by **Prairie Coachworks**, painted in crisp white, slate-blue, and green ***"Mountain Mist & River Ribbon"*** livery, its amber Luminator headsign reading:

```
1 CBURG TRANS CTR
```

```
via BUSINESS 460 & PEPPER PLAZA
```

As the sixty-foot *Prairie Coachworks* articulated coach kneeled its front air suspension and deployed its flip-out wheelchair ramp onto the **Commonwealth Mall** sidewalk with a smooth pneumatic hiss, both Jordan (in his power chair, *S.S. Freedom*) and the visiting VDU student in the blue manual wheelchair happened to glance toward the two transit buses at the exact same second.

Neither of them knew who the other was—just a CPI student in a power chair catching his Friday afternoon bus home to Christiansburg with his family, and a visiting VDU RHA delegate in a temporary post-surgical manual chair arriving with his sister for `SAACURC`—but as their eyes briefly met across the salted brick sidewalk, they exchanged a warm, knowing nod and a friendly wave in passing.
