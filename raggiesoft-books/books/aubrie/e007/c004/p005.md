## 9:50 PM — *Our Own Frequency* & *Brother's Keeper*

When the stage lights dimmed after *"Twin Moons: Touchdown"*, **Jordan** watched something unexpected happen out on the stage deck.

**Evan Wright** unstrapped his bass, **Tyler Wright** stepped down from the drum riser, and the twin cousins—along with all five members of the touring section (**Marcus Vance**, **Darius "Doc" Holloway**, **Claire Lindqvist**, **Julian Vance**, and **Elena Rostova**)—walked over to center stage, gave **Ryan** and **Cassidy** a long, warm hug, and walked off into the wings.

Across the massive stadium stage, every towering amplifier stack and orbital truss light went dark—leaving only a single, warm golden spotlight shining down at the front lip of center stage.

**Cassidy O'Connell** slid over to the right half of her gleaming black grand-piano bench, and **Ryan O'Connell** rolled his manual wheelchair flush against the left end of the bench and locked his wheel brakes—sitting hip-to-hip and shoulder-to-shoulder with his sister, a sunburst acoustic guitar resting across his lap angled slightly outward to clear the keys while their shoulders touched.

"Evan and Tyler are gonna take five backstage for a little bit," Cassidy said softly into her piano microphone across the hushed stadium. "Back in two thousand and eight, right in the middle of our ten-year hiatus, Ryan and I couldn't face the cameras or the stadiums anymore. So we locked the front door of our house right down the road on Progress Street, sat down in the living room with just an acoustic guitar, a piano, and two microphones, and recorded an album we never toured."

"We just uploaded it on a Tuesday morning with one sentence: *'We're still here, and we're okay,'*" Ryan added into his mic. "We never thought we'd play these songs in a stadium... but tonight, sixty-five thousand of you feel just like our living room."

In `Section 104, Row A`, **Daniel Brooks**’s breath caught as Ryan fingerpicked the first gentle acoustic chord and Cassidy joined him on the grand piano.

"No way..." Daniel whispered to Laura and Jordan. "That's [**'Our Own Frequency'**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-14-our-own-frequency). They've never played a single note of that album in front of a crowd!"

In the hushed stillness of the sixty-five-thousand-seat stadium, brother and sister sang *"Our Own Frequency"* with just an acoustic guitar and a grand piano, turning the loudest football stadium in the Commonwealth into a quiet Blacksburg living room.

When the final chord faded into pin-drop silence, Ryan rested his hands on the body of his acoustic guitar and leaned closer to his microphone.

"Earlier in *Star City: Orbital Ward*, we sang about the four-hour clock and the maintenance protocols in sci-fi metaphors," Ryan said softly, his voice echoing clearly off the quiet limestone stands. "This next song is the centerpiece of [**Our Own Frequency**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2008-our-own-frequency), and it's the most private song Cassidy and I have ever written—without any metaphors at all. People look up at a stage and see two hours of lights and guitars. They don't see what T-ten paralysis actually takes behind closed doors. They don't see Cassidy and Evan and Tyler helping me through my daily care and toileting routines, or stretching my legs every morning so they don't atrophy. They don't see how we push the bed against the wall at night so I can sleep on the inside, and my sister sleeps on the outside edge as a human bed rail so I don't roll off in the dark."

Beside him at the grand piano, Cassidy smiled tenderly through her glasses and spoke into her mic:

"And they don't see how you keep me anchored when the world gets too loud for my head to handle. We take care of each other. That's what family does."

Down in `Section 104, Row A`, nobody moved a muscle.

**Jordan** felt a lump rise in his throat so thick he could barely swallow. On his left, **Aubrie Jean**’s fingers tightened warmly around his hand; on his right, **Madison** and **Megan** leaned against his shoulder and armrest; and down the row, **Mark**, **Hannah**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Isabel** were all looking at Jordan with shining, misty eyes. Every single thing Ryan and Cassidy were describing on that stage—stretching legs in the morning, helping with daily care without a shred of shame, building a human bed-rail in the dark so someone you love never falls, and anchoring each other when sensory overload hits `100`—was the exact life they had built together in `Suite 214/216` and on `Geneva Street`.

Up on stage, Ryan took a slow breath.

"Doing all of those daily routines on tour buses and in hotel rooms across the world was one thing when I was twenty-six in nineteen-ninety," Ryan continued, his voice honest and steady. "Twenty-six years later... I'm gonna be honest with you, Forger Nation. Accommodating this chair on the road has gotten too hard on my body, and on my family. Tonight is the final stop of the *Reignition* tour... and tonight is the last concert a touring Stardust Engine will ever play."

An audible gasp rippled across sixty-five thousand people inside **The Crucible**. Down in `Row A`, **Madison** pressed both hands over her mouth, and **Mark** whispered, *"No..."* Around the stadium, thousands of fans stood in stunned heartbreak, thinking they were witnessing the band's farewell announcement—before a warm, deeply respectful wave of supportive applause rose from the field to thank Ryan for everything he had given them.

And then Cassidy leaned forward into her piano microphone, her eyes sparkling behind her glasses:

"Wait—don't be sad! We aren't retiring!" Cassidy called out over the murmur. "If touring the world is too hard on Ryan, then the world can come to Blacksburg! To give the **Town of Blacksburg** and **CPI** time to build this right, starting in **August 2017**, we're launching the annual **Forger Nation Summer Festival**! A massive CPI Open House for incoming and prospective freshmen, the biggest downtown Blacksburg block party of the summer with stages for signed and unsigned bands, and every single year we'll close the festival right here inside **The Crucible** with fireworks—and sleep in our own beds on Progress Street every night!"

For ten full seconds, the roar that erupted inside **The Crucible** was deafening—heartbreak turning instantaneously into pure, roof-shaking euphoria as the 120-foot curved LED screen behind the piano lit up in warm gold lettering:

```
THE FORGER NATION SUMMER FESTIVAL
CPI OPEN HOUSE • DOWNTOWN BLACKSBURG BLOCK PARTY • THE CRUCIBLE RESIDENCY
INAUGURAL WEEKEND: AUGUST 2017
```

"They're staying home in Blacksburg forever!" **Mark** cheered, wiping his eyes and grinning at Jordan. "Every August, right here in our backyard!"

As the cheers gently hushed back to pin-drop silence, Ryan whispered, *"This is 'Brother's Keeper,'"* and Cassidy’s fingers began the delicate, melancholy grand-piano intro of [**"Brother's Keeper"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-15-brothers-keeper), joined seconds later by Ryan’s steady, comforting fingerpicked acoustic guitar:

> *"They look at the chair and they think it's weak*<br>
> *They whisper their pity whenever we speak*<br>
> *But they don't see the mornings, the stretches and pain*<br>
> *The quiet routines keeping us sane*<br>
> *Sleeping on the edge, a human bed rail*<br>
> *Catching the other whenever we fail..."*

When Ryan and Cassidy harmonized on the chorus—*"We trade all the armor, we trade all the scars / We built our own world underneath all the stars / When the body won't work, we become our own feet / When the pressure is heavy, we make it retreat"*—Jordan tapped his `mPad` softly so only Aubrie Jean, Madison, and Megan beside him could hear:

<aac>"That song is us."</aac>

"Every single word of it, J," Aubrie Jean whispered, kissing his temple as sixty-five thousand fans held up quiet white phone lights across the dark stadium bowl and brother and sister flowed directly into the gentle acoustic assurances of 2008's [**"Safe Person"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-16-safe-person)—and then, after Cassidy told the crowd, *"On Reignition, we recorded the sequel to 'Safe Person' with the full stadium band... but since we're still sitting here in our living room with you guys, Ryan and I wanted to strip this one all the way down to just the piano and the six-string,"* stripped 2015's [**"Still Your Safe Person"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=still-your-safe-person-live-at-the-crucible) all the way down to Cassidy’s grand piano and Ryan’s fingerpicked acoustic guitar (*"When the static hits a hundred and the room begins to spin / I am still your safe person—let the quiet in! / Put your headphones on or lean against my shoulder tight / I will hold the perimeter throughout the longest night!"*), bringing the entire stadium to its feet in a deeply moved ovation.

## 10:15 PM — *Saturday Night Lights* & *Last Bus Home*

As the ovation for *"Still Your Safe Person"* swelled across the valley, **Evan**, **Tyler**, and the touring musicians walked back out onto the stage and strapped on their electric instruments. Starting softly at Cassidy’s grand piano before the full electric stadium rig roared back to life, the band soared through the emotional power ballad [**"Echoes in the Wire"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-17-echoes-in-the-wire).

And the instant *"Echoes in the Wire"* ended, all four massive **Ironstone** stadium floodlight towers high above the east and west rims blazed back on at one hundred percent power as Ryan wheeled to the front of the stage and struck a ringing, unmistakable stadium guitar chord.

"Back in two thousand and four, we gave this next track to the university to play over the stadium speakers between the third and fourth quarters!" Ryan shouted into his microphone as sixty-five thousand CPI students and alumni gasped and leaped out of their seats. "We made a promise twelve years ago that we would **never** play this song live at a concert unless we were standing right here inside **The Crucible**! Blacksburg—welcome to the fourth quarter!"

"Hold those four fingers up, Forger Nation!" Cassidy called out, raising four fingers toward the sky. "Let's light up the mountains!"

"No way—it's [**'Saturday Night Lights: The Crucible'**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-18-saturday-night-lights-the-crucible)!" **Mark** shouted at the top of his lungs, grabbing Hannah and Jordan’s shoulders as every wristband in the stadium flashed Foundry Maroon and Molten Orange.

From the lower bowl to the top rim of the upper decks, sixty-five thousand people jumped in unison (`"GO FORGERS! GO CPI!"`) as the band sprinted headlong through a joyous hometown celebration. Without a single lull, *"Saturday Night Lights"* poured straight into [**"Progress Street: Where the Fire Began"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-19-progress-street-where-the-fire-began)—the middle song the **CPI Marching Forgers** had played during the 2014 Homecoming halftime show, now belted out by Jordan’s entire row in pure triumph.

As the final chord of *"Progress Street"* rang out, **Marcus Vance** handed **Ryan** a shimmering twelve-string electric guitar.

"When we're home in Blacksburg and the sun starts going down behind the mountains," Ryan called out over the twelve-string arpeggios, "there's one place the four of us always go—six miles of paved rail-trail between here and Christiansburg, with **The Crucible** tower lights glowing like neon in the evening sky!"

"Now, if you're visiting from out of town tonight, you might not know what the Huckleberry is all about!" **Cassidy** laughed into her microphone. "But if you're from Blacksburg or Christiansburg, or if you're a Forger who's walked that path from the **Pendleton Library** lawn—or if you're old-school enough to remember the actual **Huckleberry Train**—this one belongs to you!"

"Wait—no way!" **Mark** gasped, grabbing **Hannah**’s arm the instant Ryan struck that first chorused twelve-string chord and Cassidy’s analog synth bells chimed in at 124 BPM. "Are they seriously playing ***'Huckleberry Trail'*** right now?!"

What happened next during [**"Neon on the Huckleberry Trail"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=neon-on-the-huckleberry-trail-live-at-the-crucible)—known affectionately to every local and Forger simply as ***"Huckleberry Trail"***—turned an unannounced deep cut into the emotional standout number of the entire night: **because to the residents and Forgers inside The Crucible, it was HOME.**

Two rows behind `Section 104, Row A`, the out-of-town visitor in the golf polo who had driven into Blacksburg for the weekend blinked in total confusion, looking left and right down his row and wondering what on earth was going on as sixty-five thousand people stayed on their feet and lost their minds over a song about a local rail-trail.

He didn't know. Someone not familiar with the region couldn't know. **But Forgers? They knew.**

Every student in **Jordan**’s row knew the six-mile paved path winding from the **Pendleton Library** lawn past the cattle pastures and Price Mountain all the way to **Christiansburg**—the exact trail where Jordan pushed his `Vanguard Pro` alongside **Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, and **Mark** on crisp autumn evenings at five-thirty. And across the aisle from `Section 104`, when Cassidy sang the closing lines of the first verse—*"Six miles of asphalt from the library lawn / Where the old Huckleberry train used to roll on"*—a silver-haired gentleman in a vintage `CPI CLASS OF 1956` letterman cardigan wiped a tear from his cheek and beamed at **Daniel** and **Stephen**, remembering the actual slow-chugging **Huckleberry Train** that had carried students and coal up the mountain from Christiansburg's Cambria Depot until 1958.

By the second chorus, the sixty-five-thousand-voice roar of students, Blacksburg and Christiansburg locals, and three generations of CPI alumni was so thunderous that Cassidy called out, *"Take it, Forger Nation! It's yours!"* and stepped completely back from her microphone alongside Ryan—letting the stadium claim the song forever:

As Cassidy and Ryan traded their grinning sign-off (*"Race you back to Progress Street!"* / *"You're on, Cass!"*) to a deafening hometown ovation, the band tore straight into the soaring 1989 synth-rock chorus of [**"Neon Hearts"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-20-neon-hearts).

Then the stage lights softened to warm candlelight gold, and Cassidy began the sweeping 1987 piano ballad [**"My Anchor Holds"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-21-my-anchor-holds)—the exact song Jordan and **Aubrie Jean** had chosen for their wedding processional two months from now.

Throughout the entire song, Aubrie Jean stood directly behind Jordan’s wheelchair with her arms wrapped warmly around his chest and her chin resting on his shoulder, swaying with **Madison**, **Megan**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Isabel**, while **Daniel** and **Laura** danced slow and close in the aisle beside **Mark** and **Hannah**, and little **Stella** slept peacefully in **Stephen** and **Elana**’s arms under her pink ear defenders.

![During the romantic 1987 piano ballad 'My Anchor Holds' inside The Crucible bathed in warm candlelight gold and starlight white, Jordan Fuller sits peacefully in his wheelchair at the Section 104, Row A railing while Aubrie Jean Kelly wraps her arms warmly around his shoulders beside Madison Scott, Zoë Wren Brooks, and Mark Anderson.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/aubrie/e007/c004/p005/my-anchor-holds-section-104.jpg#fullwidth)

After one more explosive burst of fireworks on the comeback anthem [**"Reignition: Lights Up on Progress Street"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-22-reignition-lights-up-on-progress-street) (*"Good morning, Forger Nation."* / *"The switches are back on! Let's go!"*), the clock on the stadium fascia ticked past `10:35 PM`, and the band eased into the warm, nostalgic groove of [**"Last Bus Home: Lights Out on Progress Street"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2016-live-at-the-crucible?track=1-23-last-bus-home-lights-out-on-progress-street).

Every Stardust Engine fan in the building knew what *"Last Bus Home"* meant: since 2004, it had always been the final goodnight song of the show, where Ryan, Cassidy, Evan, and Tyler powered down their stage switches one by one before the house lights came up.

In `Seat 1`, Jordan felt a warm, peaceful glow settle over his chest. They had heard *"The Stardust Engine"* and *"Progress Street"* tonight, and even if the band hadn't played the 1987 pop version of *"Electric Color"*—which everyone knew they hated because of Apex Records, and which they had already nodded to earlier during the space set with *"The Spectrum (True Color)"*—this night had already given him back everything he had lost in September 2014.

As Cassidy sang softly into her microphone, *"Lights out on Progress Street..."*, Ryan spoke quietly into his mic (*"Goodnight, Forger Nation. We love you."*) and reached over to flip the main toggle switch on his amplifier rack.

Every light on the stage clicked off to pitch black.
