## Sunday, May 22, 2016 • 2:00 PM — No Voice in the Natatorium

Fifteen hours after the final firework faded above **The Crucible**, **Jordan**’s heart was still full—and every muscle in his calves, hamstrings, and shoulders was reminding him that he had spent three straight hours sitting upright at the fifty-yard line cheering, clapping, and raising his arms to the sky.

So at `14:00` on Sunday afternoon, **Jordan**, **Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Mark**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Isabel** rode down to **Christiansburg**, scanned their **`$30.00/month` Town of Christiansburg Non-Resident** barcode keytags at the front desk of the **Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center**, and headed straight for the private, wheelchair-accessible **Family Changing Suites**.

Inside the dry changing room, before transferring out of his everyday wheelchair, Jordan carefully powered down his **`mPad`** and his **`mPhone`** and placed both devices inside the heavy steel dry locker, watching **Aubrie Jean** turn the key and loop the elastic locker wristband around her wrist.

In a sixty-four-thousand-square-foot municipal natatorium filled with humid air and chlorinated splash water, electronics did not survive on deck. Out here in the water, Jordan did not have his digital voice.

Yet as Aubrie Jean and **Madison**—his original **Room 214** roommates from freshman year—helped him transfer onto one of the facility’s white waterproof PVC deck chairs and rolled him out onto the warm tile deck, Jordan didn't feel anxious about leaving his tablet behind. After nearly two full years of living together every single day, Aubrie Jean and Madison knew him better than anyone on earth. They knew every micro-expression of his eyes behind his neoprene-strapped glasses, every subtle shift of his shoulders, every finger-tap against their wrists, and every non-verbal cue he used when his screen wasn't in his lap.

Parking the waterproof PVC transfer chair at the bottom of the non-slip zero-depth ramp inside **The Cove**—the quiet, sunlit therapy pool maintained year-round at a constant **`95.5°F`**—Jordan floated out of the seat into the steaming water between Aubrie Jean and Madison.

"Oh, I felt that knot in your right hamstring let go from here, J," Madison murmured warmly, supporting his right hip in the buoyant `95.5°F` water while Aubrie Jean supported his left shoulder and slowly guided his legs through his afternoon range-of-motion stretches.

Jordan let his head rest back against the padded foam collar Aubrie Jean held beneath his neck, closing his eyes with a long, contented exhale as the `95.5°F` water and the gentle underwater hydrotherapy jets melted the three-hour stadium stiffness out of his lower back.

***

## 2:15 PM — Five More Non-Resident Keytags

Ten minutes into Jordan’s stretch, the glass door separating the main **50-Meter Competition Pool** deck from the warm therapy alcove swung open.

Down the gentle zero-depth ramp of **The Cove** rolled a second white waterproof PVC transfer chair—and parked dry up against the tiled wall beside the ramp entry, right next to Jordan’s own dry fourteen-pound titanium manual wheelchair, ***Shuttlecraft Galileo*** (`Vanguard AeroLite-Ti`), sat a familiar matte-black titanium manual wheelchair.

Sitting in the waterproof transfer chair was a forty-eight-year-old man in black swim trunks with powerful manual-chair shoulders (**Ryan O'Connell**). Walking right behind him with her hands on the wet PVC push-handles was his sister in a navy one-piece swimsuit and fogged glasses (**Cassidy O'Connell**), while **Holly**, **Evan**, and **Tyler** walked down the ramp into the `95.5°F` water alongside them.

Across the therapy pool, Mark, Zoë, Lauren, Hannah, Sarah, Jessica, and Isabel didn't gasp, didn't point, and didn't whisper. Just like on the **Huckleberry Trail** in October, everyone in the pool adhered instinctively to the unwritten covenant of Montgomery County.

And from thirty feet away in the `95.5°F` water, Jordan watched something that made his chest ache with quiet recognition.

Over by the opposite hydrotherapy bench, Cassidy, Evan, and Tyler eased Ryan off the waterproof transfer chair into the warm water—and immediately began the exact same routine Aubrie Jean and Madison were doing for Jordan. While Holly supported Ryan’s shoulders near the coping tile, Cassidy and Evan gently held Ryan’s paralyzed legs in the `95.5°F` buoyancy, slowly flexing his knees and ankles through his afternoon anti-atrophy stretches to work out the physical toll of playing three hours of stadium rock from a manual wheelchair the night before.

No stage lights. No sixty-five-thousand-person roar. Just two families in a municipal therapy pool in Christiansburg, taking care of the men they loved.

***

## 2:25 PM — *"Were You in Section 104 Last Night?"*

Once Ryan’s leg stretches were finished and he was floating comfortably along the stainless-steel handrail in the chest-deep `95.5°F` water, Ryan glanced across the steaming pool toward the two dry everyday wheelchairs parked side by side up on the tile deck—and then looked straight across the water at Jordan, Aubrie Jean, Madison, Mark, and Zoë.

A slow, genuine smile broke across Ryan O'Connell’s face.

Using one hand on the submerged stainless-steel rail, Ryan drifted effortlessly across the warm pool until he was six feet away from Jordan, with Cassidy, Holly, Evan, and Tyler floating a respectful step behind him.

"Hey, man—sorry to interrupt your stretch," Ryan said in a quiet, polite voice, speaking directly to Jordan, one wheelchair user to another. "Were you sitting right in the front row of `Section 104` at **The Crucible** last night... and wait a second, were you the guy in the manual chair who waved to us on the **Huckleberry Trail** back in October at five-thirty?"

Jordan’s breath caught completely in his throat.

His eyes went wide behind his water-spotted glasses as three different layers of disbelief hit him all at once. Less than eighteen hours ago, he had been sitting down at the fifty-yard line watching **Ryan O'Connell** command sixty-five thousand screaming people, pyrotechnics, and a three-hundred-and-thirty-piece marching band from a stage the size of a cathedral. Even after two years of healing at CPI, eighteen years of being hidden away in a back bedroom in Virginia Beach had conditioned a part of Jordan’s brain to assume that "important" people would never voluntarily want to know him—or that if a famous person ever spoke to a kid in a wheelchair, it was only out of fleeting charity or pity. Yet there was zero pity in Ryan’s eyes: Ryan was another paralyzed man whose own sisters and cousins had just finished stretching his legs in the exact same `95.5°F` pool, politely asking Jordan a question because he genuinely wanted to connect with a fellow manual wheelchair user.

For two full seconds, Jordan was so stunned that he froze. He opened his mouth slightly, felt the familiar cerebral palsy tightness lock his vocal cords, and instinctively glanced down at the water where his lap tray would normally be—remembering with a sudden jolt of helplessness that his **`mPad`** was locked inside a steel locker fifty yards away so the municipal pool water wouldn't destroy his voice. The man whose music had helped save his life was waiting for an answer six feet away, and Jordan had no way to speak out loud and no screen to type it on.

Right beside his left and right shoulders in the warm water, **Aubrie Jean** and **Madison** didn't let him hang in the silence for even half a second.

Feeling Jordan’s right fingers tap twice against her wrist underwater and reading the wide, overwhelmed look in his eyes, Aubrie Jean gave his hand a reassuring squeeze—making sure Ryan and Cassidy saw that she was looking at Jordan first before speaking for him.

"He’s saying a huge *yes* to both," Aubrie Jean said warmly, her voice gentle and natural. "This is my fiancé, **Jordan**. I'm **Aubrie Jean**, this is **Madison**—the three of us have been roommates in Bennett Hall since freshman year—and these are our family, **Mark**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Isabel**. Jordan’s cerebral palsy makes speaking out loud really tough, so he uses an AAC tablet for his voice, and we had to lock it in the dry locker so the pool water wouldn't fry the electronics."

The instant Aubrie Jean explained why Jordan hadn't spoken out loud, every trace of potential awkwardness vanished from the water.

Behind her fogged glasses, **Cassidy O'Connell**’s eyes softened with immediate, unmistakable kinship—the look of a woman who knew all too well what it felt like when your voice couldn't get through the room and your Safe Person stepped in to hold the perimeter.

"You never have to apologize for that around us, Jordan," Ryan said warmly, extending his right hand across the surface of the `95.5°F` water. "I'm Ryan, these are my sisters Cass and Holly, and our twin cousins Evan and Tyler. It is really, really good to finally meet you, man. Just point, nod, or let Aubrie Jean and Madison translate—we speak roommate fluently in this family."

A huge, relieved smile broke across Jordan’s face. He reached his right hand out of the warm water and shook Ryan’s hand firmly—and then, lifting his right hand to his forehead, Jordan tapped two fingers to his temple in the exact fellow-wheelchair-user salute they had traded between the split-rail pasture fences on October 21, 2014, before placing his open palm over his heart and gesturing toward Mark and then toward the trail.

**Madison**, watching Jordan’s hands and eyes with the effortless fluency of two years in Room 214, translated immediately while glancing at Jordan for his confirming nod:

"He’s saying that back in October 2014 when we passed you guys on the Huckleberry Trail, he had zero clue who **The Stardust Engine** was," Madison grinned as Jordan nodded earnestly, his cheeks flushing pink above the water. "He grew up in a house where he wasn't allowed to listen to music, so when Mark whispered your names on the trail, it meant nothing to him—he was just happy to see another guy in a manual chair out on the path!"

"And now he’s tapping my arm to add the second part," Aubrie Jean laughed softly, reading Jordan’s quick hand sign toward the ceiling and then three fingers against her palm. "Three months after that walk on the trail, in January 2015, Zoë’s dad **Daniel** put the 1987 vinyl LP of *Electric Color* on his turntable in Christiansburg—and the second Daniel said your names, Jordan realized you were the siblings and cousins from the Huckleberry Trail!"

Ryan threw his head back and laughed—a warm, delighted laugh that echoed softly off the tiled alcove walls while Cassidy pressed both hands to her cheeks in pure joy.

"Are you serious?!" Ryan grinned at Jordan. "Man, you have no idea how much I love hearing that! Because I'll tell you a secret, Jordan: that October evening at five-thirty when you and your friends walked past us in your CPI hoodies by the cattle pastures? When you looked over at my chair and just gave me a regular neighbor's wave without anybody pulling out a camera or treating us like a circus act—that exact moment was why Cass stopped us under the maple tree five minutes later and sang the opening lines of [***'Neon on the Huckleberry Trail'***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/2015-reignition?track=1-08-neon-on-the-huckleberry-trail)!"

Jordan’s eyes went wide behind his glasses. He pointed a finger at himself and his friends in stunned disbelief, mouthing *Us?!*

"You guys!" Cassidy beamed, nodding enthusiastically as she floated beside her brother. "When I sang *'October five-thirty and the air turns crisp'* and *'Students in their hoodies jogging two by two / Nodding with a smile as we roll on through / No autographs asked, just a neighbor's wave'*—that was literally you and your family! That’s why Ryan recognized you on the camera monitor in `Section 104, Row A` before the show last night!"

Ryan looked at Jordan’s wide eyes behind his water-spotted glasses, then glanced toward the tiled corridor leading to the changing suites.

"Listen, Jordan," Ryan said warmly, "I know your voice is locked up in the dry locker right now, and I don't want to make you play charades in the pool when I've been wanting to actually sit down and talk with you since last night. What do you say we finish rinsing off, get changed into our street clothes so you have your tablet back, and meet out in the dry lobby lounge in twenty minutes for a real conversation?"

Jordan stared at Ryan for a full second, his heart doing a complete backflip in his chest, before nodding so fast his glasses nearly slipped down his nose.

***

## 3:10 PM — Street Clothes, Two Manual Chairs & *"You Mean THE Progress Street?!"*

Twenty minutes later, showered, dried off, and dressed back in his warm maroon **`CPI`** hoodie and jeans—with his digital behind-the-ear hearing aids clicked into place, his dry fourteen-pound titanium ***Shuttlecraft Galileo*** (`Vanguard AeroLite-Ti`) underneath him, and his **`mPad`** snapped securely onto his lap mount—Jordan rolled out of the accessible **Family Changing Suite** corridor into the sunlit upper lobby lounge of the **Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center** alongside **Aubrie Jean**, **Madison**, **Mark**, **Zoë**, **Lauren**, **Hannah**, **Sarah**, **Jessica**, and **Isabel**.

Half of Jordan’s brain still half-expected the hallway to be empty—figuring that a world-famous rock guitarist had just been being polite in the pool before slipping out a side exit.

Instead, sitting right there by the wide glass windows overlooking the natatorium—dressed in faded blue jeans, sneakers, and worn CPI alumni sweatshirts with their wet towels tucked into their canvas gym bags—were **Ryan**, **Cassidy**, **Holly**, **Evan**, and **Tyler**.

The moment Ryan spotted Jordan rolling out of the corridor, Ryan pushed his matte-black titanium manual wheelchair forward, spun a crisp turn on the low-pile lobby carpet, and parked right beside Jordan’s chair—wheel to wheel, push-rim to push-rim, at the exact same eye level.

Jordan looked from Ryan’s face, to the two manual wheelchairs sitting side by side within two inches of each other, and back to Ryan’s warm, unhurried smile.

Jordan’s mind was still reeling. *A celebrity actually wants to talk to me?! Not a five-second wave on the way to the parking lot—he actually waited outside the locker rooms in his street clothes to sit down and have a real conversation with me?! And wait—he's a fellow wheelchair user sitting at my exact eye level, AND he's rich and famous?!*

With his **`mPad`** finally back in front of his hands, Jordan’s fingers flew across the glass screen—blurting out the exact cascade of thoughts spinning inside his nineteen-year-old head as the crisp Australian cadence of *EchoType* spoke aloud for the first time that afternoon:

<aac>"I still cannot believe you actually waited to talk to me! And wait—I hope this isn't a dumb question, Ryan, but you guys are rich and famous and sold millions of albums all over the world. Don't famous rock stars live in massive mansions in Beverly Hills?! Wait... when your 2004 album is called Progress Street, do you mean THE Progress Street right here in Blacksburg?!"</aac>

For half a second, the lobby lounge was quiet—and then **Ryan**, **Cassidy**, **Holly**, **Evan**, and **Tyler** all burst out laughing at once, a warm, uproarious belly laugh that made Jordan and his entire family grin from ear to ear.

"Beverly Hills?!" Ryan laughed, slapping the armrest of his titanium chair in delight. "Oh, man, Jordan—I love you already! Do you want to know a secret? Back in 1992, when we were still stuck on **Apex Records** after *Electric Color* and *Neon Hearts*, the label executives *did* rent a massive gated mansion for us out in Beverly Hills while we were out in L.A. for our third album, *Friction*—and billed every dime of the rent straight against our royalties! We were completely miserable!"

"We hated every single second of it!" **Cassidy** chimed in, leaning forward in her lobby chair and adjusting her dry glasses with an emphatic nod. "Everything was fake, the air smelled like eight lanes of freeway exhaust, the mansion had twenty marble steps at the front door with zero wheelchair ramps so Evan and Tyler had to carry Ryan's chair up and down the stairs every single day, and most of all—it wasn't Blacksburg. It wasn't home."

"And yes," **Holly** grinned, pointing her thumb north toward Blacksburg, "when we say **Progress Street**, we literally mean *the* **Progress Street** right here in Blacksburg—two blocks north of **Commonwealth Mall**! That 1900s farmhouse originally belonged to **Aunt Martha Wright**—Evan and Tyler's mom, and Ryan's, Cassidy's, and my maternal aunt. When the guys and Cass started as CPI freshmen in the fall of 1985 and I was only sixteen years old in dual enrollment, Aunt Martha acted as our live-in landlord and rented the house to us for one dollar a year so we could legally check the 'Living with a Local Relative' box on CPI's housing form and skip the freshman dorms!"

"We started the band right out back in Aunt Martha's basement and unheated cinder-block garage," **Evan** added warmly. "When we walked out on Apex Records and drove straight back from Beverly Hills in 1992, that house was our sanctuary—and in 1996, Holly bought the property outright from Mom so we could gut-renovate the interior with a three-stop hydraulic elevator, a hidden 1:12 wraparound porch ramp, and a sound-dampened basement lounge."

"Wait—seriously?!" **Mark** grinned, shaking his head in amazement. "I grew up ten miles away in Radford and walked down Progress Street a hundred times during CPI summer camps, and you guys actually live in that house full-time?!"

"Three hundred and sixty-five days a year when we're not on the road," Ryan smiled, looking right back at Jordan, one manual chair user to another. "Why on earth would I want to sit in a sterile mansion in Beverly Hills surrounded by Hollywood sycophants when I can live on **Progress Street** with my sisters and my cousins, roll six miles down the **Huckleberry Trail** under the autumn leaves without a single person hassling us, buy our groceries on South Main, and pay thirty bucks a month for a Town of Christiansburg non-resident pass to soak in a ninety-five-point-five-degree therapy pool?"

Jordan stared at Ryan, a slow, radiant smile spreading across his face as he typed again:

<aac>"Okay, that makes total sense. Ninety-five-point-five degrees in The Cove beats Beverly Hills any day. Plus, Beverly Hills doesn't have Blue Ridge Transit kneeling ramps."</aac>

"Speaking of **Blue Ridge Transit** kneeling ramps!" Ryan grinned, leaning an elbow on his armrest so he and Jordan were talking straight wheel-to-wheel. "Cass and I were watching the external security feed from the production truck at `10:50 PM` last night when you guys boarded the articulated bus at the **Crucible Bus Loop**. Who taught you that 180-degree reverse climb up the steep front bus ramp in your titanium chair? Because you backed that **`Shuttlecraft Galileo`** up a twelve-degree diamond-plate incline in two pulls flat without touching the yellow side curbs once!"

Jordan’s cheeks flushed with pure pride as he typed back rapidly:

<aac>"I figured it out on Route 1 last year! If you roll up a steep front bus ramp forward in a manual chair, your center of gravity tips back and you get trapped between the front wheel wells trying to make a three-point turn. If you spin 180 degrees on the sidewalk and pull up backward in reverse, your big rear drive wheels hit the lip first and you glide straight into the securement bay!"</aac>

"That is textbook manual-chair physics right there," Ryan said with genuine veteran respect, tapping his knuckles against the anodized push-rim of Jordan’s fourteen-pound **`Vanguard AeroLite-Ti`**. "When I first got out of Star City General after my T10 spinal cord injury in 1991, it took me six months to figure out reverse ramp boarding without popping a caster."

For the next thirty-five minutes in the dry upper lobby of the **Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center**, the two families sat together in their street clothes like neighbors who had known each other for years:
* With his **`mPad`** right on his lap—and **Aubrie Jean** and **Madison** chiming in beside him whenever Jordan got so excited that he gestured with both hands and typed at the same time—Jordan told Ryan, Cassidy, Holly, Evan, and Tyler about his 350-pound ***S.S. Freedom*** (`Vanguard Titan-X350`) power chair for steep campus hill climbs, how **Daniel Brooks** had bought both the *S.S. Freedom* and *Shuttlecraft Galileo* so he could retire his mother’s old hospital chair to the scrap heap, and how he and Aubrie Jean were getting married on **July 15** with a string quartet playing [**"My Anchor Holds"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/artists/the-stardust-engine/1987-electric-color?track=1-03-my-anchor-holds) as their processional.
* Cassidy’s eyes misted over behind her glasses when Jordan typed what *"My Anchor Holds"* and *"The Roche Limit"* had meant to him in `Section 104, Row A` the night before, and **Tyler** showed Jordan and Mark photos on his **`mPhone`** of the custom hydraulic wheelchair lift inside **Unit 01 (*The Iron Horse*)** and the 5-second front flip-out ramp on their **Prairie Coachworks RLFD30** low-floor Progress Street shuttle parked right outside in the oversized lot.

When they finally rolled out through the automatic sliding glass doors into the sunny Sunday afternoon at `15:50`, Ryan paused his manual chair beside Jordan’s under the covered entrance canopy and shook Jordan’s hand firmly.

"I am really glad I asked you if that was you from `Section 104`, Jordan," Ryan said warmly. "We swim down here every Tuesday and Thursday afternoon when we're not tracking in the basement on Progress Street. Next time we pass each other in **The Cove** or out on the **Huckleberry Trail** at five-thirty—now we don't just have to wave. We're neighbors."

<aac>"See you on Tuesday or Thursday, Ryan,"</aac> Jordan’s **`mPad`** replied, his heart overflowing as he tapped two fingers to his temple in their signature salute. <aac>"Thank you for waiting to talk to me."</aac>
