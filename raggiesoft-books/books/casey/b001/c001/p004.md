# Part 4: The Crown Burger Debrief

Once we unloaded our luggage into the adjoining motel rooms, my parents realized exactly how exhausted they were from the four-leg transit gauntlet. Rather than attempting to navigate a sit-down restaurant, we opted for the immediate, high-caloric efficiency of the Crown Burger located right next door in the same parking lot.

We pushed a couple of plastic tables together near the window. The air-conditioning in the fast-food dining room was blasting, providing welcome relief from the thick Virginia heat.

Casey was practically vibrating as she tore into the small cardboard box of her kids' meal, immediately extracting a cheap, plastic wind-up toy. At ten years old, I had finally decided I was too old for the kids' menu. I sat across from her, unwrapping my standard Crown Burger with a quiet sense of maturity. I felt like I was starting to outgrow the childish stuff, but as I watched Casey gleefully wind up her plastic car and send it skittering across the Formica table, I kept my mouth shut. I didn't want to spoil it for her. I wanted my kid sister to just enjoy being a kid.

"Alright, let's review the timeline for tomorrow," Casey announced between bites of French fries. She pulled a folded, slightly crumpled printed itinerary from her pocket and smoothed it out on the table. "Registration is at 0800 in the student union. The welcoming speech is at 0830. After that, we need to divide and conquer to maximize our coverage of the—"

"Casey, I appreciate the tactical support," Cassandra interrupted gently, placing her hand softly over our little sister's printed schedule. "But I have the logistics for tomorrow secured."

Casey blinked, looking up at her older sister. "You do?"

"I do," Cassandra nodded, taking a sip of her soda. "It's going to operate exactly like the VDU open house we attended last week. They're going to try and funnel everyone into massive, generalized campus tours. We are going to bypass the general tour and proceed directly to the Computer Science department. I need to verify their hardware infrastructure before I commit."

My father paused, wiping his hands with a napkin. "What exactly are you looking for, Cassandra? Doesn't every college have computers?"

"There is a massive discrepancy in departmental funding right now," Cassandra explained, her eyes lighting up with that intense, analytical fire she always got when discussing systems architecture. "The industry is shifting entirely toward object-oriented programming. C++ is the established standard, but the major Silicon Valley firms just announced a new cross-platform runtime architecture in May. The entire structure of the World Wide Web is about to fundamentally change."

Mom stared at her, completely lost. "Right. The... Web."

"If George Wythe University is still teaching archaic procedural languages on outdated mainframes, they are useless to me," Cassandra continued smoothly, ignoring the blank stares of our parents. "I need to see their advanced server labs. I need to verify that they have T1 internet backbone access for their undergraduates, not just the graduate researchers. With Quantum OS 95 launching next month, the home PC market is about to explode, and I refuse to spend four years at an institution that is technologically stagnant."

Casey nodded solemnly, despite definitely not understanding what a T1 backbone was. "So we bypass the general tour. We secure the advanced server lab."

"Exactly," Cassandra smiled, leaning back in her manual wheelchair and picking up a French fry. "Then, at 1300 hours, we meet with the disability counselor to inspect the freshman dormitories. I need to physically measure the desk clearance to ensure my custom desktop rig will fit. It has a heavy CRT monitor and a full-tower ATX case, so standard dorm furniture might require structural modification."

"I can measure the desks," Casey offered eagerly, winding up her plastic toy car again. "I'll bring the tape measure."

"You are officially assigned to measurement duty," Cassandra agreed with a serious nod, delegating the task perfectly to keep our little sister engaged. 

I took another bite of my burger, looking around the plastic tables of the Crown Burger. Tomorrow was going to be a massive, exhausting day of academic tours and ADA compliance checks. But as I watched Cassandra seamlessly manage Casey's energy and outline her ruthless technological requirements, I realized something. 

Cassandra didn't just want to attend college. She was planning to conquer it.
