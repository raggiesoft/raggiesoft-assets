The George Wythe University Student Union was a massive, echoing atrium of brutalist concrete and large glass windows, designed to look impressive on brochures but currently functioning as a highly inefficient echo chamber. 

By 7:30 AM, the space was already swarming with nervous high school upperclassmen and their overly caffeinated parents. Tables were laden with massive stainless steel coffee urns, towers of slightly stale bagels, and sweating pitchers of orange juice. 

While my parents navigated the chaotic registration tables to collect Cassandra's printed itinerary and campus map, Casey immediately took tactical control of the dining area.

"Here," Casey ordered, dropping her brightly colored backpack onto a chair at a large circular table near the perimeter of the room. "This table has an unobstructed sightline to the podium, but it's positioned close enough to the exit that we won't get bottlenecked when the welcoming speech concludes. David, secure the perimeter. I am going to procure provisions."

I sat down at the table, suppressing a laugh. I was ten, and I was entirely content to let my eight-year-old sister run point on breakfast logistics. "I'll guard the backpacks, Casey. Get me a blueberry muffin if they have them."

A few minutes later, Cassandra rolled smoothly up to the table, locking her brakes. She had a campus map resting on her lap, but she barely glanced at it. She was already scanning the room, evaluating the crowd, the faculty standing near the walls, and the overall operational flow of the event. 

"The accessibility access to this building is acceptable," Cassandra noted quietly, picking up one of the brochures my mother had brought over. "The ramp grading was within standard tolerances, and the automatic doors functioned perfectly. However, the elevator bank is undersized for a student union of this capacity."

Mom sat down, looking exhausted before the day had even officially begun. Dad returned a moment later, carrying a tray loaded with coffee, bagels, and a massive plate of fruit. 

"I have secured the high-value items," Casey announced, marching back to the table with two blueberry muffins clutched in her small hands. She dropped one onto my napkin and took her seat next to Cassandra. 

As the family settled in to eat, a microphone screeched at the front of the room. A sharply dressed university administrator stepped up to the podium, adjusting the mic stand. 

"Welcome, prospective students and families, to George Wythe University!" the administrator's voice boomed, echoing harshly off the concrete walls. "We are thrilled to have you here today. GWU is an institution that prides itself on looking to the future..."

Cassandra quietly took a bite of her bagel, her eyes fixed on the administrator. She was listening to the speech, but I could tell her processor was already running completely different calculations. She wasn't here to be sold on the university's prestige or their marketing brochures. She was here to stress-test their infrastructure. 

"When this concludes," Cassandra leaned over and whispered to Casey, completely ignoring the booming speech echoing around us, "we bypass the guided walking tour entirely. We proceed straight to the academic quad. I need to see the Computer Science building."

Casey nodded, taking a bite of her muffin. "I have the route memorized. We move on your signal."
