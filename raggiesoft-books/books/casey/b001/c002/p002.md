# Part 2: The Architecture of the Past

Despite Cassandra's earlier insistence on bypassing the general campus tour, she quickly realized that the George Wythe University campus layout was highly irregular. Because we lacked the necessary topographic data to navigate the winding footpaths and dense brick facades autonomously, she made the tactical decision to join the massive herd of prospective students for the guided academic walking tour. 

For the next hour, we slowly followed a painfully enthusiastic student ambassador walking backward and pointing out the library, the liberal arts buildings, and the various dining halls. Cassandra wheeled herself near the back of the group, conserving her energy and mentally mapping the concrete pathways, curb cuts, and elevator locations. 

When the tour finally wound its way past a low, squat, heavily-air-conditioned brick building, the guide pointed enthusiastically. "And right here is the Computer Science building, home to our advanced programming labs!"

Cassandra immediately locked her eyes on the entrance. She had the coordinates. 

When the tour eventually circled back and terminated at the student union, the massive crowd began to disperse toward various breakout sessions. Cassandra didn't hesitate. She pivoted her wheelchair sharply. "Breakaway," she ordered quietly. "We are heading back to the CS building."

Casey adjusted her backpack, marching faithfully beside her older sister's wheelchair as we navigated back across the quad. My parents followed, looking slightly bewildered as we officially went off-script.

We pushed through the heavy glass double doors of the Computer Science building, immediately hitting a wall of chilled, aggressively processed air. The lobby was lined with recruitment tables, manned by smiling faculty members handing out glossy pamphlets detailing the department's cutting-edge curriculum. 

Arranged on the tables were several massive desktop computer towers, their heavy CRT monitors humming with static electricity. 

Cassandra wheeled herself up to the closest display, adjusting her glasses as she inspected the screen. Casey leaned in right next to her, staring at the blocky, colorful interface. 

"Is this the new Quantum OS 95?" Casey whispered eagerly. 

"No," Cassandra replied, her voice perfectly flat. "It is Atlas OS/2."

A faculty member leaned over the table, smiling brightly. "That's right! We just completely overhauled the entire department's infrastructure. Every single machine in our student labs is now running Atlas OS/2, networked via a brand new Nexus NetShare topography. It is the absolute pinnacle of stable, 32-bit architecture."

Cassandra offered him a tight, incredibly polite smile. "It certainly is an investment."

She wheeled herself slowly down the line of tables, her processor working overtime. IBM—or rather, Atlas—had successfully convinced the university to commit their entire departmental budget to a platform that was practically already dead. With Quantum OS 95 dropping in a month, betting the farm on Atlas OS/2 was a catastrophic failure of market foresight. 

At the very end of the row of tables, Cassandra paused. Set up on a small pedestal was a thick, beige keyboard with a massive, blocky casing. A small handwritten sign next to it read: *Where it all began!*

It was an Apex 64. 

Cassandra let out a soft breath, reaching out and gently tapping the heavy plastic chassis of the vintage machine. She remembered the simpler times. She remembered sitting in front of a glowing television screen as a kid, watching the blinking cursor of the Apex 64, learning the absolute basics of logic and syntax. It was a beautiful machine. 

But times were changing. The industry was accelerating at a terrifying, exponential rate, and nostalgia wasn't going to secure a career in the coming tech boom. 

"Is something wrong, Cass?" I asked quietly, standing next to her wheelchair. 

Cassandra kept her polite, pleasant expression firmly in place for the benefit of the faculty watching her, but her eyes were cold as she looked at the rows of Atlas OS/2 machines. 

"This department is a museum, David," she murmured, her voice so low only Casey and I could hear it. "They just don't realize it yet."
