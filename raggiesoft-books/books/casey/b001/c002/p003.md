We left the oppressive chill of the Computer Science building and made our way back across the academic quad to the Student Services center. At precisely 1300 hours, we were sitting in the small, warmly lit office of the George Wythe University Disability Services coordinator. 

The coordinator was a younger woman named Sarah. She was incredibly welcoming, pulling up campus housing blueprints on her desktop computer the moment Cassandra mentioned her desk measurements. 

"We absolutely have fully wheelchair-accessible dorms in the freshman housing blocks," Sarah assured her, turning her monitor so Cassandra could see the floor plan. "The standard suites have two Twin XL beds connecting to a second dorm room via a shared bathroom. In the accessible suites, the bathroom turning radius is expanded, the shower is a roll-in, and the desks are fully modular. We can adjust the desk height before you even arrive on move-in day."

"That is excellent," Cassandra nodded, offering a genuine smile. "I will be bringing a rather large desktop rig. It requires a heavy CRT monitor and a full-tower ATX case, so standard fixed-height dorm furniture is usually a critical failure point."

Sarah laughed, leaning back in her chair. "You're building your own rig? My brother is a computer science major out on the West Coast, and that's all he talks about." She glanced at the door before lowering her voice playfully. "He goes to Northwest Pacific University. His department has actually been beta testing the new Quantum OS 95 for months."

Cassandra sat up slightly straighter, her interest suddenly piquing. "NPU has beta access?"

"Oh, absolutely," Sarah nodded, smiling at the sudden intensity in my sister's eyes. "He said it's completely changing the way they network their labs. In fact, they just got their hands on an early developer build for Quantum's new enterprise networking software. I think they're calling the beta *Project Citadel*. It's supposed to be the 32-bit architecture that will eventually run all the university servers."

"Project Citadel," Cassandra repeated softly, committing the codename to memory. "What will the retail version be called?"

"I think he said Quantum NT 4.0," Sarah replied, shrugging. "I'm not a tech person myself, but he loves it. Which is why I always try to steer our tech-focused students carefully. Between you and me, I know our CS department here at GWU just invested heavily in Atlas OS/2."

"I saw," Cassandra said politely.

Sarah offered a sympathetic smile. "I don't have any say in what the academic departments do with their budgets, but even I know Atlas isn't the future. If you're building an ATX rig to run Quantum OS 95, you might be a little frustrated in our student labs."

Cassandra looked around the small office. The environment at GWU was undeniably inviting. The disability counselor was accommodating, the accessibility infrastructure was perfectly sound, and the campus was physically beautiful. She could very easily see herself living here, navigating the pathways and catching the CCT bus into Vienna. 

But as Casey eagerly pulled out her tape measure to double-check the desk dimensions on the blueprint, Cassandra remained quiet. She wasn't going to make her final decision today. She was going to wait. 

Because once Casey and I finished our summer camps at VDU, our family was flying to Seattle. And Cassandra suddenly needed to see exactly what Northwest Pacific University was doing with Project Citadel.
