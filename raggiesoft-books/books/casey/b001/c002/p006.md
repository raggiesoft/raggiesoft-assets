When the bathroom door finally opened, Cassandra was already dressed in her pajamas. Mom helped her wheel across the carpet and execute a smooth pivot transfer into the nearest queen bed. 

Once Cassandra was settled under the covers, Mom turned off the main overhead light, leaving just the small lamp on the nightstand casting a warm glow across the room.

"Alright, you two," Mom whispered, looking over at Casey and me. "Brush your teeth. It's late."

The accessible room was equipped with two large queen beds. Since Cassandra needed a dedicated bed to ensure she had enough space to adjust her position throughout the night, Casey and I were sharing the other one. 

Once we finished in the bathroom, I climbed into the bed and pulled the heavy hotel comforter up. Casey scrambled in right behind me, immediately cuddling up against my side. For all her intense logistical planning and insistence on being treated like a tactical operative, she was still just an eight-year-old kid who liked sleeping next to her big brother when we were in a strange place.

Mom came back out of the bathroom, picking up a few stray towels. She leaned over Cassandra's bed first, kissing her forehead. "Goodnight, Cass. Call out if you need anything."

"I will," Cassandra promised, her voice thick with exhaustion. 

Mom walked over to our bed next, smoothing Casey's hair and kissing the top of her head. "Goodnight, Casey. Goodnight, David."

"Goodnight, Mom," I whispered. 

Mom walked over to the adjoining door that connected our room to theirs. Instead of shutting it, she carefully pushed it wide open until it clicked against the wall stop. In the event of an emergency, or if Cassandra needed a glass of water or help shifting her weight in the middle of the night, our parents needed to be able to hear her instantly. 

Once Mom retreated into the other room, Dad reached over and turned off the final lamp on their side, leaving our room illuminated only by the faint orange glow of the streetlights filtering through the heavy curtains.

The room was perfectly quiet, except for the hum of the air-conditioning unit under the window. 

Casey shifted against my side, resting her head on my shoulder. 

"David?" Casey whispered, her voice barely carrying across the dark room. 

"Yeah, Case?" 

"The tactical deployment tomorrow is completely different," Casey mumbled sleepily, her eyes already slipping shut. "We aren't doing any university tours."

"I know," I whispered back. 

"We're taking the Orange Line all the way into the District," Casey added, a small, genuine smile creeping onto her face in the dark. "We're going to the National Zoo."

I smiled, pulling the comforter up a little higher over her shoulders. The stress of the GWU infrastructure analysis was officially over. Tomorrow, we just got to be kids again. 

"I know, Case," I whispered. "Go to sleep. We have a lot of animals to see tomorrow."
