# Part 7: Rocket Burger and the Return Vector – 5:00 PM

At precisely 5:00 PM, the overhead lights in the Space Hall flickered. A polite but firm announcement echoed over the PA system, informing us that the Finch Air and Space Museum was officially closing for the day. 

I gave the Apollo 11 Command Module one last, lingering look before Cassandra nudged my arm, motioning toward the exit. 

We spilled out of the massive glass doors onto Independence Avenue. The rain had finally stopped, leaving the D.C. air feeling like a wet, heavy blanket. The streets were swarming with federal workers pouring out of the surrounding brutalist concrete buildings, eager to begin their evening commutes.

"Dinner," Dad announced, rubbing his stomach. "Before we attempt to navigate the subterranean maze again, we need calories."

"I have a visual on a Rocket Burger," Casey reported instantly. She stood on her tiptoes, pointing past the massive headquarters of the Department of Education. 

Sure enough, tucked into a small plaza on C Street SW and 4th Avenue SW, the familiar glowing neon rocket logo beckoned to us. 

"Perfect," Mom said, grabbing the handles of Cassandra's wheelchair to help push her up the slight incline of the sidewalk. "And it's only two blocks from the street-level elevator for L'Enfant Plaza. Highly efficient."

The Rocket Burger was packed with tourists and bureaucrats, but the fast-paced, loud environment felt comforting. We claimed a corner booth near the windows. Dad went up to the counter and returned balancing a tray loaded with cheeseburgers, heavily salted fries, and sodas. 

We ate quickly, exhausted by the sheer volume of walking, wheeling, and sensory input we had processed throughout the day. I was still buzzing from seeing the Lunar Module, meticulously recounting the orbital mechanics of the Apollo program to Casey, who was dutifully pretending to listen while dipping her fries in ketchup. 

"Alright," Mom sighed, wiping her hands with a napkin and looking out the window at the fading sunlight. "We have a long transit vector ahead of us."

We cleared our trays and walked the two blocks back to the corner of 7th and Maryland. 

The L'Enfant elevator maze was somehow even more chaotic in reverse. We packed into the street-level elevator and rode down to the mezzanine. After passing through the ADA faregates, we executed a tight U-turn to the right to reach the second elevator, taking it down to the upper platform level which serviced the Green and Yellow lines towards Anacostia and Huntington. Exiting that elevator, we made a sharp left to reach the final elevator, riding it down to the Blue and Orange center-island platform.

When the doors opened, an Orange Line train heading toward Vienna was just pulling into the station. 

"Execute!" Cassandra barked, gripping her push-rims and launching herself forward. 

We sprinted across the platform, slipping through the sliding doors just before the warning chimes sounded. We collapsed into the padded vinyl seats, breathing heavily as the train accelerated into the dark tunnel, carrying us away from the federal district and back toward the Virginia suburbs. 

By the time the train finally breached the surface and rolled into the median of Interstate 66, the sun had fully set. The bright fluorescent lights of the Vienna surface station welcomed us back. 

We took the elevator down to the bus bays, boarded the CCT bus, and made the short, exhausted ride back to the motel in Fairfax.
