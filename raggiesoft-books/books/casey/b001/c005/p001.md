# Part 1: Burton Station Road – 1:30 PM

The calendar eventually surrendered.

On the afternoon of December 17th, the moment my final exam at the community college concluded, my mother was waiting for me in the parking lot. But Wendy Vance didn't drive us back to her house. She bypassed the highway exits for the main public terminal of Norfolk International Airport and instead navigated her standard sedan down Burton Station Road, pulling up to the discrete, heavily gated General Aviation terminal.

I sat in the passenger seat, staring through the chain-link fence at the private tarmac. With Kristine’s toxic surveillance permanently eradicated from my life, Cassandra had absolutely zero reason to hide her leverage. She had completely abandoned the commercial charade.

A sleek, twin-engine corporate jet touched down and taxied directly toward our gated perimeter.

When the cabin door opened and the hydraulic accessible ramp deployed, Casey practically bounced down to the tarmac. She was clutching her duffel bag, her eyes wide with absolute, unfiltered awe. It was her first time ever flying on a private corporate jet, and the sheer scale of the luxury had left my seventeen-year-old sister completely stunned.

Cassandra wheeled herself down the ramp right behind her, looking entirely composed and unbothered, her heavy messenger bag resting on her lap.

I jumped out of Mom's car and met them at the gate. Casey collided with me, wrapping her arms fiercely around my neck for a massive, deep-pressure hug. The physical contact sent a shockwave of relief through my system. The twenty-one days of isolation were officially over.

"This beats commercial First Class by a mile," Casey rasped into my shoulder, vibrating with excitement.

"Quantum's logistics desk actually wanted to route our flight path into PVG over in Chesapeake," Cassandra noted casually, rolling up to the car and offering Mom a warm, familiar smile. "But I told them ORF was vastly superior. PVG is too far of a drive for you, Mom, especially with the High Rise Bridge traffic. Burton Station Road is much closer to home."

"Well, I appreciate the executive override," Mom laughed, helping us load the bags into the trunk.

We drove back to Virginia Beach, the east-coast consolidation of our pack finally underway. But the perimeter wasn't quite complete yet.
