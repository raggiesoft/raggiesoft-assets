# Part 2: The Flight Data – 2:00 PM

The drive back to Virginia Beach operated on a completely different frequency than the tense, quiet car rides of November.

With Mom navigating the afternoon traffic and Cassandra sitting comfortably in the front passenger seat, the backseat belonged entirely to me and Casey. The moment the car doors closed, Casey leaned heavily against my shoulder, seeking her baseline physical grounding while simultaneously offloading a massive stream of rapid-fire data.

She was practically vibrating with excitement, her hands animating every single detail of the cross-country flight.

"David, you cannot even comprehend the logistical efficiency," Casey began, her voice slightly raspy but moving at lightning speed. "On Thanksgiving, we had to wait in that massive TSA line, take our shoes off, and sit at the gate with hundreds of people. The ambient noise alone was exhausting. But today? We just rolled straight up to the plane. There was zero friction."

I laughed, wrapping my arm around her and pulling her closer. "Zero friction sounds exactly like a Cass operation."

"It's entirely different architecture inside, too," Casey continued, her dark eyes wide as she twisted slightly to look at me. "Commercial First Class is just wider seats in rows. But the corporate jet has these massive, swiveling leather captain's chairs. You can actually turn around and face a mahogany table. Cass had her laptop out, doing Quantum server reviews, and I had an entire couch to myself in the back!"

From the front seat, Cassandra glanced in the rearview mirror, offering a fond, satisfied smile as she listened to her younger sister break down the executive perks.

"And the cabin pressure," Casey added, tapping my arm to emphasize the structural difference. "It's pressurized to a much lower altitude than a standard commercial airliner. My ears didn't pop once. There were no overhead announcements interrupting us, no strangers bumping into the armrests. Just total, uninterrupted quiet all the way across the country."

"Did they at least give you those tiny bags of pretzels?" I joked, leaning my head against hers.

Casey scoffed playfully, waving the idea away. "David, there was a dedicated flight attendant. For just the two of us. She brought out actual, hot catering on ceramic plates. I had a perfectly grilled chicken wrap and fresh fruit while we flew over the Rocky Mountains. It was flawless."

I just sat there, smiling and absorbing her sheer, unfiltered joy. Hearing her completely dissect the mechanical and hospitality differences between the two flights was the exact anchor my system had been starving for. She wasn't carrying an ounce of the fear or hesitation that Kristine had forced into our environment just three weeks prior.

"Well, get used to the layout, Case," Cassandra called back from the front seat, her tone completely matter-of-fact. "Because when we fly back to Washington on Monday, all four of us are getting on that exact same plane."

Casey gasped, grabbing my hand and squeezing it tightly. "You and Liz are going to love it," she promised fiercely. "It's the ultimate perimeter."
