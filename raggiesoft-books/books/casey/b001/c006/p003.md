# Part 3: The Sovereign Airspace – 11:30 AM (EST)

Once we reached cruising altitude, the seatbelt sign chimed off, and the true scale of the aircraft's interior became apparent. It didn't feel like a plane; it felt like a high-end corporate boardroom wrapped in cream-colored leather and polished mahogany.

Cassandra didn't waste a single minute. She wheeled herself away from the forward lockdown track and positioned her chair at the head of the massive mid-cabin conference table, flipping open her silver laptop.

"David, Elizabeth, grab a seat," Cass instructed, her tone dropping into her familiar, efficient baseline. "We need to finalize your educational logistics before we land."

Liz and I sank into the plush leather captain's chairs across from her.

"You are both enrolling at Northwest Pacific University, specifically the main Seattle campus, for the Winter Quarter starting in January," Cass stated, pulling up a web browser. "I am paying your out-of-state tuition entirely out-of-pocket, so we don't have to wait a year for residency requirements. We just need to get your applications filed today."

Liz stared at Cassandra’s screen, completely bewildered. "Wait... are we online right now? Up here?"

"Enterprise-grade, in-flight satellite Wi-Fi," Casey chimed in from the window seat, entirely used to her sister's bleeding-edge tech ecosystem. "Quantum doesn't do dead zones."

Liz just shook her head in quiet awe. Cass efficiently guided us through the NPU portal, helping us set up our student accounts and walking us through the application forms. When it came to the permanent residence section, she dictated her Federal Way apartment address for both of us, officially locking in our new West Coast perimeter. As soon as the forms were complete, Cass pulled a sleek card from her wallet and paid both application fees directly over the secure connection.

By the time the applications were submitted, the smell of roasted chicken and fresh vegetables began wafting from the forward galley.

It was lunchtime. For Liz and me, eating a hot, high-end catered meal on a private jet was a surreal, staggering first. For Casey, it was just her second time experiencing Quantum's aviation hospitality, but she dug in with just as much enthusiasm.

As I ate, I looked around the mahogany table. Liz was sitting next to me, our knees touching. Casey was happily chatting about her high school classes. Cassandra was sipping a sparkling water, reviewing a deployment spreadsheet. My internal processor ran a quick diagnostic and returned absolute, profound peace. The pack was reunited for good.

And the absolute best part? There was no Kristine.

I let out a sudden, involuntary laugh.

"What's so funny?" Liz asked, pausing with her fork halfway to her mouth.

"I just pictured Kristine's face," I chuckled, shaking my head. "If she could see me right now. She probably thought I'd be stuck flying commercial into SEA in coach. Instead, I'm eating roasted chicken on a private corporate jet flying direct to Boeing Field."

Cassandra offered a sharp, satisfied smirk without looking up from her screen. "Let her imagine whatever she wants. She no longer exists on our grid."

Cass wiped her mouth with a linen napkin and pushed her plate aside. "Alright, I have to actually get some work done for the rest of this flight. When we land at BFI, Quantum has a wheelchair-accessible van waiting on the tarmac to take us down to the Federal Way apartment."

"Is that how you normally get around?" I asked, looking at the sheer luxury of our current setup. "A private driver in a customized van?"

"Absolutely not," Cass replied immediately, her disdain for operational friction bleeding through. "There are rumors floating around Building 33 that I'm being groomed as William Keswick's heir, which is ridiculous. I'm twenty-six years old and I've only been at the company for four years. But even if I had access to a dedicated driver every day, I wouldn't use it. You have to book it, schedule it, and hope dispatch doesn't screw up the timing. I much prefer the city bus. I can just show up at the bus stop on my own schedule, deploy the ramp, and ride. True autonomy doesn't require a reservation."
