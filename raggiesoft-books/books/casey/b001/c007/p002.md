# Part 2: Comfort Food and The Ledger – 6:15 PM

Casey eagerly grabbed one end of the heavy sofa cushions while Liz took the other. Together, we dragged the mattress frame out from the depths of the couch. The metal springs groaned, unfolding to take up nearly the entire footprint of the small living room.

"I think we just leave this out permanently," I suggested, tossing a heavy blanket over the mattress. "It’s going to be a tight squeeze in this apartment for the next few weeks, but it saves us having to fold it up every single morning."

"Agreed," Liz smiled, smoothing the sheets down. "We'll see how apartment living goes. I honestly don't care if it takes up the whole floor, as long as we're all together."

Right on cue, Cassandra wheeled herself out of the kitchen, carefully balancing a tray on her lap.

"Dinner is served," she announced, a warm, genuine smile breaking across her face.

She handed out massive, steaming bowls of the ultimate childhood comfort food: thick macaroni and cheese mixed with sliced, cheesy hot dogs. But it was the drinks she passed out next that made my processor stall for a fraction of a second. She handed me a tall, ice-cold glass of whole milk.

For the last three months, Kristine’s lactose intolerance had dictated every item in the pantry, forcing me to eat dry cereal and completely eliminating milk from my diet. Sitting on the edge of the pulled-out sofa bed, holding a glass of milk and a bowl of mac and cheese, the stark contrast between Kristine’s restrictive control and my sister's unconditional hospitality hit me like a wave. I took a long drink, feeling a profound sense of relief wash over my system.

We all sat cross-legged on the edge of the mattress and the floor, eating in comfortable, easy silence for a few minutes before Cassandra shifted into her logistical baseline.

"Now that we are all physically consolidated, we need to update the financial architecture," Cassandra explained, setting her bowl on the end table. She looked directly at me. "David, I am officially stopping the ACH transfers."

I blinked, momentarily surprised. While I had been isolated in Virginia, she had been using Quantum's internal payroll portal to automatically route a portion of her pay directly into my checking account via ACH transfer.

"I am not cutting off your support," Cassandra clarified instantly, sensing my momentary confusion. "This is strictly a maneuver to bypass the IRS. Because the annual total I was sending you exceeded the IRS gift tax exclusion limit for 2005, my CPA firm had to silently file Form 709 in the background. Every dollar over the limit deducts the excess from my lifetime exemption. I refuse to needlessly burn through that cap."

"So how does it work now?" Liz asked, genuinely fascinated by how Cassandra treated the federal tax code like a solvable software bug.

"Now that David is living under my roof, the legal classification changes," Cass explained smoothly. "I will simply absorb your daily living expenses directly at the point of sale. However, I am not always going to be at your side. You and Liz will be on the NPU campus taking classes, and you will need to buy lunch, supplies, and your monthly TAPS Passes."

Cassandra pulled two sleek, metallic cards from her pocket and set them on the table.

"I have already ordered authorized user cards in both of your names," Cass stated. "They are linked directly to my primary accounts. If you need something, you swipe the card. It bypasses the gift tax entirely and ensures you maintain total operational autonomy when I am at work."

"Thank you, Cass," I said softly, completely overwhelmed by the sheer, friction-less safety net she had engineered for us.

"Speaking of school," Casey chimed in proudly, practically vibrating with excitement as she scooped up a bite of hot dogs. "I got my progress reports back yesterday. Straight A's in every single class. My calculus teacher said I have the highest average in the district."

"That is incredible, Case!" Liz beamed, reaching over to give Casey a high-five. "You are going to completely destroy college next year."

"I expect nothing less," Cass smiled, radiating absolute pride for our younger sister.

"What about you, Cass?" I asked, leaning back against the sofa cushions. "What's going on at Quantum? Any big deployments?"

Cassandra, who works in Building 33 alongside Quantum's founder William Keswick, let out a rare, exasperated sigh and rolled her eyes.

"The deployments are fine," she dismissed, waving her hand. "It is the internal rumor mill that is currently malfunctioning. Half the executive suite is whispering that Keswick is secretly grooming me to be the next CEO of Quantum."

Liz choked slightly on her milk, her eyes going wide. "Are they serious?"

"They are, but I refuse to believe it," Cass stated flatly, shaking her head at the sheer mathematical improbability of the gossip. "I am twenty-six years old. I have only been with the company for four years. The idea that the board of directors would hand the keys to the largest tech empire on the planet to someone in her twenties is completely absurd. They are just rumors."

I watched my older sister brush off the idea with pure, pragmatic logic. She might not have believed it, but as I looked at the flawlessly executed perimeter she had built around our pack—navigating family law, federal tax codes, and cross-country logistics without breaking a sweat—I knew exactly why William Keswick was watching her. If anyone was capable of running the empire, it was Cassandra Vance.
