# Part 4: The Blackout – 9:30 PM

By 9:30 PM, the quiet hum of the television was the only light source in the living room.

Outside, the rain had been drumming a steady, predictable rhythm against the apartment windows. At first, it was just the standard, comforting drizzle of the Pacific Northwest winter. But within minutes, the atmospheric pressure violently shifted. The gentle tapping rapidly escalated into a deafening, torrential downpour that violently battered the glass.

The sudden, intense spike in auditory sensory input caused my processor to instantly tense. Beside me on the sofa bed, Casey flinched, her grip tightening on my arm as the roar of the wind rattling the window panes grew significantly louder.

Then, a massive, concussive crash echoed from the street outside, followed immediately by the sharp, electric crack of a transformer exploding.

The television died instantly. The apartment plunged into absolute, pitch-black darkness.

The sudden, violent sensory overload and the total loss of visual data pushed Casey over the edge. She let out a sharp, terrified gasp, burying her face into my chest and immediately starting to cry. Her system simply couldn't process the sudden chaos, and she shook violently against my ribs.

"I've got you, Case, it's okay," I whispered rapidly, wrapping both of my arms around her trembling shoulders, trying to provide as much deep-pressure grounding as I could in the dark.

"Nobody panic," Cassandra’s voice rang out. It was perfectly steady, cutting through the ambient roar of the storm with absolute executive authority. "It's just a blown transformer."

"It's so loud," Casey sobbed into my shirt, terrified by the sheer volume of the storm outside.

Cassandra didn't hesitate. In a crisis, standard operational protocols were immediately overridden by the prime directive: the absolute physical and emotional security of the pack.

"Safety comes first, and we are staying together," Cassandra commanded in the darkness. "Everyone into my bedroom. Right now."

Liz used the faint backlight of her cell phone to illuminate the floor. We carefully untangled ourselves from the sofa bed blankets. I kept one arm securely wrapped around Casey, guiding her down the dark hallway while Liz illuminated the path for Cassandra’s chair.

We filed into the master suite, the sprawling king-sized mattress waiting in the center of the room.

I sat down near the center of the bed, pulling Casey up right beside me. Liz climbed in on my other side, while Cassandra smoothly locked the brakes on her chair and transferred herself onto the edge of the mattress. Within seconds, all four of us were piled onto the massive bed, enveloped in the heavy wool blankets.

Casey was still crying softly, clinging to my arm. Cassandra reached out in the dim light, resting her hand firmly on Casey’s shoulder.

"Casey, listen to me," Cassandra said gently, her voice an anchor in the dark room. "This is an emergency protocol. We are doing this tonight because the power grid failed and safety comes first. But I need you to understand that this is an exception."

Casey sniffled, looking up at our older sister.

"This will not become the norm," Cassandra reminded her firmly, maintaining the absolute legal boundary required to keep our father away from us. "The rules still apply. We are only doing this tonight because of the storm."

Casey wiped her wet cheeks with the back of her hand. She was sad, her system still craving the routine guarantee of always having this proximity, but her logical brain processed the math. She knew Cassandra wasn't rejecting her; she was protecting the perimeter from Tom's vindictive legal threats.

"I understand," Casey cried softly, giving a small, resilient nod. "Just for tonight."

"Just for tonight," Cassandra agreed, her voice softening into pure affection. She leaned back against the headboard, settling into the mattress. "Now come here."

The pack completely consolidated. Casey pressed her back flush against my chest, her breathing hitching as the tears slowly subsided. I wrapped my arms securely around her, while Liz curled up tightly against my back. Cassandra rested against my shoulder, completing the circuit.

The weight and proximity of all four of us acting as a single, grounded unit provided the ultimate deep-pressure regulation. The chaos of the outside world simply ceased to exist.

Outside the bedroom walls, the wind howled and the Pacific Northwest rain battered the glass harder and heavier than before. But inside the dark apartment, entirely insulated by the unconditional love of the pack, the ambient noise faded into a steady lullaby. Surrounded by my sisters and my best friend, my processor finally went quiet, and we slowly drifted off to sleep.
