# Part 7: The Contract Execution – 3:00 PM

We left the property and began the short trek back toward the Quantum campus. Cassandra had mapped out a route to the bus stops along NE 40th Street, planning to catch the transit grid back toward the Overlake Transit Center so we could begin the long journey back to Federal Way.

We hadn't even made it to the main intersection before the low hum of a luxury engine pulled up alongside us.

Marcus rolled down the passenger window of his black sedan, looking entirely frantic. "Ms. Vance! Hold on a second!"

Cassandra squeezed her brakes, bringing her chair to a smooth stop on the sidewalk. She looked at the agent with mild amusement. "Is there a problem, Marcus?"

"The problem is that I am not letting a 1.5 million dollar all-cash offer get on a King County public bus," Marcus stated, throwing the car into park and stepping out. "My office is in Downtown Bellevue. I want to generate this paperwork immediately before anyone else has a chance to submit another bid. Please, let me drive you."

Cassandra looked up at me, an unmistakable glint of triumph in her eyes. The nuclear strike had worked perfectly; she had completely monopolized the agent's attention.

"Transport mode, David," Cassandra instructed.

For the second time in an hour, I collapsed the manual chair and loaded it into the trunk, while Liz and I transferred Cassandra smoothly into the front seat of Marcus's sedan. Casey and Liz piled into the back with me, and Marcus immediately merged onto SR-520 West, speeding us straight toward the towering glass skyline of Downtown Bellevue.

Ten minutes later, we were sitting in a sleek, high-end real estate office on the fourteenth floor.

Marcus didn't even offer us coffee. He practically sprinted to his desk, booted up his computer, and began frantically generating the standard Purchase and Sale Agreement.

"Let's review the terms to ensure the legal framework is absolute," Cassandra said, wheeling herself directly up to his desk to oversee the drafting process.

"Purchase price, \$1,500,000," Marcus read aloud, typing rapidly. "Earnest money... let's set it high to show absolute intent. One hundred thousand dollars, held in escrow."

"Done," Cassandra agreed without blinking.

"Now, the contingencies," Marcus said, looking over his monitor. "I know you want to waive the inspection, but I just have to officially advise you as your agent that waiving it means you assume all structural risks—"

"Marcus, I am bringing a sledgehammer to this property the exact second I am handed the keys," Cassandra interrupted, her tone polite but entirely unyielding. "I am stripping the bathrooms to the studs, leveling the floors, widening every single doorframe to thirty-six inches, and completely rebuilding the interior architecture to accommodate my wheelchair. An inspector telling me the drywall has a crack in it is a waste of my time. Waive the inspection addendum. Waive the financing contingency. Make it a clean, frictionless transfer."

"Understood," Marcus nodded, deleting the standard contingency clauses from the digital contract.

A few moments later, the office printer whirred to life, spitting out the heavily modified, razor-thin Purchase and Sale Agreement. Marcus laid the pages out on the desk and handed Cassandra a heavy, blue-ink pen.

Cassandra reviewed the legal jargon with the same precise, calculating focus she used for Quantum deployment spreadsheets. Finding no errors, she smoothly signed her name on the buyer's line and initialed the waivers. Marcus immediately countersigned as her representative.

He didn't wait for a courier or draft an email. He took the signed documents directly to the heavy, industrial fax machine in the corner of the office—the absolute standard for secure real estate transactions in 2004.

We all sat in silence, listening to the mechanical process. The dial tone hummed through the office speaker, followed by the aggressive, high-pitched screech of the digital handshake. The machine slowly pulled the signed contract through the feeder, transmitting Cassandra's devastating financial strike directly to the seller's agent.

The machine beeped, flashing a green *Transmission Successful* light.

Marcus let out a long breath and leaned back against the copy machine. "Alright. The offer is in their hands. They have a fiduciary duty to present it to the seller immediately."

Cassandra settled back into her chair, perfectly calm, perfectly anchored. She looked over at me, Liz, and Casey, offering a quiet, confident smile.

Now, we waited.
