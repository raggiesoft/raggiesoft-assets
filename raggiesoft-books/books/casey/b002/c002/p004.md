# Part 4: The Global Broadcast – 11:00 AM

The heavy, frosted-glass double doors of the boardroom swung open, and the hum of executive applause echoed down the carpeted hallway of Building 33.

Cassandra wheeled herself smoothly into the antechamber, Steve Ballantine and William Keswick walking on either side of her, while David held his customary position directly behind her chair. Steve was practically beaming, his high-energy presence radiating satisfaction, while William adjusted his glasses with a quiet, contemplative smile.

The quiet solitude of the antechamber lasted less than five seconds.

The moment the doors locked behind them, Quantum’s executive machinery descended like a synchronized storm. A flurry of senior vice presidents, corporate legal counsel, and public relations directors swarmed the hallway, clutching glowing MagnaByte mPads and encrypted tablets.

"The board vote is officially certified," the Chief Legal Officer announced, stepping alongside Cassandra’s wheelchair with a stack of digital signature forms. "We are pushing the regulatory disclosures to the Securities and Exchange Commission immediately. Form 8-K is queued for transmission."

"Do it," Cassandra instructed calmly, authorizing the digital signature with a quick tap of her thumb.

Within seconds, the institutional dominoes began to fall across the global financial network.

At 11:05 AM, the SEC filings hit the wire. Simultaneously, the Quantum News Center published the official press release to every major news outlet across the world, formally announcing that Steven Ballantine would retire the following month, William Keswick would transition to a senior technology advisory role, and thirty-six-year-old Cassandra Vance had been unanimously selected by the Board of Directors to take the helm as President and Chief Executive Officer.

Cassandra wheeled herself back into the privacy of her corner office, with David moving smoothly to his dedicated workstation. She triggered a remote on her desk, and three high-definition monitors mounted to the wall flashed to life, displaying live feeds from CNBC, Bloomberg, and financial ticker networks.

For three tense minutes, the market digested the news. Analysts on television debated the sudden, bold hardware retreat—the cancellation of the Quantum Phone—and the aggressive shift toward cross-platform enterprise software and cloud infrastructure.

Then, the ticker for Quantum (QNTM) ignited in bright green.

Wall Street responded not with panic, but with a massive, surging vote of confidence. The stock price spiked sharply upward on the Bloomberg terminals. Institutional investors saw past the death of a failing smartphone line, recognizing the staggering, recurring profit margins of the cloud-and-subscription model Cassandra had engineered.

A PR Director stepped into the office, offering Cassandra a printed draft. "Ms. Vance, we have drafted the global press statement and the internal company-wide email for your review. It highlights your credentials and includes standard quotes on continuity—"

Cassandra held up a single hand, stopping the director mid-sentence.

"Thank you, but I write my own communications," Cassandra said politely.

She turned her chair toward her mahogany desk, snapped her laptop open, and pulled up an empty compose window. She bypassed the sanitized corporate buzzwords, the corporate comms templates, and the polished marketing jargon.

She looked at her laptop screen, thinking of the engineering floors in Building 35, the QA testers sitting in dark testing labs, the shuttle drivers, and the junior developers debugging C# code in the rain.

She typed out the message directly from her keyboard:

**From:** Cassandra Vance

**To:** All Employees

**Subject:** The Next Chapter of Quantum

Today, the Board of Directors has entrusted me with the role of CEO. It is an incredible honor, but more importantly, it is an absolute mandate to remove the friction from your daily workflows.

Steve Ballantine and William Keswick built a foundation that revolutionized the digital age. Our job now is to ensure that foundation can support the future. We are no longer going to waste our energy or resources fighting a consumer hardware war we cannot win. Instead, we are going to build the enterprise software, cloud infrastructure, and developer tools that power every device on the planet, regardless of whose logo is stamped on the glass.

My office door in Building 33 remains open, but as many of you know, you are far more likely to find me out in the courtyards or down in the engineering bullpens. Let's get to work.

Cassandra hit send.

Without a single PR filter, the email deployed simultaneously to over ninety thousand Quantum employees across every continent on Earth.

Within seconds, internal chat channels, team message boards, and campus email servers across Redmond, Bellevue, Austin, London, and Tokyo lit up with an outpouring of excitement. The floorboards had heard their CEO—and the new era had officially begun.
