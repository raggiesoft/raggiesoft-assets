# Part 10: The Express Route – 5:15 PM

Cassandra spent the entire afternoon holding court in the Bellevue communal workspace. She didn't just dispense executive wisdom; she actively engaged with the newest generation of Quantum engineers, absorbing their modern frameworks and asking incisive, highly technical questions. By the time 5:00 PM rolled around, her piano-black laptop was filled with notes on new asynchronous routing protocols and database mappers.

As the winter sun dipped below the Seattle skyline, casting long shadows across the Bellevue towers, she finally unmounted her laptop and slipped it into her bag. It was time to head home to David, Liz, and Casey.

Cassandra joined the massive exodus of Quantum employees pouring out of the glass towers at the end of the workday. There were no security guards clearing a path for her, and she was completely unescorted by PR teams or executive handlers. She simply blended into the sea of blue corporate badges, her manual power-assist wheelchair humming quietly as she rolled along the paved sidewalks toward the Bellevue Transit Center.

The transit hub was operating at peak rush-hour capacity. Cassandra navigated through the dense crowd and positioned herself near the front of the queue at the designated bay for the northbound express routes, waiting patiently for whichever TAPS bus arrived first: the 564 or the 565.

A few yards away, standing near a concrete pillar, a pair of newer mid-level developers were watching her with wide, skeptical eyes.

"Is that actually Cassandra Vance?" one of them whispered, clutching his messenger bag. "The global email just went out this morning. She's the CEO-designate. Why is she waiting at a public bus stop?"

"I don't know," the other developer muttered, glancing around the plaza as if looking for hidden cameras. "It has to be a publicity stunt, right? Like, some kind of 'Woman of the People' photo op? Where is her black car? Where is her security detail?"

A gray-haired database architect—one of the legacy managers who had greeted Cassandra earlier that afternoon—happened to be standing right behind them. He let out a low, amused chuckle.

"You guys really are new here," the veteran architect said, shaking his head. "There are no cameras. It’s not a stunt. I've known Cass since she rolled into her first orientation back in the summer of 2000. She has taken the city bus every single day of her career."

The newer developers stared at him in disbelief. "But she's about to run a trillion-dollar company."

"And she absolutely despises traffic," the older architect smiled, adjusting his coat against the January chill. "She hates having to book rides with executive dispatch, and she hates waiting for chauffeurs. If you want to survive at Quantum, learn this right now: the CEO doesn't wait for a motorcade. She taps her transit pass and rides."

Right on cue, the heavy, articulated TAPS Express 564 bus hissed to a halt at the curb.

The doors swung open, and the driver immediately hit the switch to deploy the mechanical ramp. Cassandra rolled up the incline, completely ignoring the staring commuters. She tapped her blue TAPS Pass against the reader, the machine letting out a sharp, confirming beep.

She wheeled herself into the designated ADA bay, locked her brakes with a swift, practiced motion, and pulled out her MagnaByte mPhone to check a message from David.

As the 564 bus merged onto Interstate 405 North, effortlessly bypassing the gridlocked surface streets before banking onto the SR-520 corridor toward Overlake, Cassandra looked out the window at the dark, cold waters of Lake Washington.

The Board of Directors loved her, Wall Street had surged on her announcement, and the employees respected her because she was an architect who genuinely cared about the people building the foundation. But as the bus sped toward her final stop in Redmond, just three flat, paved blocks from her front door, none of that corporate gravity mattered.
