# Part 2: The Executive Reality – 3:30 PM

I sat on the edge of the hotel bed, my eyes locked on the silver flip phone resting on the small table. Casey was still pressed flush against my side, her breathing finally evening out as the deep-pressure contact did its job.

To my left, Cassandra sat perfectly still in her travel chair, letting me process the monumental scale of the safety net she had just deployed. My internal processor, which had been violently redlining since the kitchen ambush, began to physically cool down. I mentally looped a heavy, predictable rhythm in my head, letting the steady beat regulate my system.

"I can't choose," I finally whispered, the sheer frustration of the binary decision making my chest ache. "I want both of my options, Cass. I want you and Casey, and I want Liz."

Cassandra’s expression softened. "I know you do, David. And it is completely unfair that geography is forcing you to make a choice tonight. But I need you to understand something critical: neither of these paths leads to a dead end. This is not a permanent severance."

Cassandra leaned forward, resting her arms on the wheels of her chair.

"If you move into my apartment in Federal Way," she explained, shifting smoothly back into her logistical baseline, "we will share my bed. You will have all the physical pressure and proximity you need to regulate your system every single night. And the moment Casey turns eighteen this May, she will join us."

I nodded slowly, understanding the legal firewall she was maintaining. Mom had worked meticulously with the lawyers to legally delegate custody to Cassandra, ensuring our father couldn't suddenly try to assert parental rights. Cassandra guarded that court order with absolute precision; she wasn't going to risk a single technicality by letting a seventeen-year-old share a bed with her nineteen-year-old brother, no matter how innocent the sibling dynamic was.

"And what about Liz?" I asked, my voice cracking slightly.

"If you choose Federal Way, Liz is always welcome," Cassandra promised instantly. "She needs to finish out her current Fall semester, and her Spring 2005 semester at Shenandoah Valley University. But the moment she is ready, whether she graduates from SVU or decides to transfer to Northwest Pacific University, she can move into our apartment. Quantum compensates me very well, David. I will pay her out-of-state NPU tuition completely out-of-pocket."

My brain stalled. "Wait. Out-of-state tuition? Cass, that's tens of thousands of dollars. You can't just pay that."

Cassandra offered a small, calm smile. "David, do you actually know what I do at Quantum?"

"You work in Building 33," I said, reciting the fact I had known for years. "You're in management."

"Building 33 is the executive suite, David," Cassandra clarified gently, her tone completely matter-of-fact. "I am on the same floor as William Keswick. I am the youngest executive in the building."

The air in the hotel room seemed to physically shift. My processor completely froze as the data points violently realigned.

I knew exactly who William Keswick was—everyone did. He was the architect of the modern digital age, the billionaire founder of Quantum. I had always known my sister was smart, but I had entirely misunderstood her scale. She hadn't just gotten a good job out in Seattle; she had walked into the most powerful tech corporation on the planet, put her head down, paid her dues, outworked everyone in the room, and taken a seat at the absolute pinnacle of the empire.

I stared at the wheels of her manual chair, realizing for the first time just how much weight she carried. The out-of-state tuition. The last-minute cross-country flights. The ability to pull me out of Portsmouth without a single second of financial hesitation. It wasn't a struggle for her. It was pocket change. She was offering me a blank check because she actually had the absolute power to write it.

"And if you choose to go to Dayton," Cassandra continued, completely unbothered by the realization washing over my face, "and you move in with Liz in Virginia, the door to Washington State stays wide open. Dayton is barely an hour from SVU, so your commute to Harrisonburg is easy. You two can move out to the west coast together whenever you are ready. And until then, I promise you, on my life, that we will see each other every single break. I will either fly you and Liz to Seattle, or I will fly Casey and myself to the east coast. You are not losing anyone."

I looked down at my hands. The logistics in Dayton, Virginia, were incredibly appealing. Liz and I shared the exact same unconditional frequency. If I went to the Shenandoah Valley, we would share a bed, enveloped in the same fierce, protective affection I got from my sisters. Because she wasn't blood, Liz and I had already crossed the line into a sexual relationship, and since she was strictly managing her birth control, it was a completely safe, stress-free dynamic. But my processor wasn't calculating sex as the priority variable. The true gravity pulling me toward Dayton was the absolute, undeniable guarantee of Liz's deep-pressure grounding, combined with the fact that my powerful older sister was entirely underwriting the operation.

I was standing at the ultimate crossroads.

I could board a plane on Sunday, leave the east coast entirely, and build a new perimeter in the shadow of NPU with the two sisters who had literally rescued me from the void. Or, I could go to Dayton, anchor myself at SVU, and fall back into the arms of the childhood best friend who knew my baseline flawlessly.

I gently untangled my arm from Casey, who let out a soft, sleepy hum of protest, and stood up from the bed. I walked over to the small table and picked up Cassandra’s silver phone.

I flipped it open, staring at the blank screen, the dial pad waiting for my input. The hotel room was perfectly quiet. I had a choice to make.
