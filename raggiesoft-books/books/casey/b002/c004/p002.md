# Interlude: The Firewall – Wednesday, December 1, 2004 – 2:15 PM

The next three weeks were agonizingly slow. Moving back into Mom’s house in Virginia Beach was the safest logistical move, but my system was completely starved of its primary regulatory anchors. I threw all of my excess processing power into my classes, burying myself in textbooks and study guides to force the clock to move faster. Every single night, I was on the phone with Liz. Her voice became my only tether, talking me down when the isolation felt too heavy, while she powered through her own SVU finals in Dayton.

But the perimeter Cassandra built was stronger than I knew.

I had left my cell phone plugged into the charger on the kitchen counter when I stepped away to use the bathroom. I was washing my hands, the water running over the porcelain sink, completely oblivious to the fact that the screen in the other room had just lit up with Kristine Mercer’s name.

Wendy Vance was wiping down the kitchen island when the phone started vibrating against the granite. She glanced at the caller ID.

Cassandra’s tactical briefing from Thanksgiving had been absolute. She had laid out every single one of Kristine’s psychological maneuvers, but the detail that had completely enraged my mother was the kitchen incident. The sheer, predatory audacity of Kristine cooking completely naked in front of Casey—forcing my highly sensitive, youngest sister into an incredibly distressing and inappropriate situation—had crossed a line Wendy was not willing to forgive.

My mother picked up the phone and pressed the green accept button.

"David?" Kristine’s voice drifted through the speaker, coated in that familiar, sugary-sweet register she always used when she wanted something. "I miss you. Can we just talk about—"

"David is not available," Wendy interrupted. Her voice wasn't raised, but it was forged from absolute, freezing steel.

There was a sudden, jarring pause on the other end of the line. "Oh. Hi, Mrs. Vance. I was just hoping to catch David for a minute so we could clear some things up."

"There is nothing to clear up, Kristine," Wendy fired back, her grip tightening on the phone. "And you will not be speaking to my son. Not today, not ever."

"I... excuse me?" Kristine stammered, her customer-service mask slipping as she tried to recalibrate. "I just want to talk to my boyfriend."

"He is not your boyfriend," Wendy stated, every syllable clipped and lethal. "And you listen to me very carefully. Cassandra told me exactly what happened on Thanksgiving. She told me exactly what you did in that apartment."

The silence on Kristine's end was deafening.

"Did you really think I wouldn't find out?" Wendy continued, her maternal fury finally uncoiling. "Did you think you could just strip down naked in the kitchen right in front of Casey and nobody was going to say anything? My youngest daughter was trapped in that room with you, and you intentionally subjected her to that disgusting, inappropriate display just to mark your territory."

"I—that's not—" Kristine tried to stammer out a defense, her voice trembling as the reality of being caught completely leveled her.

"Do not interrupt me," Wendy snapped, bringing the hammer down. "You tried to isolate my son, you tried to manipulate him, and you humiliated my daughter. You are toxic, you are deeply unwell, and you are entirely unwelcome in this family. If you ever try to contact David again, or if you ever come anywhere near him, Cassandra and I will make sure it is the biggest mistake of your life. Lose this number."

I walked out of the bathroom just in time to see my mother pull the phone away from her ear, hit the red 'end call' button with extreme prejudice, and immediately navigate to the settings to permanently block the contact.

She set the phone face-down on the counter, took a deep, steadying breath, and picked her dish towel back up as if she hadn't just completely obliterated my ex-girlfriend.

She looked up and saw me standing in the hallway.

"Spam call," Wendy said smoothly, though the fierce, protective fire was still burning brightly in her eyes. "I took care of it."
