# Part 3: The Honorary Sister – 7:45 PM

By the time the winter sun had fully set over Virginia Beach, the anticipation in Mom's living room had reached a fever pitch.

I was sitting on the sofa with Casey tucked securely against my side. We were watching a movie, but my internal clock was violently counting down the seconds. The twenty-one-day bridge was functionally over, but my system wouldn't fully settle until the final piece of the perimeter was physically in the room.

Suddenly, a double set of headlights swept across the living room blinds.

"She's here," I breathed, my heart hammering against my ribs.

I practically bolted off the cushions. Under any other circumstance, abruptly breaking my physical proximity without warning would have sent Casey’s processor into an immediate tailspin. She relied on me as her primary anchor, and losing that deep-pressure contact usually triggered a wave of intense distress.

But Liz Carter was the single, absolute exception to the rule.

Liz was the only person on the planet who could pull my attention entirely away from Casey without causing my little sister to panic. Because Liz wasn't a threat to the pack—she *was* the pack. Casey didn't view Liz as someone taking her brother away; she viewed Liz as an extension of our safe space. Casey simply smiled, pulling her knees to her chest as I rushed for the front door.

I threw the door open and sprinted down the concrete steps.

Liz put her parents' sedan into park just as the heavy air brakes of a rented moving truck hissed loudly behind her, signaling that her mother and father had successfully completed the convoy from the Shenandoah Valley.

Liz threw her car door open and ran across the driveway. We collided in the cold night air, her arms wrapping fiercely around my neck, pulling me into a breathless, desperate hug. The sheer relief of feeling her in my arms sent a massive, regulating shockwave through my entire system.

"I've got you," Liz whispered fiercely into my ear, burying her face in my shoulder. "I told you I was coming."

"I missed you so much," I choked out, refusing to let her go.

When we finally pulled apart, the rest of the family was waiting on the porch. The dynamic was seamless. Even though Liz shared absolutely zero blood relation with us, the Vance family treated her exactly like a sister.

Liz jogged up the steps and immediately knelt down to wrap Casey in a massive hug, which Casey happily returned. Cassandra wheeled forward, offering Liz a warm, deeply affectionate embrace, while my mother walked down the steps to greet Mr. and Mrs. Carter with open arms.

The rest of the evening was a blur of joyous, insulated family time. We hauled a small mountain of delivery pizza boxes into the kitchen to feed both families. The Carters were incredibly supportive, treating Liz's cross-country move not as a disruption, but as the natural, inevitable evolution of our lives.

As we all crowded into the living room with our paper plates, the layout of the pack naturally engaged. I sat on the floor, my back resting against the front of the sofa. Casey immediately sat down on my left side, leaning her weight against my ribs, while Liz sat on my right, resting her head on my shoulder.

I was perfectly sandwiched between my favorite girl and my best friend, eating a slice of pizza while listening to Cassandra and Mr. Carter discuss the logistics of the moving truck.

The perimeter was locked. My system was flawless. For the first time in what felt like a lifetime, I had absolutely everything I needed right in the palm of my hand.
