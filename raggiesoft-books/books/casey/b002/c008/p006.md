# Part 6: The Redmond Compound Acquisition – 2:00 PM

The yellow cab pulled onto the sprawling, immaculately landscaped grounds of the Quantum campus. Instead of directing the driver to the residential address, Cassandra had him pull up directly in front of the massive, glass-fronted architecture of Building 33.

"Why are we stopping at your office?" Casey asked as I paid the driver and hopped out to grab the wheelchair from the trunk.

"Because a map only provides theoretical data," Cassandra explained, waiting for me to lock her wheels into place before I transferred her into the seat. "I need to physically test the commute perimeter."

Once Cassandra was secure, we began the walk. The infrastructure was flawless—wide, fully paved sidewalks with perfect curb cutouts. Cassandra wheeled herself effortlessly under the heavy canopy of Pacific Northwest evergreens, maintaining a brisk, steady pace. Exactly eight minutes later, the commercial zoning of the tech campus faded seamlessly into a quiet, heavily wooded residential cul-de-sac.

A sleek black luxury sedan was parked in the driveway of the property. A man in a sharp, tailored suit stood on the front porch, reviewing a dossier on his clipboard. He looked up as we approached, his eyes darting to the empty driveway and then back to us, visibly surprised that a high-level Quantum executive hadn't arrived via a private town car.

"Ms. Vance?" the agent asked, stepping down the walkway to greet us. "I'm Marcus. I assumed you would be driving over."

"I prefer to test my deployment routes manually," Cassandra replied smoothly, shaking his hand. "This is my brother David, my sister Casey, and Liz. Let's see the property."

Marcus nodded, pulling out his keys. "It's a beautiful property—a sprawling, 3,800-square-foot Pacific Northwest rambler situated right on the edge of the Quantum campus. The seller currently has it listed for \$745,000. However, I need to be completely transparent: because it is dangerously underpriced during a massive Eastside tech money surge, a bidding war is absolutely guaranteed."

He unlocked the front door and pushed it open, wincing slightly as he looked at the narrow entryway and the sunken living room. "Ms. Vance, I must warn you, the current layout is entirely inaccessible for your chair."

"That is completely irrelevant," Cassandra stated, locking her wheels at the base of the front porch. She looked up at me and held out her arms. "David, transport mode."

I grinned, crouching down in front of her chair. Just like we used to do when she needed to get up the stairs in our childhood home, Cassandra leaned forward and wrapped her arms securely around my neck. I hooked my arms under her legs and stood up, effortlessly carrying my older sister piggyback into the house.

For the next twenty minutes, Marcus gave a detailed tour of the property while Cassandra, speaking from her perch on my back, systematically verbally demolished it.

"The structural bones are excellent," Marcus pointed out as we walked through a narrow hallway into the master bathroom.

"The bones are fine, but the drywall is gone," Cassandra corrected, her executive baseline running the architectural math. "We are going to violently gut the interior for commercial-grade ADA retrofitting. Every doorframe will be widened to thirty-six inches. The sunken living room floor gets raised and leveled. This bathroom will be stripped to the studs to install a zero-entry, roll-in shower. The condition of the plumbing or the roof is entirely irrelevant to me."

Marcus blinked, quickly scribbling notes on his clipboard as he realized he wasn't dealing with a standard homebuyer. He was dealing with a corporate tactician.

When the tour concluded, I carried Cass back out to the front porch and gently lowered her back into her manual chair.

"Alright, Marcus," Cassandra said, adjusting her sweater. "I want the property. Draft the paperwork. We will execute the transaction via straight cash with zero financing or appraisals. Furthermore, because I am completely gutting the interior, I legally waive all inspection contingencies."

Marcus nodded slowly, processing the aggressive play. "Waiving the inspection will definitely strengthen the offer. But as I mentioned, we are facing heavy resistance. There are currently seven competing offers, including two all-cash tech couples and five aggressive conventional mortgages with escalation clauses."

"I am willing to put in a cash offer for one million dollars right now," Cassandra countered, setting her baseline.

Marcus shook his head apologetically. "It won't be enough, Ms. Vance. I've run the math, and the winning bid will need to clear at least \$1.1 million. I was preparing to suggest we draft a highly competitive offer at \$1.15 million to win by a safe, calculated margin."

Cassandra stared at the agent for a fraction of a second. She looked at the house, then looked at me, Casey, and Liz standing securely by her side.

"No," Cassandra vetoed immediately, flatly rejecting the incremental approach. "I refuse to leave my family's perimeter to a fraction of a percent of risk."

She pulled her cell phone from her purse, her tone turning devastatingly corporate.

"Rewrite the contract, Marcus," Cassandra ordered, initiating a preemptive financial strike. "Submit a bid for \$1.5 million—more than double their original asking price. It will be executed via a single, unencumbered wire transfer from a liquid Quantum holding account. Go vaporize the competition and cancel the open house."

Marcus stared at her, utterly speechless at the sheer, overwhelming financial weight of the nuclear strike she had just authorized. He finally swallowed hard and nodded. "Yes, ma'am. I'll have the sellers sign the paperwork within hours."

Cassandra turned her chair back toward the paved sidewalk leading to Building 33, having successfully secured the sovereign perimeter for our pack without breaking her stride.
