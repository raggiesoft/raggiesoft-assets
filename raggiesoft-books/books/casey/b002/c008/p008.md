# Part 8: The Accepted Strike – 4:30 PM

By the time we left Marcus’s office, the adrenaline of the rapid-fire transaction had finally worn off, leaving us all starving. We hadn't eaten anything since breakfast at the Federal Way apartment, completely missing lunch during the chaotic cross-county deployment.

Cassandra navigated us to a high-end, heavily windowed steakhouse right in the heart of Downtown Bellevue. It was early enough that the dinner rush hadn't started, and the hostess easily accommodated Cass's manual chair at a large, polished wood booth in the corner.

Plates of hot food had just been set down in front of us when Cassandra’s sleek, silver cell phone began to vibrate against the table.

She glanced at the caller ID, holding up a single finger to pause the conversation.

"Marcus," Cassandra answered, her voice dropping instantly into her cool, professional baseline.

The three of us completely froze, our forks hovering over our plates. We watched Cassandra’s face for any sign of a reaction. For a few agonizingly long seconds, she just listened to the voice on the other end of the line.

Then, a slow, triumphant smile spread across her face.

"Excellent," Cassandra said smoothly. "Open the escrow account first thing tomorrow morning. Send me the routing numbers. Good work, Marcus."

She snapped the phone shut and set it back on the table. She looked at me, Liz, and Casey, her eyes practically shining.

"The seller accepted," Cassandra announced. "They didn't even counter. The house is ours."

Casey let out a massive, uncontained squeal of excitement, throwing her arms around my neck from the other side of the booth. Liz clapped a hand over her mouth, a huge laugh escaping her.

"I can't believe it actually worked," I grinned, shaking my head in pure disbelief. "You just bought a 1.5 million dollar house on a Sunday afternoon in less than three hours."

"Absolute financial force is the universal solvent for operational friction," Cassandra replied, taking a calm sip of her water. "It works every time."

"So what happens now?" Liz asked, finally cutting into her steak. "Do they just mail you the keys?"

"Not quite, but because we utilized a cash strike, the timeline is radically accelerated," Cassandra explained, effortlessly shifting into logistical mode to break down the real estate mechanics for us.

- **The Earnest Money Wire:** "The immediate next step happens tomorrow morning. I have to fund the escrow account to legally lock the contract. I will contact the priority desk at Quantum Employees Federal Credit Union and authorize an immediate, \$100,000 wire transfer directly to the title company."

- **The Title Search:** "Normally, this is where a bank would demand an appraisal and loan underwriting, which takes a month. We are bypassing all of that. The only thing the title company has to do is verify the legal deed and ensure the seller doesn't have any hidden liens or unpaid taxes attached to the property."

- **The Accelerated Closing:** "Because I am paying cash and waived the inspection, the only limiting factor is how fast the title company can process the paperwork. We will likely close and have the physical keys in our hands in less than fourteen days."

"Fourteen days?" I repeated, my processor running the calendar math. "That means we take possession of the house right after New Year's."

"Exactly," Cassandra nodded, a fiercely protective look settling over her features as she looked around the table at her pack. "Which gives me just enough time to hire the commercial demolition crew. We are going to rip the interior of that house down to the studs and rebuild it to our exact specifications."

She raised her water glass, holding it out over the center of the table.

"To the perimeter," Cassandra smiled.

I raised my glass to meet hers, followed instantly by Liz and Casey. The glass clinked together with a sharp, resonant ring.

"To the perimeter," we echoed.
