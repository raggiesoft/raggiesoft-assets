# Part 9: The TAPS Grid – 6:45 PM

The ride back across Lake Washington was entirely different from our daytime cab ride. We boarded the TAPS 550 express bus in Downtown Bellevue, and as the heavy articulated vehicle merged onto the Interstate 90 floating bridge, Liz and I got our first real look at the Seattle skyline at night.

The towering, illuminated skyscrapers reflected beautifully off the black water of the lake. But the real surprise came when the bus reached the western shore. Instead of merging onto the congested surface streets of downtown, the bus driver flipped a switch on the dashboard. The heavy diesel engine abruptly cut out, replaced by the quiet, high-pitched hum of an electric trolley motor, and the bus dove straight underground into the Downtown Seattle Transit Tunnel.

"Wait, the buses go underground here?" Liz asked, her eyes widening as we cruised past the brightly lit, subterranean concrete walls.

"The TAPS tunnel is the spine of the downtown grid," Cassandra explained from her spot in the secure wheelchair area. "It bypasses all the surface-level traffic. But since we are heading south to Federal Way, this is where we get off."

The bus glided to a smooth stop at the International District/Chinatown Station. Unlike the fully enclosed stations deeper in the city's core, this station was a massive, open-air trench situated slightly below street level.

We stepped off onto the northbound platform. Because the tracks and the bus passing lanes separated the two sides of the station, there was no way to simply walk across to catch a southbound bus.

"Elevators, David," Cassandra pointed.

We moved as a synchronized unit. We took the heavy glass and steel elevator up from the northbound platform, emerging onto the brick-paved surface plaza near the historic Union Station. The cold, damp December air hit us immediately. We quickly hustled across the open plaza, walking over the top of the transit trench, and caught the opposite elevator to ride it back down to the southbound platform.

We stepped out of the elevator doors at exactly 6:54 PM.

"Perfect timing," Cassandra noted, checking the heavy silver watch on her wrist. "We have exactly five minutes until the last outbound 194 of the evening."

"The buses stop running at seven?" I asked, pulling my coat a little tighter against the evening chill.

"The express routes do on Sundays," Cassandra corrected. "If we miss this one, we would be forced to take the 174 local all the way from downtown, which takes two hours. The 194 is our fast-track."

Right on schedule, a massive TAPS 194 bus roared into the station. The doors hissed open, and we quickly boarded. Because the 194 was leaving the tunnel and heading straight for the highway, the driver fired up the heavy diesel engine.

The ride was a masterclass in transit efficiency. The bus rocketed out of the tunnel and merged directly onto Interstate 5 South. We bypassed the sprawling suburban gridlock entirely, making only a quick pit stop at the Sea-Tac Airport terminal before utilizing the HOV lanes. We hit the Kent-Des Moines Freeway Station, the Star Lake Freeway Station, and finally exited the highway, pulling smoothly into the Federal Way Transit Center just forty-five minutes after leaving Seattle.

We stepped off the 194 into the damp, misty night, walking across the transit center platform to our final connection.

A few minutes later, a standard, slightly older transit bus pulled up to the curb. I looked up at the glowing orange destination sign above the windshield and immediately froze.

**174 SEATTLE**

I looked at Liz, who looked equally confused. I turned back to my older sister. "Cass, we just *came* from Seattle. Are we getting on the wrong bus? Is this going backward?"

Casey giggled, leaning against my arm. "You guys don't know the grid yet."

"The sign says Seattle because that is its final destination, David," Cassandra explained patiently, wheeling herself toward the front doors as the driver lowered the accessibility ramp. "The 174 originates here in Federal Way and crawls all the way up Pacific Highway back into the city. We are taking the northbound route."

"But we live south of Seattle," Liz pointed out, trying to make the mental map work.

"We do," Cassandra agreed, rolling up the ramp. "But the Federal Way Transit Center is south of our apartment. By taking the northbound 174, it will drive us exactly two miles up SR-99. More importantly, it drops us off on the east side of the highway—our side. We don't have to navigate a dangerous, unlit crosswalk in the rain. It is a zero-friction extraction."

I shook my head, thoroughly impressed by the sheer logistical perfection of her route planning, and followed my sisters onto the bus. Five minutes later, the 174 dropped us off perfectly at the corner of South 308th Street, leaving us with nothing but a short, paved sidewalk stroll back to the warm, secure perimeter of our apartment.
