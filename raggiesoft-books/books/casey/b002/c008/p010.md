# Part 10: The Sunday Night Reset – 8:30 PM

By the time we unlocked the door to the Federal Way apartment and dropped our bags, the damp Pacific Northwest chill had thoroughly seeped into our bones. The ambient warmth of the apartment’s HVAC system was a massive relief after the cold transit commute, but the consensus among the pack was unanimous: everyone needed a hot shower.

Cassandra immediately shifted into logistical mode, establishing the evening sequence to maximize efficiency with only two bathrooms.

"Alright, let's execute a staggered rotation," Cassandra instructed, pulling off her damp coat. "Casey, you take the hallway bathroom first. David and Liz, come with me to the master suite."

Casey grabbed her pajamas and disappeared into her bathroom, quickly starting the water for the bath-and-shower combo unit.

Liz and I followed Cassandra down the short hallway into her bedroom. Just like the night before, Liz stayed in the main bedroom to unpack their sleepwear and wind down, completely respecting the operational boundaries, while I followed Cass into her highly modified ensuite bathroom.

The routine was seamless. I acted as her physical anchor, stabilizing her manual chair while she managed the complex toileting transfers. Once she was ready for the shower, I braced my stance and provided the leverage she needed to safely transition from the chair onto the heavy-duty transfer bench inside the zero-entry shower stall. I handed her the soap and towels, ensuring she was completely secure before stepping out to give her privacy.

By the time I heard the water shut off in Cassandra's bathroom, the door to the hallway bathroom clicked open.

"All yours, Liz!" Casey called out from the hall, bundled up in a massive, fluffy robe.

Liz grabbed her clothes and swapped places with Casey, taking over the hallway bathroom. Meanwhile, I stepped back into the master ensuite, helped Cassandra transfer safely back into her chair, and then jumped into the hot shower myself to wash off the travel fatigue.

Twenty minutes later, the entire pack reconvened in the living room, dressed in clean, oversized pajamas.

"I am officially calling it," Liz announced, dropping onto the edge of the pull-out sofa and looking at the thin mattress. "My back cannot handle another night on these metal springs. Cass, if the offer still stands, David and I are migrating to the master suite."

"The offer absolutely stands," Cassandra smiled from her wheelchair. "There is more than enough room on the king-sized mattress for the three of us."

Casey, who had been happily curled up in the corner of the sofa, immediately stopped smiling. Her shoulders slumped, and a heavy, definitive teenage pout settled over her face. She looked at me, then at the hallway leading to her empty queen-sized bed, and finally at Cassandra.

"Why can't I just sleep in there with you guys?" Casey asked, her voice tight with frustration. "Or why can't David sleep in my room? We always slept by each other when we were younger. It's not fair."

Cassandra wheeled her chair forward, her expression softening instantly. She didn't dismiss Casey's feelings, but she also didn't yield an inch on the perimeter.

"I know it feels unfair, Case," Cassandra said gently. "And you are entirely right—you and David used to sleep next to each other every single night, and there was nothing wrong with it. But you remember the rule. Last night was a strict emergency exception because the grid failed and the structural safety of the apartment was compromised."

Cassandra gestured toward the window, where the streetlights were glowing brightly and the rain was only a light, manageable mist.

"Tonight, the weather is fine, the power is on, and the environment is secure," Cassandra continued, making sure Casey understood the math. "Which means the legal perimeter goes back into full effect. I will not give Tom a single piece of theoretical ammunition to use against us."

Casey let out a long, frustrated sigh. Her system deeply missed the continuous physical proximity of her brother, but her logical processor understood exactly what was at stake. The threat of Tom finding a loophole was terrifying enough to override her pout.

"I know," Casey mumbled, looking down at her fuzzy socks. "Until May 25th."

"Until May 25th," I echoed, walking over and giving her a tight, deep-pressure hug. "It's only five months away, Case. And by the time you turn eighteen, the new house in Redmond will be completely finished, and you can make the rules."

Casey leaned into the hug, letting out a small, reluctant smile. "Okay. Fine."

"Good," Cassandra nodded warmly, shifting her chair toward the hallway. "Now, let's initiate a full system shutdown. Tomorrow is Monday. I have to be at Building 33 for the morning executive briefing, and Casey, you have zero period calculus. It is time for bed."
