The Northwest Pacific University student union was a massive, cavernous building echoing with the chaotic noise of thousands of students returning for Winter Quarter. I navigated through the dense crowd to the food court, finding Liz sitting at a small corner table with two coffees. 

I dropped my backpack onto an empty chair, grabbed one of the coffees, and pulled out my flip phone. 

"Is it done?" Liz asked, taking a sip from her cup. 

"I'm officially a pre-nursing IT major," I confirmed. I navigated to my contacts, initiated a three-way conference call, and set the phone down in the center of the table on speaker. "Bio 101 starts tomorrow."

The phone rang for a few seconds before two distinct streams of background noise merged onto the line. On one end, the distinct, chaotic chatter of the Rhode Island Delaney household filtered through. On the other end, the quieter, familiar ambient noise of the Virginia Beach house. 

"Vance!" Erin's voice boomed over the speaker from Rhode Island. "Are you freezing to death in Seattle yet?"

"It's just raining," I said, leaning closer to the phone. "Is everyone on?"

"We're all here, David," Mrs. Carter’s voice replied from Virginia Beach. 

"Meredith is making us do chores, so make it quick," Kelly complained in the background, earning a sharp reprimand from her older sister. 

I smiled, instantly missing the sheer gravity of their combined presence. The week we had spent in Virginia Beach during the deployment had completely rewired my understanding of family. Being separated from them by three thousand miles felt entirely unnatural. 

"I just walked out of the academic advising office," I announced to the combined pack. "I officially altered my degree track. I'm running my IT major concurrently with the nursing prerequisites. I'm going for my LPN."

There was a brief moment of silence on the line before the Rhode Island feed erupted into cheers. 

"Oh my god, David, that's amazing!" Megan yelled over the noise of her younger sisters. 

"Wait, you're actually going to be a nurse?" Chloe asked, sounding genuinely impressed. 

"Not a full-time hospital nurse," Liz clarified, leaning toward the phone. "He's getting his clinical license so he has legal medical authority over Cassandra's care. He's locking out the third-party agencies."

The realization hit the Virginia Beach line immediately. 

"That is brilliant," Meredith said, her tone shifting to absolute, profound respect. "David, that is such an incredible way to step up for her. She must be so relieved."

"She is," I said quietly. 

Suddenly, a synthesized, mechanical voice cut clearly through the Virginia Beach audio feed. It was David Delaney's AAC device. 

*"I T AND NURSING. YOU ARE A GLUTTON FOR PUNISHMENT."* 

The entire conference call burst into laughter. 

*"PROUD OF YOU,"* Kate's AAC device immediately followed up, her synthesized voice layering over the laughter. *"CASSANDRA IS LUCKY."*

"I'm the lucky one," I replied, meaning every single syllable. I took a deep breath, looking across the table at Liz, who was beaming at the phone. "I miss you guys. Seattle feels way too quiet without thirty people trying to talk over each other."

"Just survive until March, Vance," Erin ordered from Rhode Island. "Because the second Spring Break hits, both houses are flying out to Washington State. We're going to completely crush-load that massive new house Cassandra is buying in Redmond."

"I can't wait," I said, a massive surge of relief washing over me. 

The perimeter was secure, my academic path was locked, and the entire pack was backing me up. I drank my coffee, listening to the Delaneys argue over the phone, and for the first time in my life, I felt completely, utterly unstoppable.
