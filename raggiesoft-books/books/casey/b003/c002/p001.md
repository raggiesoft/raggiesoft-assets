# Part 1: The Food Truck Festival – 12:00 PM

The sprawling, open-air plazas and forested courtyards of the Redmond campus had been completely transformed.

Instead of a closed-door VIP luncheon in the executive suite of Building 33, Cassandra had authorized a massive, campus-wide street fair. Dozens of local Seattle food trucks were parked in a massive ring around the central courtyards, their generators humming cheerfully as the scent of gourmet tacos, Korean BBQ, and artisanal grilled cheese drifted through the crisp February air. Heated outdoor pavilions and fire pits were scattered across the grass, accommodating the thousands of employees wearing Quantum Blue Badges.

Every single food and beverage tab for the afternoon was completely covered by Quantum. There were no deployment deadlines, no server maintenance crunches, and absolutely no corporate hierarchies.

Naturally, Quantum’s Public Relations department had attempted to capitalize on the moment. A senior PR director had approached Cassandra the second she rolled out of the William Keswick Auditorium, flanked by an official corporate photographer. The director had practically begged to stage a photo op of the new CEO handing out burgers from a food truck window.

Cassandra had explicitly and ruthlessly forbidden it.

*"I am not a prop, and my employees are not extras,"* Cassandra had informed the PR director, her tone leaving absolutely zero room for negotiation. She was not on the campus floor for a staged photo op; she was there because she genuinely cared about her workforce.

Now, thirty minutes later, the CEO of a trillion-dollar tech empire was sitting in her manual wheelchair, parked exactly halfway back in a fifty-person line for a highly popular pulled-pork sandwich truck.

David stood on her right, his hands stuffed into his coat pockets as he chatted with Liz. Casey was a few steps ahead, happily snapping photos of the food trucks and the massive crowds of engineers with her digital camera.

A twenty-two-year-old junior developer, wearing a heavy fleece pullover and holding a laptop bag, suddenly realized who he was standing in front of. His eyes went wide. He looked at the massive line stretching out ahead of the truck, and then back to the woman who had just accepted the keys to the entire company.

"Ms. Vance?" the developer stammered, awkwardly stepping out of the queue. "I—you don't have to wait back here. You're the CEO. You should go to the front. We can all step aside."

A ripple of agreement went through the line as a few other developers turned around, fully prepared to part like the Red Sea to let the newly minted executive bypass the twenty-minute wait.

Cassandra offered the junior developer a warm, perfectly grounded smile. She didn't move her wheelchair an inch.

"Call me Cass," she corrected gently, her voice carrying a relaxed, pragmatic authority. "And thank you, but absolutely not. If the junior developers have to wait their turn for a pulled-pork sandwich, then so can the CEO. Nobody cuts the line today."

"But... your schedule," the developer protested weakly, conditioned by years of watching executives sprint from meeting to meeting.

"There are no deployment schedules today," Cassandra pointed out, gesturing toward the food trucks. "The only mandate for this afternoon is that Quantum is footing the bill, and I expect you all to enjoy the food. Go back to your spot in line, take a breath, and figure out what kind of barbecue sauce you want."

The developer blinked, completely disarmed by her utter lack of ego, before smiling and stepping back into his place in line.

David looked down at his older sister, a fierce, undeniable pride radiating from his posture. He leaned down, resting his hand gently on the back of her wheelchair.

"You know they're never going to stop talking about this, right?" David murmured quietly. "The CEO waiting twenty minutes for a sandwich. You just broke their entire understanding of corporate leadership."

"Good," Cassandra said simply, pulling her wheelchair forward a few inches as the line slowly advanced. "An ivory tower is a very lonely place to eat lunch. I prefer the courtyard."
