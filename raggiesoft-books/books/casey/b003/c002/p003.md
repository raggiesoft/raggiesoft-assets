The walk from Westlake Station to the Cascade Crest Hotel was a short, flat, perfectly paved two-block commute. After enduring the chaos of the airport and the subterranean rumble of the transit tunnel, the massive glass doors of the hotel lobby offered a luxurious, quiet sanctuary. 

We swarmed the lobby. Moving fourteen people into any space instantly changed its dynamic, and the East Coast pack immediately claimed a large section of the plush leather couches near the front windows. Suitcases and duffel bags were piled into a defensive perimeter. 

Cassandra rolled directly up to the front desk, the heavy marble counter sitting at eye level for her. I stood right behind her right shoulder, offering a silent physical escort. 

"Good afternoon, I am checking in under Vance," Cassandra announced to the front desk agent. "It is a large family block reservation. We are expected."

The agent tapped on her keyboard, her eyes widening slightly as she pulled up the massive reservation. "Yes, Ms. Vance. Seven suites on the twelfth floor. The reservations were made months ago, and since it's just past three o'clock, all of your rooms are fully serviced and ready for entry."

"Excellent," Cassandra said, reaching into her suit jacket. 

She absolutely despised using credit cards. Cassandra was a strict, meticulous accountant who vastly preferred the immediate transaction settlement of a debit card. Debt of any kind, even temporary rolling debt, bothered her. But she also understood hotel logistics. Hotels despised debit cards for incidentals and holds. So, playing the game, she pulled out a sleek, heavy personal credit card and handed it over. 

"I am placing the entire block on this card," Cassandra instructed firmly. "That includes all room charges, taxes, and a hundred percent of all incidentals. Any room service, any meals charged to the suites by anyone in my party—it all routes to this master account."

"Cassandra, absolutely not," Wendy interjected, having walked up to the counter just in time to hear the mandate. She reached into her purse. "Tom and I are paying for our own suite, and the Rhode Island girls can pay for theirs. You are not covering a fourteen-person hotel bill."

Cassandra didn't even turn her head. She just held her hand up in a gentle, but utterly immovable stop gesture. 

"Wendy, you flew across the continent to support my brother," Cassandra said, her tone carrying the absolute, uncompromising weight of a Quantum executive. "You provided him with a sanctuary in Virginia Beach when he needed it most. You are my guests. Your money is no good in the Pacific Northwest."

"It's easily thousands of dollars, Cassandra," Tom warned, stepping up beside his wife. 

"It is a rounding error," Cassandra corrected him without a trace of arrogance. It was just a logistical fact. "The hotel is paid for. The meals are paid for. You are here to enjoy Spring Break with my family."

The front desk agent, clearly recognizing an executive power play when she saw one, quickly swiped the credit card and began encoding a massive stack of keycards. 

Wendy looked at me, completely stunned by the sheer financial force of my sister. 

"Just let her do it," I smiled, leaning against the marble counter. "It's how she takes care of the pack."

"She is terrifyingly generous," Megan laughed from the couches, having overheard the entire exchange. 

"Alright, room assignments," Cassandra announced, taking the stack of keycards from the agent and spinning her wheelchair around to face the lobby. "Everyone grab your bags. We are taking over the twelfth floor."
