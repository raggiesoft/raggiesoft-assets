The reality of our massive birthday weekend eventually gave way to the undeniable logistics of Monday morning classes. 

By eight o'clock on Sunday morning, the Great Hall was buzzing with organized chaos as the two bloodlines prepared to execute a synchronized mass exodus. Extracting thirty-seven people from an isolated mountain fortress before the public roads were officially cleared for the season required military-grade orchestration. 

Fortunately, the Keep was staffed entirely by teenagers who treated heavy logistics as a casual morning chore. 

"Ridgebacks are staged at the Portico!" Emma shouted over the ambient noise, zipping up her heavy, insulated work jacket. She and a half-dozen of the older teenage cousins were tasked with getting us down from the summit. 

The Ridgebacks—the Keep’s massive, heavily modified 4x4 passenger vans—were practically indestructible. Built to handle the lethal ice storms and 5,400-foot avalanche zones of the North Cascades, they idled with a deep, throaty growl under the massive timber overhang of the cobblestone portico. 

Victoria and the Vanguard gathered by the heavy timber doors of the Great Hall to see us off. The goodbyes were completely devoid of the usual awkward, estranged tension. Cassandra and Victoria exchanged a brief, deeply respectful nod—the silent acknowledgment of two apex predators who finally trusted their flank. 

Johnny flashed me a massive grin from his manual chair. "See you in July."

"July," I confirmed, gripping his shoulder before climbing into the back of Emma's Ridgeback. 

The descent down Prospector Road was a masterclass in aggressive off-road handling. Emma threw the massive Ridgeback into gear, navigating the treacherous, snow-packed switchbacks with the terrifying confidence of someone who had learned to drive on a glacier. 

Thirty minutes later, the caravan reached the bottom of the private road, pulling into the subterranean Lower Motorpool. 

This was where the Vanguard's jurisdiction officially ended. Emma and her crew helped us crossload our luggage from the Ridgebacks back into the fleet of corporate rental vans Cassandra had secured for the weekend. After a final round of goodbyes, the blast doors opened, and the Vanguard retreated back up the mountain. 

Cassandra and the Delaneys took the wheel of the rental vans. We emerged from the motorpool, hit the asphalt of State Route 20, and accelerated toward civilization. 

Our final destination for the morning wasn't Seattle. The entire motorcade drove straight to Eldridge Field (EBF), the massive regional airport serving Meridian City, to return the rental fleet. 

The Delaneys’ corporate logistics had already cleared the way. Two sleek, private charter jets were waiting on the tarmac, their engines whining as they spun up for departure. 

"Alright, people, we are splitting the deployment!" Morgan Delaney yelled, coordinating the chaos on the freezing tarmac. "Virginia Beach branch, get your gear onto the first bird! We're flying direct to ORF. Rhode Island branch, you're on the second plane heading to UUU!"

Liz stood on the asphalt, hugging her mother and father tightly before they boarded the Virginia Beach charter. Beside her, Casey and I were hugging our own parents, aunts, uncles, and cousins. The East Coast pack had flown across the country to celebrate with us, but now they had to return to their respective strongholds. 

"We'll see you in July, kids," Morgan Delaney promised, giving me a fierce, brief hug before grabbing her tactical duffel bag and heading up the stairs of the Virginia Beach charter. 

Cassandra, Casey, Liz, and I stood near the chain-link fence of the terminal, watching the twin jets taxi down the runway and launch into the grey, overcast Pacific Northwest sky. The deafening roar of the engines faded, leaving the four of us standing quietly on the edge of Eldridge Field. 

"Alright," Cassandra announced, her corporate efficiency instantly re-engaging. She pulled out a small leather itinerary notebook. "The corporate jets are airborne. We are now transitioning to public transit infrastructure. Everyone stay sharp."

We walked away from the private hangars and crossed the massive, sprawling concrete footprint of the airport until we reached the EBF Metro Station—the above-ground terminus for the Meridian Transit Authority (MTA). 

We tapped our transit cards and boarded the MTA Red Line. The train hissed shut and began its rapid, twenty-five-mile descent into the hyper-dense core of Meridian City. For half an hour, we watched the quiet, unassuming pine forests outside the window rapidly give way to towering, brutalist skyscrapers and massive concrete overpasses. 

The Red Line dumped us deep inside the subterranean transit hub of Meridian City, where we immediately transferred to the regional rail network. 

At 11:15 AM, we boarded the ARTS Cascades—the sleek, high-speed regional train that mirrored the Amtrak Cascades lines. The heavy train pulled out of the massive Meridian station, running parallel to the grey, churning waters of the Puget Sound as it carried us south toward Seattle. 

I leaned my head against the cold glass of the train window, my eyes drooping heavily as the rhythmic clatter of the tracks vibrated through the carriage. Liz was sound asleep against my shoulder, her hand loosely holding mine. Across the aisle, Casey was intensely focused on a thick textbook, while Cassandra reviewed a stack of legal briefs on her lap. 

An hour later, the ARTS train rolled into the beautiful, historic architecture of Seattle's King Street Station. 

We weren't done yet. 

We hauled our luggage up to the street level, leaving the MTA infrastructure behind and integrating into the Seattle TAPS network. We caught the heavy, articulated TAPS 194 bus out of downtown, riding the massive Interstate 5 corridor south to the Federal Way Transit Center. 

From there, it was a quick, exhausting transfer to the local TAPS 174 route, which slowly wound its way through the familiar, rain-slicked suburban streets until it finally dropped us at our apartment complex. 

It was mid-afternoon by the time I slid my key into our front door. 

We had utilized off-road tactical vehicles, private corporate aviation, rapid transit heavy rail, regional high-speed rail, and two separate municipal bus networks. We were physically and mentally exhausted, utterly drained by the sheer geographical logistics of the Pacific Northwest. 

But as I dropped my heavy bags onto the carpet of the living room and watched Liz kick her shoes off with a massive, satisfied sigh, the exhaustion didn't matter. 

We had successfully breached the Sovereign Perimeter, and we had returned home with the absolute certainty that in exactly three months, we were going back to the mountain for a wedding.
