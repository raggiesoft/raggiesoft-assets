# Part 7: The Finch Expedition – 11:15 AM

The gavel struck the wooden block, officially adjourning the Senate Judiciary Committee hearing.

As the gallery cleared and congressional aides scrambled to gather briefing binders, Cassandra wheeled herself out of the witness box to rejoin her family. But before they could clear the committee room exit, a familiar, warm voice called out across the aisle.

"Cassandra! Hold up a second!"

Walking down the center carpet was Senator Patricia "Patty" Morrison of Washington State. Born and raised in Bothell, the veteran lawmaker was a proud product of the Puget Sound area, currently in the thick of her 2014 reelection campaign. As a relentless champion for Pacific Northwest infrastructure, Morrison had watched the morning's testimony with sheer delight.

"Senator Morrison," Cassandra greeted warmly, stopping her chair and extending a hand. "It's good to see you."

"Fabulous opening statement," Morrison smiled, shaking Cassandra's hand before greeting David, Liz, and Casey with an easy, practiced warmth. "You completely neutralized the opposition. But listen—while I have you in the building, walk with me. I want to show you something, and we need to talk about home."

Rather than navigating the crowded public corridors, Senator Morrison led the Vance pack through a private, secured side door into the subterranean network of tunnels connecting the Senate office buildings directly to the United States Capitol.

She guided them onto the platform of the Capitol Subway System—the small, open-top underground train that shuttles lawmakers beneath Capitol Hill.

David's eyes lit up instantly. An open-top electric monorail running through private, vaulted government tunnels was an unexpected transit treat.

"I wanted to talk about the regional expansion," Senator Morrison said as they boarded the open subway car, the wind blowing through the quiet tunnel as the train sped along its track. "TAPS is pushing hard on the proposed light rail extension across Lake Washington out to Redmond. I know local opposition in a few Eastside neighborhoods is getting noisy, but if we want this regional grid to function, we need the 2 Line anchored at the Quantum campus."

Cassandra offered a confident, knowing smile.

"Senator, you don't have to sell me on the 2 Line," Cassandra assured her over the hum of the electric motor. "Quantum is already fully on board. In fact, we are finalizing the paperwork with the City of Redmond to establish a Community Facilities District. Quantum is agreeing to act as the single ratepayer to drop over thirty-three million dollars into the Overlake Transit Center, building the weather-shielded bus canopies and the pedestrian bridge over SR 520."

Senator Morrison raised an eyebrow, genuinely impressed. "Thirty-three million out of your own corporate pocket? That's going to make my job in the Senate Appropriations Committee a hell of a lot easier."

"Infrastructure isn't an expense, Senator; it's a foundation," Cassandra replied smoothly as the subway car hissed to a stop beneath the Capitol. "And next time you're back home in Bothell, let me know. I'll personally buy your fare and we can take a TAPS bus ride together down the I-405 corridor."

Morrison let out a rich, genuine laugh. "You've got a deal, Cassie. I'll hold you to that."

Exiting the Capitol through the plaza doors, the pack stepped out into the brilliant Mid-Atlantic sunshine. The air was warm, a soft breeze rustling the trees along the Mall. It was a beautiful day.

"Where to next, Cass?" David asked, looking out over the wide green lawns.

"We go to the Finch," Cassandra announced, pointing down the paved pedestrian path.

She wasn't referring to a high-priced lobbying steakhouse or a corporate boardroom. She meant the world-famous **Finch Air and Space Museum**—the crown jewel of **The Finch Endowment**, founded by the 18th-century British scientist Lord Alistair Finch, who had famously bequeathed his massive fortune to the United States to establish an institution dedicated to the "increase and diffusion of knowledge."

They set off down the wide, flat, wheel-friendly concrete pathways of the Mall, enjoying the open air. Casey immediately pulled out her camera to photograph the towering monuments framing the horizon, while Liz and David walked side-by-side, soaking in the quiet afternoon.

Halfway down the path, Cassandra’s phone began to vibrate violently in her messenger bag.

It was Greg, the K Street lobbyist. Having finally abandoned his trapped motorcade of Apex Mountaineers on the 14th Street Bridge and sprinted up Capitol Hill, he had arrived at the committee room only to find it completely empty.

Cassandra tapped the speakerphone button on her mounted dashboard. "Go ahead, Greg."

"Ms. Vance!" Greg’s voice crackled through the speaker, breathless and bordering on sheer panic. "I am at the Dirksen Building! Where did you go?! Did you leave the Capitol grounds?! Is there an emergency executive briefing at the White House?! Did you schedule an unscheduled press conference?!"

Behind Greg, the entire K Street handling team was in a tailspin, frantically trying to figure out why the CEO of Quantum was moving on foot toward the Mall instead of heading to a high-priced corporate luncheon.

Cassandra glanced back at David, who was smiling as Casey pointed out a vintage aircraft hanging in the glass atrium of the museum ahead.

"There is no emergency, Greg," Cassandra answered calmly, her tone dropping into a warm, unyielding register. "I finished my testimony, I had a lovely subway ride with Senator Morrison, and now I am fulfilling a promise."

"A promise?!" Greg stammered. "To who?! What conference?!"

"To my family," Cassandra said smoothly. "We are going to The Finch Endowment's Air and Space Museum. Do not call dispatch, do not send an Apex Mountaineer, and do not follow us. We will see you tomorrow evening for the gala at the Zoo."

She hung up the phone, flipped the hardware mute switch, and rolled effortlessly through the wide, level-boarding entrance of the Finch Museum, completely content to leave the world of corporate handlers behind for the rest of the afternoon.
