# Part 8: The Telescope Protocol – 1:00 PM

The large-format theater inside the Finch Air and Space Museum required a strict adherence to civilian etiquette. Before joining the queue for the documentary, the pack stopped at the front visitor counter, checking their heavy weekend duffel bags with the attendant so they wouldn't have to haul them through the crowded theater aisles.

As they found their seats in the massive, steeply tiered auditorium, the lights began to dim.

"Phones off," Cassandra instructed, pulling her device from her pocket and holding the power button until the screen went completely black.

David, Liz, and Casey followed suit. To Cassandra, shutting down her digital connection in a movie theater wasn't just common courtesy; it was a sacred boundary. Quantum’s executive suite in Redmond knew her itinerary down to the minute. Marcus, her Chief of Staff, had explicitly noted the ninety-minute communications blackout on the master schedule.

The K Street lobbyists, however, did not have the master schedule.

An hour and a half later, the credits rolled, the house lights came up, and the pack made their way out into the sunlit exhibition halls. Cassandra reached into her pocket and held down the power button to boot up her phone.

The moment the device reconnected to the cellular network, it practically convulsed in her hand.

*Bzz. Bzz. BZZ-BZZ-BZZ-BZZ.*

The haptic motor went into overdrive as the phone frantically downloaded an avalanche of missed calls, panicked voicemails, and dozens of text messages. Cassandra stopped her wheelchair near the entrance of the Apollo exhibit, letting out a sharp, annoyed sigh as she opened the "VANCE DEPLOYMENT - D.C." group thread.

\[1:15 PM\] Greg (D.C. Lobbyist): Ms. Vance, your GPS signal just dropped off the map. Please confirm your location.

\[1:30 PM\] Greg (D.C. Lobbyist): Did you go underground? Are you in a secure bunker?

\[2:05 PM\] Pierce (Manhattan PR): Greg says you've been offline for an hour. Are you still on the Mall? Do we need to dispatch Capitol Police to your last known coordinates?!

\[2:12 PM\] Marcus (Quantum Chief of Staff): Stand down, both of you. She is in a movie theater.

\[2:15 PM\] Greg (D.C. Lobbyist): A MOVIE THEATER?! In the middle of the afternoon?! On a deployment day?!

\[2:17 PM\] Marcus (Quantum Chief of Staff): She promised her brother a trip to the Finch. Do not call the police, Greg. Just wait for the documentary to end.

Cassandra shook her head. Her fixers were operating on a frequency of pure, unchecked paranoia. She wasn't going to validate their hysteria with an explanation, a phone call, or an apology for being unreachable.

She tapped a single emoji into the group thread and hit send.

\[2:31 PM\] Cassandra Vance: 🔭

She locked the screen, dropping the phone back into her pocket before Greg could even begin to type a response.

"Let me guess," David smiled, walking up beside her chair. "Greg?"

"Greg," Cassandra confirmed, rolling her eyes. "He was about five minutes away from initiating a federal manhunt. Now, where to?"

David didn't hesitate. He was completely in his element, his hyperfixation fully engaged as he looked up at the towering rockets and orbital command modules suspended from the ceiling. For the next two hours, the family happily deferred to his expertise. David guided them through the space exploration wing, passionately explaining the intricacies of orbital mechanics, gravitational slingshots, and lunar descent trajectories to Liz and Casey. Cassandra just wheeled quietly beside him, profoundly content to watch her brother light up with absolute, unburdened joy.

By late afternoon, the sensory input of the crowded museum finally began to wear on the pack.

"Alright, let's head to the hotel," Cassandra called out, recognizing the subtle shift in David and Casey's posture.

They returned to the front counter, retrieved their duffel bags, and exited the museum through the southern doors onto Independence Avenue. Rather than walking back across the National Mall to the crowded Finch station, Cassandra mapped the optimal, frictionless route. They simply walked two blocks south down 7th Street, descending into the L'Enfant Plaza Metro Station.

The sprawling subterranean transit hub was perfectly climate-controlled. Cassandra pulled her CapitalPass card from her pocket, tapped the glass faregate, and rolled through without breaking her momentum. David, Liz, and Casey tapped their own cards right behind her, acting as a flawless, synchronized perimeter.

They descended in the elevator to the lower platform, bypassing the Red and Yellow lines entirely. Within minutes, a silver Blue Line train hissed to a halt. The doors opened flush with the platform, and the pack boarded effortlessly.

The ride through the heart of the District was smooth and cool. After just a few stops, the train arrived at Farragut West.

Cassandra rolled off the train, guiding her family to the street-level elevator. When the doors opened, they stepped out into the bustling evening air of Farragut Square. The heavy, humid D.C. heat had begun to break, and the lush green park in the center of the square was filled with commuters heading home.

Cassandra led them exactly one block over to their luxury hotel. There was no motorcade, no frantic lobbying team waiting in the lobby, and no press pool. Thanks to the absolute autonomy of the WMATA grid, the CEO of Quantum checked in at the front desk looking like nothing more than a standard tourist, her family safe and insulated by her side.
