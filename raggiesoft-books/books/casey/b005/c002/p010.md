# Interlude: The Escalator Trap – 4:15 PM

While Cassandra and her pack were enjoying a heavily air-conditioned, frictionless ride on the Blue Line toward Farragut West, a completely different scene was unfolding a mile away on the National Mall.

Greg and Pierce were standing on the crushed gravel path near the center of the Mall, their designer suits sticking to their backs in the suffocating D.C. humidity. They were positioned strategically next to the concrete entrance canopy of the **Finch Metro Station** (the central transit hub for the Mall).

Greg was aggressively wiping his forehead with a monogrammed handkerchief. He had finally managed to corral Pierce—who had taken an Acela express train down from New York in a panic—and together, they had formulated what they believed was a foolproof intercept strategy.

"It’s basic deductive reasoning, Pierce," Greg said, checking his luxury watch for the fifteenth time. "She sent the telescope emoji. Quantum confirmed she went to the Finch Air and Space Museum. She refuses to take cars. Therefore, when she leaves the museum, she has to take the train. This is the Finch Metro Station. It’s the closest hub. We intercept her here, apologize for the motorcade mix-up, and escort her back to the hotel like professionals."

Pierce nodded, trying to look authoritative while a group of tourists in cargo shorts pushed past him. "Right. We regain control of the narrative. We show her we understand her transit preferences."

They stood in silence for another ten minutes, watching the steady stream of tourists riding the mechanical stairs up from the subterranean mezzanine.

Pierce narrowed his eyes, peering past the lip of the concrete entrance. He watched the steep, descending metal grade of the escalators plunging deep into the earth. He looked left. He looked right.

Pierce slowly turned his head to look at Greg.

"Greg," Pierce said, his voice completely flat.

"What?" Greg snapped, refreshing the tracking app on his mPad that still showed zero signal.

"Ms. Vance is in a wheelchair," Pierce stated slowly.

"I am aware of her medical profile, Pierce," Greg huffed, rolling his eyes. "That is exactly why she needs us to coordinate her logistics."

"Right," Pierce continued, pointing a shaking finger toward the station entrance. "So... how exactly is she supposed to get down *that*?"

Greg frowned, stepping over to the edge of the entrance. He looked down the long, steep, plunging shaft of the escalator. There were no stairs. There was no mechanical lift.

There was absolutely no elevator.

The blood drained completely out of Greg's face.

"Where is the elevator?" Greg whispered, a cold sweat breaking out over his collar. He frantically pulled up the Capital Transit accessibility map on his mPad. "It has to be here! It's a federal transit system!"

"Greg, the elevator for this station is three blocks away, on the other side of Independence Avenue," Pierce read over his shoulder, his voice rising in sheer, unadulterated panic. "This specific entrance is escalator-only!"

"Why would she come this way?!" Greg gasped, his hands shaking.

"She wouldn't!" Pierce shouted, grabbing Greg’s mPad and zooming out on the digital map. He traced a line from the Air and Space Museum to their current location, and then traced a second line south.

Pierce stopped breathing.

"Greg," Pierce whispered, pointing at a completely different station on the map. "L'Enfant Plaza."

"What about it?"

"It's on 7th Street. It's literally right across the street from the museum’s south doors," Pierce explained, the horrifying realization washing over him. "It has three commercial elevators. It connects to the Blue, Orange, Yellow, Green, and Silver lines. We are guarding a broken chokepoint a quarter-mile in the wrong direction."

Greg stared at the digital map. The sheer, mathematical efficiency of Cassandra’s actual route mocked him from the glowing screen. She hadn't just bypassed them; she had done it because their "foolproof" intercept point was physically impassable and geographically inferior.

Before Greg could formulate a response, his phone buzzed. It was a text from Marcus, Quantum’s Chief of Staff back in Redmond.

**\[4:22 PM\] Marcus (Chief of Staff):** *Ms. Vance has successfully checked into the hotel at Farragut Square. Please stop loitering on the National Mall. Your billable hours are currently under review.*

Greg slowly lowered his phone, looking down into the escalator shaft. They hadn't just failed to intercept the CEO of Quantum; they had actively waited at a structural dead end, proving once again that the trillion-dollar architect knew their city's grid infinitely better than they did.

"Call a cab," Greg sighed, his shoulders slumping in total defeat.

"I thought we were taking the Metro to prove a point?" Pierce asked weakly.

"Pierce, I don't know where the damn elevator is," Greg snapped, marching toward the street. "Just call a cab."
