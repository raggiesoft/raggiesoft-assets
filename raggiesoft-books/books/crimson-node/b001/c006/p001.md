Principal Bernard looked at the boy sitting across the desk from him, his expression heavy with protective concern. Matt Vance had wheeled his manual wheelchair into the administrative office right after the final bell rang on Thursday afternoon, just two days before Senior Prom. 

Bernard had expected Matt to ask for special accommodations or perhaps express anxiety about attending the dance in the wake of the Omni-Q incident. Instead, Matt's request had caught him completely off guard.

Matt reached down to his lap tray and slowly, deliberately, typed out a message on his AAC device. He hit the playback button, the synthesized voice cutting through the quiet office.

"I want the order lifted. I want to see Courtney."

Bernard let out a long, heavy sigh. He leaned forward, resting his forearms on his desk. "Matt, I know this is complicated. I know you two have a history. But what she did—what she asked of you—it crossed a severe boundary. My job is to protect the students in this building, and in this specific case, my job is to protect *you*."

Matt shook his head immediately. His face was intensely earnest. He tapped at his device again. 

"Sarah tried to explain the hookup word. I still do not understand. It was just a fight. She made a mistake."

Bernard softened. The sheer purity of Matt's perspective was heartbreaking. To the school administration, Courtney Evans was a predator who had crossed a line with a vulnerable, non-verbal student. But to Matt, the complexities of sexual pressure and physical boundaries were abstract concepts that his sister Sarah had fruitlessly tried to translate. He didn't see a villain. He just saw his friend.

Matt typed one final sentence and hit play. 

"I miss my friend. Give her a second chance."

"I admire your forgiveness, Matt. Truly, I do," Bernard said quietly, adjusting his glasses. "But my hands are tied by policy and liability. The No Contact Order is administrative. It must remain in place through the end of the school year. I'm sorry."

Matt stared at him for a long moment, his jaw set in a quiet, stubborn resolve. He didn't argue further. He simply turned his wheelchair around and rolled out of the office.
