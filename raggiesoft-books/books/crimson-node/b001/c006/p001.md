The afternoon sun cast long, angular shadows across the linoleum floor of the main office. Inside Principal Bernard’s office, the atmosphere was heavy with unspoken tension. The hum of the air conditioner provided a steady drone underneath the steady, rhythmic breathing of Arthur Vance, who stood quietly near the door. 

Arthur was Matt Miller’s shadow—a dedicated one-on-one aide who never strayed far from his side. Today, however, he was simply an observer. This was Matt’s meeting.

Matt sat in the center of the room in his manual wheelchair, his posture calm but his expression resolute. On the desk in front of him rested his AAC device, a small screen filled with symbols and words that gave him a voice.

Principal Bernard sat behind his desk, his hands folded, his brow furrowed with a mix of sympathy and bureaucratic exhaustion. It had been weeks since the Omni-Q incident, the event that had forced him to suspend Courtney Evans and instate a strict Administrative No Contact Order between her and Matt. 

"Matt," Bernard began, his voice gentle but firm. "I understand why you wanted to see me. But you have to understand, what happened... the boundaries that were crossed. We have policies in place to protect students. To protect *you*."

Matt’s fingers moved across the screen of his device. A moment later, the synthesized voice spoke, clear and uncompromising.

*"I am safe."*

Bernard sighed, taking off his glasses and rubbing the bridge of his nose. "It’s not just about physical safety, Matt. It’s about the precedent. Courtney acted in a way that was completely inappropriate. She tried to pressure you into a situation that—"

Matt interrupted, tapping another sequence of buttons.

*"I do not understand hookup. Sarah explained. Still do not understand. Just a disagreement."*

His sister, Sarah, had tried her best to explain the sexual connotations of what Courtney had demanded that night, using the simplest terms she could find. But to Matt, the abstract concept of physical intimacy was less important than the reality of what he had lost. Courtney hadn't been a predator in his eyes; she had just been a friend who got mad when he said no.

Bernard leaned forward, his elbows resting on the desk. "Matt, whether you fully understand the implications or not, the administration has to look at the facts. She crossed a severe line. The No Contact Order is standard procedure."

Matt didn’t flinch. He looked directly into Bernard’s eyes, his own gaze piercing and remarkably clear. His fingers danced across the screen one last time.

*"I miss my friend. Second chance."*

The synthesized voice hung in the air, echoing slightly in the small office. Arthur shifted his weight near the door, his face softening. Even Bernard looked deeply moved, the bureaucratic shield he wore beginning to crack under the weight of Matt’s pure, unadulterated capacity for forgiveness.

"Matt..." Bernard’s voice cracked slightly, and he cleared his throat. "I admire your heart. I really do. It takes a lot of strength to forgive someone who hurt you. But my hands are tied. The school board, the legal liability... I can’t lift the order. It has to remain in place through the end of the year. I’m sorry."

Matt stared at him for a long moment. He didn't look angry, just profoundly disappointed. He reached out, turned off his device, and slowly pushed the wheels of his chair backward, turning toward the door. Arthur opened it for him, offering a small, comforting nod as Matt rolled out into the hallway.

The order remained. But Matt had made his choice.
