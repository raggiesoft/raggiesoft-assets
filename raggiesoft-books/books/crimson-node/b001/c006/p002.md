The bass from the gymnasium speakers vibrated through the floorboards of the hallway, a rhythmic thumping that synced perfectly with the anxious pounding in Courtney Evans’s chest.

It was Senior Prom. The culmination of four years of high school, the night she had spent months planning, campaigning for, and obsessing over. And she had never felt more alone.

She stood near the edge of the gym doors, wearing a dress that felt suddenly too extravagant for a girl who was supposed to be a ghost. The suspension had ended, but the social exile was in full effect. Word had spread—distorted, exaggerated, but grounded in enough truth to make her a pariah. She had crossed a line with Matt Miller. She had pushed him, demanded things from him, and when he didn't understand, she had snapped.

The guilt was a physical weight, pressing down on her shoulders, making it hard to breathe. 

She hadn't gone looking for a new boyfriend to bring tonight. The thought hadn't even crossed her mind. How could she? She had thrown away the most genuine, unfiltered connection she had ever experienced because of her own selfish, superficial expectations. 

Her family hadn't made it any easier. Her mother had adored Matt. Her brothers, Ryan and Jason, who usually intimidated every guy she brought home, had actually liked him. They had treated him like a person, not a fragile glass doll. And Courtney had ruined it.

She hugged her arms around herself, watching the crowd from the shadows. She had spotted him earlier. Matt was near the punch bowl, looking handsome in a tailored suit, Arthur Vance standing dutifully a few feet away. Courtney’s heart had ached at the sight of him. She missed his laugh. She missed the way his eyes lit up when he successfully conveyed a complex joke through his device. She just missed him.

But the Administrative No Contact Order was absolute. She knew Principal Bernard was in the room, likely keeping a watchful eye on her. She had been a straight-A student her entire academic career. A single misstep tonight—a single step too close to Matt's wheelchair—and she would be recommended for expulsion. Her college prospects, her future, everything would burn to the ground.

So, she stayed in the exile she had earned. She watched the couples sway on the dance floor, swallowed the lump of regret in her throat, and waited for the night to end.
