Saturday evening arrived with the heavy, humid promise of impending summer, but inside Courtney's bedroom, the atmosphere was suffocatingly bleak. 

She stood in front of her full-length mirror, staring blankly at the expensive, deep maroon dress she had bought over a month ago. Back when she had aggressively campaigned for Prom Queen. Back when she had envisioned walking through the gymnasium doors on Matt Vance's arm, proving to the entire school that their relationship was picture-perfect.

It felt like a lifetime ago. 

Her suspension was over, meaning she was legally permitted to attend the dance, but the social exile was absolute. The rumor mill had been ruthless. The student body had uniformly decided she had tried to take advantage of a disabled student, turning her overnight from the golden girl of the cheer squad into a pariah. 

But as she reached up and clipped a simple necklace around her throat, Courtney realized that the rumors weren't what was hurting her. It was the guilt. 

The time alone in her room over the last two weeks had forced her into a brutal reckoning. She recognized how incredibly selfish her physical demands had been. She had let her own shallow frustrations tear away the sweetest, most genuine boy she had ever known. She hadn't even entertained the thought of looking for another date. What was the point? 

There was a soft knock on her bedroom doorframe—the door itself was still missing, a lingering testament to the thunderstorm weeks prior. She turned to see her mother standing there, her expression a mix of sorrow and disappointment. Her brothers, Ryan and Jason, were lingering in the hallway behind her. They all loved Matt. And they all knew exactly why he wasn't here tonight to pin a boutonniere to his lapel.

"You don't have to go, Court," her mother said softly. "No one would blame you if you stayed home."

"I have to go," Courtney whispered, her voice cracking slightly. She picked up her clutch from the bed. "It's my punishment. I have to stand there and watch him from across the room, and I have to know that it's all my fault."
