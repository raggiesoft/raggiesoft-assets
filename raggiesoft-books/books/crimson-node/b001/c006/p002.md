Saturday afternoon at the Chesapeake Bay Mall was a chaotic blend of weekend shoppers and panicked high school seniors making last-minute preparations for Prom. 

Despite the crowds, the Miller and Evans families had managed to secure a large, circular table in the center of the food court, right across from Aunt Mabel's Twists. It was their first time gathering together since the horrific Omni-Q incident two weeks prior, and the initial dynamic was undeniably tense. 

Matt sat in his wheelchair, quietly eating a warm cinnamon sugar pretzel. Across the table, Courtney sat flanked tightly by her two younger brothers, Jason and Ryan. Both boys had their arms resting on the back of her chair, acting as fiercely protective physical barriers. They knew Matt wasn't dangerous, but their sister had spent the last two weeks crying herself to sleep, and their teenage protective instincts were on full display.

But as the lunch wore on, the tension slowly began to melt away. 

David, Matt's massive 6'11" father, cracked a joke about his own disastrous high school prom, pulling a genuine laugh from Courtney. Sarah playfully stole a piece of Jason's pretzel, sparking a brief, laughing argument. By the time Valerie Evans returned to the table with a tray of fresh lemonades, the invisible electric fence had fully powered down. The boys relaxed their posture, and Courtney finally looked across the table at Matt with a soft, unguarded smile. He tapped his AAC device, proudly announcing *"Good pretzel"*, and the table laughed.

As they ate, Valerie glanced up and spotted a familiar figure walking through the food court. Principal Bernard Morgan, dressed casually in a polo shirt, was carrying a small shopping bag and walking toward the mall exit. 

When Bernard saw the two families sitting together, he stopped. He looked at Courtney laughing, and Matt happily chewing his food. As a principal, he knew they were technically violating the spirit of the No Contact Order, but they were entirely off school property. A warm, complicit smile spread across his face.

Instead of looking away, Valerie stood up from the table and waved her hand. "Bernard! Over here!"

Bernard blinked in surprise, but altered his path and walked over to the table. "Valerie. David. Linda. It's good to see all of you." He looked at Matt and Courtney, giving them a small nod. "Enjoying the weekend?"

"We are," Valerie said, her tone shifting to something much more serious. She stepped away from the table, motioning for Bernard to join her a few feet away from the teenagers. Sarah quietly stood up and followed them, her legal guardianship giving her a seat at the adult table.

"Bernard," Valerie said quietly, ensuring her voice didn't carry. "Look at them. This order... it's killing both of them. We know you're just doing your job, but we need to know how to fix this. How do we lift the No Contact Order?"

Bernard sighed, running a hand over his face. "Valerie, my hands are tied. The district is terrified of a negligence lawsuit. The Risk Management department won't let me lift it."

"There has to be a way," Sarah pressed, crossing her arms. "I'm his medical proxy. Matt's mental health is deteriorating because of this separation."

Bernard looked around the bustling food court, ensuring no one from the district office was within earshot. 

"Off the record?" Bernard murmured, leaning in close. "You have to remove their fear of a lawsuit. If your two families sit down together, draft a Joint Liability Release explicitly releasing the school from any negligence claims regarding these two, and walk it directly into the Superintendent's office... they might just grant a waiver. But it has to come from both families, united. That's the only way around the bureaucracy."

Valerie and Sarah exchanged a sharp, determined look. 

"A Joint Liability Release," Sarah repeated. 

"Off the record," Bernard said firmly. He stepped back, offering a polite smile to the table. "Have a wonderful time tonight at Prom. I'll see you all later."
