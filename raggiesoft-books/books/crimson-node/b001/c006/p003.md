The music faded, replaced by the screech of microphone feedback as the student body president tapped the mic on the makeshift stage. The gym quieted down, a collective murmur of anticipation rippling through the crowd.

"Alright, everyone! It’s time for the moment you’ve all been waiting for," the president announced, holding a sealed envelope. "Your 1999 Prom King and Queen are..."

He tore open the envelope. 

"...Matt Miller and Courtney Evans!"

The gym went dead silent. It wasn't the eruption of cheers Courtney had envisioned months ago when she first started campaigning. It was a suffocating, heavy silence. Everyone knew about the suspension. Everyone knew about the No Contact Order. 

Courtney felt the blood drain from her face. She remained rooted near the gym doors, paralyzed. The stage felt miles away.

Near the punch bowl, Arthur Vance gently placed a hand on Matt’s shoulder, a silent question. Matt looked up, his expression unreadable, and gripped the wheels of his chair. 

Before Matt could move, Principal Bernard stepped out from the shadows near the bleachers. He moved quickly, positioning himself squarely between Courtney and the center of the gym. His face was a mask of administrative duty; he was fully prepared to intercept her, to enforce the mandate that kept her away from the boy she had wronged.

Courtney’s breathing grew shallow. She was terrified. If she took even one step toward the stage, toward Matt, Bernard would document it. It would be a violation. It would mean expulsion. All her straight A's, her spotless academic record, her future—gone. She couldn't move. She wanted nothing more than to run to him, to apologize, to hold him, but she couldn't.

And Matt knew it.

From across the room, Matt pushed his wheels. He didn't look at Arthur, and he didn't look at the stage. He moved forward, his chair gliding smoothly across the polished hardwood floor, straight toward the gym doors. Straight toward Courtney.

Bernard held up a hand, stepping into Matt’s path. "Matt, you know we can't—"

Matt didn't slow down. He rolled right past the Principal, fixing Bernard with a firm, unyielding stare. He shook his head once, sharply, and held up a hand to signal *stop*. 

Bernard froze. The dynamic had instantly shifted. Matt was the protected party. He was the one the order was designed to shield. And he was actively, intentionally crossing the room to dismantle it. In a split-second calculation of liability and human decency, Bernard made the choice to step back, lowering his hand.

The crowd parted like the Red Sea as Matt wheeled himself toward the edge of the gym. 

Courtney’s hands flew to her mouth, tears finally spilling over her eyelashes as he stopped inches away from her. The physical distance mandated by the state had been broken. 

Sensing her sheer terror, understanding exactly why she was glued to the floor, Matt took the burden of breaking the rules onto himself. He leaned forward in his wheelchair, reaching out his arms, and initiated the embrace.

He touched her first.

The moment his hands grasped her waist, the paralyzing fear shattered. Courtney let out a sob, leaning down and wrapping her arms tightly around his neck. She buried her face in his shoulder, the smell of his cologne mixing with the scent of her own tears. It was a silent, powerful moment of grace. He had crossed the room to save her from herself, protecting her future while giving her the second chance she hadn't dared to ask for.

As they held each other, a smattering of applause broke out, slowly growing into a roar of cheers that drowned out the silence.
