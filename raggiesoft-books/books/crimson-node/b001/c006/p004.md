When Sarah wheeled Matt through the double glass doors of the Crystal Affairs Banquet Hall, the sensory environment hit them instantly. The lobby was lined with crystal chandeliers and heavy velvet drapes, and the booming, muffled bass of late-90s pop music echoed from the main ballroom. 

Unlike normal chaperones who were teachers or administrators, Sarah was attending the Prom in her official capacity as Matt's 1:1 medical proxy and private duty LPN. Because Arthur Vance was strictly a daytime district employee, Sarah was required to shadow Matt to ensure his medical needs were met. It gave her an all-access pass to the night. 

For the first three hours, the dance was relatively normal. Matt stayed near the edge of the floor, his eyes constantly scanning the crowded room. He knew Courtney was there. Every so often, he would catch a glimpse of her, but she was always maintaining a terrified, fifty-foot distance. 

Near the DJ booth, Principal Bernard Morgan stood watching the crowd. Standing next to him was Richard Sterling, the District Director of Risk Management, who had attended as an official observer to ensure the district's strict zero-tolerance policies were enforced at the off-campus venue.

At 10:30 PM, the booming music finally cut out. The DJ tapped his microphone, and the crowd of three hundred seniors fell into an excited hush.

"Alright, Northwood!" the DJ announced. "It is time for the moment you've all been waiting for. I hold in my hand the sealed envelope containing the results of your senior class vote."

Matt perked up in his wheelchair, his hands resting eagerly on the handrims. 

"Your 1999 Prom King and Queen are... Matt Miller and Courtney Evans!"

The ballroom erupted into deafening applause. Matt let out a loud, unmistakable squeal of pure, unadulterated delight. Completely unbound by district politics and radiating joy, he grabbed his wheels and immediately propelled himself onto the polished wood of the dance floor, heading straight for the center to claim his crown.

Across the floor, Courtney instinctively took two steps forward before the reality of the No Contact Order crashed down on her. She stopped dead in her tracks, frozen under the spotlight, staring in terror at Matt wheeling toward her. 

Near the DJ booth, Principal Bernard's posture rigidified. He took a heavy breath and stepped onto the floor. "I have to stop him," Bernard murmured, terrified of the district's liability. 

Before Bernard could take a full step, Sarah moved. She stepped cleanly between the principal and Matt, placing a firm hand on Bernard's forearm. 

"Bernard, look at her," Sarah whispered intensely. "She isn't moving. If you walk out there and tell him no, he isn't going to understand why. The lights, the crowd, the sudden rejection—he will have a massive meltdown right on that floor. As his LPN and his legal guardian, I am giving you a direct medical order to stand down."

Bernard stared at Sarah for three agonizing seconds, his mind rapidly calculating the warning. He exhaled a long, shaky breath, giving Sarah a subtle, grateful nod, and took a deliberate step back into the shadows. He had chosen compassion over policy.

But Richard Sterling had not.

Furious that the principal had just backed down from enforcing a mandate, the District Risk Manager marched onto the floor, completely bypassing Sarah and Bernard. 

Matt was halfway to Courtney, his face lit up with a massive smile, when Richard suddenly stepped behind him and violently grabbed the push handles of the wheelchair. With a sharp, aggressive yank, Richard forcibly pulled the chair to a dead halt. 

"You are in violation of a district order," Richard snapped loudly, his voice carrying over the front rows of the crowd. "You need to exit this floor immediately."

Matt was violently thrown back against his seat by the sudden stop. The joy vanished in an instant, replaced by sheer shock. An angry stranger had just physically assaulted him by grabbing his chair—an extension of his own body—and was yelling at him under the glaring spotlight. 

The sensory overload, the sudden physical restraint, and the inexplicable rejection were too much. Matt's threshold completely shattered. He let out a piercing, terrified scream, thrashing against his seatbelt, the onset of a massive, public autistic meltdown echoing across the suddenly silent ballroom.
