The drive home was quiet, save for the rhythmic thrum of the van’s engine cutting through the empty, late-night streets. Matt sat secured in the back, staring out the window at the passing streetlights. 

When they finally arrived at the house, the clock on the dashboard read 12:30 AM. Sarah lowered the ramp, unclipped the tie-downs, and helped Matt navigate into the quiet house. Once inside the familiar comfort of his bedroom, Matt powered on his AAC device. The bright screen illuminated his face in the dim room.

*"Sad,"* the device announced in its flat, synthesized tone. *"No goodbye to Courtney."*

Sarah paused, unbuttoning her jacket, and offered a soft, sympathetic smile. "I know, Matty. But honestly, it was probably for the best. The No Contact Order is still active. If you had lingered to talk to her, someone might have reported it, and she could have gotten in serious trouble. You protected her by leaving when you did."

Matt looked down at his lap, processing her words. He slowly nodded, though the melancholy didn't fully lift from his shoulders. 

"Come on," Sarah said gently, shifting into her caretaker routine. "Let's get you cleaned up. It's been a long night."

The evening ADL routine was a familiar, well-choreographed dance between the siblings. Sarah helped Matt into the accessible bathroom, assisting him with his shower and ensuring he was comfortable. She helped him with his toileting routine, preserving his dignity with the effortless grace of someone who had been doing this for him for years. Once he was dried off, she helped him change out of his formal suit and into a soft, comfortable set of pajamas.

After Matt was settled in his wheelchair, Sarah took her own quick turn in the bathroom, showering, taking care of her own nighttime routine, and slipping into her pajamas. 

They returned to the bedroom, the house completely silent around them. Sarah helped Matt transfer from his wheelchair into the large bed they shared. She pulled the blankets up over his shoulders, making sure his limbs were positioned comfortably, before climbing into the bed beside him.

Sarah turned off the bedside lamp, plunging the room into darkness. She scooted closer, wrapping an arm gently around him and cuddling up against his side. Matt leaned into her embrace, the warmth of his sister a comforting anchor. 

But as they drifted toward sleep, Sarah could sense the subtle tension in his breathing, the restless energy beneath the surface. He was safe, and he was home, but she knew his mind was still miles away in a crowded gymnasium. He was still upset over the whole situation with Courtney, the bureaucratic walls keeping him from the friend he missed so dearly. 

She held him a little tighter in the dark, hoping the morning would bring a clearer path forward.
