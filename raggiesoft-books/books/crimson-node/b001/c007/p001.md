The morning sun streamed through the blinds, casting warm, golden slats of light across the bedroom. It was Sunday, the day after Prom, and the house was comfortably quiet. Their parents had already left for their weekend shifts at the hospital, leaving the house to the two siblings.

Sarah stirred first. She sat up, stretching her arms above her head, before turning to look at Matt. He was already awake, lying on his back and staring up at the ceiling. The heavy, unresolved tension from the night before still seemed to linger in the air around him. 

She scooted over and leaned down, wrapping her arms securely around his shoulders. Matt leaned into her, burying his face in her shoulder as they exchanged a massive, grounding hug. For a long moment, they just held each other, the unspoken bond between them communicating more than words ever could.

When Sarah finally pulled back, she offered him a warm, determined smile. "I know you're still upset about what happened last night," she said softly, brushing a stray piece of hair from his forehead. "But I couldn't tell you this in the car because I wanted you to actually get some sleep. I have an idea for Courtney. Principal Bernard gave me the blueprint last night."

Matt’s eyes widened slightly, a spark of hope replacing the melancholy. 

"I'm going to call her parents this morning," Sarah continued, her voice full of quiet resolve. "We're going to fix this. But first, let's get our day started."

She threw off the covers and moved effortlessly into their morning routine. First, she helped Matt transfer into his manual wheelchair and navigated him into the accessible bathroom. She assisted him with his morning toileting routine, handling the deeply personal task with the gentle, practiced dignity she always provided. Once he was finished and his hands were washed, Sarah took a quick turn using the toilet and washing up herself.

Back in the bedroom, she helped Matt out of his pajamas and into a fresh set of comfortable weekend clothes—a soft cotton t-shirt and loose-fitting athletic pants. Once he was settled and comfortable in his chair, she quickly changed into a pair of jeans and a casual sweater.

"Alright," Sarah said, clapping her hands together lightly. "Let's get some breakfast."

She wheeled Matt out into the bright, open living room and parked him at the dining table. The house was silent, save for the distant hum of the refrigerator. She walked into the kitchen and opened the pantry, pulling out a large box of toasted oat cereal. 

She poured a generous bowl for Matt, covering the cereal in rich, whole milk. Next, she grabbed a carton of chocolate syrup from the fridge, mixing up a large, cold glass of chocolate milk to go with it. She set the breakfast down in front of him, then poured a simple bowl of cereal for herself, taking a seat across the table. 

As they ate in the quiet morning light, Sarah’s mind was already racing, piecing together exactly what she was going to say when she picked up the phone.
