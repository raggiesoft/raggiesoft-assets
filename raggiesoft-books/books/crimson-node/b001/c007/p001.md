The morning sun streamed through the blinds, casting warm, golden slats of light across the living room. It was Sunday, the day after Prom, and the Miller household was quiet. David and Linda had already left for their weekend shifts at the hospital, leaving the house to their children—and their unexpected overnight guest.

Courtney woke up slowly on the plush living room sofa. She blinked against the sunlight, taking a moment to remember where she was. When the memories of the banquet hall and the terrifying meltdown came rushing back, she sat up quickly, her eyes darting toward the hallway. 

A moment later, Sarah wheeled Matt out of the bedroom. He was dressed in a soft cotton t-shirt and loose-fitting athletic pants, looking significantly more rested than he had at 1:00 AM. 

When Matt saw Courtney sitting on the sofa, his face immediately lit up. He reached out a hand, and Courtney didn't hesitate. She threw the blankets off and walked over, taking his hand and giving it a gentle, grounding squeeze.

"Good morning," Courtney smiled softly. "How are you feeling?"

Matt tapped a button on the AAC device mounted to his wheelchair. *"Safe,"* the synthesized voice replied simply. 

"Good," Sarah said, walking up behind the chair and resting her hands on the push handles. She was dressed casually in jeans and a sweater, her protective energy from the night before having settled into a focused, calm determination. "Because you two are safe here. But today, we need to make sure you're safe everywhere else, too."

Sarah parked Matt at the dining table, and Courtney took the seat right next to him. The house was silent, save for the distant hum of the refrigerator. Sarah walked into the kitchen and opened the pantry, pulling out a large box of toasted oat cereal. 

She poured a generous bowl for Matt, covering the cereal in rich, whole milk, and quickly mixed up a large, cold glass of chocolate milk to go with it. She set the breakfast down in front of him, then poured bowls for herself and Courtney, taking a seat across the table. 

As they ate in the quiet morning light, the heavy, unresolved tension of the No Contact Order seemed a million miles away. For the first time in a month, Matt and Courtney were just allowed to exist in the same room without fear of suspension or reprimand. 

"So," Sarah began, setting her spoon down and leaning forward. "Valerie is coming over after lunch. We are going to sit down at this table, and we are going to write the Joint Liability Release."

Courtney looked up, her expression a mix of hope and lingering anxiety. "Do you really think it will work? The district was so adamant about keeping us apart."

"The district was adamant about not getting sued," Sarah corrected firmly. "That's all the No Contact Order ever was, Courtney. It was a bureaucratic shield. Principal Bernard told Valerie and me yesterday—if both families sign a document stating that we hold the school harmless, they lose their only excuse to enforce the order."

Matt reached over and gently tapped Courtney’s arm to get her attention. When she looked at him, he offered a determined nod. 

"He's right," Sarah said, smiling softly at her brother. "After what Richard Sterling did last night, the district is terrified. They assaulted a disabled student in front of three hundred witnesses. When we walk into the Superintendent's office tomorrow morning with a signed release, they aren't going to fight us. They're going to sign it immediately to keep us from going to the press."

Courtney absorbed the information, a small, genuine smile finally breaking across her face. The nightmare was actually ending. She looked at Matt, who was happily eating his cereal, completely at peace now that she was back by his side.

"Okay," Courtney said, her voice steadying. "Let's write it."
