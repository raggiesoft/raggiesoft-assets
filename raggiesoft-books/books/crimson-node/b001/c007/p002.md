By early afternoon, the Miller household had transformed into a makeshift legal war room. 

Valerie Evans arrived just after 1:00 PM. She walked into the living room and immediately saw her daughter sitting on the floor next to Matt’s wheelchair. They were watching a movie on the television, sharing a large bowl of popcorn. The sight of them together, so relaxed and happy, solidified everything Valerie had realized the night before. Separating them had been a terrible mistake. 

David and Linda Miller, having returned from their half-day shifts at the hospital, welcomed Valerie warmly. There was no animosity between the parents. They all understood that the school district was the real enemy in this situation, and they were united in their goal to protect their children.

Sarah cleared off the large dining room table, laying out several notepads, pens, and her laptop. She motioned for David, Linda, and Valerie to sit down. 

"Alright," Sarah said, pulling up a blank document on her screen. "Principal Bernard was very specific about what this document needs to say. It has to be a Joint Liability Release, signed by both the Evans family and the Miller family, explicitly stating that neither party will hold the school district liable for any interactions between Courtney and Matt."

Valerie nodded, pulling a pen from her purse. "I'm ready to sign whatever it takes. I saw what happened to Matt last night. That administrator treated him like a piece of property, not a human being. I am not letting the district dictate my daughter's friendships anymore."

"Thank you, Valerie," Linda said warmly, reaching across the table to squeeze the other mother's hand. 

Sarah began typing, her fingers flying across the keyboard with practiced efficiency. She drafted the letter with precise, clinical language, leaving absolutely no room for district lawyers to misinterpret their intent. 

*"We, the undersigned legal guardians of Matthew Miller and Courtney Evans, hereby jointly agree to release the school district from any and all liability regarding the interpersonal boundaries and physical contact between our children. We formally revoke the No Contact Order and assert that both students are free to interact without restriction."*

Sarah read the paragraph aloud to the table. 

"Add a line about medical proxy," David suggested, his arms crossed over his chest. "Make it clear that Sarah retains full authority over Matt's medical and physical safety, and that the district has no right to intervene unless Sarah explicitly requests it."

"Good call," Sarah nodded, quickly typing the addition. 

Once the document was polished and reviewed by everyone at the table, Sarah connected her laptop to the wireless printer in the corner of the room. The machine hummed to life, printing three crisp copies of the Joint Liability Release.

Sarah placed the documents on the table. David and Linda signed first, their signatures bold and decisive. Valerie took the pen next, signing her name with a deep sense of finality. The No Contact Order was officially dead, at least in the eyes of the families. 

Sarah carefully placed the signed copies into a manila folder and slid it into her bag. 

"Tomorrow morning, 8:00 AM," Sarah announced, looking around the table. "Matt and I are going straight to the Superintendent's office at the Municipal Center. We hand them this folder, and this whole nightmare is over."

Courtney, who had been listening from the living room, stood up and walked over to the table. She looked at the manila folder, then up at Sarah. "Can I come with you?"

Sarah smiled softly, shaking her head. "No. You're going to go to school and enjoy your Monday as a senior. Matt and I will handle the bureaucrats. When we're done, we'll bring him to school, and you two can finally walk down the hallway together without looking over your shoulders."

Matt, who had rolled up beside Courtney, tapped his AAC device. *"Victory,"* he announced, a bright, confident smile on his face. 

The entire room laughed, the heavy weight of the past month finally lifting from their shoulders. The battle wasn't entirely over—they still had to face the Superintendent—but for the first time, they were armed with exactly what they needed to win.
