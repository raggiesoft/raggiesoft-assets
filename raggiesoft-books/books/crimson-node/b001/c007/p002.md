After breakfast, Sarah helped Matt transition from the dining table to the large, plush sofa in the living room. It was still relatively early in the morning, the house bathed in the quiet, lazy atmosphere of a Sunday. 

Sarah sat down next to him and immediately pulled him into her side. Matt rested his head against her shoulder, melting into the comforting weight of his sister's embrace. They sat like that for a while, the silence broken only by the faint ticking of the wall clock. Matt needed this—a quiet, uncomplicated moment to just exist after the emotional whiplash of the previous night.

"Mom and Dad already know," Sarah said softly, her fingers gently tracing circles on his back. "They're on board with whatever you decide you want. If you want this order gone, they'll sign whatever it takes to make it disappear."

Matt shifted slightly, turning his head to look up at her. He didn't have his AAC device within reach, but his eyes asked the question clearly enough.

"Principal Bernard told me off the record last night," Sarah explained, her voice low and steady. "The district is terrified of liability. That's all this No Contact Order is—a shield. If we go to them with a Joint Liability Release... a legal document signed by both our parents and Courtney's parents saying that nobody will hold the school accountable for what happened... it forces the superintendent's hand. They won't have an excuse to keep the order in place."

Matt nodded slowly, absorbing the information. A spark of genuine hope ignited in his chest.

"But, Matty," Sarah cautioned gently, her expression sobering. "We don't know if Courtney's mom is going to agree to this. Or Courtney, for that matter. She's been through the wringer, and her mom was furious when she got suspended. They might just want to keep their heads down, serve out the rest of the year, and graduate without making any more waves."

Matt closed his eyes for a moment, resting his head back against Sarah. He knew how scared Courtney was. He remembered the sheer terror in her eyes when he hugged her on the dance floor, the paralyzing fear that she was about to be expelled. But he also remembered the way she had melted into the hug, the profound relief of two friends who had just desperately missed each other. 

He firmly believed that if they gave Courtney an out, she would take it.

"I'm going to call them in a little bit," Sarah said, giving him a tight, reassuring squeeze. "I'll ask them to come over so we can explain it. If they say yes, we draft the release today, and we march into the superintendent's office tomorrow morning."

Matt snuggled closer, letting the warmth of his sister and the promise of a concrete plan wash over him. For the first time in over two weeks, the oppressive weight on his chest began to lift.
