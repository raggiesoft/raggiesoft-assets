Just shy of lunchtime, Sarah picked up the kitchen phone and dialed the Evans' household. It took a few rings, but Courtney's mother, Valerie, finally answered. She sounded guarded, her voice tight with the nervous instinct of a mother protecting her daughter from further fallout. But when Sarah gently explained that they wanted to help Courtney, not hurt her, Valerie hesitantly agreed to meet. Sarah suggested a quiet, local family diner and insisted on treating them all to lunch.

When Sarah and Matt arrived at the restaurant, the Evans family was already waiting in a corner booth. Valerie looked exhausted but resolute, sitting across from her three children. Courtney sat pinned in the middle of the opposite bench, flanked by her two younger brothers, Jason and Ryan. Both boys, fiercely protective, had their arms draped securely around their older sister's shoulders.

Sarah wheeled Matt up to the edge of the table and took the remaining seat next to Valerie. The tension in the air was palpable, thick with anxiety and unspoken apologies. 

A waitress came by, and they quickly ordered a round of sodas and a large plate of appetizer fries for the table. Once the waitress left, Sarah leaned forward, resting her hands on the table.

"Valerie, Courtney," Sarah began, her tone gentle but strictly business. "I want to be clear right off the bat: Matt and I are not here to drag this out. We want the No Contact Order gone just as much as you do."

Valerie blinked, visibly surprised. The boys' grips on Courtney tightened slightly. They all knew Sarah was one of Matt's legal guardians, fully authorized to make decisions on his behalf, and they had clearly braced themselves for a scolding. 

Sarah pulled a manila folder from her bag and slid a neatly typed document across the table. "This is a Joint Liability Release. Principal Bernard told me, off the record, that the district's only concern right now is liability. If we all sign this document stating that we hold the school harmless and waive our right to sue over anything regarding Matt's physical boundaries, they won't have a reason to keep the order in place."

Courtney stared down at the paper, her eyes wide. Valerie picked it up, reading through the legal jargon with a trembling hand. "You... you'd really do this for her?" Valerie asked, her voice cracking slightly.

"We're doing it for both of them," Sarah corrected, glancing warmly at Matt. Matt tapped a button on his AAC device. 

*"I want my friend back,"* the synthesized voice stated plainly. 

A tear slipped down Courtney's cheek, and Jason pulled her a little tighter against his side. The waitress returned, breaking the heavy moment as they all ordered their entrees. 

Over the next half hour, they hashed out the finer details of the release. Because both Matt and Courtney were officially eighteen, their signatures were required alongside their guardians'. Valerie signed her name with a flourish of profound relief. Sarah signed next, utilizing her Joint and Several guardianship authority. She then passed the pen to Courtney, who signed her name with a shaky hand. 

Finally, the document was placed on Matt’s wheelchair tray. Because he couldn't grip a pen to sign a full name, Sarah guided his hand, helping him firmly mark a large 'X' on the signature line. Sarah then signed her own name again directly beneath it as his official witness.

By the time their food arrived, the atmosphere had transformed entirely. The paralyzing anxiety was gone, replaced by a tentative but genuine camaraderie. They ate their lunch in comfortable conversation, the brothers finally relaxing their protective hold on Courtney. 

When the plates were cleared, Sarah settled the bill and neatly packed the signed release back into her folder. "Alright," she said, looking around the table. "This is a massive step, but the order isn't officially lifted yet. I'm taking this directly to the District Superintendent tomorrow morning right after I drop Matt off at school."

They stepped out into the bright afternoon sun to say their goodbyes. Matt navigated his chair over to Courtney. She looked at him nervously, though the raw terror of Prom night was gone. She leaned down, and Matt wrapped his arms around her neck, giving her a firm, reassuring hug. She hugged him back, burying her face in his shoulder for a brief moment.

As the families parted ways, Sarah loaded Matt into their adapted van, while Valerie ushered Courtney, Jason, and Ryan into their sedan. It had been a long two weeks, but for the first time, the end was finally in sight.
