By the time Sarah parked the van in their driveway and helped Matt back into the house, the emotional exhaustion of the afternoon was beginning to set in. The sheer adrenaline of the lunch meeting was fading, leaving Matt with the quiet, overwhelming reality of everything that had just transpired.

Once he was comfortably situated in the living room, Sarah dialed their cousin, Shiloh, putting the phone on speaker and resting it on the coffee table. Shiloh had been a constant source of support for Matt, and Sarah effortlessly fell into the role of his translator, articulating the complex emotions he couldn't easily convey through his AAC device.

"So she signed it?" Shiloh’s voice crackled warmly through the speaker. "That's incredible, Matty. You’re going to get your friend back."

Matt stared down at his hands, his fingers twisting anxiously in his lap. The thought of having Courtney back in his life was everything he had fought for over the past two weeks. But sitting there, listening to Shiloh's encouraging voice, a heavy, sinking realization suddenly crashed over him. 

The big move to Charlottesville was looming. It was coming soon. 

If the superintendent approved the Joint Liability Release tomorrow, Matt would finally have his best friend back. The No Contact Order would be dissolved. But then, almost immediately, he would have to pack up his life and leave her behind anyway. 

The crushing weight of that contradiction was too much. A quiet, frustrated sob broke from Matt's chest. The tears he had been holding back since the morning finally spilled over. He was so confused, so intensely torn between the joy of fixing his friendship and the heartbreak of knowing he was moving away.

Sarah didn't hesitate. She abandoned her spot on the armchair and moved onto the sofa, pulling Matt firmly into her arms. 

"I've got you," she whispered, holding him tight as he cried against her shoulder. "I know. It's too much. It's all just too much."

On the other end of the line, Shiloh fell completely silent. She didn't offer empty platitudes or try to rush him through his feelings. She just stayed on the line, an anchoring, supportive presence, fully understanding that her cousin was overwhelmed and needed the space to let it out. 

It took a several long minutes for the storm to pass. When Matt's breathing finally steadied and he pulled back, Sarah wiped his cheeks with the sleeve of her sweater, offering him a gentle, reassuring smile. 

"You back with us, Matty?" Shiloh asked softly through the speaker. 

Matt tapped a button on his device. *"Yes."*

Sensing the delicate emotional balance, Shiloh seamlessly pivoted the conversation. She made an unspoken executive decision to drop any mention of Charlottesville, the impending move, or the complexities of his friendship with Courtney. Instead, she launched into a lighthearted story about a ridiculous movie she had watched the night before, filling the living room with easy, uncomplicated chatter.

Matt relaxed back against the sofa cushions, letting Shiloh's voice wash over him. The distraction worked perfectly, pulling him out of his own head and grounding him back in the safety of his family.

After another half hour of comfortable conversation, it was time to wrap things up. 

"I'll let you guys go," Shiloh said warmly. "Get some rest, Matty. You’ve had a huge day. I love you."

*"Love you too,"* Matt’s device replied.

Sarah reached forward and disconnected the call. She looked over at Matt, giving his knee an affectionate squeeze. "Mom and Dad will be home from their shifts soon. We should probably figure out exactly how we want to tell them about our little lunch date with the Evans family."
