David and Linda Miller walked through the front door just after five o'clock, both still wearing their hospital scrubs. The familiar sound of their keys hitting the entryway table signaled the official end of the workday. 

They found Sarah and Matt right where they had left them that morning, settled comfortably in the living room. Sarah had the manila folder sitting on the coffee table in front of them.

"Hey, you two," Linda said, her tired eyes softening into a warm smile as she walked over and kissed the top of Matt's head. David followed close behind, dropping a heavy hand on Matt's shoulder in a silent, affectionate greeting. 

"How was your day?" David asked, taking a seat on the armchair. 

"Eventful," Sarah replied, sitting up straight. She picked up the folder and handed it to her father. "We took a pretty big step today. I had lunch with the Evans family."

David’s eyebrows shot up. He opened the folder, with Linda leaning over the armrest to read the document inside. 

Because Sarah wasn't a lawyer, the Joint Liability Release was typed up in plain, unmistakable English:

*We, the undersigned families of Matt Miller and Courtney Evans, formally agree that neither student holds the other, nor the school district, responsible or liable for any physical boundaries crossed on May 3, 1999. We agree that the incident was a misunderstanding between friends, not an act of malice or predation. Furthermore, we ask the District Superintendent to immediately dissolve the Administrative No Contact Order currently separating the two students, as both families are fully aware of the situation and consent to their renewed friendship.*

Beneath the text were the signatures of Valerie Evans, Courtney Evans, Sarah Miller, and the unmistakable, firm 'X' drawn by Matt.

David read the document twice before looking up at Sarah, a mix of pride and awe on his face. "You actually got them to sign this? After everything that happened?"

"I did," Sarah said, a quiet triumph in her voice. "Principal Bernard practically gave me the blueprint last night. The district is just terrified of a lawsuit. If we give them this, they don't have an excuse to keep the order active."

Linda moved from the armrest to sit on the sofa beside Matt. She took his hand in hers, giving it a gentle squeeze. "This is a big step, Matty," she said softly. "Are you sure this is what you want? We know how much you care about her, but we just want to make sure you're feeling safe and ready for this."

Matt didn't hesitate. He tapped the keys on his AAC device with steady conviction. 

*"I am ready. I want my friend."*

David nodded, closing the folder and handing it back to Sarah. He looked directly at his son, his expression full of unwavering support. "Then we're with you, a hundred percent. We told Sarah this morning, and we’re telling you now: whatever decision you make, Mom and I are on board." 

He then turned his gaze to his daughter. "And Sarah has the authority to make it happen. You're his guardian too, Sarah. If you take this to the superintendent tomorrow, you speak for all of us."

Sarah nodded, carefully sliding the release back into the folder. The path forward was finally clear. The paperwork was signed, the family was united, and the only thing left to do was march into the district office first thing Monday morning.
