The smell of rubbing alcohol and stale coffee made Claire nauseous. She stood at the bank of payphones in the corner of the ER waiting room, her fingers trembling so violently she dropped the quarter twice before finally forcing it into the slot. 

She pressed the heavy receiver to her ear, listening to the dial tone as she stared blankly at the swinging double doors of Trauma One. That was where they had taken him. That was where her baby was. 

She dialed the number from memory. It rang three times before a warm, familiar voice picked up.

"Hello, pet," Mrs. Bea answered, her thick Newcastle accent a sudden, jarring reminder of the safe, normal world Claire had just been ripped away from. In the background, Claire could hear the faint sound of the television. Maggie was there, nursing her fever on Mrs. Bea's sofa. 

"Bea," Claire choked out. The sound of her own voice broke the dam, and a sob ripped through her chest. She pressed her forehead against the cool metal of the payphone enclosure, trying to muffle the sound. 

"Claire? Good heavens, child, what's wrong? Where are you?" The older woman's voice instantly sharpened with maternal alarm. 

"I'm at the children's hospital in Norfolk," Claire gasped, squeezing her eyes shut. "It's Drew. We're in the ER. A whole team of doctors just took him back."

"Lord almighty," Mrs. Bea breathed. "I'll pack up the girl and we'll come straight down."

"No, Bea, wait," Claire said, wiping at her eyes. "Maggie's temperature was 101 this morning. I know it's falling, but you can't bring a sick six-year-old into a hospital. Especially not now. She shouldn't see him like this."

Mrs. Bea hesitated, but she knew Claire was right. "Okay. I've got Maggie. Don't you worry about her for a second. What happened to the boy?"

"He... he said the instructor stepped on him," Claire stammered, shaking her head. Her mind still violently rejected the image she had seen in the bathroom. "But he's six, Bea. Maybe he fell, and the man tripped over him? Who steps on a child? It had to be an accident..."

Before Mrs. Bea could answer, a heavy set of footsteps stopped directly behind Claire. 

She turned. Standing there was a tall, uniformed police officer, his face incredibly serious. Next to him was a woman in a sweater holding a clipboard, wearing a hospital ID badge that read *Clinical Social Worker*. 

"Claire?" Mrs. Bea's voice crackled through the receiver. "What's happening?"

"Bea, I have to go. Call me when you can." 

"I will." Claire hung up the phone. 

The police officer took his hat off, holding it in front of him. "Ma'am, are you Drew's mother?"

"Yes," Claire said, her voice shaking as she backed up against the payphone. "Is he... is my son okay?"

"The trauma surgeons are working on him right now, ma'am," the social worker said softly, stepping forward. "My name is Sarah, and this is Officer Miller. I know this is terrifying, but we need to ask you a few questions."

Claire wrapped her arms around herself. "He said his instructor stepped on him. But... what if I'm wrong? What if—"

"Ma'am, please stop," Sarah interrupted gently, offering a reassuring smile. "We are not here to accuse you. The footprint bruised into your son's abdomen is a men's size ten or eleven barefoot imprint. It is far too large to belong to you. Furthermore, in cases of sudden domestic rage, an abuser does not take the time to remove their shoes before striking a child. We already know it wasn't you."

Claire exhaled a shaky breath, the terrifying weight of suspicion lifting, only to be replaced by a cold, horrifying reality. 

"Because of the nature and severity of the injury, the hospital is required by law to contact us and Child Protective Services immediately," Officer Miller explained, pulling a small notepad from his chest pocket. "This is being treated as an aggravated assault on a minor. I need to know exactly where this happened."

"It's called Pinnacle Mastery School of Karate," Claire said, her voice hardening. "It operates out of the Tidewater Tumblers building in Chesapeake. 912 Professional Place, Suite A. Today was supposed to be his and his twin sister's first day... but Maggie stayed home sick with our neighbor, Mrs. Bea."

Officer Miller's eyebrows raised slightly in recognition. "Mrs. Bea? The older British woman? She's watched my own kids in the past. If your daughter is with her, she's perfectly safe." He clicked his pen. "Did you see anything else when you were there?"

Claire thought back to the lobby. "When we were leaving, I saw an older boy, maybe ten years old. He was practically carrying a little girl out of the lobby. She was crying, holding her ribs like she was in absolute agony. I assumed they were heading to the parking lot because she pulled a muscle."

Claire paused, a sickening realization washing over her. "They both had brown belts tied around their uniforms. Drew is a white belt, but brown belts are advanced students. If an advanced student is being broken in half... my god, this wasn't an accident."

Officer Miller's jaw tightened. He snapped his notepad shut. "No, ma'am, it wasn't." He reached for the radio on his shoulder. "I'm dispatching units to that address right now."
