The smell of rubbing alcohol and stale coffee made Claire nauseous. She stood at the bank of payphones in the corner of the ER waiting room, her fingers trembling so violently she dropped the quarter twice before finally forcing it into the slot. 

She pressed the heavy receiver to her ear, listening to the dial tone as she stared blankly at the swinging double doors of Trauma One. That was where they had taken him. That was where her baby was. 

She dialed the number from memory. It rang three times before a warm, familiar voice picked up.

"Hello, pet," Mrs. Bea answered, her thick Newcastle accent a sudden, jarring reminder of the safe, normal world Claire had just been ripped away from. In the background, Claire could hear the faint sound of the television. Maggie was there, nursing her fever on Mrs. Bea's sofa. 

"Bea," Claire choked out. The sound of her own voice broke the dam, and a sob ripped through her chest. She pressed her forehead against the cool metal of the payphone enclosure, trying to muffle the sound. 

"Claire? Good heavens, child, what's wrong? Where are you?" The older woman's voice instantly sharpened with maternal alarm. 

"I'm at the children's hospital in Norfolk," Claire gasped, squeezing her eyes shut. "It's Drew. We're in the ER. A whole team of doctors just took him back."

"Lord almighty," Mrs. Bea breathed. "What happened? Did he fall? Was there a crash?"

"I... I don't know," Claire stammered, shaking her head even though Mrs. Bea couldn't see her. Her mind violently rejected the image she had seen in the bathroom. "He said... he said the instructor stepped on him. But he's six, Bea. He must be confused. Maybe he fell, and the man tripped over him? Who steps on a child? It had to be an accident..."

"Claire, listen to me," Mrs. Bea said gently but firmly, cutting through the panic. "Is the boy breathing?"

"They said his blood pressure was dropping. He was so cold, Bea. He was turning blue." Claire pressed her hand over her mouth, her eyes darting back to the trauma doors. "Oh god, Bea, I was just sitting in the lobby. I was reading a book. I thought they were just doing a tough workout. When we were leaving... I saw another pair of kids."

"What kids?" 

"An older boy. Maybe ten years old. He was practically carrying this little girl out to the parking lot," Claire whispered, the realization slowly, sickeningly piecing itself together in her mind. "She was holding her ribs and crying. I thought she had just pulled a muscle. But now... Bea, what if it wasn't an accident?"

Before Mrs. Bea could answer, a heavy set of footsteps stopped directly behind Claire. 

She turned. Standing there was a tall, uniformed police officer, his face incredibly serious. Next to him was a woman in a sweater holding a clipboard, wearing a hospital ID badge that read *Clinical Social Worker*. 

"Claire?" Mrs. Bea's voice crackled through the receiver. "What's happening?"

"Bea, I have to go," Claire whispered. 

"I've got Maggie. Don't you worry about her for a second. You just look after that boy. Call me when you know."

"I will." Claire hung up the phone. 

The police officer took his hat off, holding it in front of him. "Ma'am, are you Drew's mother?"

"Yes," Claire said, her voice shaking as she backed up against the payphone. "Is he... is my son okay?"

"The trauma surgeons are working on him right now, ma'am," the social worker said softly, stepping forward. "My name is Sarah, and this is Officer Miller. The doctors found a footprint bruise across your son's abdomen."

"He said his instructor stepped on him," Claire blurted out, the words finally feeling real as she said them to a police officer. "At Ray's Dojang in Chesapeake. Master Curtis."

The officer's jaw tightened. He pulled a small notepad from his chest pocket. "Ma'am, because of the nature and severity of the injury, the hospital is required by law to contact us and Child Protective Services immediately. This is being treated as an aggravated assault on a minor. I need you to tell me everything you saw, and exactly where this Dojang is located."

Claire stared at the officer. The denial finally shattered, replaced by a cold, overwhelming wave of rage. It wasn't an accident. That monster had hurt her baby. 

She wiped her tears away, her hands balling into fists. "I'll tell you exactly where he is."
