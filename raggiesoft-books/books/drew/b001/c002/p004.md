Claire pressed the heels of her hands against her eyes. The tears were threatening to spill over, but she fiercely blinked them back. Crying wasn't going to help Drew. Crying wasn't going to catch the man who had done this.

"You need me to tell you everything I saw," Claire said, dropping her hands to her lap. Her voice was trembling, but it was anchored by a sudden, protective fury. "I need you to catch him."

Officer Miller leaned forward, his pen poised. "Exactly. I need you to walk me through the entire hour. Did you go back into the training area at any point?"

"No," Claire shook her head. "I paid the woman at the front desk, and then I sat on the couch in the lobby. There are blinds blocking the window into the actual gym, so I couldn't see what was happening. I just read my book until class ended at six-thirty."

"Did you hear anything unusual? Yelling, crying?"

"It’s a martial arts class," Claire said bitterly. "They were all yelling the whole time. Kiai, counting in Korean. I didn't hear any screams of pain. Or if I did, they blended in with the rest of it." She paused, her brow furrowing as a memory snapped into sharp focus. "Wait. When class ended... I did see something."

Miller’s pen paused. "What did you see?"

"When all the students came out into the lobby at six-thirty, there were two kids," Claire said, her words tumbling out faster now. "A boy, maybe ten or eleven, and a little girl, around Drew's age. The boy was practically carrying her. She was crying and holding her ribs."

"Did you catch their names?"

"No," Claire said, frustrated. "But I remember noticing their belts. They both had brown belts on. The woman at the front desk told me earlier that brown belts are the advanced students."

"And they both looked injured?" Miller pressed.

"Yes. The boy looked like he was in pain, too. He was favoring his leg, maybe? But he was focused on getting the little girl out of there. At the time, I just assumed they had collided during a drill, or maybe they were practicing some advanced throws." Claire’s breath hitched as the sickening reality washed over her. "But it wasn't an accident, was it? He didn't just hurt my son. He hurt the advanced students, too."

Miller wrote furiously, underlining the words 'brown belts'. "This changes things," he muttered, almost to himself. "If we have multiple victims in one session... Chesapeake PD needs to lock down that entire roster immediately."

Sarah, the social worker, looked at Claire with deep sympathy. "You’ve given us incredibly important information, Claire. You may have just helped protect a lot of other children tonight."

"It doesn't protect my son," Claire whispered, her gaze dropping to the floor. The fury was receding, leaving only the crushing weight of helplessness. 

"I'm going to step out and relay this to the units at the Dojang," Miller said, standing up. "They need to find those two brown belts. Claire, I know how hard this is, but you are doing exactly what Drew needs you to do right now."

As Miller left the room, the heavy hospital door clicked shut behind him. The silence rushed back in, thick and suffocating. Claire looked up at the beige walls, realizing that the hardest part of the night hadn't even begun. 

Somewhere, floors above her, a surgical team was cutting into her six-year-old baby. And all she could do was wait.
