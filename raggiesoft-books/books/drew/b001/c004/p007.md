Claire sat in the vinyl recliner beside Drew's hospital bed, staring blankly at the small pocket calendar she held in her lap. With the tip of a blue ballpoint pen, she counted the weeks again. 

One, two, three... 

Drew’s accident had been on October fifth. It was now December twenty-second. The Family and Medical Leave Act, a law passed just two years prior, guaranteed her twelve weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave. Twelve weeks sounded like a lifetime when she first filled out the HR paperwork, a massive safety net to get her son through the worst of his recovery.

Eleven weeks and one day. That was where she stood today.

She had less than a week left before her job protection expired right after the new year. If she didn't return to work, she would be terminated. If she was terminated, they would lose their health insurance. The sheer terror of losing coverage while Drew was racking up a six-figure hospital bill felt like a physical weight pressing down on her chest.

"Mom?" Drew’s voice pulled her from the spiral. 

Claire quickly snapped the calendar shut, slipping it into her purse. "Yes, sweetie?"

"Can we put the star on the tree today?" He gestured toward the small, two-foot artificial Christmas tree sitting on his windowsill. It was covered in miniature ornaments and a string of battery-operated lights, but the top branch remained bare. 

"We absolutely can," Claire said, forcing a bright, cheerful smile. "But we have to wait for your sister. You know she'd be heartbroken if we did the star without her."

Right on cue, the heavy door to the room swung open. Maggie burst in like a miniature hurricane, wearing a reindeer antler headband and a coat that wasn't fully zipped. She held a construction paper snowflake in one hand and a half-eaten candy cane in the other. 

"I'm on Christmas break!" Maggie announced triumphantly to the room. "No more school until next year!"

Mrs. Bea followed closely behind her, carrying Maggie’s heavy backpack. "Half-day," Bea said with a tired but affectionate smile. "I picked her up right from the carpool line and we came straight here."

"Maggie!" Drew grinned, maneuvering the joystick of his power chair to turn and face her. "Did you have your party?"

"Yeah! We had cupcakes with green frosting, and Mrs. Miller gave us all pencils with snowmen on them," Maggie said, immediately dropping her snowflake on Drew's lap. "I made this for you. We can tape it to the window."

Claire stood up, kissing the top of Maggie’s head before stepping over to give Mrs. Bea a grateful hug. "Thank you for grabbing her. I really appreciate it."

"Of course, honey," Mrs. Bea murmured. She pulled back slightly, her sharp eyes studying the dark circles under Claire's eyes. "You look like you're carrying the weight of the world. What’s going on?"

Claire glanced over her shoulder. Maggie was currently regaling Drew with a dramatic reenactment of a boy in her class spilling red juice all over the rug. The twins were safely distracted. 

Claire stepped slightly out into the hallway, motioning for Mrs. Bea to follow. Once they were out of earshot, Claire let out a shaky breath. "My FMLA leave expires next week, Bea. I've been out for almost twelve weeks."

Mrs. Bea’s expression sobered immediately. "Oh, Lord. What are you going to do?"

"I don't know," Claire whispered, rubbing her temples. "Drew isn't being discharged until January. I can't leave him here alone all day, but if I don't go back to the office, they'll fire me. We'll lose the insurance. We can barely afford the mortgage on just my husband's salary right now with all this unpaid time off."

"Hey," Mrs. Bea said firmly, reaching out to grip Claire's arms. "You listen to me. We are going to figure this out. If I have to come sit in that room with Drew every day while you work, I'll do it. You are not going to lose that house, and you are not going to lose that insurance."

Claire nodded, tears prickling at the corners of her eyes. She wanted to believe her, she really did. But the medical bills were already piling up on the kitchen counter at home, and the twelve-week clock was ticking down to zero.
