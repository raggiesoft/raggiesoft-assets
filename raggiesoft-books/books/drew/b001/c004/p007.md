Claire sat in the vinyl recliner beside Drew's hospital bed, staring blankly at a yellow legal pad covered in numbers. She was still wearing her uncomfortable heels and her grey office skirt. She had sacrificed half a day of PTO just to be here on the Friday before Christmas, and every hour she wasn't at her desk felt like a financial failure. 

When Drew’s accident had happened back in October, the HR department had offered her twelve weeks of unpaid leave under the newly passed Family and Medical Leave Act. It had sounded like a lifeline, until she actually looked at their bank accounts. Unpaid leave was a luxury they simply couldn't afford. 

So, she had kept working. Every single day, she dropped Maggie off at school, drove to the office, and worked a full eight hours while her paralyzed six-year-old son sat in a hospital bed miles away. The guilt of not being by his side constantly was eating her alive, but the terror of losing her paycheck—and their health insurance—forced her out the door every morning.

And yet, looking at the scribbled math on her legal pad, working wasn't enough. The insurance deductibles, the co-pays, and the out-of-network specialty charges were mounting into the six figures.

"Mom?" Drew’s voice pulled her from the spiral. 

Claire quickly flipped the legal pad over and set her pen down. "Yes, sweetie?"

"Can we put the star on the tree today?" He gestured toward the small, two-foot artificial Christmas tree sitting on his windowsill. It was covered in miniature ornaments and a string of battery-operated lights, but the top branch remained bare. 

"We absolutely can," Claire said, forcing a bright, cheerful smile. "But we have to wait for your sister. You know she'd be heartbroken if we did the star without her."

Right on cue, the heavy door to the room swung open. Maggie burst in like a miniature hurricane, wearing a reindeer antler headband and a winter coat that wasn't fully zipped. She held a construction paper snowflake in one hand and a half-eaten candy cane in the other. 

"I'm on Christmas break!" Maggie announced triumphantly to the room. "No more school until next year!"

Mrs. Bea followed closely behind her, carrying Maggie’s heavy backpack. "Half-day," Bea said with a tired but affectionate smile. "I picked her up right from the carpool line and we came straight here."

"Maggie!" Drew grinned, maneuvering the joystick of his power chair to turn and face her. "Did you have your party?"

"Yeah! We had cupcakes with green frosting, and Mrs. Miller gave us all pencils with snowmen on them," Maggie said, immediately dropping her snowflake on Drew's lap. "I made this for you. We can tape it to the window."

Claire stood up, her heels clicking softly against the linoleum. She kissed the top of Maggie’s head before stepping over to give Mrs. Bea a grateful hug. "Thank you for grabbing her, Bea. I couldn't afford to take the whole day off."

"Of course, honey," Mrs. Bea murmured. She pulled back slightly, her sharp eyes studying the dark circles under Claire's eyes and the tension in her shoulders. "You look like you're carrying the weight of the world. Are you sleeping at all?"

Claire glanced over her shoulder. Maggie was currently regaling Drew with a dramatic reenactment of a boy in her class spilling red juice all over the rug. The twins were safely distracted. 

Claire stepped slightly out into the hallway, motioning for Mrs. Bea to follow. Once they were out of earshot, Claire let out a shaky breath, leaning back against the cool plaster wall. "I'm just so tired, Bea. I'm working forty hours a week. I'm missing his physical therapy sessions. I leave him here with the nurses all day, and it breaks my heart. But even with me working, the medical bills..." Her voice cracked. "We're drowning. I don't know how we're going to pay for all of this."

"Hey," Mrs. Bea said firmly, reaching out to grip Claire's arms. "You listen to me. You are doing what a mother has to do to keep a roof over their heads and insurance in their pockets. There is no shame in working to provide for your boy."

"It feels like I'm failing him," Claire whispered, a single tear escaping down her cheek. 

"You are keeping him alive," Mrs. Bea corrected gently. "Now, wipe your face. We have a star to put on a tree, and two six-year-olds who need a little bit of Christmas magic."

Claire nodded, taking a deep breath and wiping quickly at her eyes. She wanted to believe Bea. She really did. But the stack of bills waiting on her kitchen counter at home told a terrifyingly different story.
