The living room coffee table had been cleared of Claire's endless stacks of medical bills and insurance paperwork, replaced entirely by a lopsided, homemade rectangular cake. Half of it was covered in thick chocolate frosting, and the other half was vanilla. 

Seven brightly colored candles burned in the center, casting a warm, flickering glow across the dimly lit room. 

Drew sat in his heavy black power wheelchair, pulled right up to the edge of the low table. Sitting directly across from him on the thick carpet was Maggie. 

They were fraternal twins. They didn't look like identical copies of each other, but sitting there in the candlelight, the fierce, unspoken bond between them was undeniable. They had survived six years of standard childhood together, and then they had survived the last five months of a waking nightmare. Today, they were finally seven. 

"Okay, you two," Claire said, her voice thick with emotion but her smile wide and bright. She was holding a cheap disposable camera, winding the plastic wheel with her thumb. "Make a wish."

Drew looked across the candles at his sister. Maggie’s eyes were reflecting the tiny flames. She had spent the entire first half of her first-grade year terrified, doing her homework in hospital cafeterias and sleeping in uncomfortable waiting room chairs. This was *her* day just as much as it was his. 

"Ready?" Drew asked, resting his hand on his joystick. 

"Ready," Maggie nodded. 

They both took a deep breath, leaned forward, and blew as hard as they could. The seven tiny flames flickered and died, sending thin trails of gray smoke twisting up toward the ceiling. 

"Happy birthday!" Claire cheered, the flash of the disposable camera blinding them for a split second. 

Maggie scrambled up from the floor and threw her arms around Drew’s neck, carefully avoiding the joystick. "We're seven!" she declared proudly.

"Seven," Drew echoed, hugging her back. 

Money was incredibly tight. Claire had gently warned them earlier in the week that birthdays and Christmases were going to look a little different from now on. But as Claire handed them each a small, brightly wrapped box, Drew didn't care about the size of the present. 

He tore the paper off his box. Inside was a used, slightly scuffed cartridge for the Super Kinoka Entertainment System: *Kinoka Kart*. 

Maggie tore hers open. It was a second controller. 

"I figured since you're both stuck inside for a few more days before school starts, you might want something you can actually play together on the big TV," Claire explained, pulling a knife from the kitchen to cut the cake. "Mrs. Bea found it at a yard sale."

"Thank you, Mommy!" Maggie yelled, holding the plastic controller up like it was a gold trophy. 

Drew looked down at the game cartridge in his lap. It was the perfect gift. For the last five months, he had been trapped in a hospital bed, playing single-player games on his handheld PlayBrick while Maggie watched over his shoulder. But this was something they could do on equal footing. On a television screen, it didn't matter that his legs didn't work. On a screen, he and Maggie could race side-by-side.

"I'm gonna beat you," Drew told his sister, a competitive spark returning to his voice for the first time in months. 

Maggie grabbed her slice of half-chocolate, half-vanilla cake and smirked at him. "In your dreams, wheelchair boy. I get to be the dinosaur."

Drew laughed, a genuine, unrestrained sound that filled the living room. For the first time since August, the crushing weight of the house's narrow hallways and inaccessible bathrooms faded into the background. 

They were seven, they were together, and they were ready for whatever came next.
