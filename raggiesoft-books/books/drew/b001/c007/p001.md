The Wednesday before Thanksgiving brought a strange, hollow quiet to the pediatric rehabilitation wing. 

For the past three days, the doctors had been doing a massive push to discharge any child who was medically stable enough to eat turkey at their own dining room table. The little girl with no hair had gone home on Monday. The boy with the arm cast left yesterday. 

And now, Claire stood near the nurses' station, watching as the teenager with the massive leg cast was wheeled toward the elevator by a transport technician, his parents walking behind him carrying a stack of duffel bags and balloons. 

"Have a great Thanksgiving, Marcus!" Nurse Brenda called out cheerfully from behind the counter, waving a clipboard. "Don't eat too much pie!"

"I won't! Thanks, Brenda!" the teenager called back, a massive, relieved smile on his face as the elevator doors dinged open. 

Claire forced a polite smile, truly happy for the boy and his family. But as the elevator doors slid shut, the silence that rushed back into the hallway felt incredibly heavy. She turned around and looked back into Room 412. 

Drew was sitting in his red wheelchair just inside the doorway. He had a perfect view of the hallway. He had watched every single discharge over the last three days. 

Maggie was sitting cross-legged on the center of the hospital mattress, surrounded by orange, brown, and yellow construction paper. She was meticulously tracing her hand with a brown crayon to make a turkey. 

"Look, Drew," Maggie said brightly, holding up a slightly wobbly cut-out. "I wrote 'I am thankful for Mrs. Bea's sweet potatoes' on the thumb. Do you want me to trace your hand next?"

Drew didn't answer. He just stared out the doorway at the empty stretch of linoleum where the teenager had just disappeared. 

Claire felt a sudden, sharp ache in her chest. She walked into the room and crouched down next to his wheelchair. "Hey, buddy," she said softly. "Are you okay?"

Drew’s lower lip began to tremble. His small hands gripped the padded armrests of his chair, his knuckles turning white. He didn't throw a tantrum. He didn't yell. It was just a slow, crushing realization of his reality. 

Tears welled up behind his glasses and spilled hotly down his cheeks. "I want to go home, Mom," he whispered, his voice cracking with a profound, exhausted sorrow. "Why does everyone else get to go home? I want to go home."

The sound of his quiet weeping shattered Claire's heart into a million pieces. 

She reached out, wrapping her arms carefully around his shoulders, mindful of his healing back. She pressed her cheek against his hair, fighting back her own tears. "Oh, my sweet boy. I know. I know you do."

"It's not fair," Drew sobbed, burying his face into her shoulder. "I don't want to be in the hospital for Thanksgiving. I want to be in our house."

Maggie dropped her crayon. The cheerful distraction she had been trying to maintain instantly evaporated. She scrambled to the edge of the mattress, dropping down to the floor and rushing over to wrap her arms around Drew's waist from the other side, sandwiching him in a fierce, protective hug. 

"I'm sorry, Drew," Maggie sniffled, her own eyes watering as she felt her twin brother's pain. "I'm sorry they get to leave."

"Listen to me, Drew," Claire murmured, pulling back just enough to look him in the eyes. She wiped the tears off his cheeks with her thumbs. "The doctors are so proud of how hard you are working in physical therapy. But your tummy had a very big surgery. We have to make absolutely sure that your body is completely, one-hundred percent strong before we leave. Because when we finally go home, I want to make sure we never, ever have to come back to this hospital."

Drew sniffled, wiping his nose with the back of his hand. "But what about Thanksgiving?"

"We are going to have Thanksgiving right here," Claire promised gently. "Mrs. Bea was on the phone with me this morning. She wanted to cook a massive turkey and bring the entire kitchen here."

"Can she?" Drew asked, a tiny spark of hope breaking through the sadness. 

"Well, the hospital has very strict rules about outside food because they have to make sure no one gets sick," Claire explained. "But Nurse Brenda is a very good friend of ours. She talked to the doctors, and they gave us special permission. Mrs. Bea is going to cook her famous sweet potato casserole, and she’s putting it in a super-sealed container just for us. She's bringing it tomorrow, and we are going to reserve the big table in the family dayroom."

"Just us?" Drew asked softly. 

"Just us," Maggie said fiercely, gripping his hand tightly. "I told Mrs. Bea I wasn't eating turkey at home without you. We're going to have a hospital Thanksgiving, and it's going to be the best one ever."

Drew looked at his twin sister, feeling the absolute certainty in her grip. The heavy, suffocating sadness of the empty hospital ward didn't completely disappear—he still desperately missed the familiar smell of his own bedroom and the safety of their house. 

But as Claire kissed his forehead and Maggie handed him a brown crayon to trace his hand, the harsh reality of the hospital felt just a little bit warmer. He wasn't going home for Thanksgiving, but his home was coming to him.
