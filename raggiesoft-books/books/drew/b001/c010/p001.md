Christmas morning in room 412 smelled like industrial antiseptic and latex, not pine needles and cinnamon. 

Drew woke up to the soft, rhythmic hum of the oxygen concentrator down the hall. For a brief, hazy second, he forgot where he was. He thought he was in his own bed at home, and that if he just threw off his covers, he and Maggie could sprint down the hallway together to see what Santa had left under their big living room tree.

Then he felt the rigid metal bars of the hospital bed rails pressing against his arm, and the illusion shattered. 

He turned his head. Maggie was asleep on the pull-out vinyl chair next to his bed, tangled in a scratchy hospital blanket. Claire was already awake, sitting at the edge of the mattress, gently stroking his hair. She looked utterly exhausted, but she was smiling. 

"Merry Christmas, buddy," Claire whispered. 

Drew didn't say it back. He looked past her, toward the open doorway. The hallway was painfully quiet. Yesterday afternoon, on Christmas Eve, he had watched from his wheelchair as the nurses cheered for a boy in the next room who was finally leaving with his parents. "A Christmas miracle," Nurse Brenda had called it, wiping away happy tears as the elevator doors closed.

Drew didn't feel like a miracle. He felt left behind. 

"I want to go home," Drew whispered, his voice thick with sleep and a heavy, suffocating sadness. "Why did that other boy get to go home for Christmas, but I have to stay here?"

Claire’s smile faltered, but she didn't let it fall. She leaned down, pressing a soft kiss to his forehead. "His bones healed fast enough, sweetie. Your spine and your tummy had much bigger injuries. The doctors just have to make sure you are absolutely, perfectly safe before we leave. But you are so close. January is right around the corner."

"But Santa..." Drew sniffled, tears pooling behind his glasses. "Did he know I wasn't at the house?"

"Of course he knew!" Maggie’s voice piped up. She sat up on the vinyl chair, her hair a messy bird's nest, rubbing her eyes. She pointed excitedly toward the small windowsill. "Look, Drew!"

Drew turned his head. Beneath the tiny, two-foot artificial tree they had decorated with the star, there was a small pile of brightly wrapped presents. A red felt stocking was carefully taped to the metal rail of his hospital bed, bulging with candy and small toys. 

Nurse Brenda popped her head into the room. She was wearing a red Santa hat with a white pom-pom that jingled when she walked. "Ho, ho, ho! Merry Christmas, Ragsdale family!" she whispered loudly, carrying a tray with small cups of apple juice and a special plate of holiday-shaped graham crackers. "Santa dropped off some extra goodies at the nurses' station for my favorite patients."

Claire helped Drew sit up, adjusting the pillows behind his back. Maggie scrambled up onto the hospital bed, carefully avoiding his IV lines, and handed him the first wrapped box. 

Drew tore the paper. Inside was a brand new PlayBrick cartridge—*Gorilla King Land*. Under normal circumstances, he would have been bouncing off the walls with excitement. 

He held the small plastic box in his hands. He looked at Maggie, who was beaming with pure Christmas joy, and at his mother, who was watching him with desperate hope in her eyes. He knew they were trying to make it a good day. He knew they loved him. 

"Thank you," Drew said softly, managing a small, fragile smile. 

He let Maggie help him open the rest of the gifts, and he ate his snowman-shaped graham crackers. It was a nice morning, surrounded by his favorite people. But as he looked down at his motionless legs hidden beneath the thin hospital sheets, the dull, heavy ache in his chest refused to leave. The presents were fun, but they couldn't fix his spine. 

He just wanted his own room. He just wanted to go home.
