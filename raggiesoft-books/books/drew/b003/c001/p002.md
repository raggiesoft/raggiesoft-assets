The digital alarm clock on the nightstand clicked to 11:14 PM. 

Maggie was fast asleep, the oppressive heat of the apartment finally giving way to the exhaustion of the day. Her arm was draped loosely over her twin brother's waist, anchoring her to him in the dark. 

Suddenly, Drew jerked. 

Maggie groaned softly in her sleep, shifting slightly, but she didn't wake. Beside her, Drew's breathing hitched, turning rapid and shallow. A sudden, sharp cramp seized his lower abdomen, sending a jolt of pure panic through his chest. He felt the heavy, undeniable pressure building in his bowels. He needed to use the toilet. *Now.*

His hands began to shake as he carefully gripped Maggie's arm, trying to gently lift it off his waist without waking her. He gritted his teeth, silently pleading with his own paralyzed body to just hold on. If he could just drag his legs to the edge of the mattress, if he could just slide over the gap and into the seat of his power wheelchair parked beside the bed...

He pushed himself up onto his elbows, dragging his dead weight forward. 

But it was too late. 

The muscles in his lower half, completely disconnected from his brain's frantic commands, simply let go. Drew gasped, his entire body going rigid as he soiled his pajamas. The warm, sickeningly heavy mess spread instantly, soaking into the fabric of his sleepwear and the thin cotton sheets beneath him. 

A choked, desperate sob tore from his throat. He buried his face in his hands, his shoulders violently shaking as he began to cry. 

The sound woke Maggie instantly. She bolted upright, the sudden movement causing the mattress to bounce. "Drew?" she whispered groggily, her hand instinctively reaching out for him in the dark. 

Her fingers brushed against his shaking back. At the same moment, the unmistakable smell hit her. 

Maggie froze. Her mind raced, the last vestiges of sleep instantly evaporating. In the dim amber light filtering through the blinds, she could see Drew curled into himself, quietly hyperventilating through his tears. 

"I'm sorry," Drew choked out, his voice thick with shame and devastation. "Maggie, I'm so sorry. I tried to get up."

"Hey. Hey, it's okay," Maggie said instantly. Her heart hammered in her chest, but she forced her voice to remain steady and calm. "It's okay, Drew. Don't cry. I've got you."

She scrambled out of bed, her bare feet hitting the warm linoleum. She knew she wasn't supposed to be doing this. This was Mom's job. This was the home health nurse's job. As his teenage sister, she wasn't supposed to be involved in his toileting, and she definitely wasn't supposed to see him like this. 

But Mom was exhausted, fast asleep in the living room, and the nurse wouldn't be back until tomorrow afternoon. Drew was crying, humiliated, and sitting in his own mess. It was an emergency. 

Maggie grabbed his power wheelchair, pulling it flush against the side of the bed and locking the heavy brakes. 

"Okay, come here," Maggie said gently. She leaned over the bed, wrapping her arms securely under his armpits. Drew clung to her neck, burying his tear-streaked face in her shoulder as she hoisted his dead weight off the mattress and carefully lowered him into the seat of his chair. 

She quickly wheeled him out of the bedroom and down the short hallway into the cramped apartment bathroom. The layout of their new apartment was less than ideal—really meant for a single parent and one child, or two roommates, rather than a disabled teenager and his family. The bathroom itself was a complete hack job. The landlord had hastily retrofitted it to make it barely wheelchair accessible, awkwardly bolting heavy metal grab bars onto the walls around the standard porcelain bathtub. She flipped on the fluorescent vanity light, the harsh bulb humming to life. 

"Let's get these off," Maggie said softly, kneeling in front of the chair. She worked quickly and methodically, unbuttoning his soiled pajama pants and carefully pulling them down over his paralyzed legs, trying her hardest not to make a big deal out of the mess. 

She helped him pivot over the armrest and onto the toilet, letting him clear out whatever remained of his bladder and bowels. While he finished, she bundled the ruined pajamas and the contaminated bedsheet into her arms, sprinting out of the bathroom to the small, stacked washer and dryer unit in the hallway. She jammed the clothes inside, dumped a heavy cup of cheap detergent over them, and slammed the lid down, starting the emergency load on the heaviest cycle. 

She ran back into the bathroom. "Okay, let's get you in the tub," she said. 

She helped him transfer over the lip of the porcelain tub, getting him seated safely on his waterproof shower bench. She grabbed the detachable showerhead, turning the knob until the water ran comfortably warm. 

Maggie averted her eyes as much as possible, focusing on the tiled wall behind him as she squeezed body wash onto a rough loofah. She handed it to him so he could scrub his chest and arms, while she took another soapy washcloth and carefully cleaned his legs and the lower half of his body. The silence in the small bathroom was heavy, broken only by the sound of running water and Drew's occasional, leftover sniffles. 

"I'm sorry you have to do this," Drew whispered, looking down at his hands. 

"Stop apologizing," Maggie replied fiercely, rinsing the soap away. "You didn't do it on purpose. It's just a mess, Drew. We clean it up, and it's over."

Once she was absolutely certain he was clean, she shut off the water. She grabbed two large, fluffy towels from the rack, draping them heavily over his lap and wrapping one tightly around his waist to maintain whatever shred of dignity and modesty he had left. She didn't want to see anything she wasn't supposed to. 

She helped him transfer back into his wheelchair, his wet hair sticking up in odd directions. Maggie quickly dried his legs and feet, grabbing a fresh pair of clean cotton pajamas from his dresser and helping him dress. 

They made their way back to the bedroom. Maggie stripped the rest of the soiled bedding off the mattress, tossing it into a pile in the corner, and pulled a fresh fitted sheet over the mattress. 

She helped Drew back into bed, pulling the thin summer quilt up to his chest. He looked exhausted, the adrenaline of the panic wearing off and leaving him pale and drained. 

"Go to sleep," Maggie said softly. 

"What about you?" Drew asked, his voice barely above a whisper. 

"I have to stay up and wait for the washer to finish so I can transfer the clothes to the dryer," Maggie said, settling onto the edge of the mattress. 

She looked at the empty expanse of her side of the bed, then at the makeshift curtain hanging uselessly from the ceiling. Without a second thought, she climbed under the covers on Drew's side. She pressed her back against his, wrapping her arm securely over his waist just like she had before the accident. 

Drew let out a long, shaky breath, the tension finally leaving his small frame. He closed his eyes, and within minutes, the steady rhythm of his breathing told her he was asleep. 

Maggie lay awake in the sweltering dark, listening to the heavy, rhythmic thumping of the washing machine in the hallway. She squeezed her brother's hand, fiercely promising the dark room that she would always be there to clean up the mess.
