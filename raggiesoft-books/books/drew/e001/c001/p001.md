## 5:00 PM — The Core Check

The linoleum floor of the Dojang was freezing against the back of Drew’s neck. 

He was only six years old, and his brand-new white uniform was stiff, smelling faintly of the plastic bag it had come in. It was a little too big, the sleeves rolled up twice at the wrists. He felt impossibly small in the massive, echoing room. He kept glancing toward the front lobby, his heart aching. Maggie was supposed to be right here next to him. They had practiced their stances in the living room for a month. But Maggie was home in bed with a fever, and she had made him promise to go without her. *Be brave,* she had said.

So, Drew tried to be brave.

A towering man named Master Curtis stood at the front of the room. When he barked the command to drop to the floor for "leg lifts," Drew scrambled to copy the older kids. He placed his small hands flat under his bottom and lifted his feet into the air. 

"Six inches!" the giant man's voice boomed. "Hold them. One!"

Drew’s little legs began to shake almost immediately. He bit his lip, trying so hard not to let them drop. He wanted to do a good job. He wanted to tell Maggie he had been strong.

"Two!"

Drew’s stomach muscles burned. Then, out of the corner of his eye, he saw something confusing. Master Curtis was walking down the line, but he wasn’t walking on the floor. He was stepping *on* the students. 

Drew blinked, his six-year-old brain struggling to understand the rules of this place. Was this a game? A test? But none of the grown-ups were laughing. The teenagers looked scared. Drew watched as the heavy man stepped onto an older boy, then a girl, moving closer and closer.

Drew was at the absolute end of the line because he was the youngest and the newest. There was no one after him. 

The heavy, rhythmic slapping of Master Curtis’s bare feet on the tile grew louder. Then, the fluorescent lights above Drew were suddenly eclipsed. A massive shadow fell over him.

Drew looked up. Master Curtis was terrifyingly huge from this angle. 

Before Drew could even brace himself, a large, calloused foot settled onto his small stomach. 

There was no time to breathe. Master Curtis shifted his weight. 

For a six-year-old, the pressure was completely incomprehensible. It didn't just feel heavy; it felt as though a building had been dropped directly onto him. The sheer, crushing weight forced every single ounce of air out of Drew's lungs in a silent, violent rush. 

Deep inside his abdomen, something gave way under the immense pressure. A sharp, terrifying heat flared near his ribs, radiating outward with a deep, sickening intensity. 

His brain panicked. His legs hit the freezing linoleum with a dull thud. Drew curled onto his side, his hands instinctively clutching his stomach. He opened his mouth to scream, to cry, to ask for his mother, but he couldn't find any air. He was completely paralyzed, trapped in a silent, gasping void.

"Lock your core, Drew!" Master Curtis’s voice barked coldly from somewhere far above, echoing off the cinderblock walls. "Get your legs back up! Crying is weak!"

The giant shadow moved away, but the crushing pain didn't. 

Drew lay curled in a tight ball on the cold floor, tears spilling hot down his cheeks. Through his blurred vision, he watched the giant man walk back up the line of students. The man didn't step on everyone this time. He stopped directly in front of the older boy and the young girl from earlier.

Drew whimpered as another wave of heat spiked near his ribs, clutching his stiff uniform jacket. He watched in terrified confusion as Master Curtis placed his foot on the girl again, leaning all his weight into her. The girl’s face turned a terrifying, dark red. The man leaned down, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper that Drew couldn't hear over the pounding in his own ears, and then did the exact same thing to the older boy. 

Drew didn't understand why this was happening. He didn't understand what the older kids had done wrong, or why the giant man was so mean. The sounds of the Dojang—the counting, the muffled whimpers, the slap of bare feet—began to sound distant and hollow. The sharp heat inside his tummy was bleeding into a deep, alarming ache that made the room spin. 

He didn't care about being a ninja anymore. He didn't care about being brave. As he squeezed his eyes shut against the blinding white lights of the ceiling, alone and hurting, he just wanted his mommy. He just wanted his sissy.

## 5:30 PM — The Rest of the Hour

"Stand up! Fall in!"

The sudden, sharp crack of Master Curtis clapping his hands together echoed off the cinderblock walls. Drew flinched. All around him, the grown-ups and older kids were pushing themselves up off the cold linoleum floor, breathing hard. 

Drew tried to stand up fast so he wouldn't get yelled at again. But the second he rolled onto his knees, a blinding flash of heat shot through his side. He gasped, wrapping his little arms around his middle. It didn't feel like a normal tummy ache. It felt like something sharp and hot was trapped deep inside him, pushing outward with every breath he took. 

He managed to get his bare feet under him and wobble into a standing position, squeezing his eyes shut until the spinning stopped. 

Down the line, the older boy had stepped out of his spot to help the little girl stand up. Drew watched, hoping the giant man wouldn't be mad. But Master Curtis sneered at them. His booming voice echoed across the room, calling them weak and telling them they didn't belong.

Drew froze, his heart hammering against his ribs. If the monster got that angry just because someone helped their friend stand up, what would he do to a six-year-old who couldn't stop crying? Drew quickly wiped the tears off his face with the stiff, scratchy sleeve of his uniform. He couldn't let the monster see him crying.

"Basic punching drills! *Juchum Sogi*! Horse stance!" Master Curtis yelled.

Everyone dropped into a wide stance, bending their knees. Drew tried to copy them. He spread his feet apart and bent his legs, but the movement pulled at his side. He let out a tiny whimper that got swallowed by the loud *snap* of fifty uniforms moving at once.

He tried to throw a punch, pushing his little fist forward just like he and Maggie had practiced in the living room. But the twist of his body made the hot pain inside him flare so violently that his vision went fuzzy at the edges. 

This wasn't what karate was supposed to be. When he begged his mom to sign them up, he thought it would be like the *Samurai Geckos*. He thought he would learn how to do cool kicks and break boards and be a hero. He thought it would be fun. 

Instead, it was just a freezing cold room with a terrifying man who hurt people for no reason. 

Drew kept his head down, staring at the shiny linoleum floor. He barely moved his arms, throwing tiny, weak punches so he wouldn't have to twist his aching tummy. He was so tired. The heavy, sick feeling inside him was making him dizzy, and he felt cold and sweaty at the same time. 

He never wanted to do karate again. He never wanted to put this white uniform back on. He just wanted to go home. He wanted to crawl into his bed, pull the blankets all the way up over his head, and have his mommy and Maggie tell him that the monster couldn't get him anymore. 

For the rest of the long, terrible hour, Drew just tried to stay completely invisible, silently praying that he wouldn't fall over before his mom came to pick him up.

## 6:30 PM — The Front Office

The bathroom tiles were cold beneath Drew's bare feet, a sharp contrast to the terrifying, hot balloon feeling expanding inside his tummy.

His mommy, Claire, knelt on the floor in front of him. Her hands were soft and gentle as she helped him out of his sweaty, heavy uniform top. But the moment the fabric cleared his stomach, she completely froze. 

Drew looked down. Right across his belly, pressed deep into his skin, was the dark, angry shape of a grown-up’s foot. It hurt just to look at it.

"Drew," his mommy whispered. Her voice sounded strange, shaky and hollow, and her hands were trembling as she held them in the air, too afraid to touch the dark mark. "Who did this to you?"

"Master Curtis," Drew whimpered, wrapping his little arms around himself to protect his tummy. "He walked on us."

Something shifted in his mommy’s eyes. The soft, gentle mommy who always read him bedtime stories disappeared. Suddenly, she looked terrifying—but not scary to him. Scary like a superhero who was about to fight a bad guy. 

She didn't ask any more questions. She shoved his t-shirt and sweatpants onto him so fast it made him dizzy, grabbed his hand, and threw the bathroom door open. 

The lobby was a blur of noise and bright lights. Drew’s feet stumbled to keep up as his mommy marched right past the other moms and dads, pulling him straight into the small office behind the front counter. 

A girl with a ponytail—Megan, the girl who had smiled and given him a sticker when he signed up—was sitting at the desk. She jumped when his mommy marched in.

"Where is Ray?" his mommy demanded. Her voice wasn't loud, but it sounded like sharp ice. 

"M-Master Ray?" Megan stammered, looking really confused. "He takes Thursdays off. He isn't in the building. Can I help you with—"

"Call him," his mommy ordered. She pulled Drew forward and lifted the bottom of his t-shirt. "Call him right now, or I am calling 9-1-1 from this desk."

Megan leaned over her desk to look. 

All the color vanished from the girl's face. She slapped her hands over her mouth, making a sick, choking sound in the back of her throat, and pushed her rolling chair away like she had just seen a monster. Her eyes were huge and terrified as she stared at Drew’s stomach. 

"Oh my god," Megan breathed, looking at his mommy. "Oh my god, what happened?"

"I want Ray on the phone. Now."

Megan’s hands were shaking so badly she almost dropped the phone. She dialed a number and slammed a button that made the phone ring out loud in the room. 

A man answered. "Ray speaking." He sounded annoyed, like they had interrupted his nap. 

"Ray, it's Megan," the girl said, her voice squeaking. "I have a mother in the office. Drew’s mother. He’s in the white belt class."

"What’s the problem?" the man sighed. 

Drew’s mommy leaned right over the desk, talking straight into the little box. 

"The problem, Ray, is that I just took my six-year-old into the bathroom to change, and he has the footprint of a grown man bruised into his internal organs," she hissed. "Your instructor stepped on my child."

Drew squeezed his eyes shut. His tummy was burning, and he felt dizzy. He just wanted to go home and climb into bed with Maggie.

The man on the phone didn't gasp like Megan did. He just let out a heavy breath. "Ma'am, please calm down. Master Curtis is old-school. He runs a highly disciplined floor. It’s tough love. The conditioning drills are designed to build character and teach them how to brace their cores."

"Tough love?!" his mommy shrieked. It was the loudest Drew had ever heard her yell. "He is in kindergarten! You don't build character by crushing a baby!"

"It’s a martial arts facility, ma'am," the man on the phone argued back. "If your son is going to earn his belts, he has to learn how to take the pressure. It’s part of the curriculum."

Drew didn't know what a 'curriculum' was, but it sounded like a bad word. 

"It's not just my son!" his mommy yelled back, her grip on Drew's hand tightening protectively. "When I was walking to the bathroom, I watched an older boy practically carrying a little girl off the mats! She was holding her ribs and crying! Are you telling me that a grown man breaking little girls in half is part of your curriculum?!"

Drew remembered them. The boy and the girl who Master Curtis had stepped on so many times. The ones who had held hands on the mat. He felt a wave of sadness through his dizziness. Master Curtis was a monster. He hurt everyone.

"Curtis knows what he's doing," Ray said, sounding mad now. "The children are fine. They just aren't used to being pushed—"

"Ray, stop!"

Drew jumped. It wasn't his mommy who yelled. It was Megan. 

The girl behind the desk leaned over, her face pale and her eyes full of angry tears. "Ray, I am looking at the boy right now!" she screamed at the little box. "This isn't a tough drill! This is a massive, barefoot bruise right across his stomach! He is a little boy! This is sick!"

"Megan, you are out of line," the man snapped.

"No, *you* are out of line," his mommy interrupted. Her voice was cold and terrifying again. She reached down and squeezed Drew’s hand. "You listen to me very carefully, Ray. This conversation is over. You will be hearing from my lawyer, and you will be hearing from the Chesapeake Police Department. And if I were you, I would find out exactly what your 'old-school' instructor is doing behind those closed blinds before they put you in handcuffs right next to him."

His mommy slammed her finger onto the phone, making the man's voice disappear. 

She looked down at Megan. The girl was crying, staring at Drew with a look of helpless sadness. 

"You need to quit," his mommy told her. "You don't want to be in this building when the police get here."

Then, his mommy turned to him. She didn't grab his hand this time. She reached down and scooped him up into her arms. 

Drew whimpered. Being picked up made the hot balloon in his tummy feel like it was going to pop, but he buried his face in his mommy's shoulder and held on tight. She marched straight out of the office, carried him through the big glass doors, and practically threw him into the back seat of their car. 

"Hold on, sweetie," she promised, her voice thick with tears as she slammed the car into drive. "Mommy's taking you to the children's hospital. You're going to be okay." 

Drew laid his head against the car window as the building disappeared behind them. He closed his eyes, the dizziness finally taking over, and just wished Maggie was there to hold his hand.

## 6:50 PM — Unannounced Arrival

The world outside the car window was a blurry, dizzying streak of streetlights and passing buildings. The engine of the family’s four-door sedan roared every time his mommy forcefully slammed the stick shift into a new gear, the car jerking forward with a frantic, desperate speed. 

Drew lay slumped across the back seat. He couldn't sit up anymore. The hot balloon in his tummy had grown so heavy and tight that it felt like it was crushing his lungs. Every time his mommy downshifted and the car lurched, a sickening wave of pain washed over him, making black spots dance in his vision.

"Stay with me, Drew. We're almost there," his mommy called out from the front seat. Her voice cracked, tight with panic. She laid on the car horn, a blaring, angry sound that didn't stop as she swerved around a slow-moving van. "Just keep your eyes open, sweetie. Please."

Drew tried to answer her, but he didn't have enough breath left to make a sound. He felt incredibly cold, even though it was the middle of August. His fingers and toes were numb, and a strange, heavy sleepiness was trying to pull him under. 

The car suddenly swerved hard to the right, throwing Drew against the seatbelt. The tires screeched against the pavement as his mommy tore into the brightly lit circular driveway of the children's hospital. She didn't bother finding a parking spot. She slammed the brakes, throwing the car into park right in the middle of the emergency room drop-off lane, leaving the engine running and the driver's side door wide open. 

Because they hadn't come in an ambulance, no one was waiting for them. The sliding glass doors of the ER were quiet. 

His mommy ripped the back door open. The bright, sterile lights of the hospital overhang stung Drew's eyes. She didn't hesitate; she reached in, unbuckled him, and scooped him up into her arms, sprinting toward the entrance. 

"Help!" his mommy screamed as the sliding doors parted. The sound of her voice echoed off the high ceilings of the waiting room, shattering the quiet hum of the room. "I need help! My son is hurt! Please, somebody help him!"

The sudden noise made Drew flinch, but he couldn't lift his head from her shoulder. Through his half-closed eyes, he saw people in the waiting room turning to stare, their faces pale and shocked. 

Behind the glass window of the reception desk, a triage nurse in blue scrubs immediately shot to her feet. She took one look at Claire carrying a limp, pale six-year-old and slammed her hand down on a panic button on her desk. 

"We need a gurney, now!" the nurse yelled down the hallway behind her, her voice cutting through the air with practiced urgency. She rushed out from behind the glass, bypassing the waiting room chairs entirely. "Ma'am, what happened? Was he in a car accident?"

"No!" his mommy cried out, her chest heaving as she held Drew tighter. "He was at a martial arts class. The instructor stepped on his stomach. He’s turning blue, and he’s freezing cold."

The nurse reached out, pressing two fingers against Drew's neck. Her expression instantly hardened. 

"Trauma team to the ER, stat! Pediatric abdominal trauma, hypovolemic shock!" the nurse shouted over her shoulder. 

Suddenly, the hallway erupted into motion. A pair of double doors slammed open, and a team of doctors and nurses came sprinting out, pushing a rolling bed toward them. The squeaking of the wheels on the shiny floor sounded incredibly loud. 

"Put him here, Mom, lay him flat!" a doctor ordered, grabbing the rails of the bed. 

His mommy gently lowered him onto the firm mattress of the gurney. The moment she let go, a terrifying flurry of hands descended upon him. Someone was ripping his t-shirt open, exposing his stomach and the dark, angry footprint branded across it. Someone else was shining a painfully bright light into his eyes. 

"Blood pressure is bottoming out!" a nurse yelled. 

"Get a line in him, we need fluids wide open, now!" the doctor commanded. "Let's move him to Trauma One!"

Drew felt the gurney jolt forward as the team began to sprint down the hallway, the ceiling lights flashing past him like a strobe light. 

"Mommy!" Drew tried to cry out, but it came out as nothing more than a breathless whisper. 

"I'm right here, Drew! I'm right here!" his mommy yelled, running alongside the bed. She grabbed his tiny, freezing hand and squeezed it. Her face was soaked with tears, but her grip was fiercely strong. "I love you! Mommy's right here!"

The gurney crashed through another set of double doors, entering a massive, chaotic room filled with bright lights and beeping machines. 

"Ma'am, you have to stay back!" a nurse said, stepping in front of his mommy as they wheeled Drew into the center of the room. 

"No, I'm not leaving him!" she screamed, fighting against the nurse's hold. 

Drew watched his mommy's terrified face slip further and further away as the doctors surrounded his bed. The hot balloon in his stomach finally felt like it was bursting, pulling the rest of the world away into a cold, quiet darkness. He closed his eyes, and the shouting voices faded into silence.
