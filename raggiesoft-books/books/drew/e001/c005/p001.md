The pediatric rehabilitation wing didn't look like a hospital anymore. It looked like a very long, very clean haunted house. 

Black and orange crepe paper was twisted across the ceiling, and friendly paper bats were taped to the walls next to the brightly painted jungle animals. Someone had even hung fake, stretchy cobwebs over the corners of the nurses' station, though they were careful to keep them high enough so they wouldn't tangle with any IV poles. 

Maggie stood excitedly at the end of the hallway holding an orange plastic pumpkin bucket. She was wearing a sparkling blue princess dress, a silver tiara, and her comfortable school sneakers. 

Next to her was Drew. He was sitting securely in his bright red wheelchair, but tonight, it wasn't just a wheelchair. Mom had found two checkered flags and taped them to the back handles. She had also bought Drew a red-and-white jacket with racing patches on it. He looked exactly like a real racecar driver. 

"Attention, all ghouls, ghosts, and racecar drivers!" Sarah, Drew's physical therapist, called out from the center of the hallway. She was wearing a fuzzy brown bear costume that made her look big and cuddly. "Trick-or-treating is officially open! Remember, no running, watch out for equipment, and have fun!"

A collective, excited cheer echoed down the corridor. 

It was a strange, bittersweet Halloween. There were no dark neighborhood streets, no crunchy leaves on the sidewalk, and no ringing strangers' doorbells. Instead, there was a procession of children moving down the linoleum floor, each of them fighting their own heavy, invisible battles. 

Maggie watched as a teenager with a heavy cast on his leg crutched his way over to a table set up outside Room 410. Behind him, a little girl with no hair, wearing a beautiful fairy costume, was being pulled in a red plastic wagon by her dad, a towering IV pole rolling smoothly alongside them. Other kids were sitting up in their rolling hospital beds, being pushed by their parents so they could join the line. 

No matter how broken or sick they were, for tonight, they were just kids getting candy. 

"Ready, Drew?" Maggie asked, gripping the push-handles of his wheelchair. 

"Ready," Drew grinned, holding his own plastic pumpkin bucket in his lap. "Let's go, Mags."

Maggie carefully pushed the wheelchair forward, merging into the slow-moving traffic of costumes and medical equipment. Their first stop was the main nurses' station. 

Nurse Brenda was wearing a tall, pointy witch hat and a black cape over her scrubs. As Maggie pushed the wheelchair up to the tall counter, Brenda leaned over with a giant, plastic cauldron full of candy. 

"Trick or treat!" Maggie and Drew chorused together. 

"Well, if it isn't a beautiful princess and the fastest driver on the ward," Brenda beamed, dropping a handful of chocolate bars into each of their buckets. "Happy Halloween, you two."

"Thank you!" Maggie said brightly. 

They moved down the hall, stopping at various doors where off-duty staff members and volunteers had set up small tables with bowls of candy, stickers, and little plastic toys. 

"You're steering really good, Mags," Drew said over his shoulder as they carefully navigated around a boy dressed as a pirate who had stopped to adjust his eye patch. 

"I told you I was going to be a good driver," Maggie reminded him proudly. "I practice every day."

It was true. Ever since Drew got his chair in September, Maggie had completely taken over the job of pushing him whenever she was allowed. She knew exactly how to take the corners wide so the wheels wouldn't clip the doorframes, and she knew how to tilt the chair back just slightly if they had to go over the raised metal threshold of the elevator. 

Mom was walking a few steps behind them, holding her camera. She raised it to her eye, and the flash blinked brightly, capturing the two of them—the princess and the paralyzed racecar driver—smiling in the middle of the decorated hospital hallway. 

"Over there!" Drew pointed toward the physical therapy gym at the end of the hall. The double doors were propped wide open, and spooky music was playing quietly from a boombox inside. 

"Hold on tight," Maggie instructed. She sped up just a little bit, pushing the chair into a smooth glide. 

They rolled into the gym. It was usually a place of hard work, where Drew cried from frustration trying to learn how to transfer his weight, but tonight, the blue exercise mats were covered in carved pumpkins, and the therapists were handing out full-sized candy bars. 

Maggie helped Drew reach into the bowls, making sure his pumpkin bucket was getting just as full as hers. 

For the first time since August, the heavy, suffocating weight of the tragedy felt like it was finally lifting. The hospital wasn't just a place of fear and pain anymore. It was the place where they were surviving together. And as Maggie pushed her brother back out into the hallway, matching the slow, careful pace of the other kids on the ward, she knew she wouldn't trade her Halloween with him for anything in the world.
