The dual-motor drive base of John's power wheelchair hummed in a steady, low-frequency harmony with Erin's chair beside him as they crested the final paved incline leading up from the Commonwealth Green to the Ironstone archway of Kincaid Hall.

Anyone who tried to navigate Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute in a manual chair learned the hard way within forty-eight hours of freshman orientation that Blacksburg was carved into the side of the Blue Ridge Mountains. Between the sweeping oval bowl of the Commonwealth Green, the steep Ironstone steps climbing toward Faraday Hall on the Engineering Quad, and the grades leading off campus toward Progress Street NE, torque and battery reserves were non-negotiable.

John eased his thumb off the rubberized joystick mounted on his right armrest, bringing his chair to a smooth, zero-radius halt just inside the warm atrium of Kincaid Student Center. Beside him, his fraternal twin sister Erin tapped her own joystick into neutral and reached up to adjust her round tortoiseshell glasses, her long wavy brown hair catching the warm amber glow of the hall sconces.

"Check your battery gauge," Erin said, glancing over at his armrest console. "That climb up from Faraday Hall always eats an extra bar."

"Four green LEDs out of five," John replied, adjusting his own matching tortoiseshell frames and smoothing the hem of his maroon sweater. He looked down the second-floor corridor toward the open double doors of the lounge, where a hand-lettered poster taped to the Ironstone pillar read *FRIDAY NIGHT CAMPUS SPEED-DATING MIXER — 7:00 PM*. "Which means I physically have more than enough range to turn around, take the elevator back down, and roll the two blocks home to Progress Street."

"Not a chance," Erin grinned, nudging the rubber bumper of her footplate lightly against the side of his caster wheel. "You promised me one hour out of the lab. Twenty tables, three minutes per round. If it's a disaster, we hit The Foundry for late-night waffle fries and call it field research."

They rolled side by side into the Second-Floor Lounge. The student activities volunteers had arranged twenty small circular tables in a wide ring around the room, leaving generous four-foot lanes between them so both John and Erin could maneuver their power chairs into the odd-numbered anchor spots without scraping a single chair leg. Erin claimed Table 1 near the windows overlooking Commonwealth Mall, while John parked at Table 4, flipping the standard wooden chair aside and locking his drive wheels into position flush against the tabletop.

At the front of the room, a sophomore wearing a CPI Student Activities polo stood behind a rolling media cart equipped with a dual-deck AudioStar CD system and two amplified PA speakers.

"Alright, everyone, welcome!" the organizer called out into a handheld microphone, tapping the grille twice. "Instead of using an obnoxious kitchen timer or a whistle to signal the end of each rotation, we're keeping things civilized tonight. Every round is timed to Track 3 here—*Seconds Into Forever*. It's an eighty-five-BPM power ballad, and it runs exactly three minutes and zero seconds. When the synth pad fades out at the end of the song, you thank your partner and rotate one table to the left!"

He pressed play on the first round. Warm, sparkling synthesizer pads and clean electric guitar arpeggios floated out of the speakers:

*The clock is ticking on the classroom wall*
*But time stands still here in the hall*
*I saw your face and the world went blurred*
*The sweetest song that I ever heard*
*No need to run and no need to hide*
*I'm pulling up a chair right by your side*

John let his shoulders drop an inch. The first three rotations were pleasant, polite, and entirely predictable—three minutes with a civil engineering major from Joule Hall debating bridge trusses, three minutes with an architecture student sketching on her napkin, and three minutes with a quiet girl from Roanoke who spent two of the minutes asking about the top speed of his power chair on the Commonwealth Green paths.

Across the room at Table 1, Erin caught his eye as the third round's sustained synth chord faded out. She flashed him a quick thumbs-up from her armrest.

*See?* her expression said. *Harmless.*

"Alright, folks, rotate to Round Four!" the organizer announced over the PA.
