A woman in a fitted black leather jacket stepped into the open space opposite Table 4 and pulled out the wooden chair across from John's power wheelchair.

Unlike the previous three students, who had glanced nervously at their name tags or at the empty floor space where his chair was parked, she didn't look down at his drive wheels or his joystick controller. She rested her elbows on the small round table, leaned forward into his immediate field of view, and locked her dark eyes directly onto his behind his tortoiseshell glasses.

"I've been waiting three tables to get to this side of the room," she said, her voice low, smooth, and unhurried. "I'm Valerie."

"John," he said, offering a polite smile. "Wait—weren't you over by Table 9 a minute ago? That's five tables back."

"I traded numbers with two girls in line so I wouldn't have to wait," Valerie replied without missing a beat, flashing a sharp, magnetic smile that made John's pulse jump. "Everyone else in this room is making small talk about syllabus week. You've been actually listening to every person who sat down in front of you. You're rare, John."

Up at the front media cart, the student organizer was juggling a stack of sign-in clipboards with one hand while reaching blindly for the "|<<" restart button on the AudioStar CD player with the other. In his rush, his thumb missed the back button by half an inch and jammed down hard on ">>|" instead—skipping right past Track 3 and landing on Track 4.

For ten seconds, a lush, cinematic Vangelis-style analog synthesizer swell filled the lounge. Half the room assumed it was an extended intro to the ballad.

Then—*CRASH.*

An explosive acoustic drum fill and a blistering C-minor synthesizer stab tore through the PA speakers at 150 beats per minute, followed by a roaring, high-gain stadium guitar riff that rattled the water pitchers on the tables:

*The clock on the wall is a guillotine blade*
*Cutting down the promises we never made*
*I see you standing there across the crowded floor*
*Like a lighthouse burning on a midnight shore!*

"Whoa, wrong track!" the organizer yelped, dropping his clipboards as papers scattered across the carpet. He fumbled with the volume knob, accidentally turning the master fader up before the remote slipped under the cart.

Across the room, students laughed and shouted over the sudden wall of stadium rock, waiting for him to fix the deck.

At Table 4, Valerie didn't flinch or look toward the speakers. Instead, she reached across the narrow tabletop and placed her hand directly over John's wrist where it rested near his joystick controller. Her palm was warm, her grip surprisingly firm.

*Don't tell me to wait, don't tell me to stop*
*I'm a runaway train and I'm blowing the top*
*The neon signs are flashing "Danger" in red*
*But I'd rather be living than safe and dead*
*Hold on tight, baby, don't let go*
*We're dancing on the edge of the volcano!*

"Leave it!" Valerie laughed over the roaring chorus, her eyes shining as she leaned closer to John. "Everyone else gets a polite little three-minute lullaby. You and I get the storm. Tell me that isn't a sign."

John's heart hammered against his ribs in time with the 150-BPM kick drum. Used to being treated either as an academic study partner or as a Fragile Accommodation Request by strangers on campus, the sheer, undivided intensity of her focus felt intoxicating. He didn't register the red flags in the way she had skipped the rotation rules, or the way her fingers were already subtly resting over his chair's control hand, or the literal warning screaming from the speakers.

He just smiled back into the spotlight of her attention.

"Yeah," John said over the thunder of the guitars. "Maybe it is."
