Cold November rain slanted sideways beneath the concrete canopy of the CPI Transit Center, drumming against the steel roof shelter and pooling in dark, oily puddles along the boarding curb of University Mall.

Erin sat in her power wheelchair near the Bay 1 route sign, her maroon CPI windbreaker zipped to her chin and her hands tucked into fleece-lined gloves. Every thirty seconds, her eyes flicked from the glowing amber LED countdown board overhead down the long, wet concrete incline leading up from the academic quad.

It had been sixty-two days since the speed-dating mixer in Newman Hall.

In the first two weeks after that Friday night, Valerie had seemed like a whirlwind romance. She had walked beside John's power chair to every class, brought him coffee at the library, and praised Erin as "the coolest twin sister on campus."

Then, week three had arrived, and the perimeter had begun closing like a vise.

First came the canceled Friday dinners on Progress Street—always explained away by a sudden "migraine" or an "emergency" that required John's undivided attention at Valerie's off-campus duplex. Next came the phone calls where Valerie's voice was audible in the background, coaching John on what to say, followed by nights when John's cell phone went straight to voicemail for thirty-six hours at a stretch. When Erin had finally bumped into them outside the bookstore the previous week, Valerie had answered every single question directed at John, smiling coldly while resting both hands on the rear push-handles of John's power wheelchair as if she owned the chassis.

Erin's jaw tightened as two figures finally appeared at the bottom of the rain-slicked pedestrian ramp.

John's power wheelchair was climbing the grade at half its normal speed. Even from forty feet away, Erin could see why: Valerie was walking right beside his left wheel, one hand clamped tightly around the upright frame of his backrest while the other held a single black umbrella angled entirely over her own head, leaving cold rainwater to drip steadily off the rims of John's tortoiseshell glasses.

As they rolled under the concrete shelter of Bay 1, Erin felt a cold knot twist in her stomach.

John looked hollowed out. Dark, bruised shadows hung beneath his eyes. His shoulders were hunched inward over his armrests, his gaze fixed on the wet concrete three feet ahead of his front casters—a quiet surrender in a crowded place. He wore a polite, practiced smile as he looked up at Erin, but the edges of the mask were completely frayed.

"Hey, Er," John said quietly, his voice raspy with exhaustion.

"You're soaking wet, John," Erin said, driving her chair forward half a length so she was directly facing her brother. She pulled a dry microfiber cloth from her side pouch and held it out for his glasses. "I thought we were meeting inside the Newman atrium at four-thirty so we could go over the thermodynamics packet together."

Before John could reach for the cloth, Valerie stepped half a pace forward, positioning her hip right beside John's armrest controller.

"We had errands to run, Erin," Valerie said, her tone syrupy and sharp all at once. She took the cloth out of Erin's hand and wiped the rain off John's frames herself, as though John's own arms didn't work. "And honestly, John is completely burned out from carrying your shared study load this semester. He doesn't have time to ride back to Progress Street with you tonight. We only stopped by the Transit Center so he could drop off your textbook before we head back to my place for the weekend."

Erin didn't look at Valerie. She kept her eyes locked straight on her twin brother's face.

When Valerie mentioned going back to her place for the weekend, John's right hand—resting near his joystick—flinched Almost imperceptibly, his knuckles turning white against the black foam pad. He didn't argue. He didn't correct her. He just looked down at his lap, reaching into his backpack with trembling fingers to pull out the heavy hardcover textbook.

In twenty years of sharing a life, John had never once flinched at the thought of going home.

*He's trapped,* Erin realized, the cold math locking into place in her chest. *She's isolating him for the entire weekend, and he thinks he has to endure it.*
