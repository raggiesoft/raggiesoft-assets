## 8:00 AM • Eira Rowlands — The Breach

The cold December wind howling off the Virginia peninsula usually meant one thing in the Cross household: it was time to lock the deadbolts, turn up the baseboard heaters, and settle into the absolute, predictable routine that kept Julian safe.

Gabriel Cross sat on the edge of his mattress, his calloused fingers absentmindedly running over the fretboard of his acoustic guitar. At eighteen, he was a coiled spring of frontline energy, built broad and solid, naturally assuming the role of the family’s physical shield the moment the ink had dried on his parents' incredibly ugly divorce papers. The house in Ewell was supposed to be a fortress now. The court had stripped their father of all visitation rights. The monster was legally barred from the property.

But Gabriel had always known deep down that a piece of paper wouldn't stop a monster. He had feared his father would try something like this. And now, the perimeter was about to shatter.

The sound didn't register as a word at first. It was a raw, visceral shriek that tore through the drywall from the front of the house, carrying a pitch of pure, unadulterated terror.

Gabriel dropped his guitar. It hit the carpet with a hollow thud as he bolted through his bedroom door, his heavy boots skidding against the hardwood of the hallway.

"Mom?!" Gabriel shouted, his chest heaving as he sprinted into the living room.

Eira Cross was collapsed on her knees near the open front entryway, the freezing winter wind whipping her hair across her face. The corded receiver of the wall phone was dangling from its coiled wire, swinging against the drywall. Eira wasn't dialing. She was just staring out the open doorway at the frost-covered driveway, hyperventilating.

"Mom, what is it? Are you hurt?" Gabriel demanded, his protective instincts flooding his nervous system. He instinctively stepped in front of her, positioning his body to block whatever threat was coming through the door.

But there was no one in the driveway. There were only fresh, frantic tire tracks carving through the morning frost, leading aggressively toward the main road.

"He took him," Eira gasped, her voice breaking into a ragged, breathless sob. "Gabriel... he took him."

Gabriel froze. His eyes darted away from the driveway and snapped to the corner of the living room.

The space by the window was empty. Julian’s heavy, customized manual wheelchair was completely gone. But the visual of what was left behind was what made Gabriel's stomach violently drop. Julian's heavy winter coat was still hooked over the back of the sofa, entirely untouched. The weighted sensory blanket he always kept over his lap was crumpled in a heap on the floor, discarded like trash.

"No," Gabriel breathed, the word catching in his throat. The sheer impossibility of it crashed into him. Julian was fourteen. He had Cerebral Palsy. He was completely non-verbal. He relied entirely on the strict, unconditional safety of this exact room. "No, he can't be out there. He doesn't have his coat."

"Your father," Eira wept, her hands shaking uncontrollably as she pointed toward the driveway. "He was waiting by the hedges. When I opened the door to get the mail... he just shoved past me. He grabbed Julian right out of his seat. He carried him to the car, and then he came running back. He grabbed the manual chair and shoved it haphazardly into the trunk like it was a piece of luggage! He just slammed it shut and drove away!"

A blinding, white-hot rage detonated in Gabriel’s chest. His hands curled into violent fists at his sides.

"<lang="cy" trans="I knew it,">Roeddwn i'n gwybod,</lang>" Gabriel snarled under his breath, a stream of furious, jagged Welsh spilling from his lips as he paced frantically near the door. "<lang="cy" trans="I knew the monster would try something! I should have been at the door!">Roeddwn i'n gwybod y byddai'r anghenfil yn ceisio rhywbeth! Dylwn i fod wedi bod wrth y drws!</lang>"

He wanted to punch straight through the doorframe. He wanted to run down the asphalt and tear the engine out of his father's car with his bare hands. He was the older brother. He was supposed to block the doorway. He was supposed to be the shield. But he had been in his bedroom playing a guitar, completely oblivious while his disabled brother was ambushed and violently ripped from his sanctuary.

Footsteps echoed sharply from the kitchen. Gwendolyn rounded the corner, taking in the open door, their mother on the floor, and the empty space where the wheelchair belonged.

Unlike Gabriel’s explosive, physical fury, Gwendolyn’s reaction was a terrifying, absolute freeze. Her sharp, observant eyes calculated the scene in a fraction of a second.

"How long ago?" Gwendolyn asked, her voice dropping into a cold, tactical register as she knelt beside their mother, ignoring the freezing wind. "Mom, look at me. How many minutes?"

"Two," Eira choked out, gripping Gwendolyn’s arm. "Maybe three. I couldn't stop him, Gwen."

Gabriel spun around, his chest heaving, his Welsh tirade cutting off. "I'm getting in the car. I can catch him."

"No, you can't," Gwendolyn snapped, standing up to block his path. "Gabriel, look at the tracks. He didn't go toward the county route. He went toward the interstate. By the time you get your keys, he’ll be on the highway. We don't even know if he went north or south."

"I have to do something!" Gabriel roared, his voice cracking with the sheer, crushing weight of his helplessness. The image of Julian—terrified, freezing without his coat, entirely overstimulated, and trapped in the back of a speeding car with his abuser—was an agony so profound it felt like a physical wound.

Gabriel lunged for the wall, snatching the swinging phone receiver out of the air. His furious Welsh instantly evaporated, locked away as he forced himself into the rigid, terrifyingly necessary English of the outside world. He hammered the three digits into the keypad with a shaking finger.

The line rang twice before a voice crackled through the earpiece. "9-1-1, what is your emergency?"

Gabriel gripped the plastic receiver so hard it groaned. "My brother has just been abducted. It's a non-custodial kidnapping. He is fourteen, he is non-verbal, and he is physically disabled. The man who took him is driving toward the interstate, and my brother is in immediate medical danger."

The monster had broken through their perimeter, and the quiet, festive winter they had promised Julian was entirely, devastatingly gone.

## 8:05 AM • Gabriel Rowlands — The Beacon

Gabriel slammed the heavy plastic receiver of the wall phone back into its cradle. The sharp clack echoed like a gunshot in the freezing living room.

Eira remained collapsed against the linoleum of the entryway, her knees pulled to her chest as the bitter December wind whipped through the open front door. Gabriel stepped over and gently, firmly pulled the heavy wooden door shut, engaging the deadbolt. Closing it felt like a sickening defeat—like locking Julian on the outside—but the house was dropping to a freezing temperature, and his brother wasn't coming back up the driveway.

"<lang="cy" trans="He is on the interstate by now,">Mae e ar y draffordd erbyn hyn,</lang>" Gabriel muttered, his voice a low, vibrating growl as he began to pace the length of the living room. The English language was entirely discarded, replaced by the jagged, rapid-fire Welsh that fueled his frontline fury. "<lang="cy" trans="Five minutes head start. At sixty-five miles an hour, he is already out of the county.">Pum munud o flaen llaw. Ar chwe deg pump milltir yr awr, mae e eisoes y tu allan i'r sir.</lang>"

Gwendolyn stood by the kitchen island, her sharp, tactical mind ruthlessly calculating the exit vectors. She didn't cry. The analytical freeze had entirely taken over her nervous system, mirroring her twin's native tongue.

"<lang="cy" trans="He wouldn't take the ferry,">Ni fyddai'n cymryd y fferi,</lang>" Gwendolyn fired back, her eyes tracking an invisible map across the wallpaper. "<lang="cy" trans="He wouldn't want to be trapped on a boat. And he wouldn't go north over the toll bridge because of the cameras.">Fyddai e ddim am gael ei ddal ar gwch. Ac ni fyddai'n mynd i'r gogledd dros y bont doll oherwydd y camerâu.</lang>"

"<lang="cy" trans="So he's going west,">Felly mae e'n mynd i'r gorllewin,</lang>" Gabriel concluded, his heavy boots thudding against the carpet. His hands were curled into fists so tight his fingernails bit into his palms. "He's taking him west. He's getting him off the peninsula. He's going to bury him somewhere in the interior where we can't reach him."

"The police are on their way," Eira whispered from the floor, her voice hollow and devastated.

"The police are going to treat this like a domestic dispute first, Mom," Gwendolyn said, the cold, suffocating reality of the legal system crashing down on her. She switched back to English for her mother's sake. "They have to process the paperwork to prove Tom doesn't have custody before they escalate it to a kidnapping. It’s going to take hours. Maybe days."

"We can't wait days," Gabriel snarled, stopping his pacing to stare at the empty space by the window. The visual of the discarded weighted blanket was an agony so profound it felt like a physical wound. "He's terrified, Gwen. He doesn't have his coat. He doesn't have his routine. That monster doesn't care if Julian is in pain or completely overloaded. We have to go after him ourselves."

"How, Gabriel?" Gwendolyn’s voice finally broke, a sharp, desperate sound that completely shattered her tactical facade. "Look around! We are broke. We barely make enough to cover the heating bill. We don't have the thousands of dollars it takes to hire private investigators. We don't have cars fast enough or resources wide enough to comb the entire interstate system. We are entirely cut off!"

Gabriel stared at his twin sister. The sheer, crushing helplessness of being eighteen years old hit him like a freight train. They were just kids. They had spent years building an impenetrable fortress for their brother, and Tom had bypassed it in three minutes.

The silence in the house was deafening. It was a sensory void where Julian's happy, rhythmic vocalizations usually lived.

Gabriel slowly walked back into his bedroom. He looked down at the acoustic guitar he had dropped on the carpet. He picked it up, gripping the wooden neck and feeling the worn, familiar grooves under his calloused fingers. It was the only thing in the house that actually belonged to him, the only tool he knew how to wield with any real power.

Gabriel walked back out to the living room, the guitar strapped across his chest.

"Then we don't look for him," Gabriel said, his voice dropping into a heavy, resonant register. His fierce, protective energy locked onto a new, desperate target. "We make him hear us."

Gwendolyn looked at the acoustic guitar, and then slowly turned her head toward the upright piano sitting in the corner of the room. The tactical gears in her mind violently shifted direction.

"A song," Gwendolyn whispered, the sheer impossibility of the idea sparking a terrifying, brilliant light in her eyes. "If we write it... if we can get it recorded and beg the local radio stations to play it..."

"A beacon," Gabriel finished, stepping toward the piano. The heavy, frontline defense he carried wasn't physical anymore; it was auditory. "Tom will have the radio on in the car. Julian will hear it. He'll feel the vibration of the strings. He'll know we haven't stopped fighting."

Gwendolyn sat down on the wooden piano bench. Her hands hovered over the black and white keys, trembling slightly as she channeled every ounce of her grief and tactical precision into her fingertips.

"It can't just be noise," Gwendolyn commanded, her mind already arranging the strings and the tempo. "It has to start soft. A music box. Something that sounds like his room, to keep him from panicking."

"And then we blow the doors off," Gabriel agreed, his jaw set in absolute determination. He was ready to go to war. "We write it for him. The Winter Palace."

Gwendolyn pressed her fingers into the highest octave of the piano, playing a fragile, plinking, delicate melody that sounded exactly like a child's wind-up toy. It was the quiet, desperate genesis of a rock opera, and the exact moment Upon This Winter's Night was born into the freezing Virginia air.

## 8:20 AM • Gwendolyn Rowlands — The Winter Palace

The heavy, suffocating silence of the living room was finally broken by movement.

Gwendolyn stood up from the piano bench, the rigid, tactical freeze of her posture finally cracking. She crossed the short distance between them and threw her arms around Gabriel’s neck, pulling him into a fierce, desperate hug.

Gabriel hugged her back, his broad frame hunching over her shoulder. For the last ten minutes, they had been entirely paralyzed by the sheer, crushing helplessness of being eighteen-year-old kids without the resources to hunt down a kidnapper. But Gwendolyn’s idea had just handed him a weapon.

"We do it," Gwendolyn whispered fiercely against his heavy flannel shirt. "We record it. We make the local stations play it."

Gabriel nodded, pulling back. His eyes drifted down the hall toward the small, soundproofed spare room they used as a makeshift studio. It was where they usually retreated to isolate themselves, to build their tracks in perfect, obsessive privacy.

"Not in the studio," Gabriel said, his voice a low, resonant rumble. He looked over at their mother, Eira, who was still sitting on the floor by the entryway. She had her knees pulled to her chest, utterly hollowed out by the ambush. "We hash it out right here. In the living room. Mom needs to hear it. She needs to know we aren't just sitting here waiting for the police to file domestic paperwork."

Gwendolyn followed his gaze and nodded in absolute agreement. She walked over to Eira, gently guiding their mother up from the cold linoleum. She settled Eira onto the floral sofa, wrapping a thick blanket around her shaking shoulders to ward off the chill from the open door they had finally managed to close.

Gabriel dragged a dining chair into the center of the living room, resting his acoustic guitar on his knee. The frontline defender was actively building his perimeter.

"If we're going to send a track out to the local stations, we can't just be 'Gabriel and Gwendolyn'," Gabriel pointed out, his brow furrowed in concentration. "We need a band name. Something solid. Something that commands attention on the dial."

Gwendolyn sat back down at the upright piano. Her eyes drifted to the window, where the December frost clung to the glass panes, illuminated by the outdoor holiday lights their mother had strung up just a few days ago.

"Think about Julian," Gwendolyn said softly, her tactical mind weaving the emotional core into the logistics. "What is his absolute favorite thing in the world? It's right now. It's the winter holidays. He loves the festive lights, he loves the decorations, the predictability of it... it's the only time of year he feels completely magical and safe."

She looked at Gabriel, her eyes catching the ambient light of the living room. "The Winter Palace."

Gabriel let the name sit in the air. It was perfect. It sounded massive, structural, and fiercely protective. It was an auditory castle, built entirely out of strings and chords, designed to shield a fourteen-year-old boy who couldn't protect himself.

"The Winter Palace," Gabriel confirmed, a heavy, definitive nod sealing the pact. "Okay. You said we start soft. We start with his baseline to keep him from panicking in the back of Tom's car."

"Wait," Gwendolyn said, suddenly standing up. She hurried down the hall to Julian's bedroom. A moment later, she returned carrying a small, plush, mechanical music box.

It was a relic from when Julian was a baby—a tactile toy he used to hold against his cheek when his unmedicated muscles spasmed and he needed to ground his sensory inputs. Gwendolyn sat back at the piano and turned the small metal key on the back of the toy.

The delicate, metallic tines plucked out a slow, fragile, incredibly familiar lullaby. To the rest of the world, it was the classic, immortal melody of Johannes Brahms's Wiegenlied. But to Gabriel and Gwendolyn, it didn't have a formal German title. It was simply Julian's song—the exact sequence of notes their family used to play to rock him to sleep when the world was too loud.

The pristine, tinkling notes filled the tense air of the living room. On the sofa, Eira let out a quiet, shuddering breath, closing her eyes as the familiar melody washed over her, grounding her shattered nerves.

"That's the intro," Gabriel said, his voice thick with emotion. He watched the small gears turn inside the music box. "We record that exactly as it is. It's the map in the dark."

Gwendolyn hovered her fingers over the high keys of the piano, perfectly matching the fragile pitch of the mechanical toy.

"And then," Gabriel growled, his hand gripping the neck of his acoustic guitar with bone-crushing force, "we bring the storm."

## 8:45 AM • Gabriel Rowlands — The Garrison

Gabriel watched his twin's fingers ghost over the keys. The fragile, mechanical melody of the wind-up toy spun down, its tiny brass gears slowing to a halt. Gwendolyn instantly picked up the exact pitch on the upright piano, letting the pristine, fragile notes hang in the freezing air of the living room.

It was beautiful, but it was entirely too quiet. The silence was exactly where Julian's panic lived. Gabriel gripped the neck of his acoustic guitar, his knuckles turning white. He needed to shatter that silence.

"Okay," Gabriel muttered, his voice a low, gravelly rumble that vibrated in his chest. "The music box plays. It gives him his baseline. It tells him we're here. But Tom is driving. The road is loud. We need to cut through the engine noise and the tires on the asphalt."

Gwendolyn nodded, her sharp eyes focused on the board. Her left hand dropped to the lowest register of the piano. She struck a heavy, driving progression, the dark, minor chords echoing off the drywall.

"I'll arrange this for a cello," Gwendolyn commanded, her tactical mind building the architecture of the track in real-time. "Something dark and relentless. A driving string section. Like a heartbeat he can feel in his chest."

Gabriel felt the rhythm lock into his own pulse. He brought his calloused fingers down on the steel strings of his acoustic, striking a concussive, aggressive beat. He closed his eyes, imagining the worn acoustic was his solid-body electric guitar, plugged directly into a towering stack of overdriven amplifiers.

The lyrics bled out of him, raw and entirely unpolished, pulled directly from the freezing frost clinging to the living room windowpane.

"The clock upon the mantel chimes," Gabriel sang, his deep, dramatic baritone filling the space, carrying a terrifying weight of grief. "Reminding me of better times."

Gwendolyn seamlessly wove her voice into his, her vocal harmony acting as the perfect, haunting counterpoint as her fingers danced between the heavy bass notes and the delicate upper melody.

"The window pane is frosted white... I'm searching for a sign tonight."

On the floral sofa, Eira pulled the heavy blanket tighter around her shaking shoulders. She lifted her head, her red, tear-stained eyes watching her children. The sheer, desperate power in their voices was a physical force in the room. They weren't just singing; they were building an auditory fortress to protect a boy who couldn't protect himself.

"The pre-chorus," Gwendolyn directed, her voice sharp as she kept the tempo. "The strings need to build the tension here. We wind it up tight, like a bowstring."

Gabriel struck the chords harder, his pick hand moving in a rapid, aggressive blur. "Can you hear me calling? Can you see the glow? Through the miles of silence... and the drifting snow?"

"And then the drop," Gabriel said, his chest heaving as he abruptly slapped his hand flat against the strings, killing the sound. The sudden absence of music was jarring.

He looked down at his acoustic guitar. It just wasn't enough. The hollow wooden body couldn't hold the absolute, monolithic rage he needed to unleash.

"It hits on the word 'Candle'," Gabriel paced a tight circle on the carpet, the protective, frontline energy roaring back to life. "But Gwen... this isn't going to work with just the two of us. Not if we want to reach him."

Gwendolyn let her hands rest heavily on the keys. "It's too thin," she agreed, her analytical brain immediately recognizing the deficit. "An acoustic guitar and an upright piano won't rattle the floorboards of a car. It won't dominate a radio broadcast. We need the concussive kick. We need the heavy toms, the cymbals, and the distortion. We need it to sound massive."

They were aiming for a symphonic rock epic—a towering, theatrical wall of sound that blended classical orchestration with the raw, bleeding power of heavy metal. They needed the rest of the garrison.

Gabriel set his guitar down on the dining chair. He walked over to the kitchen wall phone, grabbing the same coiled receiver their mother had clung to just an hour ago.

"I'm calling Marcus," Gabriel decided, his jaw setting into absolute stone. "And Sarah. She still has her electric cello from the summer gigs, and Marcus has his full drum kit set up in his parents' garage."

Gwendolyn stood up from the piano bench, her eyes burning with unyielding resolve. "Tell them to grab their gear and get over here now. Tell them Julian is gone."

Gabriel punched the numbers into the keypad with rigid, determined strikes. They weren't just two broke eighteen-year-olds from James City County anymore, waiting helplessly for the police to file paperwork. They were The Winter Palace, and they were mobilizing for war.
