The cold December wind howling off the Virginia peninsula usually meant one thing in the Cross household: it was time to lock the deadbolts, turn up the baseboard heaters, and settle into the absolute, predictable routine that kept Julian safe.

Gabriel Cross sat on the edge of his mattress, his calloused fingers absentmindedly running over the fretboard of his acoustic guitar. At eighteen, he was a coiled spring of frontline energy, built broad and solid, naturally assuming the role of the family’s physical shield the moment the ink had dried on his parents' incredibly ugly divorce papers. The house in Ewell was supposed to be a fortress now. The court had stripped their father of all visitation rights. The monster was legally barred from the property.

But Gabriel had always known deep down that a piece of paper wouldn't stop a monster. He had feared his father would try something like this. And now, the perimeter was about to shatter.

The sound didn't register as a word at first. It was a raw, visceral shriek that tore through the drywall from the front of the house, carrying a pitch of pure, unadulterated terror.

Gabriel dropped his guitar. It hit the carpet with a hollow thud as he bolted through his bedroom door, his heavy boots skidding against the hardwood of the hallway.

"Mom?!" Gabriel shouted, his chest heaving as he sprinted into the living room.

Eira Cross was collapsed on her knees near the open front entryway, the freezing winter wind whipping her hair across her face. The corded receiver of the wall phone was dangling from its coiled wire, swinging against the drywall. Eira wasn't dialing. She was just staring out the open doorway at the frost-covered driveway, hyperventilating.

"Mom, what is it? Are you hurt?" Gabriel demanded, his protective instincts flooding his nervous system. He instinctively stepped in front of her, positioning his body to block whatever threat was coming through the door.

But there was no one in the driveway. There were only fresh, frantic tire tracks carving through the morning frost, leading aggressively toward the main road.

"He took him," Eira gasped, her voice breaking into a ragged, breathless sob. "Gabriel... he took him."

Gabriel froze. His eyes darted away from the driveway and snapped to the corner of the living room.

The space by the window was empty. Julian’s heavy, customized manual wheelchair was completely gone. But the visual of what was left behind was what made Gabriel's stomach violently drop. Julian's heavy winter coat was still hooked over the back of the sofa, entirely untouched. The weighted sensory blanket he always kept over his lap was crumpled in a heap on the floor, discarded like trash.

"No," Gabriel breathed, the word catching in his throat. The sheer impossibility of it crashed into him. Julian was fourteen. He had Cerebral Palsy. He was completely non-verbal. He relied entirely on the strict, unconditional safety of this exact room. "No, he can't be out there. He doesn't have his coat."

"Your father," Eira wept, her hands shaking uncontrollably as she pointed toward the driveway. "He was waiting by the hedges. When I opened the door to get the mail... he just shoved past me. He grabbed Julian right out of his seat. He carried him to the car, and then he came running back. He grabbed the manual chair and shoved it haphazardly into the trunk like it was a piece of luggage! He just slammed it shut and drove away!"

A blinding, white-hot rage detonated in Gabriel’s chest. His hands curled into violent fists at his sides.

"<lang="cy" trans="I knew it,">Roeddwn i'n gwybod,</lang>" Gabriel snarled under his breath, a stream of furious, jagged Welsh spilling from his lips as he paced frantically near the door. "<lang="cy" trans="I knew the monster would try something! I should have been at the door!">Roeddwn i'n gwybod y byddai'r anghenfil yn ceisio rhywbeth! Dylwn i fod wedi bod wrth y drws!</lang>"

He wanted to punch straight through the doorframe. He wanted to run down the asphalt and tear the engine out of his father's car with his bare hands. He was the older brother. He was supposed to block the doorway. He was supposed to be the shield. But he had been in his bedroom playing a guitar, completely oblivious while his disabled brother was ambushed and violently ripped from his sanctuary.

Footsteps echoed sharply from the kitchen. Gwendolyn rounded the corner, taking in the open door, their mother on the floor, and the empty space where the wheelchair belonged.

Unlike Gabriel’s explosive, physical fury, Gwendolyn’s reaction was a terrifying, absolute freeze. Her sharp, observant eyes calculated the scene in a fraction of a second.

"How long ago?" Gwendolyn asked, her voice dropping into a cold, tactical register as she knelt beside their mother, ignoring the freezing wind. "Mom, look at me. How many minutes?"

"Two," Eira choked out, gripping Gwendolyn’s arm. "Maybe three. I couldn't stop him, Gwen."

Gabriel spun around, his chest heaving, his Welsh tirade cutting off. "I'm getting in the car. I can catch him."

"No, you can't," Gwendolyn snapped, standing up to block his path. "Gabriel, look at the tracks. He didn't go toward the county route. He went toward the interstate. By the time you get your keys, he’ll be on the highway. We don't even know if he went north or south."

"I have to do something!" Gabriel roared, his voice cracking with the sheer, crushing weight of his helplessness. The image of Julian—terrified, freezing without his coat, entirely overstimulated, and trapped in the back of a speeding car with his abuser—was an agony so profound it felt like a physical wound.

Gabriel lunged for the wall, snatching the swinging phone receiver out of the air. His furious Welsh instantly evaporated, locked away as he forced himself into the rigid, terrifyingly necessary English of the outside world. He hammered the three digits into the keypad with a shaking finger.

The line rang twice before a voice crackled through the earpiece. "9-1-1, what is your emergency?"

Gabriel gripped the plastic receiver so hard it groaned. "My brother has just been abducted. It's a non-custodial kidnapping. He is fourteen, he is non-verbal, and he is physically disabled. The man who took him is driving toward the interstate, and my brother is in immediate medical danger."

The monster had broken through their perimeter, and the quiet, festive winter they had promised Julian was entirely, devastatingly gone.
