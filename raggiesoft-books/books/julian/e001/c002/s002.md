Gabriel slammed the heavy plastic receiver of the wall phone back into its cradle. The sharp clack echoed like a gunshot in the freezing living room.

Eira remained collapsed against the linoleum of the entryway, her knees pulled to her chest as the bitter December wind whipped through the open front door. Gabriel stepped over and gently, firmly pulled the heavy wooden door shut, engaging the deadbolt. Closing it felt like a sickening defeat—like locking Julian on the outside—but the house was dropping to a freezing temperature, and his brother wasn't coming back up the driveway.

"<lang="cy" trans="He is on the interstate by now,">Mae e ar y draffordd erbyn hyn,</lang>" Gabriel muttered, his voice a low, vibrating growl as he began to pace the length of the living room. The English language was entirely discarded, replaced by the jagged, rapid-fire Welsh that fueled his frontline fury. "<lang="cy" trans="Five minutes head start. At sixty-five miles an hour, he is already out of the county.">Pum munud o flaen llaw. Ar chwe deg pump milltir yr awr, mae e eisoes y tu allan i'r sir.</lang>"

Gwendolyn stood by the kitchen island, her sharp, tactical mind ruthlessly calculating the exit vectors. She didn't cry. The analytical freeze had entirely taken over her nervous system, mirroring her twin's native tongue.

"<lang="cy" trans="He wouldn't take the ferry,">Ni fyddai'n cymryd y fferi,</lang>" Gwendolyn fired back, her eyes tracking an invisible map across the wallpaper. "<lang="cy" trans="He wouldn't want to be trapped on a boat. And he wouldn't go north over the toll bridge because of the cameras.">Fyddai e ddim am gael ei ddal ar gwch. Ac ni fyddai'n mynd i'r gogledd dros y bont doll oherwydd y camerâu.</lang>"

"<lang="cy" trans="So he's going west,">Felly mae e'n mynd i'r gorllewin,</lang>" Gabriel concluded, his heavy boots thudding against the carpet. His hands were curled into fists so tight his fingernails bit into his palms. "He's taking him west. He's getting him off the peninsula. He's going to bury him somewhere in the interior where we can't reach him."

"The police are on their way," Eira whispered from the floor, her voice hollow and devastated.

"The police are going to treat this like a domestic dispute first, Mom," Gwendolyn said, the cold, suffocating reality of the legal system crashing down on her. She switched back to English for her mother's sake. "They have to process the paperwork to prove Tom doesn't have custody before they escalate it to a kidnapping. It’s going to take hours. Maybe days."

"We can't wait days," Gabriel snarled, stopping his pacing to stare at the empty space by the window. The visual of the discarded weighted blanket was an agony so profound it felt like a physical wound. "He's terrified, Gwen. He doesn't have his coat. He doesn't have his routine. That monster doesn't care if Julian is in pain or completely overloaded. We have to go after him ourselves."

"How, Gabriel?" Gwendolyn’s voice finally broke, a sharp, desperate sound that completely shattered her tactical facade. "Look around! We are broke. We barely make enough to cover the heating bill. We don't have the thousands of dollars it takes to hire private investigators. We don't have cars fast enough or resources wide enough to comb the entire interstate system. We are entirely cut off!"

Gabriel stared at his twin sister. The sheer, crushing helplessness of being eighteen years old hit him like a freight train. They were just kids. They had spent years building an impenetrable fortress for their brother, and Tom had bypassed it in three minutes.

The silence in the house was deafening. It was a sensory void where Julian's happy, rhythmic vocalizations usually lived.

Gabriel slowly walked back into his bedroom. He looked down at the acoustic guitar he had dropped on the carpet. He picked it up, gripping the wooden neck and feeling the worn, familiar grooves under his calloused fingers. It was the only thing in the house that actually belonged to him, the only tool he knew how to wield with any real power.

Gabriel walked back out to the living room, the guitar strapped across his chest.

"Then we don't look for him," Gabriel said, his voice dropping into a heavy, resonant register. His fierce, protective energy locked onto a new, desperate target. "We make him hear us."

Gwendolyn looked at the acoustic guitar, and then slowly turned her head toward the upright piano sitting in the corner of the room. The tactical gears in her mind violently shifted direction.

"A song," Gwendolyn whispered, the sheer impossibility of the idea sparking a terrifying, brilliant light in her eyes. "If we write it... if we can get it recorded and beg the local radio stations to play it..."

"A beacon," Gabriel finished, stepping toward the piano. The heavy, frontline defense he carried wasn't physical anymore; it was auditory. "Tom will have the radio on in the car. Julian will hear it. He'll feel the vibration of the strings. He'll know we haven't stopped fighting."

Gwendolyn sat down on the wooden piano bench. Her hands hovered over the black and white keys, trembling slightly as she channeled every ounce of her grief and tactical precision into her fingertips.

"It can't just be noise," Gwendolyn commanded, her mind already arranging the strings and the tempo. "It has to start soft. A music box. Something that sounds like his room, to keep him from panicking."

"And then we blow the doors off," Gabriel agreed, his jaw set in absolute determination. He was ready to go to war. "We write it for him. The Winter Palace."

Gwendolyn pressed her fingers into the highest octave of the piano, playing a fragile, plinking, delicate melody that sounded exactly like a child's wind-up toy. It was the quiet, desperate genesis of a rock opera, and the exact moment Upon This Winter's Night was born into the freezing Virginia air.
