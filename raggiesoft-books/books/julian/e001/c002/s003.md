The heavy, suffocating silence of the living room was finally broken by movement.

Gwendolyn stood up from the piano bench, the rigid, tactical freeze of her posture finally cracking. She crossed the short distance between them and threw her arms around Gabriel’s neck, pulling him into a fierce, desperate hug.

Gabriel hugged her back, his broad frame hunching over her shoulder. For the last ten minutes, they had been entirely paralyzed by the sheer, crushing helplessness of being eighteen-year-old kids without the resources to hunt down a kidnapper. But Gwendolyn’s idea had just handed him a weapon.

"We do it," Gwendolyn whispered fiercely against his heavy flannel shirt. "We record it. We make the local stations play it."

Gabriel nodded, pulling back. His eyes drifted down the hall toward the small, soundproofed spare room they used as a makeshift studio. It was where they usually retreated to isolate themselves, to build their tracks in perfect, obsessive privacy.

"Not in the studio," Gabriel said, his voice a low, resonant rumble. He looked over at their mother, Eira, who was still sitting on the floor by the entryway. She had her knees pulled to her chest, utterly hollowed out by the ambush. "We hash it out right here. In the living room. Mom needs to hear it. She needs to know we aren't just sitting here waiting for the police to file domestic paperwork."

Gwendolyn followed his gaze and nodded in absolute agreement. She walked over to Eira, gently guiding their mother up from the cold linoleum. She settled Eira onto the floral sofa, wrapping a thick blanket around her shaking shoulders to ward off the chill from the open door they had finally managed to close.

Gabriel dragged a dining chair into the center of the living room, resting his acoustic guitar on his knee. The frontline defender was actively building his perimeter.

"If we're going to send a track out to the local stations, we can't just be 'Gabriel and Gwendolyn'," Gabriel pointed out, his brow furrowed in concentration. "We need a band name. Something solid. Something that commands attention on the dial."

Gwendolyn sat back down at the upright piano. Her eyes drifted to the window, where the December frost clung to the glass panes, illuminated by the outdoor holiday lights their mother had strung up just a few days ago.

"Think about Julian," Gwendolyn said softly, her tactical mind weaving the emotional core into the logistics. "What is his absolute favorite thing in the world? It's right now. It's the winter holidays. He loves the festive lights, he loves the decorations, the predictability of it... it's the only time of year he feels completely magical and safe."

She looked at Gabriel, her eyes catching the ambient light of the living room. "The Winter Palace."

Gabriel let the name sit in the air. It was perfect. It sounded massive, structural, and fiercely protective. It was an auditory castle, built entirely out of strings and chords, designed to shield a fourteen-year-old boy who couldn't protect himself.

"The Winter Palace," Gabriel confirmed, a heavy, definitive nod sealing the pact. "Okay. You said we start soft. We start with his baseline to keep him from panicking in the back of Tom's car."

"Wait," Gwendolyn said, suddenly standing up. She hurried down the hall to Julian's bedroom. A moment later, she returned carrying a small, plush, mechanical music box.

It was a relic from when Julian was a baby—a tactile toy he used to hold against his cheek when his unmedicated muscles spasmed and he needed to ground his sensory inputs. Gwendolyn sat back at the piano and turned the small metal key on the back of the toy.

The delicate, metallic tines plucked out a slow, fragile, incredibly familiar lullaby. To the rest of the world, it was the classic, immortal melody of Johannes Brahms's Wiegenlied. But to Gabriel and Gwendolyn, it didn't have a formal German title. It was simply Julian's song—the exact sequence of notes their family used to play to rock him to sleep when the world was too loud.

The pristine, tinkling notes filled the tense air of the living room. On the sofa, Eira let out a quiet, shuddering breath, closing her eyes as the familiar melody washed over her, grounding her shattered nerves.

"That's the intro," Gabriel said, his voice thick with emotion. He watched the small gears turn inside the music box. "We record that exactly as it is. It's the map in the dark."

Gwendolyn hovered her fingers over the high keys of the piano, perfectly matching the fragile pitch of the mechanical toy.

"And then," Gabriel growled, his hand gripping the neck of his acoustic guitar with bone-crushing force, "we bring the storm."
