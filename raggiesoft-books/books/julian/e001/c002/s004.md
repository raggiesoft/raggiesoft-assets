Gabriel watched his twin's fingers ghost over the keys. The fragile, mechanical melody of the wind-up toy spun down, its tiny brass gears slowing to a halt. Gwendolyn instantly picked up the exact pitch on the upright piano, letting the pristine, fragile notes hang in the freezing air of the living room.

It was beautiful, but it was entirely too quiet. The silence was exactly where Julian's panic lived. Gabriel gripped the neck of his acoustic guitar, his knuckles turning white. He needed to shatter that silence.

"Okay," Gabriel muttered, his voice a low, gravelly rumble that vibrated in his chest. "The music box plays. It gives him his baseline. It tells him we're here. But Tom is driving. The road is loud. We need to cut through the engine noise and the tires on the asphalt."

Gwendolyn nodded, her sharp eyes focused on the board. Her left hand dropped to the lowest register of the piano. She struck a heavy, driving progression, the dark, minor chords echoing off the drywall.

"I'll arrange this for a cello," Gwendolyn commanded, her tactical mind building the architecture of the track in real-time. "Something dark and relentless. A driving string section. Like a heartbeat he can feel in his chest."

Gabriel felt the rhythm lock into his own pulse. He brought his calloused fingers down on the steel strings of his acoustic, striking a concussive, aggressive beat. He closed his eyes, imagining the worn acoustic was his solid-body electric guitar, plugged directly into a towering stack of overdriven amplifiers.

The lyrics bled out of him, raw and entirely unpolished, pulled directly from the freezing frost clinging to the living room windowpane.

"The clock upon the mantel chimes," Gabriel sang, his deep, dramatic baritone filling the space, carrying a terrifying weight of grief. "Reminding me of better times."

Gwendolyn seamlessly wove her voice into his, her vocal harmony acting as the perfect, haunting counterpoint as her fingers danced between the heavy bass notes and the delicate upper melody.

"The window pane is frosted white... I'm searching for a sign tonight."

On the floral sofa, Eira pulled the heavy blanket tighter around her shaking shoulders. She lifted her head, her red, tear-stained eyes watching her children. The sheer, desperate power in their voices was a physical force in the room. They weren't just singing; they were building an auditory fortress to protect a boy who couldn't protect himself.

"The pre-chorus," Gwendolyn directed, her voice sharp as she kept the tempo. "The strings need to build the tension here. We wind it up tight, like a bowstring."

Gabriel struck the chords harder, his pick hand moving in a rapid, aggressive blur. "Can you hear me calling? Can you see the glow? Through the miles of silence... and the drifting snow?"

"And then the drop," Gabriel said, his chest heaving as he abruptly slapped his hand flat against the strings, killing the sound. The sudden absence of music was jarring.

He looked down at his acoustic guitar. It just wasn't enough. The hollow wooden body couldn't hold the absolute, monolithic rage he needed to unleash.

"It hits on the word 'Candle'," Gabriel paced a tight circle on the carpet, the protective, frontline energy roaring back to life. "But Gwen... this isn't going to work with just the two of us. Not if we want to reach him."

Gwendolyn let her hands rest heavily on the keys. "It's too thin," she agreed, her analytical brain immediately recognizing the deficit. "An acoustic guitar and an upright piano won't rattle the floorboards of a car. It won't dominate a radio broadcast. We need the concussive kick. We need the heavy toms, the cymbals, and the distortion. We need it to sound massive."

They were aiming for a symphonic rock epic—a towering, theatrical wall of sound that blended classical orchestration with the raw, bleeding power of heavy metal. They needed the rest of the garrison.

Gabriel set his guitar down on the dining chair. He walked over to the kitchen wall phone, grabbing the same coiled receiver their mother had clung to just an hour ago.

"I'm calling Marcus," Gabriel decided, his jaw setting into absolute stone. "And Sarah. She still has her electric cello from the summer gigs, and Marcus has his full drum kit set up in his parents' garage."

Gwendolyn stood up from the piano bench, her eyes burning with unyielding resolve. "Tell them to grab their gear and get over here now. Tell them Julian is gone."

Gabriel punched the numbers into the keypad with rigid, determined strikes. They weren't just two broke eighteen-year-olds from James City County anymore, waiting helplessly for the police to file paperwork. They were The Winter Palace, and they were mobilizing for war.
