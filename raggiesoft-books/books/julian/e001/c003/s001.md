The gravel driveway of the Cross property crunched loudly under the heavy, worn tires of a battered transit van.

Gabriel stood by the frosted front window, his jaw clenched, watching the vehicle throw the transmission into park. The doors swung open before the engine even fully cut out.

Marcus stepped out of the driver’s side, the collar of his denim jacket popped against the biting Virginia wind. He hauled a massive, heavy-duty canvas bag filled with steel drum hardware over his shoulder. From the passenger side, Sarah emerged. She didn't have her usual bright, easygoing smile; her face was set in a hard, serious line as she hoisted her thick, padded electric cello case onto her back like a shield.

They didn't bother knocking. They pushed through the front door, bringing a gust of freezing air with them into the tense, suffocating atmosphere of the living room.

The moment they crossed the threshold, the sheer gravity of the situation hit them. They saw Eira huddled on the floral sofa, a heavy blanket wrapped tightly around her shaking shoulders. And then, their eyes snapped to the corner of the room.

The empty space. The missing manual wheelchair.

"Gabriel," Marcus said, his voice dropping into a low, completely stunned register as he dropped his hardware bag onto the carpet with a heavy metallic clank. "Man... what happened? Where's Julian?"

"Tom," Gabriel stated, the name sounding like a curse in the quiet room. He didn't sugarcoat it, and he didn't use neurotypical filler to soften the blow. "He ambushed Mom at the door at eight o'clock this morning. He grabbed Julian right out of his seat, threw him in his car, and threw the chair in the trunk. He took him."

Sarah’s breath hitched. Her hands flew to her mouth in absolute horror. She knew exactly what that meant. Julian wasn't just missing; he was entirely unmoored.

"He doesn't have his weighted blanket," Sarah whispered, her eyes wide as she looked at Gwendolyn, who was standing rigidly by the piano. "He doesn't have his routine. He's trapped in a moving car with a man who doesn't know how to calm his sensory spikes."

"We called the police," Gwendolyn explained, her tactical mind stepping forward to manage the briefing. "They put out an alert on the interstate. But they are treating it as a domestic custody dispute while they process the paperwork. They aren't treating it as an immediate medical emergency. We are losing hours."

"So we aren't waiting for them," Gabriel growled, his fierce, protective energy radiating off him in waves. He looked at his two bandmates—his friends, his garrison. "We wrote a track. A beacon. If we can get it recorded, mixed, and rushed to the local radio stations, Julian might hear it. We need it to be loud. We need it to be massive."

Marcus didn't offer empty platitudes. He didn't tell them everything was going to be okay, and he didn't question the logic of trying to fight a kidnapping with a rock song. He understood the absolute, primal desperation of an older brother backed into a corner.

Marcus simply nodded, his expression hardening into stone. He reached down and grabbed the handles of his hardware bag.

"Then let's go to war," Marcus said firmly. "Where are we setting up?"

"The spare room," Gwendolyn directed, pointing down the narrow hallway. "We need to soundproof it as much as we can. We are going to push the limits of every amplifier we have."

They moved as a synchronized, completely focused unit. For the next thirty minutes, the hallway was a blur of frantic, heavy lifting. They carried the snare, the heavy floor toms, the kick drum, and the cymbals into the small, carpeted spare room.

Sarah unzipped her padded case, pulling out her sleek, solid-body electric cello. She plugged the heavy quarter-inch cable directly into an oversized bass amplifier, bypassing the delicate, classical acoustic tones for something capable of tearing the paint off the walls.

Gwendolyn dragged her heavy synthesizer keyboard into the corner, booting up the digital strings and choral patches, while Gabriel hauled his heavy amplifier and his solid-body electric guitar into the center of the cramped space.

The room was suffocatingly tight, smelling of hot dust from the warming amplifiers and the frantic sweat of sheer panic. Wires snaked across the floor like a chaotic nervous system.

Gabriel strapped his electric guitar across his chest. He turned the distortion knob on his amplifier all the way up, completely bypassing any attempt at restraint. He looked around the tiny, crowded room at Marcus, Sarah, and Gwendolyn.

"The band is called The Winter Palace," Gabriel announced over the low, vibrating hum of the live amplifiers. "The track is called Upon This Winter's Night. Gwen is going to start us off with his music box melody to establish the baseline. But when I give the signal... I want you to hit it so hard the foundation shakes."

Sarah tightened her grip on her cello bow, her eyes blazing with unwavering solidarity. Marcus settled onto his drum throne, raising his heavy wooden sticks.

The garrison was armed. The recording light clicked to a bright, violent red.
