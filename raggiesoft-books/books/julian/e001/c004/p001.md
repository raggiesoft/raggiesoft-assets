## 11:45 AM • Richard Harrison — The Override

Gabriel’s battered sedan slid violently into the VIP parking lot of the regional broadcasting hub, the tires protesting sharply against the frozen asphalt.

Richard Harrison was out of the passenger seat before the car even came to a complete stop, his corporate security badge already in his hand. Gabriel and Marcus bailed out a second later, sprinting across the frost-covered pavement to keep pace with the towering, fiercely determined father.

Inside the lobby, the atmosphere was entirely disconnected from the raw, bleeding terror of the Cross household. It was quiet, climate-controlled, and hummed with the low, mundane energy of administrative work.

Richard didn't even break his stride. He blew right past the bewildered receptionist, swiping his heavy plastic badge against the scanner of the reinforced glass security doors. He didn't look like a corporate program director right now. With the sleeves of his dress shirt rolled up to his elbows and his jaw set in absolute stone, he looked exactly like the fiercely empathetic music therapy teacher he used to be—the man who had spent hours sitting on the floor with a much younger Julian, patiently using rhythm and vibration to help a non-verbal boy regulate his sensory overloads.

"Give me the tape," Richard ordered as the heavy doors buzzed open, leading them down a long, carpeted hallway.

Gabriel shoved his hand into his denim jacket and pulled out the plastic cassette. His hand was shaking so badly he nearly dropped it.

"It's rough," Gabriel rasped, his chest heaving as he pressed the tape into Richard's palm while they practically jogged down the corridor. "We just recorded it in the spare room. But it has his music box melody at the start. You know the one. We need him to hear it."

Richard stared down at the cassette, a fierce, protective recognition flashing in his eyes. He knew exactly what that melody meant to Julian. It was the ultimate anchor.

Richard pushed open a set of heavy, soundproof double doors, leading Gabriel and Marcus directly into Master Control. It was a massive, dimly lit room dominated by a sprawling analog mixing console, racks of heavy audio processors, and a wall of monitors displaying the live feeds of a dozen different FM stations.

The lone board operator on duty spun around in his chair, his eyes going wide at the sudden intrusion. "Mr. Harrison? Sir, you're not scheduled—"

"Step away from the board, Dave," Richard commanded, his voice echoing with unyielding corporate authority. He moved the operator aside and immediately took the captain's chair, his hands moving with surgical precision across the mixing board. He slammed a heavy plastic cartridge adapter into the primary deck and slid Gabriel's cassette inside.

"We don't know what direction he took," Gabriel explained frantically, the frontline energy surging as he looked at the massive geographical map mounted on the wall. "My mom thinks he got on Interstate 64 West. But it's been nearly four hours. He could be anywhere."

"Then we blanket the grid," Richard said, his fingers flying across a row of glowing toggle switches. "I manage the coastal syndicate, but that's not going to be enough if Tom is running for the interior. I'm patching in the central Virginia repeaters. And just to be safe, I'm pulling the override codes for our towers in the Shenandoah Valley and Winchester."

Marcus’s eyes widened. "Winchester? That's way out there."

"Winchester sits right at the top of the valley," Richard confirmed, his eyes locked on the audio meters. "That tower's signal bleeds directly through the mountains and pushes deep across the state line into West Virginia. If Tom is trying to lose himself in the backwoods, that signal will reach him. We are going to hijack the frequency of every major rock and contemporary station on this network."

Gabriel stared at the glowing red lights on the console. It was a staggering abuse of corporate power, a massive FCC violation, and the most beautiful thing he had ever seen.

Richard pulled a heavy studio microphone attached to an articulating arm and swung it directly in front of Gabriel.

"I'm killing the syndicated broadcast in exactly ten seconds," Richard warned, his hand hovering over the master fader. "I'm going to open your mic. You have fifteen seconds to speak directly to Julian before I trigger the playback. Tell him what he needs to hear, Gabriel. Be his shield."

Gabriel gripped the heavy metal base of the microphone. His heart hammered a violent, bruising rhythm against his ribs. He closed his eyes, visualizing Julian. He pictured the terrified fourteen-year-old trapped in a freezing car, overwhelmed by the sensory noise of the highway, desperately needing his older brother to block the doorway.

"Three," Richard counted down. "Two. One. You're live."

A sharp, electric hum crackled through the monitors as thousands of radios across the state were suddenly plunged into dead air.

Gabriel leaned into the microphone, his deep baritone completely stripped of its tough rock-frontman armor. It was just the raw, bleeding voice of a boy terrified for his family.

"Julian," Gabriel spoke, his voice trembling but projecting with absolute clarity. "It's Gabriel. Mom is safe. Gwen is safe. I know it's loud right now, and I know you're scared. But you need to listen to me. We are looking for you. We are not going to stop. I need you to listen to this song, Julian. Close your eyes, and hold onto it."

Gabriel gave Richard a sharp, definitive nod.

Richard slammed the playback button.

Over the massive studio monitors—and simultaneously out of hundreds of thousands of car speakers across the coastal flatlands, the central piedmont, and the freezing, jagged peaks of the Blue Ridge Mountains—the pristine, fragile, metallic plinking of Julian's music box echoed into the world.

It played for ten seconds, establishing the quiet, perfect safety of the Rowlands household.

And then, Gabriel’s heavy, distorted guitar and Sarah’s dark, driving cello detonated over the airwaves.

Upon This Winter's Night had officially been unleashed.

## 12:04 PM • Julian Rowlands — Lost City

The heater in the stolen sedan was broken, blowing a pathetic, lukewarm draft against the freezing vinyl of the back seat.

Julian sat completely rigid, his knees pulled tightly to his chest. He was wearing only the thin, long-sleeved shirt he had been wearing at the breakfast table. He didn't have his heavy winter coat, and he didn't have the weighted sensory blanket that kept his nervous system grounded.

But worse than the cold was the absolute, terrifying loss of his physical autonomy.

Without his customized manual wheelchair, Julian was entirely unmoored. The heavy, unpredictable sway of the speeding car threw his body weight from side to side. His Cerebral Palsy made it impossible for him to right himself or brace against the sharp turns of the winding mountain roads. Every time the car jerked, his muscles locked in a painful, involuntary spasm.

He couldn't shift. He couldn't speak to ask for help. He was trapped in a terrifying, moving cage.

In the driver's seat, Tom kept his eyes glued to the desolate, winding asphalt of the highway. He hadn't spoken a single word to Julian since shoving him violently into the car four hours ago. There was no fatherly warmth, no explanation. Tom’s jaw was locked in a bitter, paranoid scowl, his eyes constantly darting to the rearview mirror to check for flashing police lights.

Through the frost-lined passenger window, Julian watched the towering, jagged peaks of the mountains swallow the horizon. The landscape was entirely alien. There were no flat coastal roads, no familiar marshlands, and no comforting pulse of the neighborhood holiday lights.

The car blew past a large, reflective green sign on the side of the highway.

WELCOME TO WEST VIRGINIA.

They had crossed the state line into the town of Lost City. The name was a perfect, devastating mirror of Julian's reality. He was completely, utterly lost.

A sharp, panicked whine built in the back of Julian's throat. The sensory overload of the rattling car and the sheer terror of isolation was pushing his autistic brain toward a massive, silent meltdown. His hands balled into tight fists, his unmedicated muscles trembling violently.

Annoyed by the sudden, distressed vocalization from the back seat, Tom let out a harsh, aggravated sigh.

"Keep it down back there," Tom snapped, his voice dripping with impatience.

To drown out the sound of Julian's panic, Tom reached forward and violently twisted the dial on the car's dashboard radio. He didn't care what station it landed on; he just needed noise to fill the suffocating silence of the getaway car.

Static hissed through the cheap car speakers, followed by the mid-day broadcast of a regional rock station.

Julian squeezed his eyes shut, his breathing turning rapid and shallow, completely unaware that his older brother was currently speeding down a Virginia highway with a cassette tape that was about to hijack the very airwaves currently filling the car.

## 12:15 PM • Eira Rowlands — The Near Miss

The glowing red light on the mixing console clicked off. The broadcast was out in the wild, bleeding across the Virginia state lines, carried by invisible radio waves into the freezing mountain air.

Gabriel let go of the heavy studio microphone. His hands were shaking, the adrenaline that had fueled his frantic drive to the station suddenly crashing into a hollow, agonizing void. He looked up at Mr. Harrison, whose face was a mask of grim solidarity.

"Use the desk line," Mr. Harrison offered quietly, pushing a heavy, multi-button phone across the console. "Call your mother. Let her know the beacon is lit."

Gabriel snatched the receiver and dialed the familiar number with stiff, clumsy fingers. It rang exactly half a time before the line clicked open.

"Gabriel?" Eira’s voice was breathless, frantic, and strung tight with a terrifying anticipation.

"It's me, Mom," Gabriel said, leaning his heavy frame against the edge of the mixing board. "We did it. Mr. Harrison pushed it to the repeaters. It's playing right now across the whole grid. If Tom has the radio on, Julian will hear the music box."

"Gabriel, hold on," Eira gasped, the sound of a sharp, electronic beep echoing over the line. "That's call waiting. It's the county precinct. Don't hang up!"

There was a sharp click, followed by the agonizing, hollow static of being placed on hold.

Gabriel stopped breathing. He gripped the plastic receiver so tightly the casing groaned. Marcus stepped closer, his eyes wide, reading the sudden, absolute stillness in his friend's posture. Across the room, Mr. Harrison paused his work, the entire control room holding its breath.

They found him, Gabriel thought, a massive, desperate spark of hope igniting in his chest. The highway patrol found the car. It’s over.

Two full minutes passed. It felt like an entire lifetime.

Then, the line clicked back over.

It wasn't a sigh of relief. It was a broken, devastated, guttural sob.

"Mom?" Gabriel demanded, the hope in his chest instantly turning to ice. "Mom, what did they say? Did they find the car?"

"They had him," Eira wept, her voice collapsing into absolute hysterics. "Gabriel, they were looking right at him, and they let him drive away!"

"What are you talking about?!" Gabriel roared, his frontline protector instincts violently surging. "How do you let a kidnapper drive away?!"

"He crossed the state line," Eira choked out, struggling to pull air into her lungs. "A state trooper pulled Tom over in a town called Lost City, out in West Virginia. He was doing eighty-five in a fifty-five zone. The trooper walked right up to the window. He saw Julian in the back seat."

Gabriel’s vision swam. A police officer had been standing inches away from his brother.

"Julian is non-verbal!" Gabriel yelled into the receiver, his voice cracking with sheer, unadulterated rage. "He was probably spasming! He had to be terrified! How did the cop not see that he was in danger?!"

"Because Tom lied!" Eira screamed, her heartbreak echoing through the sterile broadcast booth. "Tom told the trooper they were just on a father-son road trip, and that Julian was just having a bad day with his disability! And because it's a different state... the trooper ran the license plates, Gabriel. He ran the plates, and nothing came up."

The geographical and bureaucratic reality hit Gabriel with the force of a physical blow.

"The paperwork," Gabriel whispered, the blood draining completely from his face.

"The clerk in James City County hasn't pushed the custody breach to the national database yet," Eira confirmed, sobbing uncontrollably. "To that state trooper, the car wasn't flagged in the NCIC system. There was no federal BOLO. It was just a speeding ticket. He wrote Tom a citation, and he let him merge back onto the highway."

Gabriel stared blankly at the acoustic foam on the studio wall. The system had failed. The absolute, sluggish incompetence of the bureaucracy had given his father a free pass. Tom was in West Virginia, burying Julian deeper into the desolate, freezing interior of the Appalachian mountains.

"We are entirely on our own," Gabriel rasped, his voice dropping into a terrifyingly cold, hollow register.

"Gabriel, what do we do?" Eira begged, completely shattered.

"You aren't on your own," Detective Miller interrupted, stepping forward. His expression hardened into absolute, uncompromising authority. "The moment that citation hit the regional dispatch, it triggered the interstate net. Your father crossed a state line with a child he has absolutely no legal right to possess. This is no longer a local civil dispute. It is a federal kidnapping."

Gwendolyn’s breath hitched. The tactical shift was monumental.

"The FBI has officially been notified, and we have pushed a Critical Missing Child Bulletin to the NCIC database," the detective continued, laying out the invisible arsenal they were finally deploying. "Every highway patrol unit, every weigh station, and every toll booth from here to the Midwest has his license plate on their dashboard computers. If a single cop runs those tags, it’s going to light up like a Christmas tree. And because Julian is without his manual wheelchair and his required medication, he is officially classified as critically endangered."

Gabriel looked over at the mixing console, where the audio meters for Upon This Winter's Night were still bouncing in the red, projecting their desperate, symphonic war cry across the mountains.

"We pray he leaves the radio on," Gabriel said, his jaw setting into uncompromising stone. "I'm coming home, Mom."

He slammed the receiver down. The beacon was the only weapon they had left.
