Gabriel’s battered sedan slid violently into the VIP parking lot of the regional broadcasting hub, the tires protesting sharply against the frozen asphalt.

Richard Harrison was out of the passenger seat before the car even came to a complete stop, his corporate security badge already in his hand. Gabriel and Marcus bailed out a second later, sprinting across the frost-covered pavement to keep pace with the towering, fiercely determined father.

Inside the lobby, the atmosphere was entirely disconnected from the raw, bleeding terror of the Cross household. It was quiet, climate-controlled, and hummed with the low, mundane energy of administrative work.

Richard didn't even break his stride. He blew right past the bewildered receptionist, swiping his heavy plastic badge against the scanner of the reinforced glass security doors. He didn't look like a corporate program director right now. With the sleeves of his dress shirt rolled up to his elbows and his jaw set in absolute stone, he looked exactly like the fiercely empathetic music therapy teacher he used to be—the man who had spent hours sitting on the floor with a much younger Julian, patiently using rhythm and vibration to help a non-verbal boy regulate his sensory overloads.

"Give me the tape," Richard ordered as the heavy doors buzzed open, leading them down a long, carpeted hallway.

Gabriel shoved his hand into his denim jacket and pulled out the plastic cassette. His hand was shaking so badly he nearly dropped it.

"It's rough," Gabriel rasped, his chest heaving as he pressed the tape into Richard's palm while they practically jogged down the corridor. "We just recorded it in the spare room. But it has his music box melody at the start. You know the one. We need him to hear it."

Richard stared down at the cassette, a fierce, protective recognition flashing in his eyes. He knew exactly what that melody meant to Julian. It was the ultimate anchor.

Richard pushed open a set of heavy, soundproof double doors, leading Gabriel and Marcus directly into Master Control. It was a massive, dimly lit room dominated by a sprawling analog mixing console, racks of heavy audio processors, and a wall of monitors displaying the live feeds of a dozen different FM stations.

The lone board operator on duty spun around in his chair, his eyes going wide at the sudden intrusion. "Mr. Harrison? Sir, you're not scheduled—"

"Step away from the board, Dave," Richard commanded, his voice echoing with unyielding corporate authority. He moved the operator aside and immediately took the captain's chair, his hands moving with surgical precision across the mixing board. He slammed a heavy plastic cartridge adapter into the primary deck and slid Gabriel's cassette inside.

"We don't know what direction he took," Gabriel explained frantically, the frontline energy surging as he looked at the massive geographical map mounted on the wall. "My mom thinks he got on Interstate 64 West. But it's been nearly four hours. He could be anywhere."

"Then we blanket the grid," Richard said, his fingers flying across a row of glowing toggle switches. "I manage the coastal syndicate, but that's not going to be enough if Tom is running for the interior. I'm patching in the central Virginia repeaters. And just to be safe, I'm pulling the override codes for our towers in the Shenandoah Valley and Winchester."

Marcus’s eyes widened. "Winchester? That's way out there."

"Winchester sits right at the top of the valley," Richard confirmed, his eyes locked on the audio meters. "That tower's signal bleeds directly through the mountains and pushes deep across the state line into West Virginia. If Tom is trying to lose himself in the backwoods, that signal will reach him. We are going to hijack the frequency of every major rock and contemporary station on this network."

Gabriel stared at the glowing red lights on the console. It was a staggering abuse of corporate power, a massive FCC violation, and the most beautiful thing he had ever seen.

Richard pulled a heavy studio microphone attached to an articulating arm and swung it directly in front of Gabriel.

"I'm killing the syndicated broadcast in exactly ten seconds," Richard warned, his hand hovering over the master fader. "I'm going to open your mic. You have fifteen seconds to speak directly to Julian before I trigger the playback. Tell him what he needs to hear, Gabriel. Be his shield."

Gabriel gripped the heavy metal base of the microphone. His heart hammered a violent, bruising rhythm against his ribs. He closed his eyes, visualizing Julian. He pictured the terrified fourteen-year-old trapped in a freezing car, overwhelmed by the sensory noise of the highway, desperately needing his older brother to block the doorway.

"Three," Richard counted down. "Two. One. You're live."

A sharp, electric hum crackled through the monitors as thousands of radios across the state were suddenly plunged into dead air.

Gabriel leaned into the microphone, his deep baritone completely stripped of its tough rock-frontman armor. It was just the raw, bleeding voice of a boy terrified for his family.

"Julian," Gabriel spoke, his voice trembling but projecting with absolute clarity. "It's Gabriel. Mom is safe. Gwen is safe. I know it's loud right now, and I know you're scared. But you need to listen to me. We are looking for you. We are not going to stop. I need you to listen to this song, Julian. Close your eyes, and hold onto it."

Gabriel gave Richard a sharp, definitive nod.

Richard slammed the playback button.

Over the massive studio monitors—and simultaneously out of hundreds of thousands of car speakers across the coastal flatlands, the central piedmont, and the freezing, jagged peaks of the Blue Ridge Mountains—the pristine, fragile, metallic plinking of Julian's music box echoed into the world.

It played for ten seconds, establishing the quiet, perfect safety of the Rowlands household.

And then, Gabriel’s heavy, distorted guitar and Sarah’s dark, driving cello detonated over the airwaves.

Upon This Winter's Night had officially been unleashed.
