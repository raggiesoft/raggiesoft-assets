The heater in the stolen sedan was broken, blowing a pathetic, lukewarm draft against the freezing vinyl of the back seat.

Julian sat completely rigid, his knees pulled tightly to his chest. He was wearing only the thin, long-sleeved shirt he had been wearing at the breakfast table. He didn't have his heavy winter coat, and he didn't have the weighted sensory blanket that kept his nervous system grounded.

But worse than the cold was the absolute, terrifying loss of his physical autonomy.

Without his customized manual wheelchair, Julian was entirely unmoored. The heavy, unpredictable sway of the speeding car threw his body weight from side to side. His Cerebral Palsy made it impossible for him to right himself or brace against the sharp turns of the winding mountain roads. Every time the car jerked, his muscles locked in a painful, involuntary spasm.

He couldn't shift. He couldn't speak to ask for help. He was trapped in a terrifying, moving cage.

In the driver's seat, Tom kept his eyes glued to the desolate, winding asphalt of the highway. He hadn't spoken a single word to Julian since shoving him violently into the car four hours ago. There was no fatherly warmth, no explanation. Tom’s jaw was locked in a bitter, paranoid scowl, his eyes constantly darting to the rearview mirror to check for flashing police lights.

Through the frost-lined passenger window, Julian watched the towering, jagged peaks of the mountains swallow the horizon. The landscape was entirely alien. There were no flat coastal roads, no familiar marshlands, and no comforting pulse of the neighborhood holiday lights.

The car blew past a large, reflective green sign on the side of the highway.

WELCOME TO WEST VIRGINIA.

They had crossed the state line into the town of Lost City. The name was a perfect, devastating mirror of Julian's reality. He was completely, utterly lost.

A sharp, panicked whine built in the back of Julian's throat. The sensory overload of the rattling car and the sheer terror of isolation was pushing his autistic brain toward a massive, silent meltdown. His hands balled into tight fists, his unmedicated muscles trembling violently.

Annoyed by the sudden, distressed vocalization from the back seat, Tom let out a harsh, aggravated sigh.

"Keep it down back there," Tom snapped, his voice dripping with impatience.

To drown out the sound of Julian's panic, Tom reached forward and violently twisted the dial on the car's dashboard radio. He didn't care what station it landed on; he just needed noise to fill the suffocating silence of the getaway car.

Static hissed through the cheap car speakers, followed by the mid-day broadcast of a regional rock station.

Julian squeezed his eyes shut, his breathing turning rapid and shallow, completely unaware that his older brother was currently speeding down a Virginia highway with a cassette tape that was about to hijack the very airwaves currently filling the car.
