The glowing red light on the mixing console clicked off. The broadcast was out in the wild, bleeding across the Virginia state lines, carried by invisible radio waves into the freezing mountain air.

Gabriel let go of the heavy studio microphone. His hands were shaking, the adrenaline that had fueled his frantic drive to the station suddenly crashing into a hollow, agonizing void. He looked up at Mr. Harrison, whose face was a mask of grim solidarity.

"Use the desk line," Mr. Harrison offered quietly, pushing a heavy, multi-button phone across the console. "Call your mother. Let her know the beacon is lit."

Gabriel snatched the receiver and dialed the familiar number with stiff, clumsy fingers. It rang exactly half a time before the line clicked open.

"Gabriel?" Eira’s voice was breathless, frantic, and strung tight with a terrifying anticipation.

"It's me, Mom," Gabriel said, leaning his heavy frame against the edge of the mixing board. "We did it. Mr. Harrison pushed it to the repeaters. It's playing right now across the whole grid. If Tom has the radio on, Julian will hear the music box."

"Gabriel, hold on," Eira gasped, the sound of a sharp, electronic beep echoing over the line. "That's call waiting. It's the county precinct. Don't hang up!"

There was a sharp click, followed by the agonizing, hollow static of being placed on hold.

Gabriel stopped breathing. He gripped the plastic receiver so tightly the casing groaned. Marcus stepped closer, his eyes wide, reading the sudden, absolute stillness in his friend's posture. Across the room, Mr. Harrison paused his work, the entire control room holding its breath.

They found him, Gabriel thought, a massive, desperate spark of hope igniting in his chest. The highway patrol found the car. It’s over.

Two full minutes passed. It felt like an entire lifetime.

Then, the line clicked back over.

It wasn't a sigh of relief. It was a broken, devastated, guttural sob.

"Mom?" Gabriel demanded, the hope in his chest instantly turning to ice. "Mom, what did they say? Did they find the car?"

"They had him," Eira wept, her voice collapsing into absolute hysterics. "Gabriel, they were looking right at him, and they let him drive away!"

"What are you talking about?!" Gabriel roared, his frontline protector instincts violently surging. "How do you let a kidnapper drive away?!"

"He crossed the state line," Eira choked out, struggling to pull air into her lungs. "A state trooper pulled Tom over in a town called Lost City, out in West Virginia. He was doing eighty-five in a fifty-five zone. The trooper walked right up to the window. He saw Julian in the back seat."

Gabriel’s vision swam. A police officer had been standing inches away from his brother.

"Julian is non-verbal!" Gabriel yelled into the receiver, his voice cracking with sheer, unadulterated rage. "He was probably spasming! He had to be terrified! How did the cop not see that he was in danger?!"

"Because Tom lied!" Eira screamed, her heartbreak echoing through the sterile broadcast booth. "Tom told the trooper they were just on a father-son road trip, and that Julian was just having a bad day with his disability! And because it's a different state... the trooper ran the license plates, Gabriel. He ran the plates, and nothing came up."

The geographical and bureaucratic reality hit Gabriel with the force of a physical blow.

"The paperwork," Gabriel whispered, the blood draining completely from his face.

"The clerk in James City County hasn't pushed the custody breach to the national database yet," Eira confirmed, sobbing uncontrollably. "To that state trooper, the car wasn't flagged in the NCIC system. There was no federal BOLO. It was just a speeding ticket. He wrote Tom a citation, and he let him merge back onto the highway."

Gabriel stared blankly at the acoustic foam on the studio wall. The system had failed. The absolute, sluggish incompetence of the bureaucracy had given his father a free pass. Tom was in West Virginia, burying Julian deeper into the desolate, freezing interior of the Appalachian mountains.

"We are entirely on our own," Gabriel rasped, his voice dropping into a terrifyingly cold, hollow register.

"Gabriel, what do we do?" Eira begged, completely shattered.

"You aren't on your own," Detective Miller interrupted, stepping forward. His expression hardened into absolute, uncompromising authority. "The moment that citation hit the regional dispatch, it triggered the interstate net. Your father crossed a state line with a child he has absolutely no legal right to possess. This is no longer a local civil dispute. It is a federal kidnapping."

Gwendolyn’s breath hitched. The tactical shift was monumental.

"The FBI has officially been notified, and we have pushed a Critical Missing Child Bulletin to the NCIC database," the detective continued, laying out the invisible arsenal they were finally deploying. "Every highway patrol unit, every weigh station, and every toll booth from here to the Midwest has his license plate on their dashboard computers. If a single cop runs those tags, it’s going to light up like a Christmas tree. And because Julian is without his manual wheelchair and his required medication, he is officially classified as critically endangered."

Gabriel looked over at the mixing console, where the audio meters for Upon This Winter's Night were still bouncing in the red, projecting their desperate, symphonic war cry across the mountains.

"We pray he leaves the radio on," Gabriel said, his jaw setting into uncompromising stone. "I'm coming home, Mom."

He slammed the receiver down. The beacon was the only weapon they had left.
