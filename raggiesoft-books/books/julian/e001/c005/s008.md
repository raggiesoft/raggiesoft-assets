The radio clipped to Trooper Vance’s shoulder crackled, breaking the heavy, freezing silence of the US-33 barricade.

"Unit 42, this is Command. We have negative contact on the target vehicle at the Hocking Hills checkpoint. I repeat, No Joy on US-33. He’s a ghost."

Vance slammed his hand against the roof of his cruiser, a sharp curse escaping his lips. Tom Cross hadn’t made it to the trap. Somehow, either by sheer, blind luck or unhinged paranoia, the blue sedan had vanished into the capillary network of the Ohio backroads before the net could close.

"Command, expand the grid," Vance barked into his mic. "Pull units off the interstate. He took a secondary route. Sweep everything northbound."

Five miles to the east, Tom was entirely unaware of the massive tactical pivot happening behind him. The dark blue sedan was tearing up the center line of State Route 13, pushing sixty miles an hour through the pitch-black, winding curves of the forest.

In the back seat, Julian was clinging desperately to the edge of the vinyl upholstery. The violent, swaying motion of the car was tearing through his unmedicated muscles. He was exhausted, battered, and trapped in a terrifying, sensory nightmare.

Up ahead, the dense tree line briefly broke, giving way to the small, sleepy village limits of Jacksonville, Ohio.

Sitting in the pitch-black gravel pull-off near the edge of town, effectively invisible with her headlights cut, was a local Jacksonville police officer. She had parked there an hour ago, running a standard, boring Friday night speed trap to catch locals blowing through the village limits.

When Tom’s sedan roared past her cruiser, doing twenty over the posted rural limit, the officer didn't hesitate. She threw the cruiser into drive, flicked on her headlights, and pulled out onto SR-13 to initiate a routine traffic stop.

She picked up her radio. "Dispatch, I’m in pursuit of a speeding dark blue sedan, late model, northbound on 13 out of Jacksonville. Let me get you a plate..."

As her headlights washed over the salt-crusted trunk of Tom's car, the numbers illuminated. She read them off.

A heavy, breathless second of dead air followed.

"Unit, hold your position!" the county dispatcher's voice exploded over the radio, completely shedding all protocol. "Do not initiate a standard stop! That plate is a direct match for the federal BOLO out of Virginia! You have the critical missing child! Highway Patrol is vectoring to your location right now!"

The local officer’s heart hammered against her ribs. She slammed her hand down on the center console, activating her overhead lightbar.

A violent explosion of red and blue strobe lights suddenly flooded the dark Ohio highway, reflecting blindingly in Tom’s rearview mirror.

"No!" Tom screamed, the sound tearing from his throat in a raw, primal panic. "No, no, no!"

In the back seat, Julian violently flinched. The sudden, strobing lights pierced through the dark cabin. But Julian didn't cry out in fear. A massive, overwhelming surge of adrenaline spiked in his chest. They found us.

Tom didn't hit the brakes. He slammed his foot on the accelerator.

The engine roared, the heavy sedan surging forward to seventy miles an hour on the narrow, dangerous blacktop. The pursuit was officially on.

"Suspect is running!" the Jacksonville officer yelled into her radio, keeping pace as the blue sedan tore blindly toward the next town. "We are northbound on 13, entering Glouster limits!"

Tom was driving with absolute, reckless desperation. His eyes were wide, his breathing shallow and frantic. The quiet, desolate backroad he had used to hide was now a strobing, chaotic tunnel. He pushed the car harder, desperate to lose the flashing lights in the rearview mirror.

But as they tore into the tight, condensed grid of Glouster, Ohio, Tom realized too late that the highway patrol hadn't just been chasing him from behind. They had regrouped ahead of him.

Tom crested a slight rise, the headlights illuminating the steel beams of the Sunday Creek Bridge.

"He's at the bridge!" a trooper's voice commanded over the county radio frequency. "Deploy strips!"

Tom saw the dark silhouettes of two Highway Patrol cruisers blocking the opposite lane. He saw the trooper standing behind the open door. And then, he saw the thick, black ribbon of the hollow-spike Stinger system sliding rapidly across the asphalt directly in his path.

Tom ripped the steering wheel hard to the left to avoid them, but he was moving too fast.

POP-POP-POP!

The right-side tires completely blew out, the rubber shredding instantly against the hollow steel spikes. The sedan dropped violently to the right, the bare metal rims grinding against the pavement in a shower of brilliant orange sparks.

Tom completely lost control of the heavy vehicle.

He shot across the Sunday Creek Bridge, the steering column vibrating violently in his hands. He pumped the brakes, but the car was hydroplaning on its own shredded rubber.

Right at the end of the bridge, State Route 13 and Route 78 converged, forcing a sharp, unforgiving 90-degree right turn from High Street onto Main Street.

Tom couldn't make the turn.

"Brace!" Tom screamed, throwing his arms up over his face.

In the back seat, Julian was thrown helplessly forward as the heavy blue sedan jumped the curb.

With a deafening, metallic crunch that echoed off the surrounding brick buildings, the car slammed head-on into the faded green, painted concrete retaining wall sitting just off the intersection of West Main Street.

The impact was catastrophic. The front bumper crumpled like tin foil, the radiator hissing violently as a cloud of white steam erupted into the freezing night air. The driver's side airbag detonated with a loud, percussive BANG, filling the cabin with a thick, choking cloud of white powder and the sharp smell of burnt chemical propellant.

Then, there was only a heavy, ringing silence, broken by the mechanical ticking of the destroyed engine block.

Julian lay crumpled on the floorboards of the back seat. His head was throbbing, his shoulder burning with a sharp, bruising pain from where he had slammed against the rear passenger door. He coughed, the airbag dust burning his throat. But as he forced his eyes open, he realized he was alive. He wasn't critically injured.

In the front seat, Tom was not as lucky. The steering column had collapsed inward during the impact. Tom was pinned heavily against the crushed dashboard, his breathing shallow and rattling. He was unconscious, his face pale and bleeding from the airbag deployment.

The world outside the shattered windows suddenly exploded into motion.

A dozen state troopers and local officers swarmed the wrecked sedan, their flashlights cutting sharply through the steam and dust.

"Show me your hands!" a trooper bellowed, his weapon drawn as he approached the driver's side window. He shined the light inside, immediately seeing the pinned, unconscious driver. "Suspect is down! I need EMS, heavy rescue, and the jaws right now! Driver is pinned!"

Around the back of the car, Trooper Vance rushed to the shattered rear window. He shined his heavy Maglite into the dark, smoky back seat.

The beam of light illuminated Julian, curled defensively on the floorboards, shivering violently in his thin shirt.

"I've got him!" Vance yelled, his voice cracking with a fierce, absolute relief. "I've got the boy! He's alive!"

Vance didn't wait for the paramedics. He wrenched the rear passenger door open, the crushed metal groaning in protest before finally popping free. The freezing Ohio air rushed into the cabin, sweeping away the chemical smoke.

"Julian," Vance said, his voice dropping all of its tactical harshness, becoming instantly calm and grounding. He didn't reach in to grab him right away; he knew the boy was terrified. "My name is Officer Vance. You are safe now. Your dad cannot hurt you anymore. We're going to get you out of here, okay?"

Julian looked up at the trooper, his wide, tear-streaked eyes reflecting the red and blue emergency lights. He let out a breathless, exhausted vocalization, his rigid muscles finally, completely giving out.

"Pop the trunk!" Vance ordered the other troopers swarming the back of the car. "The BOLO said he needs a manual wheelchair. Find it!"

Two troopers pried the crumpled trunk lid open. Sitting right there in the dark, folded and completely undamaged, was Julian's chair.

As Vance reached in to carefully lift Julian out of the wreckage, the wail of approaching ambulances filled the Glouster intersection. The nightmare on the dark road was officially over. The beacon had worked.

Julian was going home.
