The battery-powered radio was still buzzing with low static when the kitchen wall phone finally screamed.

It wasn't a gentle ring. In the suffocating, funereal quiet of the house, the harsh mechanical bell sounded like a fire alarm.

Gabriel moved before his brain consciously registered the sound. He scrambled up from the carpet, his heavy boots slipping on the hardwood as he lunged into the kitchen. He snatched the plastic receiver off the cradle, his chest heaving.

"Hello?" Gabriel gasped.

"Gabriel, it's Detective Miller." The investigator's voice was stripped of its previous bureaucratic detachment. It was tight, moving fast. "Put your mother on the line, son. We found him."

Gabriel felt the floor drop completely out from under him. The air rushed out of his lungs in a single, violent exhale. He looked through the archway. Eira was already standing, trembling so violently Gwendolyn had to support her weight.

"Is he alive?" Gabriel demanded, his voice dropping to a terrified, dangerous whisper. He couldn't hand the phone over until he knew. "Miller, is my brother alive?"

"He's alive," Miller confirmed immediately. "He's safe, Gabriel. He's in Ohio."

Gabriel closed his eyes, a broken, tearing sound escaping his throat. Ohio. The sheer distance was staggering, but it didn't matter. He was alive.

"Mom," Gabriel choked out, holding the receiver out to her. "They got him."

Eira let out a devastating, guttural cry of relief. She practically collapsed against the kitchen counter as she took the phone. Gabriel kept his head close, pressing his ear near the receiver so he could hear the detective. Gwendolyn crowded in on Eira's other side, her sharp eyes locked on her mother's face.

"Mrs. Cross," Miller spoke clearly. "The Ohio State Highway Patrol intercepted the vehicle in a town called Glouster. There was a high-speed pursuit. Tom lost control and crashed the sedan into a retaining wall."

Eira gasped, her free hand flying to her mouth.

"Tom is severely injured," Miller continued, his tone clinical and completely devoid of sympathy for the man. "He's pinned and unconscious. But Julian was in the back seat. The troopers pulled him out. He's battered and he's terrified, but the paramedics on the scene stated his vitals are stable. He survived the crash."

"Where is he?" Eira pleaded, tears streaming down her face. "I want to talk to him."

"He's currently in an ambulance en route to the nearest regional hospital for a full trauma evaluation," Miller explained. "And Mrs. Cross... you can't speak to him right now. The local authorities told me he is completely inconsolable. They said he's screaming. They are having a very difficult time treating him."

Gwendolyn’s breath hitched. Her tactical mind, which had been frozen for hours, instantly snapped back online.

"Of course he's screaming," Gwendolyn said, her voice trembling with absolute, desperate clarity. "He's in an ambulance. Sirens, flashing lights, strangers touching him. It’s a sensory nightmare."

Gwendolyn looked at Gabriel, her eyes wide and panicked. She knew exactly what was happening inside Julian's autistic mind. He had been ripped from his routine, frozen in a car for twelve hours, and then subjected to a violent crash. His nervous system wasn't just overloaded; it was completely shattered.

"He doesn't understand that the paramedics are the good guys," Gwendolyn said, her voice rising in frantic urgency. "He just knows that it's loud, and it hurts, and he wants his room. Gabriel, he's crying because he needs to be squeezed. He needs his deep-pressure cuddle, and nobody there knows how to do it."

Footsteps rushed down the hardwood hallway. Clara and Maria practically collided with the kitchen archway, their identical faces pale and stricken. They had been in Julian's bedroom, preparing to sleep under his weighted blanket to keep his sanctuary safe. But the moment they heard Gwendolyn outline the horrifying reality of his sensory meltdown, the plan instantly shattered.

A cold, empty bed wasn't enough anymore. Julian was terrified, and he desperately needed his anchors.

"We are not staying here," Clara stated, her flawless pitch wavering with raw, bleeding emotion, but backed by absolute, unyielding certainty.

"We're going to Ohio," Maria agreed fiercely, stepping right up to Gabriel. She wasn't asking for permission. "He needs us. If they try to restrain him, his muscles will lock up. He needs us there to hold him."

Gabriel felt a fresh wave of agony hit his chest, but looking at his sister and the identical twins, he knew they were entirely right. When the world became too loud, Gwendolyn, Clara, and Maria were the only ones who could wrap their arms securely around Julian and apply the exact, symmetrical pressure needed to ground his terrified nervous system.

"Detective," Gabriel said, leaning over his mother to speak fiercely into the receiver. "Tell the hospital staff to back off. Tell them to turn the harsh lights down in his room and stop touching him unless it is a life-saving medical necessity. If they try to force him onto a stretcher, his muscles are going to lock up and he's going to get hurt."

"I'll relay the protocol to the medical staff and the social workers," Miller promised. "But Gabriel, listen to me. This is an interstate federal kidnapping. Because Tom is legally his father, and Julian is a minor recovered across state lines, the local child welfare agency in Ohio is required to take temporary emergency custody of Julian the moment he's medically cleared. They cannot legally release him to anyone until the primary guardian physically walks into that hospital with photo identification and the original custody paperwork."

The bureaucratic reality was a massive, immovable wall.

"How far is Glouster, Ohio?" Gabriel asked, turning his fierce, uncompromising gaze to Gwendolyn.

Gwendolyn didn't even need to look at the scattered road maps on the dining table. "It's an eight-hour drive through the mountains. Minimum."

Eight hours. Julian would be trapped in a sterile, terrifying hospital room for eight more hours, surrounded by strangers, utterly unable to communicate his needs.

"We are leaving right now," Gabriel stated, his frontline protector instincts returning with absolute, singular focus. He stepped away from the phone, pointing at Marcus and Richard in the living room. "Marcus, figure out the fastest route to Athens County."

Gabriel spun back to the Harrison twins. "Clara, Maria. Run to his room. Grab his heavy winter coat, his weighted blanket, and every sensory tool you can carry."

"Gabriel," Eira said, hanging up the phone. Her hands were still shaking, but her posture was straightening with a fierce, maternal resolve. "I have to get the legal files from the lockbox."

"Do it," Gabriel said, snatching his car keys off the counter. He looked around the room. Gwendolyn was already pulling on her heavy winter boots, her face a mask of fierce determination. Maria and Clara were sprinting down the hall to raid Julian's room.

They were going to Ohio.
