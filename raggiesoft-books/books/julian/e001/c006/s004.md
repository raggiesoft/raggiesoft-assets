"Legal custody is fully returned to you, Mrs. Cross," Brenda said, offering a warm, relieved smile as she handed over the clipboard for a final signature.

Eira took the pen, but she didn't sign it immediately. She looked down at the printed name on the hospital release form, her jaw setting into a hard, uncompromising line.

"It's Ms. Cross," Eira corrected quietly, though her voice carried an undeniable edge of absolute finality. "The divorce is finalized. And as soon as the paperwork clears the county clerk back home, it won't be Cross at all. I am legally returning to my maiden name. It's Rowlands."

Gabriel, who had been halfway to the door to retrieve Julian's belongings, froze. He turned back around, looking at his mother.

Rowlands. It sounded like a lifeline. The name Cross felt like a heavy, suffocating chain—a permanent, unavoidable tether to the man bleeding out in a wrecked car down the highway. For the last twenty-four hours, Gabriel had violently rejected everything his father stood for. He had physically placed himself as a shield against the man's legacy.

He looked at Gwendolyn. She was staring at their mother, her sharp, tactical eyes softening as a profound realization hit her. Gwendolyn gave Gabriel a single, definitive nod. They were entirely on the same wavelength.

"Mom," Gabriel said, stepping back into the center of the sterile trauma room. His voice was remarkably steady, stripped of all the frantic terror of the night. "When we get back to Virginia... I don't want his name anymore, either. I want to be Gabriel Rowlands."

Gwendolyn stepped up right beside him, resting her hand lightly on Julian’s shoulder. "Me too. I'm done carrying it. He lost the right to claim us the second he took Julian."

Eira looked at her two oldest children, her eyes welling with fresh tears. The absolute, unshakeable solidarity of her kids was a force of nature. "You're both eighteen," she whispered, her voice thick with emotion. "You're legally adults. You don't need my permission or his. As soon as we get back to James City County, you can walk right into the Circuit Court, file a petition for a name change, and pay the filing fee. That's it. It’s entirely your choice."

Before Gabriel could reply, a sharp, digitized BEEP echoed from the AAC device resting on Julian’s lap.

Julian’s hands were shaking from exhaustion, but his fingers were moving across the plastic keyboard with fierce, deliberate intent. He didn't want to be left out of the perimeter. He didn't want to carry the monster's name while his brother, his sister, and his mother all moved on without it.

He hit the speaker button.

<aac>I WANT MOMMY'S BIRTH NAME TOO.</aac>

The robotic voice filled the room, carrying the absolute, uncompromising will of a fourteen-year-old boy who had just survived a nightmare.

Brenda, the social worker, looked at Julian with deep empathy, but the bureaucratic reality of her job immediately kicked in.

"Julian," Brenda explained gently, stepping forward. "Because you are a minor, you can't petition the court yourself. Your mom has to file it on your behalf. And usually, Virginia law requires the court to notify your biological father so he can consent or object to changing your last name."

Julian’s breath hitched, a sharp spike of panic hitting his chest. He looked frantically at Gwendolyn, his muscles threatening to lock up again at the mere mention of Tom having any control over him.

"Hey, look at me," Gabriel commanded, stepping directly into Julian's line of sight, instantly throwing the shield back up. "He has no power here. None."

Eira placed a reassuring hand on Brenda's arm, but her eyes were locked onto her youngest son.

"Brenda is right about the standard procedure, Julep," Eira said, her voice a calm, unbreakable bedrock. "But standard procedure doesn't apply to kidnappers. Your father’s parental rights were already severely restricted, and after last night, he is facing federal prison. When I file the petition for you in the Circuit Court, the judge will look at the fact that he abducted you, endangered your life, and completely abandoned his duty of care."

Eira smiled, a fierce, protective warmth radiating from her. "No judge in Virginia is going to let him stop us. We will ask the court to waive his consent because it is entirely in your best interest. You are going to be Julian Rowlands."

Julian’s tense shoulders dropped. The panic evaporated. He looked down at his keyboard, a small, exhausted, but incredibly genuine smile touching his lips. He tapped two keys.

<aac>ROWLANDS. YES.</aac>

"Alright, Rowlands family," Gabriel said, the heavy, crushing weight of the last day finally lifting from his chest. He clapped his hands together once, the sound echoing sharply in the trauma room. "Let's get out of this hospital. I'm going to get the car."

Gabriel turned on his heel and sprinted through the double doors, leaving the name Cross completely behind him in the dust.
