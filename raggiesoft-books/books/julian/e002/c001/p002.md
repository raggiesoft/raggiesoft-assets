## 7:00 PM • Richard Harrison — The Blended Table

The smell of sizzling butter and rich, simmering tomatoes completely transformed the atmosphere of the house in Ewell. It pushed out the lingering, sterile chill of the Ohio hospital and replaced it with the heavy, undeniable warmth of home.

Richard Harrison emerged from the kitchen carrying a large, wooden serving tray loaded with golden-brown grilled cheese sandwiches and steaming ceramic mugs of thick tomato soup. He didn't serve it with the polished grace of a guest trying to be polite; he distributed the food with the practiced, effortless rhythm of a father tending to his flock.

He set a mug and a perfectly sliced sandwich directly in front of Julian. It wasn't the squeaky cheese curds and heavy gravy of authentic poutine from their family up in Stanstead, Québec, but as the steam curled into the air, the disappointment on Julian's face completely vanished. He was exhausted, battered, and starving. The soup was hot, it was safe, and it was made by someone he trusted implicitly.

Julian picked up half of the sandwich with trembling but determined hands. He took a bite, letting out a long, highly vocal sigh of absolute contentment. He leaned his right shoulder heavily against Maria, who was sitting right beside him, perfectly content to eat her own dinner completely pinned to his side.

The six of them filled the dining table, forming a tight, impenetrable perimeter. Julian was securely flanked by Maria and Gwendolyn. Gabriel and Richard took the outer edges, facing the front window, while Eira sat at the head of the table. They didn't look like a fractured family taking in a teacher and his daughter; they looked like a single, unified, blended front.

"<lang="cy" trans="Eat up, love,">Bwyta i fyny, cariad,</lang>" Eira said softly, her voice carrying a profound, exhausted warmth. She reached out and gently smoothed down a stray piece of Julian's hair. "<lang="cy" trans="You have a lot of energy to get back.">Mae gen ti lawer o egni i'w adennill.</lang>"

Julian looked at his mother and offered a small, exhausted smile before taking a sip of the warm soup.

As the immediate edge of their hunger began to blunt, Gwendolyn’s tactical mind—which had been running at a redline for three days—began scanning the horizon for the next threat. She looked at her mother, shifting seamlessly into Québécois French.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Mom, we need to talk about the journalists,">Maman, il faut parler des journalistes,</lang>" Gwendolyn stated, her sharp eyes flicking toward the frosted front window. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="The missing child bulletin made the regional news. And Gabriel's radio hijack caught the attention of the whole coast. They are going to find our address eventually.">L'alerte de disparition a fait les nouvelles régionales. Et le piratage de la radio par Gabriel a attiré l'attention de toute la côte. Ils vont finir par trouver notre adresse.</lang>"

Gabriel stopped mid-bite, his jaw setting into a hard line. He set his sandwich down, the frontline protector instantly waking back up.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="They won't come near this house,">Ils ne s'approcheront pas de cette maison,</lang>" Gabriel rumbled in flawless French, his deep baritone carrying absolute authority. "If a single news van pulls onto this street, I will personally walk out there and dismantle their cameras."

"Gabriel is right, but we need a buffer before it comes to that," Richard interjected in English, seamlessly weaving into the trilingual flow. He looked at Eira. "The radio station switchboard is already fielding calls from local newspapers asking for the identity of The Winter Palace. I told the station manager to institute a strict media blackout. We do not confirm your names, and we do not confirm the address."

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="That is the right strategy,">C'est la bonne stratégie,</lang>" Eira agreed, her posture straightening. "Tom is dead. The FBI closed the interstate kidnapping file this morning in Ohio. The authorities are entirely out of our lives. The only loose end left is the Circuit Court on Monday morning to legally change our names back to Rowlands."

Eira looked around the table, her eyes locking onto Richard and Maria, and then onto her three children.

"We are not giving interviews," Eira declared, switching to English so the boundary was absolute and perfectly understood by everyone at the table. "We are not selling our trauma to the five o'clock news. Julian's comfort and his safety come before absolutely everything else. If the phone rings and it's a number we don't recognize, we let it ring. If anyone knocks on that door looking for a headline, we do not answer."

Julian, who had been quietly eating his soup, paused. He reached over with his left hand and tapped a few keys on his AAC device.

<aac>NO REPORTERS. I JUST WANT TO SLEEP.</aac>

"You heard the boss," Gabriel said, a fierce, protective grin breaking through his exhaustion. He reached across the table and lightly tapped his knuckles against Julian's arm. "No reporters. Just the six of us."

Julian leaned his head against Maria's shoulder again, closing his eyes as he chewed his food. The heavy, dark shadow of Thomas Cross was gone. The chaotic noise of the police sirens and the hospital monitors had been silenced.

Here, at a crowded dining table in Ewell, surrounded by three languages, grilled cheese sandwiches, and an unbreakable garrison of protectors, Julian was finally, truly home.

## 9:00 PM • Julian Rowlands — The Open Doors

By nine o'clock, the exhaustion in the Ewell house had shifted from a dull ache to a heavy, gravitational pull. The adrenaline was completely gone, leaving behind the quiet, practical necessities of the bedtime routine.

For Julian, the physical toll of the abduction was severe. Sitting completely rigid in the freezing back seat of a sedan and bracing against the violent impact of a car crash had left his unmedicated muscles stiff, bruised, and aching.

He needed a hot bath to loosen the spasticity, and he needed help with toileting.

Gwendolyn didn't hesitate. While Gabriel began locking the deadbolts on the exterior doors, Gwendolyn stepped into the bathroom to start the warm water. She was the only person in the house with the specific core body strength and the flawless, practiced mechanical leverage required to safely lift a fourteen-year-old boy with cerebral palsy.

She worked with quiet, absolute dignity, murmuring softly in Québécois French to keep his sensory inputs calm as the hot water finally began to melt the agonizing tension out of his shoulders and legs.

While Gwendolyn managed the heavy lifting of the bath routine, Maria slipped into the second bathroom down the hall to take a quick shower, giving the siblings their privacy.

When the water was drained, Gwendolyn carefully lifted Julian and dried him off, helping him step-by-step into his softest, warmest winter pajamas. By the time she wheeled him out into his bedroom, Maria was emerging from the hallway, dressed in her own oversized flannel pajamas, her damp hair resting against her shoulders.

At the other end of the house, a different, quieter battle was taking place.

Eira stood in the hallway, staring into the dark, empty void of the master bedroom. The heavy king-sized bed dominated the space. It was the bed she had once shared with Tom. Even though the monster was dead and gone, his ghost was still suffocating the room. She felt her chest tighten, completely repulsed by the idea of crossing the threshold, let alone sleeping on that mattress.

"Don't go in there, Eira," Richard said quietly, stepping up behind her. He didn't push past her, but his presence was a solid, undeniable anchor.

"I can't," Eira whispered, her voice cracking. "I can't sleep in that room."

"Then you never have to," Richard promised, his voice carrying the calm, absolute authority of a man who was fully committed to clearing the wreckage. "First thing tomorrow morning, Gabriel and I will tear that bed frame apart. We will drag the mattress, the sheets, and every piece of furniture he ever touched straight to the curb. But tonight, you are sleeping in the living room."

Richard stepped away and moved to the floral sofa, expertly pulling the heavy cushions off and unfolding the hidden queen-sized mattress.

Across the hall, Gabriel and Gwendolyn were preparing for bed in their own shared room. They had a single queen-sized bed, and they didn't sleep in separate spaces. It had been their routine for years. Growing up in a volatile house with a dangerously unpredictable father meant they had learned to consolidate their perimeter early on. They slept huddled close, an ingrained trauma response that ensured if Tom ever came stomping down the hallway, the twins were already standing back-to-back, ready to defend themselves and Julian.

Tonight, the perimeter rules were strictly enforced. As everyone finally settled into bed, not a single interior door was closed.

Gabriel wedged his and Gwendolyn’s bedroom door wide open with a heavy textbook. Down the hall, Julian’s door remained pushed flat against the drywall. There were absolutely zero obstructed sightlines. If anyone moved, whispered, or cried out in the middle of the night, the entire garrison would be awake in seconds.

In Julian’s room, the glow of the hallway light spilled across the carpet. Julian was lying in the center of his queen-sized bed, wrapped tightly in his weighted blanket. Maria was curled up directly beside him, her arm draped heavily over his chest, her head resting on his pillow. This was their established routine—a profound, innocent, chosen-family bond built on years of absolute trust.

"Okay, Julep," Gwendolyn whispered, standing in the doorway. She reached out and flipped the wall switch.

The overhead light clicked off.

The sudden, absolute darkness hit Julian like a physical blow.

Instantly, his brain betrayed him. The safe, warm bedroom vanished. In the pitch black, he felt the phantom, terrifying sway of the stolen blue sedan. He felt the freezing West Virginia wind blowing through the broken heater. He was back on the highway, isolated, trapped, and entirely voiceless.

A sharp, panicked whine tore from Julian's throat. His body went completely rigid, his breathing hitching into a frantic, shallow hyperventilation. He squeezed his eyes shut, his hands balling into fists as the night terrors threatened to pull him entirely under.

"Julian," Maria whispered.

She didn't turn the light back on. She didn't scramble away to grab his AAC device.

Instead, Maria shifted closer in the dark. She threw her leg over his, anchoring his lower half, and wrapped both of her arms securely around his chest. She pulled him flush against her, applying a massive, unyielding deep-pressure cuddle.

"I've got you," Maria murmured directly into his ear, her voice steady and warm. "He's gone, Julian. You're in your bed. I'm right here, and I'm not going anywhere."

Julian gasped, the rigid tension in his chest fighting against her hold. But Maria didn't loosen her grip. She held him tightly, her own steady heartbeat pressing against his side, forcing his shattered nervous system to synchronize with hers.

Slowly, agonizingly, the phantom sway of the car began to fade. The smell of the hospital and the screeching tires receded, replaced by the scent of clean laundry and Maria's damp hair.

Julian let out a long, ragged exhale, his fists unclenching. He buried his face against Maria's shoulder, a few silent tears soaking into her flannel pajama top.

It was going to be a very long, difficult night. The echoes of the abduction would take a long time to fully fade. But as Maria held him in the dark, and the steady, protective breathing of his family echoed through the open doors down the hall, Julian knew the most important truth.

He was safe. He was home. And his best friend had him.
