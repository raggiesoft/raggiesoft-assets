The living room of the house in Ewell had been completely reconfigured. The armchairs were pushed back, and the heavy coffee table was shifted to the side, opening up the sightlines across the entire first floor.

Nobody was willing to let Julian out of their direct line of sight. The terrifying, agonizing hours he had spent trapped in the freezing Appalachian mountains had fundamentally altered the family's perimeter; the physical walls of the house weren't enough anymore. They needed to see him breathing.

Julian was stationed safely in the dead center of the floral sofa. He was buried under the heavy, comforting weight of his sensory blanket, his muscles completely relaxed in the warmth of the radiator. Tucked securely against his right side, sharing the warmth of the heavy blanket, was Maria.

She had her glockenspiel case resting on the floor nearby, but right now, her only focus was her best friend. Since they were seven years old, Maria had existed in a space entirely her own within Julian’s heavily guarded inner circle. She didn't just understand his non-verbal cues; she anticipated them. She was the only person outside of his immediate family who had earned the absolute trust of both Eira and Mr. Harrison to sleep in the same bed as Julian. When the night terrors or sensory overloads hit, her quiet, steady presence and deep-pressure holds were as vital as his medication.

A few feet away at the heavy wooden dining table, Eira sat surrounded by a sprawling spread of legal documents. Mr. Harrison sat across from her, reading glasses perched on his nose, methodically helping her sort through the bureaucratic red tape of completely erasing Thomas Cross from their lives.

"<lang="cy" trans="The forms are all here, Richard,">Mae'r ffurflenni i gyd yma, Richard,</lang>" Eira said, her voice dropping into the fluid, rhythmic cadence of Welsh. She tapped a pen against the top page. "<lang="cy" trans="I will file on Monday to return to Rowlands, and to change Julian's name. Tom is dead. There is nothing to stop me now.">Fe wna i ffeilio ddydd Llun i ddychwelyd i Rowlands, ac i newid enw Julian. Mae Tom wedi marw. Does dim byd i'm rhwystro i nawr.</lang>"

"<lang="cy" trans="It's about time, Eira,">Roedd hi'n hen bryd, Eira,</lang>" Mr. Harrison replied flawlessly in the same language, handing her a highlighted affidavit.

The fact that the music therapy teacher and his daughter spoke Welsh and Québécois French so perfectly was a testament to the profound, chosen-family bond they shared. When Maria and Julian became inseparable at age seven, the Harrisons hadn't just welcomed Julian; they had fully embraced the family's heritage, eagerly learning the languages.

It was a stark, bitter contrast to Tom. Tom had violently refused to learn a single word of Welsh or French, completely rejecting the cultural bedrock of Eira's family and the extended relatives in Stanstead, Québec. His stubborn, xenophobic refusal had been one of the first, heaviest wedges that ultimately shattered their marriage.

Gabriel leaned against the archway of the kitchen, nursing a mug of coffee. He hadn't taken his eyes off the sofa, but he was tracking the conversation at the table perfectly.

"<lang="cy" trans="We are going with you on Monday, Mom,">Rydyn ni'n mynd gyda chi ddydd Llun, Mam,</lang>" Gabriel rumbled in Welsh, his deep voice carrying a fierce, uncompromising loyalty. He looked at his twin sister, who was sitting cross-legged on the carpet near the sofa, sorting through their chaotic studio notes. "<lang="cy" trans="We are dropping the name Cross too. We are all Rowlands now.">Rydyn ni'n gollwng yr enw Cross hefyd. Rydyn ni i gyd yn Rowlands nawr.</lang>"

Eira looked up from the paperwork, a fiercely proud, exhausted smile breaking across her face. "<lang="cy" trans="All of us,">I gyd ohonom,</lang>" she agreed softly.

Julian let out a happy, rhythmic vocalization from the sofa, his head resting comfortably against Maria's shoulder. He didn't need his AAC device to communicate his absolute approval of the plan.

Gabriel smiled, taking a sip of his coffee. The suffocating weight of the abduction was finally giving way to the massive, impossible future Vanguard Horizon had just laid at their feet. His mind started spinning with the logistics of the multi-million dollar recording contract.

"You know," Gabriel started, seamlessly switching back to Welsh as he looked at Gwendolyn and Maria. "<lang="cy" trans="We should record the song in Welsh for the album.">Dylen ni recordio'r gân yn Gymraeg i'r albwm.</lang>" "If Elias wants a massive, theatrical winter epic, a Welsh choir on the bridge would sound absolutely incredible."

Gwendolyn didn't miss a beat. Her tactical mind instantly expanded the perimeter of the idea, effortlessly pivoting the linguistic gears.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="We should record it in Welsh, yes,">On devrait l'enregistrer en gallois, oui,</lang>" Gwendolyn replied in crisp Québécois French, her eyes lighting up with brilliant, composer-level inspiration. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="But why not a French version too?">Mais pourquoi pas une version en français aussi?</lang>"

Gabriel’s eyes widened, the sheer scale of the concept hitting him all at once. An English release for the radio, a Welsh release honoring their mother's roots, and a Québécois French release for their family up in Stanstead, Québec.

"Yes," Gabriel agreed, switching back to English so the sheer enthusiasm could ring out clearly across the room. "That is a brilliant idea, Gwen. We don't just give them the English track. Let's share all three of our languages with the world."

On the sofa, Maria translated the rapid-fire idea to Julian with a gentle squeeze of his hand. Julian’s face lit up. He reached out from under the heavy blanket, aggressively tapping the armrest of the sofa in a drumbeat rhythm.

The dark road in West Virginia was entirely behind them. They were safe, they were together, and The Winter Palace was officially preparing to go global.
