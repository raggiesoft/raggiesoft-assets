By six o'clock, the frantic, mobilized energy of the afternoon had completely burned off, leaving behind the heavy, bone-deep exhaustion of a family that had spent the last three days running on pure adrenaline.

Outside, the freezing Virginia wind rattled the windowpanes, but inside the Ewell house, the atmosphere was incredibly subdued.

On the floral sofa, the reality of Julian’s trauma was manifesting in a quiet, desperate need for physical grounding. He was terrified. The logical part of his brain knew he was home, but his autistic nervous system was still completely shattered by the memory of the isolating, freezing back seat of Tom's car.

Julian had his arms wrapped tightly around Maria's waist, his face buried against her shoulder in a continuous, unyielding hold. He refused to let her go. He was functioning under the terrifying, subconscious logic that if he broke physical contact with his anchor, the perimeter would collapse and the nightmare would start all over again.

Maria sat perfectly still, her own arms wrapped securely around Julian’s back. She was only fourteen, but she possessed an endless, patient empathy. She hadn't complained once about her arm falling asleep or needing a break.

But Gwendolyn’s tactical eyes missed nothing. She knew Maria was exhausted.

Gwendolyn set her notebook down on the coffee table and moved quietly to the sofa. She didn't try to peel Julian's hands away—that would only trigger a panic response. Instead, she knelt directly in front of him, bringing herself into his line of sight.

" Julep, " Gwendolyn whispered softly, placing her hands firmly over his. "You're safe. But Maria needs to stretch her arms, okay? I'm tagging in."

Julian let out a sharp, distressed whine, his grip tightening defensively on Maria’s sweater.

"I've got you," Gwendolyn promised, projecting absolute, immovable authority. She leaned forward, sliding her arms around his chest the exact moment Maria gently exhaled and leaned back.

It was a flawless, synchronized anchor swap. Julian transferred his desperate grip entirely to his older sister, burying his face into Gwendolyn’s neck. Gwendolyn absorbed his weight effortlessly, applying the firm, deep-pressure cuddle he desperately needed.

Maria rubbed her tired shoulders, offering Gwendolyn a small, grateful smile. Julian reached out with one trembling hand, refusing to completely sever the connection, and grasped the fabric of Maria's sleeve. Maria simply covered his hand with hers, silently promising she wasn't going anywhere.

At the dining table, Eira watched the exchange, a profound, aching lump forming in her throat. She looked down at the legal paperwork, her vision blurring. She was the mother. She was supposed to fix this, but she was so incredibly tired she could barely keep her eyes open.

A sharp, digitized BEEP echoed from the sofa. Julian had managed to pull his AAC device onto his lap with his free hand.

<aac>HUNGRY.</aac> the robotic voice announced.

"Okay, buddy," Gabriel said, stepping out of the hallway and leaning against the archway. "What do you want? Name it, and I'll go pick it up."

Julian’s fingers moved across the keys.

<aac>POUTINE.</aac>

Eira closed her eyes, letting out a soft, heartbroken sigh. Poutine was the ultimate comfort food—the taste of their extended family in Stanstead, Québec. It was exactly what his nervous system was craving: warm, heavy, and familiar.

"Julian, sweetie," Eira said, her voice cracking with exhaustion as she looked at her youngest son. "I'm so sorry. We can't get poutine here. We can't get the proper squeaky cheese curds in the States, and regular melted cheese isn't the same. You know that."

Julian’s shoulders slumped. For a boy whose world had just been violently upended, the denial of a safe, familiar comfort food was a heavy blow. He looked down at his lap, a quiet, disappointed vocalization escaping his throat.

But then, he slowly typed on his board.

<aac>I UNDERSTAND. IT IS OKAY.</aac>

Eira covered her face with her hands, the sheer, sweet compliance of her traumatized son finally breaking her composure.

Richard Harrison immediately stood up from the dining table. He didn't offer empty platitudes, and he didn't ask Eira what she wanted to do. He simply stepped over to her chair, placing a warm, supportive hand on her shoulder.

"Let me take the kitchen, Eira," Richard said, his voice a low, steady rumble of domestic authority. "You are running on fumes. Sit here and rest."

"Richard, you've done enough," Eira protested weakly, though she made no move to stand. "You hijacked a radio station for us. You shouldn't have to cook."

"I'm not a guest, Eira," Richard countered gently, offering a small, reassuring smile. "I'm making grilled cheese and heavy tomato soup. It's not poutine, but it's warm, it's heavy, and it will ground him."

He turned and walked into the kitchen, immediately pulling cast iron pans from the cabinets with the quiet efficiency of a man completely at home.

Eira watched him work for a moment, listening to the comforting sound of butter sizzling in a hot pan. She turned her head back toward the living room. Julian was still clinging tightly to Gwendolyn, his eyes heavy with exhaustion, but his hand was still firmly gripping Maria’s sleeve. If Maria moved even an inch, Julian's breathing would hitch in panic.

Eira realized, with absolute clarity, that the perimeter had permanently expanded. Gabriel and Gwendolyn were the shields, but Richard and Maria were the mortar holding the bricks together.

"Richard," Eira called out softly, her voice carrying across the open floor plan.

Richard paused, looking over his shoulder from the stove. "Yes, Eira?"

"Don't pack your things tonight," Eira requested. It wasn't just an offer; it was a deeply vulnerable plea from a mother who needed reinforcements. She looked at Maria, who was gently tracing circles on the back of Julian's hand. "Julian is too scared. He needs her right now. And I need you. Please, stay with us for a while."

Richard didn't hesitate. He looked at Eira, recognizing the profound trust she was placing in him and his daughter.

"We aren't going anywhere, Eira," Richard promised quietly, turning back to the stove. "We'll stay as long as you need us."

On the sofa, Gabriel met Gwendolyn's eyes. The frontline was secure, the garrison was fully staffed, and for the first time in three days, the house in Ewell finally felt like a home again.
