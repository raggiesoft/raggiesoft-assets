The heavy wheelchair van idled in the visitor parking lot of the local high school.

Inside the warm cabin, Julian watched the front double doors of the main office through the tinted glass. His breathing was steady, anchored by the familiar weight of Maria leaning against his shoulder and the protective, physical presence of Gabriel sitting in the driver's seat.

Five minutes later, Eira pushed through the school doors, pulling her wool coat tightly against the bitter December wind. Richard Harrison walked right beside her, offering a satisfied, decisive nod as Gabriel slid the van door open for them.

"Done," Eira announced, climbing into the middle row and pulling the heavy door shut. She looked back at Julian, a fierce, triumphant light in her eyes. "I handed the registrar the court order directly. They are printing a new student ID, updating the permanent file, and changing the homeroom roster. As far as the public school system is concerned, Julian Cross no longer exists."

Julian let out a quiet, happy vocalization, tapping his fingers against the armrest of his chair.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="It's finished,">C'est fini,</lang>" Gwendolyn murmured softly in Québécois French, offering her brother a warm smile.

Gabriel shifted the van into gear, pulling out of the school lot and turning back onto the main stretch of US-60.

As they drove, a heavy Peninsula Regional Transport bus passed them in the opposite lane, its mechanical flipdot sign clack-clack-clacking as it switched to BLUE LINE.

Julian’s eyes locked onto the bus. He watched it until it disappeared in the side mirror. He pulled his AAC device onto his lap, his fingers hovering over the keys. The sheer, crushing weight of the last four days was suddenly overshadowed by a desperate, aching need for his old routine. He just wanted his life back.

<aac>I WANT TO RIDE THE BUS.</aac>

The digitized voice filled the quiet van.

Gabriel’s eyes darted to the rearview mirror. Eira turned around in her seat, her maternal instincts instantly flaring with a mix of profound empathy and absolute terror.

"Oh, Julep," Eira sighed, her voice aching. "I know you want things to go back to normal. But a public bus... it’s too exposed right now. There are too many strangers, and you can't control who gets on or off."

Julian’s shoulders slumped. His hands balled into loose fists on his lap. He knew she was right. If a stranger bumped into his chair, or if the bus got too loud, his unmedicated, traumatized nervous system would throw him straight into a violent panic attack. But the walls of the Ewell house, as safe as they were, were starting to feel like a different kind of trap.

He typed again, the robotic voice carrying his frustration.

<aac>I DO NOT WANT TO GO BACK TO THE HOUSE YET.</aac>

Maria shifted closer, wrapping both of her arms securely around his left arm. "We don't have to go home yet," she promised softly, looking up at the rearview mirror. "Right, Gabriel?"

Gabriel gripped the steering wheel. He understood exactly what his brother needed. Julian needed the sensation of moving forward, of watching the world pass by, but he needed it from inside an impenetrable fortress.

"We aren't going home," Gabriel declared, his deep baritone leaving zero room for argument. He hit his turn signal, violently changing lanes to bypass the turnoff for their neighborhood. "We're taking the scenic route."

"Where are we going?" Richard asked from the passenger seat, though he was already smiling.

"The Parkway," Gabriel answered, steering the heavy van away from the commercial sprawl of the highway. "We're going to take it all the way from Jamestown to Yorktown."
