Elias Thorne did not look like a man who belonged in a suburban Virginia living room. The A&R executive from Vanguard Horizon Music Group wore a sharp, tailored charcoal suit and possessed the calculating, hyper-observant gaze of an industry veteran who spent his life hunting for lightning in a bottle.

He sat in the center of the floral sofa, a ceramic mug of Eira’s coffee untouched on the table in front of him.

The rest of the room was packed. Gabriel stood by the heavy wooden console of the family stereo system. Sarah and Marcus leaned against the archway. On the opposite loveseat, Julian sat securely between Gwendolyn and Maria, his weighted blanket draped over his lap. Maria reached out, lightly linking her pinky finger with Julian’s—a quiet, grounding gesture they had shared since they were seven years old.

"The tape we broadcasted on Wednesday was raw," Gabriel explained to the executive, his hand resting on the stereo's play button. "It was recorded in one take while we were terrified. This is the second draft. We spent the last three days mixing it."

"Play it," Elias requested simply, leaning back and closing his eyes.

Gabriel pushed the button.

The living room was instantly swallowed by the heavy, ominous driving riff of Sarah’s electric cello. Two seconds later, Marcus’s concussive rock drum kit and Gabriel’s distorted rhythm guitar detonated through the speakers. It was a massive, aggressive wall of sound, establishing the dark, freezing reality of the winter night.

But as the track transitioned into the first verse, the aggressive symphonic rock abruptly pulled back, leaving a vast, open auditory space.

Gabriel’s deep baritone entered: "The clock upon the mantel chimes / Reminding me of better times..."

And then, cutting through the heavy, lingering bass, came Maria.

The pristine, metallic, delicate strikes of her glockenspiel overlaid the entire verse, perfectly mirroring the melody. It was a staggering, brilliant contrast. The heavy rock instruments represented the terrifying, desolate world outside, but the glockenspiel was the sound of Julian's inner circle. It was the sound of a fourteen-year-old girl violently refusing to let the dark swallow her best friend. It was pure, unapologetic innocence acting as a shield.

Julian’s eyes fluttered shut, a small, peaceful smile touching his lips as he listened to Maria's notes carrying his brother's voice.

On the sofa, Elias Thorne’s eyes snapped open. He stared at the stereo speakers, entirely captivated.

The strings swelled, building a tight, anxious tension through the pre-chorus. "Through the miles of silence / And the drifting snow?"

When the word Candle hit, the song absolutely exploded. The full authority of the symphonic rock arsenal crashed back in. Gabriel’s soaring guitar solo tore through the bridge, backed by the desperate, sweeping rock-opera choir of his friends screaming for Julian to look to the Northern Star.

The track was a cinematic masterpiece of tension and release.

As the final, heavy orchestral hit faded out, the massive wall of rock gave way completely to the quiet, fragile, wind-up mechanism of Johannes Brahms's Wiegenlied. The music box played alone for ten seconds before the cassette finally clicked to a stop.

The silence in the living room was absolute.

Gabriel stood by the stereo, his heart hammering against his ribs, suddenly feeling entirely exposed.

Elias Thorne slowly let out a breath he had been holding for the last four minutes. He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees, and looked directly at Gabriel.

"I have been an A&R rep for twelve years," Elias said, his voice quiet but carrying an intense, vibrating energy. "Do you know how many demo tapes I listen to in a week? Hundreds. And ninety-nine percent of them are derivative garbage."

Elias stood up, buttoning his suit jacket. He looked at the teenage band, his gaze lingering with profound respect on Maria and Gwendolyn, before returning to Gabriel.

"That is not a demo tape," Elias stated, pointing at the stereo. "That is an anthem. The transition from that heavy, driving cello to the absolute vulnerability of the glockenspiel? It's brilliant. It rips your heart right out of your chest before you even realize what's happening."

Gabriel felt a massive, triumphant grin pull at the corners of his mouth. He looked over at Julian, who was typing rapidly on his AAC device.

<aac>TOLD YOU.</aac> the robotic voice chimed perfectly into the quiet room.

Elias laughed, a sharp, genuine sound. "He's right. It's incredible."

The executive paced a few steps across the carpet, his mind already spinning with logistics. He stopped, looking at Eira and the band with absolute candor.

"Here is the reality of the music industry," Elias said, shifting into pure business mode. "It's December 17th. The holiday pressing plants shut down weeks ago. The radio syndicates have already locked their seasonal playlists. I cannot physically press, distribute, and market a holiday rock single before Christmas next week. It is logistically impossible."

Gabriel’s shoulders dropped a fraction of an inch, the reality of the calendar catching up with them.

"But," Elias continued, his voice rising, refusing to let the energy die. "I don't care. I want this song. I want Vanguard Horizon to sign The Winter Palace to a development deal immediately."

Gwendolyn’s eyes widened, her tactical brain rapidly processing the offer. "A development deal?"

"We bring you up to our master studio in Richmond," Elias explained, his eyes shining with vision. "We put you in a professional booth. We track that exact arrangement on a forty-eight-channel studio mixing console. We spend the spring writing and recording a four-song EP around it. And next November, we release Upon This Winter's Night as the lead single of a massive, nationwide winter rock campaign."

Elias looked at Gabriel, offering a firm, uncompromising smile. "Your song already saved your brother. Next winter, I want it to conquer the world. What do you say?"

Gabriel looked at Marcus, who was practically vibrating out of his shoes. He looked at Sarah, who was beaming. He looked at Maria, who squeezed Julian's hand tightly. Finally, he looked at Gwendolyn, the architect of the fortress. She gave him a single, decisive nod.

Gabriel turned back to the executive and extended his hand.

"We're in."
