While the muffled, acoustic chords and Gabriel's soaring Welsh baritone vibrated softly through the drywall of the spare room, a completely different kind of reconstruction was happening at the other end of the house.

Richard Harrison wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead with the back of his hand, giving the heavy steel bolt on the headboard one final, punishing turn with a hex wrench.

The master bedroom was entirely transformed. The heavy, oppressive mattress and the dark, imposing furniture that had belonged to Thomas Cross were completely gone—dragged to the curb and hauled away by a local disposal crew hours ago. The room had been thoroughly scrubbed and aired out, the lingering ghost of the abuser violently evicted from the space.

In its place stood a brand-new, beautifully crafted king-sized bed frame and a matching dresser, delivered by a local furniture truck just that afternoon.

Richard stepped back, wiping his hands on a rag, assessing his work. The furniture was solid, sturdy, and grounded. The room smelled like fresh pine and clean linens, not terrifying memories and suffocating dread.

The floorboards creaked softly in the hallway. Eira stepped into the doorway, holding two steaming mugs of Earl Grey tea.

She stopped dead in her tracks.

She stared at the massive new king-sized bed, the clean lines of the dresser, and the vast, open space of the room. The crushing, psychological weight that had kept her sleeping on the unfolded living room sofa for the last week instantly evaporated. The room wasn't a crime scene anymore. It was just a room. A clean slate.

"You built it all yourself," Eira whispered, her voice thick with an overwhelming, exhausted gratitude.

"The delivery guys only drop the boxes at the door," Richard offered with a warm, unassuming smile, stepping forward to take one of the mugs from her hands. "It's sturdy. I made sure there aren't any loose joints. It’s a fresh start, Eira."

Eira looked at the music therapy teacher. She saw the sawdust clinging to the knees of his jeans and the quiet, unyielding reliability in his eyes. For the last week, Richard hadn't just been a guest. He had cooked the meals, managed the local media blackout, handled the school bureaucracy, and stood shoulder-to-shoulder with Gabriel on the front lawn to protect their perimeter.

He was the mortar holding the bricks of her family together.

"Richard," Eira said, her voice dropping into a register of absolute, vulnerable clarity. She stepped fully into the room, setting her tea down on the freshly assembled dresser. "You and Maria have been sleeping out of duffel bags for nearly a week."

"We don't mind, Eira," Richard assured her quickly, taking a sip of his tea. "We're happy to keep you company until the dust settles."

"The dust has settled," Eira corrected gently, stepping closer to him. She looked directly into his eyes, leaving zero room for misinterpretation. "And I don't want you going back to your apartment. Not tomorrow, not next week, not ever."

Richard froze, the ceramic mug pausing halfway to his mouth.

"Eira..." Richard started, his voice softening, clearly caught off guard by the absolute certainty in her tone.

"Look at the reality of it, Richard," Eira continued, a fierce, protective logic backing up her emotional plea. "Julian finally slept through the night yesterday, but only because Maria was in that bed holding onto him. His nervous system is shattered. We both know that if you pack her up and take her back to your apartment across town, Julian will stop sleeping completely. And honestly? I think Maria would fight you on leaving him."

Richard slowly lowered his mug. He knew she was entirely right. His daughter was fiercely, immovably tethered to Julian's recovery. Separating those two fourteen-year-olds right now would be devastating for both of them.

"The spare room where the kids are recording right now is easily big enough for a real bed for Maria," Eira planned, her tactical mind seamlessly mirroring Gwendolyn's. "We can move Gabriel's studio gear out to the garage so they have a dedicated space. And this room..."

She gestured to the sprawling, sturdy new king-sized bed he had just built by hand. "This room is finally safe."

Eira reached out, resting her hand gently against his chest. She could feel his steady, calm heartbeat under his shirt.

"You aren't just keeping us company, Richard. You're our family. I feel safe when you are here. I want you to stay. Officially."

Richard looked at the woman standing in front of him. He thought about the fiercely protective teenagers down the hall, and the boy who had anchored his own daughter's world for the last seven years. He realized that Eira wasn't just asking him to move in; she was asking him to permanently man the watchtower beside her. It was exactly where he wanted to be.

Richard set his tea down next to hers on the dresser. He reached out, covering her hand with his own, his thumb gently brushing her knuckles.

"I'll go pack up the apartment in the morning," Richard promised, his voice thick with a profound, quiet joy. "We're home, Eira."

Down the hall, the muffled, soaring cadence of Gabriel singing the final Welsh chorus of Ar Noson y Gaeaf Hon echoed through the drywall, perfectly sealing the perimeter of the newly forged Rowlands-Harrison family.
