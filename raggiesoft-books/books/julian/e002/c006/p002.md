The quiet warmth of the Réveillon celebration seamlessly migrated down the hall to the cramped, carpeted spare room that served as the family's recording studio.

Julian was wheeled into his usual spot near the door, the heavy Aran wool sweater draped comfortably over his frame. He had pushed his new, heavy-duty isolation headphones slightly off his ears, resting them around his neck so he could hear the ambient sounds of the room. Maria took her place on the floor cushion, excitedly unwrapping her brand-new brass mallets.

Gabriel strapped his acoustic guitar across his chest, but before he stepped up to the dented dynamic microphone, he paused. He looked at Gwendolyn, who gave a firm, supportive nod from her spot beside the four-track cassette deck.

Gabriel set his guitar pick on the amp and walked over to Julian's wheelchair, kneeling so they were exactly at eye level.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Julep, we have one last gift for tonight,">Julep, nous avons un dernier cadeau pour ce soir,</lang>" Gabriel said softly, keeping his voice in the safe, rhythmic cadence of their Québécois family. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="But we need your permission before we play it.">Mais nous avons besoin de ta permission avant de le jouer.</lang>"

Julian tilted his head, his hands resting on his AAC device. He looked at his brother curiously.

"<lang="cy" trans="We want to play the trilingual version of the song,">Rydyn ni am chwarae'r fersiwn deirieithog o'r gân,</lang>" Gabriel explained, switching to Welsh to ensure absolute clarity and safety. "<lang="cy" trans="It starts in Welsh and moves to French. But when the chorus hits... it is in English.">Mae'n dechrau yn Gymraeg ac yn symud i Ffrangeg. Ond pan fydd y corws yn taro... mae yn Saesneg.</lang>"

Gabriel let the reality of that statement hang in the air.

"<lang="cy" trans="If you do not want to hear English tonight, say the word, and we will not play it,">Os nad ydych chi am glywed Saesneg heno, dywedwch y gair, ac ni fyddwn yn ei chwarae,</lang>" Gabriel promised fiercely. "<lang="cy" trans="This is your room. Your rules.">Eich ystafell chi yw hon. Eich rheolau chi.</lang>"

Julian stared at Gabriel. He understood exactly why his brother was asking. English was the language of the flashing police lights, the sterile trauma ward, and the monster who had stolen him. It was a language that currently felt like broken glass against his nervous system.

But as Julian looked around the room—at Gwendolyn's tactical stance, at Maria holding her new mallets, at his mother and Richard standing safely in the doorway—he realized something vital. The monster was dead. Tom Cross could never speak another word of English again. The language belonged to the Rowlands family now.

Julian reached down to his AAC device. He didn't use English to reply, but he gave his consent in the language of his sanctuary.

<aac><lang="cy" trans="PLAY IT. TAKE THE LANGUAGE BACK.">CHWARAE O. CYMERWCH YR IAITH YN ÔL.</lang></aac>

Gabriel let out a heavy, emotional exhale, a fierce smile breaking across his face. He stood up, grabbed his guitar pick, and stepped to the microphone.

"Rolling," Gwendolyn announced softly, pressing the red record button on the deck.

Gabriel strummed the acoustic chords, letting the rich, woody resonance fill the small room. He closed his eyes and began to sing, starting in the absolute, impenetrable safety of their mother's native tongue.

"Mae cloc y fantell yn canu... Yn fy atgoffa o amseroedd gwell..."

Maria brought her new brass mallets down on the glockenspiel. The upgrade in equipment was instantly obvious. The notes weren't just clear; they were brilliant, ringing out with a heavy, pristine sustain that wove perfectly through the acoustic guitar.

As the verse transitioned, Gabriel shifted seamlessly into French, channeling the fierce, resilient spirit of Stanstead. "Le monde est froid et plein de peur... Mais un sanctuaire t'attend ici..."

Julian closed his eyes, his rigid posture melting completely into the backrest of his manual chair. The trilingual mix wasn't a clash of cultures; it was a sonic map of his exact perimeter.

Then, the pre-chorus built the tension. Gabriel stepped closer to the microphone. He didn't sing the English words with the terrifying, unpredictable cadence of their father. He sang them with the absolute, uncompromising roar of a protector.

"So keep a candle burning bright! Upon this winter's night!"

Hearing the English language weaponized for him, rather than against him, sent a powerful, grounding shiver down Julian's spine. It didn't trigger an overload; it felt like a victory lap. Gabriel's baritone carried the heavy rock anthem flawlessly, weaving the final outro into a beautiful, overlapping cascade of all three languages.

When the final glockenspiel note faded into the quiet hum of the room, Gabriel lowered his guitar. He looked at Julian, entirely out of breath.

Julian’s hands were already flying across his AAC keyboard. He had the biggest, brightest smile on his face that his family had seen in years. He hit the speaker button, the French synthesizer echoing through the room.

<aac><lang="fr-CA" trans="IT IS INCREDIBLE. I LOVE IT.">C'EST INCROYABLE. JE L'ADORE.</lang></aac>

Before Gabriel could even fully smile, Julian’s fingers kept typing, seamlessly switching his device's output to Welsh to deliver his highly specific, autistic critique.

<aac><lang="cy" trans="IT IS MESSY. THE BASS IS TOO WEAK AND THE TRANSITION TO ENGLISH NEEDS MORE PRACTICE. KEEP WORKING ON IT.">MAE'N FLERWCH. MAE'R BASS YN RHY WAN A MAE ANGEN MWY O YMARFER AR Y TROSGLWYDDIAD I'R SAESNEG. PARHAWCH I WEITHIO ARNO.</lang></aac>

The entire room erupted into laughter. The sheer, blunt honesty of the critique was exactly the kind of grounded routine they had all been missing.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="You are right, boss,">Tu as raison, le boss,</lang>" Gabriel laughed, wiping a tear of pure joy from his eye as he adjusted his microphone stand. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="It's just a rough draft. We will make it perfect.">C'est juste un brouillon. On va le rendre parfait.</lang>"

Julian let out a happy, rhythmic vocalization, pulling his heavy isolation headphones back up over his ears. He was safe, he was loved, and his band had work to do.
