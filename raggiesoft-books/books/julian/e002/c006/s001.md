Traditionally, a French-Canadian Réveillon is a massive, raucous affair. It is a holiday feast that begins strictly at midnight after Christmas Eve Mass, continuing well into the early hours of the morning with loud music, clinking glasses, and a chaotic, sprawling extended family.

But inside the Ewell house, the Rowlands-Harrison family had completely rewritten the rules of the holiday to protect their most important perimeter.

At exactly six o'clock in the evening, the house was a haven of regulated, sensory-friendly warmth. The harsh overhead lights were turned off, replaced by the soft, ambient glow of table lamps and the multi-colored twinkle of the Christmas tree in the corner. There was no loud music, just the quiet, crackling hiss of the baseboard heaters and the rich, heavy scent of roasted meats and dark, savory gravy wafting from the kitchen.

They were celebrating Réveillon on Julian's terms: early, quiet, and completely safe.

The dining table was fully extended, cementing the reality that Richard and Maria were no longer just guests—they were permanent fixtures. Julian sat in his manual wheelchair at the center of the table, wearing a soft, oversized red sweater. Maria sat flush against his left side, their shoulders pressed together in their familiar, grounding configuration.

Gabriel pushed backward through the swinging kitchen door, carrying a massive, steaming ceramic baking dish with heavy oven mitts.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Make room, the meat pie is hot!">Faites de la place, la tourtière est chaude!</lang>" Gabriel announced, his deep voice carrying the rolling, Québécois cadence of their northern relatives. He set the heavy, perfectly browned pork-and-spice pie in the center of the table.

"<lang="cy" trans="It smells wonderful, Gabriel,">Mae'n arogli'n hyfryd, Gabriel,</lang>" Eira smiled, reaching out to rest her hand warmly over Richard's where it sat on the table. She looked at her oldest son, switching seamlessly to her native tongue. "<lang="cy" trans="But where is the main dish for Julian?">Ond ble mae'r brif ddysgl i Julian?</lang>"

Gabriel grinned, a fierce, triumphant spark in his eyes. He stripped off the oven mitts. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="Gwen, bring it.">Gwen, apporte-le.</lang>"

Gwendolyn stepped out of the kitchen. She wasn't carrying a traditional holiday roast. She was carrying a deep, individual ceramic bowl, radiating heat and smelling of rich, dark beef stock.

She walked directly to Julian and set the bowl down on his wheelchair tray.

Julian looked down, his eyes going incredibly wide.

It was poutine. But it wasn't the compromised, Americanized version made with melted mozzarella that Eira had apologized for on their first night home. Sitting on top of the thick, hand-cut fries and smothered in piping hot, dark gravy were massive, irregular chunks of authentic, fresh cheese curds.

Julian looked up at Gabriel, his mouth hanging slightly open in shock. He knew you couldn't buy these in Virginia.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="It's the real thing, Julep,">C'est le vrai, Julep,</lang>" Gabriel explained, pulling up his chair across the table. He leaned forward, looking extremely proud of his logistical victory. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="I called Uncle Marc in Stanstead on Monday. He put three pounds of fresh cheese curds in a cooler with dry ice and overnighted it.">J'ai appelé l'oncle Marc à Stanstead lundi. Il a mis trois livres de fromage en grains frais dans une glacière avec de la glace sèche et l'a expédié par avion.</lang>"

"<lang="cy" trans="Literally overnight,">Llythrennol dros nos,</lang>" Gwendolyn added in Welsh, taking her seat next to her twin. "<lang="cy" trans="The delivery van dropped it off at three o'clock this afternoon. They still squeak.">Fe wnaeth y fan ddosbarthu ei ollwng am dri o'r gloch y prynhawn yma. Maen nhw'n dal i wichian.</lang>"

Julian stared at the bowl. A week and a half ago, he had been trapped in the freezing, terrifying back seat of a stolen car, surviving on a few sips of gas station water, entirely convinced he would never see his family again.

Now, he was sitting in a warm, dimly lit room, surrounded by his best friend, his mother, his fierce older siblings, and a man who had stepped up to be the father he actually deserved. And his brother had somehow orchestrated an international overnight shipment of highly perishable Canadian dairy just to make sure his traumatized nervous system had the exact comfort food it craved.

Julian’s hands began to tremble. A massive, overwhelming wave of emotion hit his chest, but it wasn't the dark, rigid terror of the trauma ward. It was a profound, overflowing joy.

He pulled his AAC device onto his lap. The device still had its primary English synthesizer fully intact, but Julian hadn't touched those specific settings in over a week. English was still too sharp, too unpredictable, and too closely tied to the monster. Instead, he actively bypassed the default output, deftly navigating to the secondary language banks Gabriel had helped him configure. He chose his safe harbor.

Julian typed quickly, toggling the synthesizer to French and hitting the speaker button.

<aac><lang="fr-CA" trans="THANK YOU. THIS IS PERFECT. I LOVE YOU ALL.">MERCI. C'EST PARFAIT. JE VOUS AIME TOUS.</lang></aac>

"<lang="cy" trans="We love you too, love,">Rydyn ni'n dy garu di hefyd, cariad,</lang>" Eira whispered, her eyes shining with unshed tears as she looked at her youngest son.

Maria bumped her shoulder affectionately against Julian’s. "<lang="fr-CA" trans="Eat, Julian. Before the gravy gets cold.">Mange, Julian. Avant que la sauce ne refroidisse.</lang>"

Julian picked up his fork. He pierced a thick, gravy-soaked fry and a massive cheese curd, taking a bite. As his teeth bit down, the fresh curd let out a distinct, audible squeak.

Julian closed his eyes, letting out a long, highly vocal sigh of absolute, unadulterated contentment. He leaned his weight heavily against Maria, entirely relaxed.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Merry Christmas, family,">Joyeux Noël, teulu,</lang>" Richard said softly, raising his glass of sparkling cider. He effortlessly blended his daughter's second language with Eira's native tongue, proving just how perfectly the perimeter had fused together.

" Joyeux Noël, " the table echoed back in a quiet, unified chorus.

Outside, the bitter Virginia wind rattled the frosted windowpanes, but it didn't matter. The monster was gone, the doors were locked, the table was full, and Julian Rowlands was exactly where he belonged.
