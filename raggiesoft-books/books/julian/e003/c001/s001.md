Freshman English Literature was, by its very nature, a daily exercise in survival for Julian Rowlands.

It wasn't that the curriculum was too difficult; it was the fact that he was trapped in a room for forty-five minutes entirely dedicated to dissecting, speaking, and analyzing the English language. English was the language of the flashing police lights. It was the language of the Ohio trauma ward. And, most terrifyingly, it was the only language Thomas Cross had ever spoken. To Julian’s unmedicated, traumatized nervous system, the English language felt like walking barefoot across broken glass.

To cope, Julian had developed a quiet, highly effective academic accommodation. He sat in his manual wheelchair at his desk in the back row, flanked by Clara and Maria, his spiral-bound notebook open. As the teacher lectured in English, Julian meticulously translated every single note into Welsh. Writing in his mother's native tongue formed a psychological buffer, filtering the terrifying language of his abuser through the impenetrable, absolute safety of his family's sanctuary.

But today, the regular teacher was out.

Sitting behind the heavy wooden desk at the front of the room was a substitute. Mr. Evans was an older, stern-looking man with impeccable posture, a tweed jacket, and the quiet, calculating gaze of a strict disciplinarian. He had spent the first ten minutes of class establishing absolute order, demanding complete silence while the students worked on their reading comprehension packets.

For a while, the silence was perfect. But then, the perimeter was breached.

Sitting one aisle over was a loud, chronically disruptive freshman named Kevin. Kevin had been lurking in the hallway before the first bell and had overheard Gabriel giving Julian a quick, affectionate farewell.

Kevin leaned across the aisle, his eyes landing on the jagged, rolling consonants filling Julian's notebook.

"What kind of alien gibberish is that, Julep?" Kevin whispered, a cruel, mocking sneer on his face.

Julian’s pen snapped to a halt. His breath caught violently in his throat.

Julep. It was the password. It was the sacred, fiercely protected name forged in his nursery and used by his siblings to keep him safe in the dark. Hearing that specific name drop casually from the mouth of an outsider wasn't just an insult; it was a catastrophic security breach.

Julian detonated.

He didn't shrink back. A massive, uncontrollable surge of protective rage and pure panic flooded his system. He slammed both of his hands down onto his desk with a loud, echoing CRACK that made half the classroom jump. He pulled his AAC device forward, his fingers hammering the keys with violent force. He didn't use the English synthesizer.

<aac><lang="cy" trans="NEVER CALL ME THAT NAME!">PEIDIWCH BYTH Â GALW FI YR ENW HWNW!</lang></aac>

The digitized Welsh roared through the quiet classroom at maximum volume, the metallic, jagged syllables slicing through the air like a siren.

Kevin jumped back, momentarily startled, but his teenage ego quickly recovered. He scowled, leaning back in his chair. "Speak English, freak! This is America! What, are you gonna go cry to mommy?"

Clara and Maria were out of their seats before Kevin even finished the sentence. The legs of their chairs screeched agonizingly against the linoleum as they threw themselves between Kevin's desk and Julian's wheelchair. Their symmetrical shields were fully raised, their identical eyes blazing with absolute, protective fury.

"<lang="cy" trans="Shut your mouth and leave him alone!">Cau dy geg a gadewch lonydd iddo!</lang>" Clara and Maria screamed at the bully in perfect, terrifying unison, their own Welsh flawlessly mirroring the cadence of Julian's family.

"Enough."

The single word wasn't shouted, but it cracked through the room with the devastating authority of a whip.

Mr. Evans was suddenly standing at the end of the aisle. The older substitute towered over Kevin's desk, his expression carved from absolute stone.

"But he's—" Kevin started to complain, pointing a finger at Julian.

"Gather your belongings," Mr. Evans interrupted, his voice deathly quiet and perfectly level. He pulled a disciplinary referral pad from his jacket pocket and began writing with rapid, ruthless precision. "You do not demand language from anyone in this room, nor do you mock a fellow student. You will take this referral to the principal's office immediately. If you speak another word before you cross that threshold, I will personally ensure your suspension lasts until the summer."

Kevin looked at the substitute's cold, unyielding eyes, swallowed hard, grabbed his backpack, and practically sprinted out of the classroom.

The heavy door clicked shut. The classroom was dead silent.

Clara and Maria were still standing defensively in front of Julian, their chests heaving. Julian was gripping the armrests of his manual chair, his knuckles entirely white, his breathing shallow and erratic. He waited for the inevitable. He waited for the strict disciplinarian to turn his wrath on the boy who had just disrupted the reading packet with a loud, electronic foreign language.

Mr. Evans tore the copy of the referral from his pad. He looked down at the identical twins, offering a slow, respectful nod to the fiercely protective girls.

Then, the substitute took a step closer to Julian's wheelchair. The severe, intimidating mask of the disciplinarian instantly melted away. Mr. Evans looked down at the spiral-bound notebook on Julian's desk. He saw the Welsh translations. He looked at the trembling boy, and then he looked at the AAC device.

In a single, brilliant moment of observation, the older man understood exactly what he was looking at. He recognized the heavy, suffocating weight of trauma. He knew why a boy would need to hide from the English language inside an English classroom.

Mr. Evans crouched down slowly, bringing himself down to Julian's eye level so he wasn't looming.

"<lang="cy" trans="It's alright, boy,">Mae'n iawn, fachgen,</lang>" Mr. Evans said softly.

Julian gasped, his eyes going wide. The words were perfectly accented, rolling with the familiar, deeply comforting cadence of his own home.

"<lang="cy" trans="You did nothing wrong,">Fe wnaethoch chi ddim byd o'i le,</lang>" the substitute continued in flawless Welsh, his tone carrying a profound, anchoring warmth. "<lang="cy" trans="I see your notes. It is okay to take refuge in your language. I understand.">Rwy'n gweld eich nodiadau. Mae'n iawn i gymryd lloches yn eich iaith. Rwy'n deall.</lang>"

The rigid tension locked in Julian's shoulders completely shattered. The classroom wasn't a threat anymore. The perimeter had been secured by an unexpected ally.

Julian dropped his hands from his armrests. He let out a long, ragged exhale, slumping back into his chair. He reached for his AAC device, keeping it firmly locked on the Welsh synthesizer.

<aac><lang="cy" trans="THANK YOU VERY MUCH.">DIOLCH YN FAWR.</lang></aac>

"<lang="cy" trans="You're welcome,">Croeso,</lang>" Mr. Evans smiled gently. He stood back up, adjusting his tweed jacket, and looked at Clara and Maria. "Take your seats, girls. You did well."

As the substitute walked calmly back to the front of the room to resume the lesson, Julian let himself relax, Clara’s hand coming to rest firmly on his left shoulder while Maria squeezed his right arm. He looked at the chalkboard, and then down at his Welsh notes. For the first time all semester, he didn't feel like he was surviving English class; he actually felt safe.

He tapped a quick, quiet message on his device and showed the screen to Clara and Maria.

<aac>I WISH HE WAS OUR REGULAR TEACHER.</aac>

Clara and Maria leaned in, identical, relieved smiles spreading across their faces.

"Me too, Julep. Me too," they whispered back in perfect unison.
