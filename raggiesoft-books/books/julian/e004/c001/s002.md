The automatic sliding doors of Richmond International Airport parted, and the Rowlands-Harrison garrison stepped out of the suffocating, 90-degree Virginia heat and into the echoing, air-conditioned expanse of the departure terminal.

For a typical family, the airport was just a stressful preamble to a vacation. But for Julian, the terminal was a sensory minefield. The high ceilings bounced the chaotic, overlapping sounds of thousands of people in a dozen different directions. The sharp clack-clack of hard plastic suitcase wheels rolling over the terrazzo floors, the blaring, distorted overhead announcements, and the chaotic hum of stressed travelers formed a suffocating wall of noise.

Julian reached up and pulled his heavy, Vanguard Horizon-funded isolation headphones firmly over his ears.

The chaotic roar of the terminal instantly vanished, reduced to a dull, manageable murmur. He let out a long, grounding exhale, his shoulders dropping an inch.

"I've got the cart," Richard announced, grabbing a heavy metal luggage trolley from the rack near the doors.

Gabriel effortlessly hoisted their three massive, scuffed canvas suitcases onto the trolley, stacking them with the precision of a frontline quartermaster.

"Formation," Gwendolyn ordered softly.

They didn't need to practice it. Clara stepped flush against Julian's left wheel, her hand resting over his. Maria took his right, her shoulder brushing against his arm. Gabriel pushed the luggage cart directly in front of them, acting as a physical snowplow to force oblivious travelers to part ways, while Eira and Gwendolyn took the lead, heading straight for the international ticketing desk of Meridian Airlines.

When they reached the front of the queue, the Meridian ticket agent offered a polite, professional smile. "Checking in for Flight 412 to New York, connecting to Manchester?"

Eira unzipped her leather crossbody bag. She reached inside and pulled out a thick, uniform stack of seven dark-blue United States passports, sliding them across the laminate counter.

Julian, watching closely from his wheelchair, frowned. A sudden, sharp spike of anxiety hit his chest. He pulled one side of his headphones back just enough to hear, his fingers quickly finding the keys of his AAC device.

<aac>WHY THE AMERICAN ONES?</aac> the robotic voice asked, the volume kept low but the urgency undeniable. <aac>WE ARE GOING HOME. WE ARE WELSH.</aac>

To Julian's incredibly literal, traumatized mind, using the American passports felt like a betrayal. It felt like holding onto the identity of Thomas Cross, instead of claiming the heritage that had literally saved his life.

Eira paused, turning away from the desk to look at her son. She understood the panic instantly. She didn't dismiss it.

"<lang="cy" trans="It's alright, Julep,">Mae'n iawn, Julep,</lang>" Eira whispered, stepping close to his chair and speaking in their native tongue so the ticket agent wouldn't understand. "<lang="cy" trans="This is only for the airline.">Dim ond i'r cwmni hedfan yw hyn.</lang>"

"She's right, Julian," Gwendolyn added, leaning in. "The airline agent just needs to match the names on the tickets to a valid photo ID to let us on the plane. But when we land in Manchester, we are walking right up to the UK Border desk, and we are handing them our burgundy British passports. We enter our country as citizens. I promise."

Julian stared at his sister, processing the tactical logic. The American passport wasn't an identity; it was just a boarding pass. He let out a quiet sigh, nodding once, and slipped the earcup of his headphones back into place.

Eira turned back to the agent, sliding a pristine, red-tabbed manila folder across the counter alongside the passports.

"Six checked bags," Eira stated briskly. "And the medical accommodation clearance for the manual wheelchair. I spoke with your accessibility director, Mr. Vance, on Tuesday. The profile is already noted in our booking."

The agent opened the folder, her eyes scanning the pre-approved airline forms and the letter from Julian's pediatrician. She typed rapidly into her terminal, pulling up the family's reservation.

"Ah, yes. I see it right here, Mrs. Rowlands," the agent confirmed, her tone shifting into one of profound professional respect. She didn't offer a generic airport transport chair; she simply reached under her desk and pulled out a bright, neon-orange gate-check tag attached to an elastic string.

She scribbled the flight number on it and handed it directly to Gabriel, along with the printed boarding passes.

"Gate-check is cleared. Just leave the chair at the end of the jet bridge, and the ramp crew at JFK has already been notified to bring it back up for the layover."

"Thank you," Gabriel said, offering a polite, appreciative nod.

He walked back to Julian and carefully looped the bright orange tag around the heavy metal crossbar under the seat of the manual wheelchair, pulling the elastic tight.

Julian looked down at the neon tag. To anyone else in the airport, it was just a piece of cheap cardboard. But to Julian, it was a profound symbol of his family's absolute, Frictionless competence. The world was chaotic, but his perimeter was bulletproof.

"Alright, Palace," Gabriel said, clapping his hands together once as Richard took control of the boarding passes. "The luggage is gone, and the chair is secured. Next stop: the metal detectors."

Julian squeezed Maria and Clara's hands. The perimeter moved forward.
