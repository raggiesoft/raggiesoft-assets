Airport security was a loud, clattering bottleneck of private security contractors, rattling plastic bins, and the monotonous, electronic beep of the walk-through magnetometers.

The chaotic chokepoint was a sensory minefield. Businessmen in oversized suits impatiently checked their pagers, and families wrestled with bulky carry-on bags. For Julian, even with his heavy isolation headphones firmly in place, the visual noise of the security lane was incredibly overwhelming.

The garrison moved to the front of the line, immediately executing their established protocol.

Gabriel and Richard stepped forward to handle the physical logistics. They grabbed a stack of gray plastic bins, rapidly emptying their pockets, removing their watches, and loading the family's carry-on duffels onto the rubber rollers of the X-ray belt.

Eira stepped up to the metal detector, presenting the boarding passes to the private security guard manning the archway.

"The boy in the wheelchair," the security guard said, gesturing lazily with a handheld metal-detecting wand. "He's going to set off the archway in that thing. Can he stand and walk through? If not, I need to wand him down and inspect the chair cushions."

"He cannot bear any weight on his legs, and he is severely autistic," Eira stated, her voice projecting a calm, absolute, and zero-tolerance authority. She didn't ask for permission; she dictated the terms of engagement. "He cannot be touched by a stranger, or he will go into severe muscular spasticity. My daughter will carry him through the archway, and my son will bring the empty chair around for your inspection."

The guard blinked, momentarily taken aback by the frictionless, military precision of the mother's instructions. He nodded, stepping aside to open the hinged bypass gate next to the metal detector. "Alright, ma'am. Bring the chair through here."

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Come on, Julep. It's time,">Allez, Julep. C'est l'heure,</lang>" Gwendolyn murmured, stepping to the front of his wheelchair.

Julian hated leaving the safety of his customized seat in public, but he trusted his sister implicitly. He unclasped his seatbelt and leaned forward slightly.

Gwendolyn possessed a flawless, practiced mechanical leverage. She wrapped her arms securely around his torso and under his knees, lifting the fifteen-year-old boy out of the chair with a smooth, effortless exertion of core strength. She cradled him securely against her chest, her steady heartbeat providing an immediate, grounding rhythm against his ribs.

The moment Julian was airborne, his anchors moved in.

Maria stepped flush against his right side, and Clara against his left. They didn't just walk next to him; Maria reached up and wrapped her hand securely around his right hand, while Clara locked her fingers through his left.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="We are here,">On est là,</lang>" Maria whispered, keeping her eyes locked on his.

Together, the three girls walked through the metal detector archway in perfect, synchronized tandem. Julian was entirely suspended, unable to touch the floor, but because he was physically tethered to Gwendolyn, Clara, and Maria, his unmedicated nervous system remained completely calm. He didn't flinch. The perimeter held him together.

Whoosh. They passed through the archway in absolute silence. No alarms. No red lights.

Immediately behind them, Gabriel pushed the empty manual wheelchair through the bypass gate. The security guard ran the handheld wand quickly over the seat and the backrest, glancing under the frame.

"Looks good. You're clear," the guard confirmed, stepping away.

Gabriel didn't wait a single second. He pushed the chair directly behind the metal detector, locking the brakes into place. Gwendolyn smoothly pivoted and lowered Julian back into his customized seat.

The moment Julian's weight settled back into the familiar, contoured cushions, he let out a long, heavy exhale. He reached down and clicked his seatbelt securely into place. Maria immediately adjusted the dark navy Aran wool sweater over his chest, while Clara ensured his heavy headphones were still perfectly positioned.

"Well done, everyone," Richard praised quietly, gathering their cleared duffel bags from the end of the X-ray belt and passing them out.

Gabriel grabbed the push-handles of Julian's chair. As they walked away from the clattering security checkpoint and merged into the wider, carpeted concourse of the domestic departure gates, the suffocating tension of the morning finally began to evaporate.

The Richmond departure lounge felt infinitely calmer. Sunlight poured through the massive, floor-to-ceiling windows, illuminating the tarmac where a sleek, silver-and-blue twin-engine jetliner belonging to the regional coastal carrier was parked at Gate B4.

Julian pulled his headphones down around his neck. The ambient noise here was a quiet, manageable hum. He looked out the window at the jet, a sudden, thrilling spark of adrenaline hitting his chest.

He wasn't trapped in the back of a freezing car. He wasn't hiding in his bedroom.

He pulled his AAC device onto his lap, his fingers flying across the keys with a bright, eager energy. He selected the Welsh synthesizer, the language of the home he hadn't seen in nine years.

<aac><lang="cy" trans="ARE WE FLYING NOW?">RYDYN NI'N HEDFAN NAWR?</lang></aac>

Gabriel chuckled, a massive, genuine smile breaking across his face as he parked the chair near the boarding door.

"<lang="cy" trans="Yes, brother,">Ydyn, brawd,</lang>" Gabriel confirmed, clapping a heavy hand affectionately onto Julian's shoulder. " Rydyn ni'n hedfan nawr. "

Eira stood beside Richard, looking out at the aircraft, her eyes shining with unshed tears of profound relief. The hardest logistical hurdles of the morning were behind them. They had successfully navigated the ticket counters, the luggage scales, and the security gauntlet without a single breach in their armor.

Next stop: New York. And then, the Menai Strait.
