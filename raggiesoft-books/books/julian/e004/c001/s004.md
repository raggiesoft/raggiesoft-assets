The transition from the customized wheelchair into the narrow, fabric-upholstered airline seat was handled with the same frictionless, military precision the family applied to everything else.

Gwendolyn carried Julian down the jet bridge, transferring him seamlessly into the middle seat of Row 12. Gabriel immediately took the empty manual chair back up the ramp, ensuring the baggage handlers saw the neon-orange tag and loaded it securely into the forward cargo hold.

By the time the heavy cabin door sealed shut and the twin-engine jet pushed back from Gate B4, the perimeter was perfectly established.

Julian sat in the middle seat, his heavy isolation headphones resting around his neck. Maria sat in the window seat to his right, while Clara took the aisle on his left. Directly behind them, Gabriel and Gwendolyn formed the rear guard, and across the aisle, Richard and Eira kept a watchful eye on the formation.

As the jet taxied toward the runway, the low, mechanical whine of the engines began to vibrate through the floorboards. Julian reached out, firmly grasping Maria’s hand with his right hand, and Clara’s with his left.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Here we go,">Ici on y va,</lang>" Maria whispered in Québécois French, offering a bright, excited smile as the plane turned onto the active runway.

The jet engines roared, a massive, deafening surge of power that pushed Julian firmly back into his seat. As the aircraft accelerated down the tarmac and the nose lifted sharply into the sweltering Virginia sky, the intense G-force pressed down on his chest.

For Julian, the heavy gravitational pull didn't trigger his anxiety. It felt exactly like a massive, full-body deep-pressure cuddle. His unmedicated muscles relaxed entirely into the incline of the climb.

He looked out the small oval window past Maria, watching the sprawling, green tidewater of James City County rapidly shrink into a patchwork of tiny neighborhoods and winding rivers.

As the ground fell away, a sudden, vivid memory surfaced in his mind, piercing through the noise of the cabin.

He had not been on an airplane since he was six years old.

He remembered the flight out of the United Kingdom, nine years ago. He remembered sitting in a much larger airplane, his small legs dangling over the edge of the seat. But mostly, he remembered the man sitting next to him.

Tom Cross hadn't been a monster back then. He had been a smiling, sharply dressed father, holding his six-year-old son's hand as they flew across the Atlantic. Tom had leaned over, his voice devoid of the terrifying, paranoid rage that would eventually consume him, and promised Julian that America was going to be a grand, beautiful adventure.

It was a profound, deeply complicated memory. For years, Tom's escalating abuse had completely overwritten the man he used to be. The abduction had violently severed whatever remaining illusion of a father-son bond existed.

But sitting in Row 12, rocketing up to thirty thousand feet, Julian didn't feel paralyzed by the memory. He felt an overwhelming, undeniable sense of closure.

The American adventure his father had promised had shattered. The promises were empty, and the man who made them was dead.

But out of that wreckage, Julian hadn't just survived. He had gained Richard, who sat across the aisle reading a paperback novel, completely unfazed by the turbulence. He had gained Clara and Maria, who were currently anchoring his hands, perfectly in sync with his breathing. He had watched Gabriel and Gwendolyn forge themselves into unbreakable shields of steel.

Julian looked away from the window, turning his head to look at Clara on his left.

He didn't need to pull his AAC device onto his lap to communicate. He just squeezed her hand twice, offering a brilliant, radiant smile that completely reached his eyes.

"<lang="cy" trans="We are going home, Julep,">Rydyn ni'n mynd adref, Julep,</lang>" Clara whispered in flawless Welsh, instantly reading the overwhelming joy on his face.

Julian let out a happy, rhythmic vocalization that was completely swallowed by the ambient roar of the jet engines. He pulled his heavy headphones up over his ears, leaning his head against Maria’s shoulder, and closed his eyes.

He wasn't flying into the terrifying unknown with a man who refused to learn his mother's language. He was flying back to the very roots of his sanctuary, completely surrounded by a garrison that spoke his language perfectly.

The flight to New York’s JFK airport was a quick, ninety-minute hop up the eastern seaboard. It was just enough time for Julian's nervous system to completely reset before they had to face the ultimate, chaotic gauntlet of the international transfer terminal.
