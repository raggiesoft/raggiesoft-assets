The wheels of the twin-engine jet hit the tarmac at John F. Kennedy International Airport with a heavy, shuddering thud, the thrust reversers roaring as the aircraft rapidly decelerated.

Julian kept his eyes closed, his hands firmly anchored in Maria and Clara’s grips. The sudden deceleration pushed him forward against his seatbelt, but the familiar, symmetrical pressure on his hands kept his unmedicated nervous system grounded.

When the seatbelt sign finally chimed off, the cabin erupted into the usual frantic, claustrophobic scramble of passengers reaching for the overhead bins.

The garrison didn't move an inch.

Gabriel stood up into the aisle, his broad shoulders effectively blocking anyone from crowding Row 12. "Hold the line," he rumbled quietly. "We wait for the chair."

Ten minutes later, the cabin had emptied. Gwendolyn effortlessly lifted Julian, carrying him down the jet bridge where Gabriel was already waiting with the customized manual wheelchair. The bright neon-orange gate-check tag was still securely tied to the crossbar.

Gwendolyn lowered him into the seat, and Julian immediately felt the tension bleed out of his spine. The airplane seat had been tolerable, but his chair was an extension of his own body. It was his mobile fortress.

"Alright, Palace," Richard said, checking his watch as he took the lead. "We are in Terminal 2. Our flight to Manchester leaves out of the International Arrivals Building, which handles the foreign carrier departures. We have to take the curbside shuttle."

Eira nodded, her expression hardening into absolute, tactical focus. "Headphones on, Julep. It's going to be loud."

Julian pulled the heavy isolation cups securely over his ears.

They marched up the jet bridge and into the terminal. JFK was not a quiet, regional airport. It was a massive, echoing cavern of stressed, shouting travelers, blaring announcements, and rushing electric carts. But the real assault began when they bypassed the baggage claims—since their luggage was checked through to Manchester—and pushed through the automatic doors onto the curbside pickup lane.

The August heat in New York was punishing, radiating off the concrete in shimmering, exhaust-choked waves. The smell of burnt jet fuel, hot asphalt, and diesel smoke was instantly suffocating.

A heavy, articulated shuttle bus hissed to a stop at the curb, its doors folding open with a loud, mechanical clatter.

"Ramp!" Gabriel shouted over the din of the traffic, catching the attention of the bus driver.

The driver stepped out of his seat, deploying a heavy metal plate onto the curb. Gabriel didn't wait for assistance. He expertly navigated Julian’s chair up the steep incline and into the designated wheelchair bay on the bus. He engaged the chair's brakes and immediately locked the heavy, yellow floor tie-downs onto the frame.

Gwendolyn, Richard, and Eira filed in, while Clara and Maria immediately took the flip-down seats directly beside Julian's wheels.

The doors hissed shut, and the bus lurched forward.

For Julian, the ten-minute ride around the perimeter of JFK was a sensory nightmare, even with the headphones blocking the noise. The bus rattled violently over the uneven tarmac, the suspension groaning at every turn. The heavy smell of diesel fumes seeped through the rubber seals of the doors, making him nauseous.

He squeezed his eyes shut, his hands balling into fists on his armrests. The phantom sway of a moving vehicle was clawing at the edges of his mind, threatening to drag him back to the dark, freezing roads of Ohio.

A hand gently covered his left fist. He opened his eyes.

Clara was leaning forward, her face mere inches from his. She didn't speak—she knew he couldn't hear her through the thick foam of the headphones—but she offered a slow, deliberate, and incredibly exaggerated smile.

On his right, Maria tapped his knee twice, holding up her hand and counting down on her fingers. Three. Two. One. The bus lurched to a halt, the air brakes hissing loudly.

"We're here," Gabriel announced, moving swiftly to unclip the floor restraints. "Let's move."

They rolled down the ramp and pushed through a set of towering glass doors into the International Departures Hall.

If the curbside was a sensory assault, the international terminal was a sprawling, echoing metropolis. It was a kaleidoscope of humanity. Tens of thousands of people swarmed the check-in desks, speaking dozens of different languages. The vaulted ceilings trapped the noise, creating a continuous, low-frequency roar.

But Eira Rowlands did not falter. She was entirely in her element. She moved with a crisp, undeniable authority, her eyes locked on the massive, mechanical flip-board high above the concourse that clattered rapidly as it updated gate numbers.

"Gate 4," Eira directed, pointing down a long, crowded corridor. "Richard, Gabriel, clear the path."

The two men moved to the front of the formation. They didn't push or shove, but their combined size and determined stride caused the crowds of international travelers to naturally part like the Red Sea.

Gwendolyn took the push-handles of Julian's chair, maintaining a brisk, smooth pace, while the twins kept their shoulders flush against the wheels, physically blocking any stray luggage carts from getting too close.

They bypassed the duty-free shops and the crowded terminal bars, marching relentlessly until the dense crowds finally began to thin out near the end of the concourse.

Gate 4 was a spacious, carpeted waiting area with floor-to-ceiling windows looking out over the massive wingspan of a wide-body, transatlantic jetliner.

Gwendolyn wheeled Julian into a quiet corner against the glass, pivoting the chair so his back was to the wall. He had a clear, unobstructed view of the entire gate area, and no one could approach him from behind.

"Perimeter secured," Gwendolyn said, letting out a sharp exhale as she dropped her heavy carry-on bag onto the carpet.

Julian pulled his headphones down to rest around his neck. The ambient noise here was manageable. He looked up at his family. They were all sweating, their clothes rumpled from the oppressive heat of the shuttle bus, but their eyes were bright with a fierce, collective triumph.

They had survived the hub.

"It's loud, Julep," Eira agreed softly, her British accent returning to its natural, unhurried cadence now that the frantic rush was over. "But that plane right there? That is the very last step. When we get off that plane, we will be standing in Manchester."
