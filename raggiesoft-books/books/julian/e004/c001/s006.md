The static crackle of the public address system echoed through the International Departures Hall, followed by the gate agent's voice announcing the pre-boarding process for passengers requiring extra time or special assistance.

Gabriel immediately hoisted his heavy canvas duffel onto his shoulder, offering Richard a sharp, ready nod. The garrison was moving out.

But in the center of their formation, Julian’s manual wheelchair jerked to a sudden, rigid halt.

Julian’s hands began patting his chest, then his lap, his eyes going wide with a sudden, frantic spike of panic. He looked toward Eira’s leather crossbody bag, his breathing turning shallow and rapid. He pulled his AAC device squarely onto his lap, his fingers hitting the keys with urgent, heavy strikes.

<aac>PASSPORT.</aac> the robotic voice barked, the volume cutting through the ambient hum of the terminal. <aac>I DO NOT HAVE MY PASSPORT.</aac>

He remembered the ticketing desk in Richmond. He remembered his mother sliding the stack of dark-blue booklets across the counter to the agent. To his literal, anxious mind, the gate door was another insurmountable border. If he didn't have his passport in his hand right now, they were going to leave him behind.

Before the panic could fully trigger a muscular spasm, Gwendolyn was there.

She dropped her carry-on bag onto the carpet and immediately knelt directly in front of his wheelchair, bringing herself perfectly into his line of sight. She didn't try to reason with him from a distance or tell him to calm down. She stepped directly into his space, wrapping both of her arms securely around his chest and pulling him into a firm, unyielding deep-pressure hug.

"<lang="fr-CA" trans="Quiet, Julep,">Tranquille, Julep,</lang>" Gwendolyn murmured, her voice an incredibly soft, grounding anchor amidst the chaos of the airport. She held him tightly, her steady heartbeat pressing against his ribs.

Julian gasped, his rigid shoulders fighting the hold for only a fraction of a second before completely melting into his older sister's embrace. He buried his face against her shoulder, his trembling hands gripping the fabric of her sweater.

"<lang="cy" trans="You do not need the passport for the plane, love,">Nid oes angen y pasbort arnoch chi ar gyfer yr awyren, cariad,</lang>" Gwendolyn explained in a low, soothing Welsh lilt, rocking him gently. "We just need the paper boarding pass to get on the aircraft. That's it. Mom has your British passport zipped away, completely safe. You will hold it tomorrow morning when we land, and you will hand it to the guard to go home. I promise you are not being left behind."

Julian let out a long, shuddering exhale against her shoulder. The terrifying logic puzzle in his mind had been solved. He squeezed her tightly one last time before leaning back, offering a small, exhausted nod.

Gwendolyn smiled, smoothing a stray piece of hair from his forehead. " Allez. Let's get on this plane."

The transition down the jet bridge was executed flawlessly. Gwendolyn seamlessly transferred Julian from his customized chair into the middle seat of Row 12, while Gabriel parked the manual wheelchair at the threshold of the aircraft door, making absolutely certain the baggage handlers saw the neon-orange gate-check tag before he stepped inside.

Within minutes, the perimeter was locked. Julian sat securely in his seat, his heavy isolation headphones resting comfortably around his neck, perfectly flanked by Maria on his right and Clara on his left. Across the aisle, Eira settled into her window seat, leaning her head against Richard’s shoulder with a profound, exhausted sigh of relief.

Directly behind Julian, Gabriel and Gwendolyn slid into Row 13.

The heavy, reinforced cabin door was pulled shut with a definitive, mechanical thud. The ambient noise of the airport vanished, replaced by the deep, resonant hum of the wide-body jet's auxiliary power unit and the rush of the overhead air conditioning.

Gabriel stowed his duffel under the seat in front of him and sat back. He let out a massive, heavy breath, the intense, volatile frontline energy that had kept his muscles coiled like springs all day finally, completely bleeding out of his jaw and shoulders.

Beside him, Gwendolyn closed her tactical binder, slipping it into the seatback pocket.

The nineteen-year-old twins looked at each other. They didn't need to speak. They had spent their entire childhoods as hyper-vigilant human shields, constantly scanning their environment for the heavy footsteps of their father, and later, standing as the impenetrable wall that kept the world from overwhelming their little brother.

But right now, the doors were sealed. The threat was entirely gone.

Gabriel shifted the armrest between them up and out of the way. He slid closer to the middle seat, wrapping his heavy, broad arm securely around his twin sister's shoulders.

Gwendolyn didn't hesitate. The brilliant, calculating tactician immediately let her guard down, leaning her entire body weight against Gabriel's side. She rested her head comfortably against his chest, tucking her feet up onto the edge of her seat, and closed her eyes.

It was a quiet, domestic cuddle—the exact same way they slept every night in their shared bedroom in Virginia to keep the ghosts at bay. But here, sitting on a transatlantic jetliner taxiing toward the runway, the embrace felt infinitely lighter.

Gabriel rested his chin against the top of her head, his eyes tracking the tarmac out the window. They were flying over the ocean, leaving the remnants of Thomas Cross's America entirely behind them. They were going back to the cobblestones, the cold salt air, and the language of their roots.

" Rydyn ni'n mynd adref, " Gabriel whispered into his sister's hair.

Gwendolyn smiled against his shirt, her breathing slowing to match his steady, rhythmic pulse. They were finally going home.
