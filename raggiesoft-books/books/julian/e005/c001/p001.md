The pneumatic hiss of the PRT Blue Line bus cutting through the crisp morning air signaled their arrival. At precisely 9:55 AM, the doors opened at the corner of Richmond Road and West Duke of Gloucester Street.

Julian pulled a deep breath of the freezing Virginia air into his lungs. The cold bit at his cheeks, but beneath his heavy winter coat, he was entirely secure. The cobblestones of DoG Street rumbled familiarly beneath the tires of his customized manual wheelchair as his older brother, Gabriel, pushed him forward.

Maria walked so closely to his right that her coat brushed against his sleeve, her hand resting naturally over his on the armrest. Clara mirrored her exactly on the left. The identical twins moved in perfect, rhythmic tandem—a living, breathing shield that had stood between Julian and the world since they were all seven years old. There was no jealousy or division between the sisters; Maria was his wife simply because he had warmed up to her first as a child, but both women held equal, fierce real estate in his heart. Gabriel and Gwendolyn led the formation, their posture radiating a dense, defensive energy.

Their family had moved to the United States from Llanfairpwllgwyngyllgogerychwyrndrobwllllantysiliogogogoch as a planned relocation when Julian was six, long before the nightmare of his abduction at age fourteen. Now, a decade after his rescue, a stranger named Samantha had summoned them.

The heavy brass bell above the Crown & Hound’s door chimed.

The immediate rush of warm air, scented with roasted coffee and the rich, earthy smoke of the massive stone hearth, thawed the January frost from Julian’s face. He let out a soft, bright hum of approval, his head turning to track the bustling morning crowd.

Then, the crowd parted, and Julian saw him.

Parked at the back of the pub was a massive, heavy-duty Axiom Mag-Drive. The nineteen-year-old young man sitting in it—Matthew—was positioned exactly as Julian was: flanked by fierce, vigilant protectors. The moment Julian’s eyes locked onto Matthew, the ambient noise of the pub seemed to dial down to a hush. He knows, Julian thought, a profound sense of recognition washing over him. He knew exactly what the dark looked like.

Gabriel pushed Julian through the tables, bringing their two families face-to-face.

Julian watched Samantha stand. She possessed the undeniable, terrifying authority of a CEO, but as she looked at his family, her eyes softened. She recognized the formation. She recognized Maria and Clara’s unbreakable bond with him. She didn't see a broken twenty-four-year-old; she saw the center of a fiercely guarded universe.

Matthew reached out with his good hand, offering a stiff, polite wave.

A brilliant, genuine smile broke across Julian’s face. He let out a loud, happy vocalization, lifting his hand to tap out a rhythmic greeting against the metal push-rim of his wheel. Matthew tapped the armrest of his Mag-Drive in return. No words were needed. The bridge was built. They were both survivors.

As they settled around the table, parking Julian safely between Maria and Clara, Samantha opened her manila folder. When she slid the printed copy of the 1997 album art across the table, Julian’s breath caught. The boy in the wheelchair looking at the Northern Star.

Samantha’s voice dropped into a terrifyingly calm register as she explained why she had summoned them. She spoke of Matthew’s abuser dragging him three thousand miles across the country two weeks before Christmas. He was entirely unmedicated, isolated, and trapped.

Julian felt Maria’s fingers instantly lace through his on the right. Clara’s hand clamped firmly onto his left shoulder. The phantom sensation of a moving car, the terrifying blur of highway lights, and the crushing weight of being taken from his mother rushed back to him.

But Samantha wasn't just sharing trauma. She looked at Jyn, explaining how Jyn had defied her own blood cousin, acted as a physical shield for Matthew, completely neutralized the threat, and bought a plane ticket to fly him back across the country to Virginia.

Samantha slid a legally binding contract across the table. She had bought Vanguard Horizon out. She owned the master recordings, the publishing rights, and the trademark to The Winter Palace.

"Sign this, and the rights instantly transfer back to you, completely free and clear," Sam told them. She was commissioning them to finish the rock opera. "Finish Julian's story," she said. "And help us tell Matthew's".

The table descended into stunned silence.

Gwendolyn turned to him. "Julian," she said softly.

Gabriel, Maria, and Clara shifted their entire focus to him. They wouldn't touch that pen without his consent. Gabriel knelt to his eye level, asking if he wanted to go back into the studio.

Julian didn't look at his siblings. He slowly turned his head, his bright, observant eyes locking directly onto Matthew. Matthew sat perfectly still in his heavy Axiom Mag-Drive. Matthew reached out with his good hand, resting it flat on the edge of the pub table, offering a silent, physical bridge of solidarity.

Julian looked at Matthew’s hand. A profound, empathetic clarity washed over Julian's features. He understood that Matthew had no voice of his own, just like him. If The Winter Palace didn't sing for Matthew, his survival would remain trapped inside the walls of the Polaris Anchor forever.

Julian let out a loud, definitive, and joyful vocalization. He slammed his hand down enthusiastically against his push-rim, a massive smile breaking across his face. He nodded aggressively at Gabriel and Gwendolyn. Yes. We sing for him.

Gabriel wiped a tear and signed. Gwendolyn signed right beneath his name. The band was back.

But Julian wasn't finished.

He tapped his wheelchair frame again—a rapid, insistent, specific rhythm. He looked directly at Clara, letting out a rising, questioning tone. He squeezed Maria’s hand twice.

Clara smiled, her eyes instantly catching his meaning. She looked at Samantha. "He wants to know about the vault tapes. The other cuts."

Samantha blinked, her corporate strategist mind pausing to calculate the logistics. "Other cuts? I only know the standard radio edit of Upon This Winter's Night from '97. Did Vanguard Horizon lock away an acoustic demo? An extended twelve-inch club mix?"

Gwendolyn let out a sharp breath, her eyes suddenly burning with a new, fierce intensity. "Vanguard Horizon didn't just lock away different musical arrangements, Samantha. They buried entire languages."

Julian watched Samantha’s face shift from polite confusion to sudden, sharp realization.

"They only released it in English because they thought it was the only version that would chart in the States," Gabriel explained. "But we didn't just record it in English. We recorded it in Cymraeg and Québécois, too. And we did a fourth cut—a Trilingual master track blending all three."

"Cymraeg? Québécois?" Samantha repeated, glancing at Jyn, who looked equally surprised.

Gabriel offered a gentle, unapologetic smile. "We weren't born in the States, Samantha. Cymraeg is the Welsh language. Our family moved to the US when Julian was six. And our mother’s extended family is from Stanstead, Québec. Québécois is their French."

"Julian has always loved the languages," Maria added proudly, squeezing her husband's hand. "His absolute favorite version of Upon This Winter's Night is the trilingual track. Vanguard locked it in a vault and refused to let anyone hear it."

Julian nodded vigorously, letting out an excited, high-pitched vocalization. The English version of the song was beautiful, but it wasn't the whole truth. It didn't sound like his mother’s lullabies, and it didn't hold the deep, anchoring safety of the Welsh language from his early childhood.

Samantha stared at the young man, a profound understanding settling over her. Vanguard hadn't just stolen their story—they had stripped away their culture and buried the exact version of the song that brought Julian the most joy.

Samantha slowly reached into her folder and pulled out a blank, finalized cashier's check drawn directly from the Polaris Anchor's primary accounts.

"I am giving you a virtually unlimited budget," Samantha declared, her voice dropping into a register that commanded absolute authority. "I want a live string section. I want a brass section. And I want a full choir behind Gabriel's vocals".

She looked directly into Julian’s eyes, making an ironclad promise.

"And Julian? If Vanguard Horizon locked your favorite song in their vault... I own that vault now. We are pulling the trilingual master track. We are remastering it. By the Holiday Season of this year, for the tenth anniversary, the Trilingual cut will be the lead single."

Julian’s face lit up with pure, unadulterated joy. He grabbed Maria and Clara’s hands simultaneously, shaking them with excitement. He looked across the table at Matthew, a massive, brilliant smile lighting up his face.

They weren't just survivors anymore. They were a symphony.
