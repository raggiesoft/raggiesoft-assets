## 6:15 PM — The Newport News ARTS Platform

The evening train pulled into the brick ARTS—American Rail Transit Service—station in Newport News with a long hiss of air brakes and a cloud of white steam rising into the cold December air.

Up on the concrete platform, Katie and I stood side by side in our winter coats with our hands tucked into our pockets, watching the stainless-steel ARTS passenger doors slide open. Moments later, Grandma, our Aunt Elizabeth, and our twelve-year-old cousin Chelsea—Aunt Liz's developmentally disabled daughter, who was three years older than us—stepped down onto the platform carrying their holiday suitcases, and the whole family crowded together to exchange hugs and greetings.

True to form, before we had even walked ten feet past the station canopy toward the parking lot, Aunt Elizabeth wasted no time digging into her coat pocket and lighting up a cigarette.

Beside us, Chelsea immediately scowled and angrily plugged her nose with two fingers.

"Mom, that's gross," Chelsea stammered, waving a hand in front of her face as all of us were forced to stand on the cold curb waiting before we could load into the vehicles.

"Liz, put that out," our dad snapped, his breath fogging in the December chill as he hefted two suitcases into the trunk of our Holt sedan. "You know damn well that's not good for you!"

"Alright, alright," Aunt Liz snapped back, stubbing the cigarette out on the heel of her boot as we sorted ourselves into the two vehicles for the drive back across the Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel.

Chelsea climbed into the middle of the back bench seat of the Holt sedan right between Katie and me, while Dad and Aunt Liz sat up front. Behind us, Mom—who was having a rare sober evening so Grandma wouldn't ask questions at the ARTS station—drove Grandma in the pickup truck following our taillights down Interstate 64.

As soon as our sedan cleared the tunnel and turned onto the familiar streets of Virginia Beach, Katie leaned forward slightly to look across Chelsea at me, her eyes bright with holiday excitement.

"Welcome to Virginia Beach!" Katie squealed happily, squeezing Chelsea's mittened hand. "Now, the three of us are going to have to squeeze into one room!"

***

## 8:00 PM — Downstairs Sanctuary

When we pulled into the driveway at home, Katie, Chelsea, and I pitched in right away to help Grandma and Aunt Liz carry their overnight bags through the front door.

Standing at the foot of the stairs in the foyer, Dad laid out the holiday room assignments.

"You three will share the sofa bed," Dad said to us kids, gesturing toward the living room. Turning to the adults, he added, "Mom, Katie's room is closer to the upstairs bathroom. Liz, you get Nathaniel's room."

In a lot of houses, getting evicted from both of our upstairs bedrooms for the holidays would have felt like a punishment. To Katie and me, however, Dad had just handed us the greatest logistical gift of Christmas. With Grandma in Katie's room, Aunt Liz in my room, and Mom and Dad behind their closed door at the end of the upstairs hall, all four adults were confined to the second floor—which meant the entire first floor belonged exclusively to the three of us kids.

"Chelsea, there's a bathroom down here we can use," Katie explained immediately, pointing down the short hallway off the living room. "It has a shower. We can stay downstairs while the adults are upstairs."

"Okay," our cousin replied, setting her backpack by the couch. "Where do we sleep?"

"The three of us will sleep down here. This sofa is a sofa bed," Katie replied proudly as the three of us finished carrying the last of the adults' bags up the stairs.

Because the train had arrived late in the evening, dinner was fast and simple—warm bowls of soup and buttered rolls around the dining table—and before nine o'clock, the adults retreated upstairs for the night, leaving the first floor completely to the three of us.

"I want to take a shower," Chelsea announced to Katie and me once the upstairs footsteps quieted down.

Katie walked her down the short hall to show her how to turn the downstairs shower faucet on and where the clean towels were stacked, then came back out into the living room where I had already changed into my long-sleeved flannel pajamas and was busy pulling the heavy metal frame of the sofa bed out from inside the couch. Working together, Katie and I unfolded the mattress, smoothed out the clean cotton sheets, and spread two thick winter quilts across the top.

A few minutes later, the bathroom door clicked open. Instead of putting her pajamas on inside the bathroom, Chelsea wandered straight out into the living room wrapped only in a bath towel, making her way casually toward the sofa bed Katie and I had just finished setting up.

"CHELSEA!" Katie stammered, her hand shooting out in a flash to clamp firmly over my eyes. "Your cousin shouldn't be seeing all of you!"

Before Chelsea could even blink, Katie spun her right back around by the shoulders and shunted her straight back into the downstairs bathroom to finish drying off and put her flannel pajamas on properly.

"You need to stop blushing," my twin sister admonished me once the bathroom door clicked shut behind Chelsea—except she didn't truly admonish me at all, letting out a quiet, bubbly giggle as she took her hand off my face. "You promise not to tell anyone?"

"Promise," I replied, rubbing the bridge of my nose as we both climbed onto the left side of the wide sofa bed in our flannel pajamas and pulled the heavy winter quilt up to our chins.

"You know Chelsea sometimes forgets the rules when she's in a different house," Katie whispered, leaning her head against my shoulder underneath the quilt and lacing her fingers through mine. "As long as we look out for each other down here, nobody upstairs is going to yell at anyone."

"Not a word to anyone," I whispered back, squeezing her hand.

Two minutes later, Chelsea came back out of the hallway wearing her full green flannel pajamas, fuzzy socks, and a sheepish grin, and climbed under the quilt on the far side of the sofa bed. With the living room lamp clicked off and only the colored lights of the Christmas tree glowing softly in the corner, Katie stayed tucked right against my side—the two of us holding down our downstairs sanctuary while the winter wind rattled the front windows.
