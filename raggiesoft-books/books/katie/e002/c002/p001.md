## 4:15 PM — Tears Under the Blue Butterfly

Middle school had a way of taking kids who had been inseparable since birth and trying to pull them into two completely separate orbits.

For the first month of seventh grade, while I spent my afternoons quiet in my room with my video games, Katie had tried her hardest to fit into the normal middle-school social script—eating lunch with the girls from her English class and going out with a seventh-grade boy named Donnie. And because both of us were stressed out by the louder hallways, the changing class bells, and Mom's drinking getting worse at home, the two of us had fallen into the stupid habit of bickering over small things whenever we passed each other in the upstairs hall.

On Thursday afternoon, all of that stopped the second I walked up the stairs and heard the sound coming through Katie's bedroom door.

Katie was crying up a storm—deep, ragged sobs that echoed right through the hollow wooden door into the hallway.

I dropped my backpack by my door and stepped into her room to check on her and see what was wrong.

Katie was standing near the foot of her bed against the bright magenta-purple wall beneath her framed blue morpho butterfly print, her shoulders shaking inside her dark navy t-shirt and her hands pressed over her face.

"Leave me alone," she sobbed, turning her head half away when she heard my sneakers on the carpet. "You'd never understand."

I didn't walk out, and I didn't snap back the way a typical middle-school brother would have. Stepping quietly across the room, I placed my hand gently on her shoulder.

"What is it?" I asked softly.

Katie continued sobbing for several seconds, her chest heaving as she tried to catch her breath. I realized immediately that this wasn't a small hallway argument—this was serious. It took her a long moment to realize that I wasn't going to tease her or make fun of her for crying.

Slowly, she lowered her hands from her tear-streaked face.

"Donnie dumped me," Katie choked out, fresh tears spilling down her cheeks. "I loved him, and he dumped me!"

I reached my hand out to gently wipe the tears from her eyes. Never having had a girlfriend myself—and never having been dumped—I didn't fully understand the mechanics of middle-school romance, but I didn't need to understand romance to see how badly my twin sister was hurting.

Katie reached out with both hands and took mine, holding onto my fingers tightly as if she were drowning and I was the only solid ground in the room.

![Early-teen Katie Prairie and her twin brother Nathaniel Prairie standing inside Katie's purple-walled bedroom in front of the framed blue butterfly artwork, holding both hands as Nathaniel comforts her after she was dumped]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b002/katie_dumped_bedroom_butterfly_holding_hands.jpg)

"Please... don't let go," she whispered, her voice raw.

I closed both of my hands around hers and held on firmly. Looking straight into my eyes with a sudden, desperate seriousness, Katie asked the question that mattered most to survival in a middle-school cafeteria:

"Will you sit with me at lunch?"

The look on Katie's upset face told me the only answer she needed to hear—and it was the exact answer I wanted to give her anyway.

"Yes," I answered without hesitation. "I'll sit with you at lunch."

Katie burst into fresh tears of pure relief, letting go of my fingers only to throw both arms around my neck and pull me into a tight hug.

"Thank you," she whispered against my shoulder.

I could hear how raspy and sore her throat was from all of her crying, so I offered to run downstairs and get her a cold glass of water, but she shook her head and held onto my shirt even tighter. As I wrapped my arms around her waist and hugged her back in the quiet room, I felt something shift permanently inside my chest. All the petty squabbling we had done over the past few weeks felt completely, utterly stupid. Katie didn't want to stop hugging me, and I didn't have a single problem with that.

"Please, no more fighting," my sister sobbed softly into my shoulder.

"I understand," I promised, resting my chin against the top of her blonde hair as we continued to hold onto each other.

"I'm still upset, but thanks for this," Katie murmured a minute later as I felt a few more warm tears soak into the shoulder of my maroon t-shirt.

"Katie, I'm here for you," I told her. And as I said the words out loud, squeezing my arms around my twin sister, I realized just how much more I loved hugging her and keeping her safe than arguing with her.

## 5:00 PM — The Pretend Date & PB&J Sanctuary

When her crying finally began to subside, the two of us sat down side by side on the edge of her green-and-gold patterned bedspread, our shoulders pressed together and our hands still linked.

"Thank you," Katie said quietly, wiping her eyes with the back of her wrist. "Thank you for being there for me. Someday, I know I'm going to repay this."

She went quiet for a beat, looking down at our joined hands, before she suddenly whispered:

"Take me to the Dinner Dance this week."

The seventh-grade Dinner Dance was tomorrow night in the school cafeteria, and half the reason Katie had been crying so hard was the thought of either staying home alone in embarrassment or walking into that room by herself while Donnie was there.

"Of course we can go together," I said immediately, knowing I didn't have a date of my own—and wouldn't have wanted to go with anyone else even if I did. "Even though you're my sister, we will go on a pretend date."

"Oh, thank you. Thank you," Katie breathed, the last of the heavy sobs finally leaving her chest as she leaned her head against my shoulder.

"Would you like it if I made you dinner tonight?" I asked, deciding right then and there to start playing my part as her protective pretend boyfriend.

"Let's just have something simple, like sandwiches," she said softly.

"Alright," I replied, giving her hand a gentle squeeze as the two of us stood up and headed downstairs together.

In the quiet kitchen, I made us both thick peanut-butter-and-jelly sandwiches and poured two tall glasses of cold milk to soothe her sore throat. After we finished eating at the table, Katie looked across at me.

"Could we watch a movie together?" she asked.

"Sure," I replied warmly. "Let's pick something together."

We walked back upstairs to my deep-blue bedroom, picked out a comedy tape from the shelf beneath my television stand, and pushed it into the VCR. As the opening studio credits began to roll across the CRT screen, Katie kicked off her shoes and climbed into my single bed right at my side, resting her head on my shoulder and pulling the blanket across our legs.

Near the end of the movie, when the closing music faded in, I glanced down and saw that my sister was fast asleep against my arm, her breathing slow and completely peaceful after an afternoon of crying her eyes out.

I reached carefully for the remote, clicked the television off, and decided right then to let her sleep.Katie was exhausted, and the last thing I wanted was to wake her up and make her trudge down the hall alone in the dark. Part of me marveled at how natural it felt sharing my narrow bed with my twin sister again—just like we had during the Blizzard of '96 and on the downstairs sofa bed at Christmas. We had chosen this close brother-and-sister bond together, and enjoying the warm, quiet company of the person who mattered most to me in the world, I slid down onto the pillow beside her and let myself drift off to sleep at her side.
