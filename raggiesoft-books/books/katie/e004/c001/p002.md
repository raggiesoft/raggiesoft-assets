## Monday, January 6, 2003, 8:05 AM — Blindsided Outside Room 204

"Save it, Nathaniel—don't you dare roll up to my locker with that innocent smile on your face and pretend you didn't do anything wrong!" Heather Collins snapped at five minutes past eight on Monday morning, slamming her locker door shut so hard that the metal vents rattled down the second-floor hallway of Northwood High School.

My right thumb froze on the joystick of my compact SwiftChair power wheelchair, bringing my chair to an abrupt halt three feet from her locker outside Room 204.

It was Monday, January 6, 2003—the very first morning back at school after winter break. Because Heather's parents had taken her and her cousin Wesley up to the Shenandoah Valley to visit her grandparents between Christmas and New Year's Day, I hadn't seen her in person for over a week. All morning long on the CVTA Route 15 bus ride and up the school elevator with Katie, I had been grinning from ear to ear, carrying a small folded note in my jacket pocket asking Heather if she wanted to meet Katie and me at Emerald Creek Mall that coming Saturday afternoon.

Instead, as Katie rolled her power chair up beside mine in the busy hallway before first period, I found myself staring into a face I barely recognized.

The warm, gentle girl who had held my hand on the second-floor bridge of the mall, who had defended my bond with my twin sister because of how fiercely she loved her cousin Wesley, was staring down at me with hard, glassy eyes and a jaw clenched so tight her chin was trembling.

"Heather, what's wrong?" I asked, completely blindsided as my stomach dropped into my shoes. "Did something happen over break? I don't even know what you're talking about!"

"Oh, you don't know what I'm talking about?" Heather laughed—a short, jagged, bitter sound that made several students walking past us turn their heads. "Really, Nate? Look down the hall by the stairwell!"

I glanced over my shoulder toward the east stairwell. Leaning against the cinderblock wall in his varsity jacket with a smug, spiteful smirk plastered across his face was Tyler—the loudmouth senior who had stumbled down our hallway with a red plastic cup on New Year's Eve and banged his fist on Katie's bedroom door when we refused to join Brittany's drunken rave.

"Tyler was in my homeroom ten minutes ago bragging to half the senior class about the wild New Year's Eve rave at your house off Military Highway," Heather said, her voice shaking with a cold, venomous fury that cut straight through the hallway chatter. "He told everybody how you got yourself and some girl wicked drunk on gas-station liquor, locked the two of you inside a back bedroom with a chair wedged against the doorknob, and spent the whole night hooking up behind a locked door while the party went on outside!"

For two full seconds, my brain couldn't even process the sheer absurdity of what I was hearing.

"What?!" I gasped, staring at her in disbelief. "Heather, that's a complete lie! Neither one of us drank a single drop of alcohol!"

"And you know what the worst part is, Nate?" Heather cut me off, her voice cracking with bitter humiliation as angry tears welled in the corners of her eyes. "When Tyler first started laughing in homeroom about how you locked yourself in a back bedroom all night with a girl, I actually defended you! I immediately assumed you were in that room with *Katie*—because I know the two of you always stick together and look out for each other! I looked right at Tyler in front of everybody and said, *'That was his twin sister Katie, you idiot!'* And Tyler looked me dead in the eye, laughed in my face, and told the whole room it *wasn't* Katie—that it was some blonde girl from the party who got wasted with you before you locked the door in his face!"

Beside me, Katie's blue eyes went wide with outrage.

"Heather, Tyler *only* saw the back of my blonde head from the hallway before Nate shut the door in his drunken face!" Katie spoke up immediately, rolling her power wheelchair six inches forward to put herself right alongside me. "It *was* me in that bedroom! Our cousin Brittany broke our dad's ground rules and invited strangers with alcohol into our house while our dad, our aunt, and our cousin Chelsea were in Newport News! Nate and I grabbed an unopened box of dry cereal out of the pantry so we'd have food to eat and locked ourselves in my bedroom all night just to stay safe and away from them! Tyler was furious that Nate wouldn't drink with him or join the rave—he's lying to get back at us!"

I reached into my jacket pocket with a shaking hand, desperate to make Heather hear the truth.

"Heather, please, listen to me—Katie's telling the absolute truth!" I pleaded, my chest aching as if someone had dropped a cinderblock onto my sternum. "We ate dry toasted oat cereal out of a cardboard box and drank two cans of ginger ale while watching the ball drop on a thirteen-inch TV! I literally promised Katie at midnight that I'm never even having a twenty-first birthday drink because watching Tyler and those kids act like drunken idiots disgusted me so much! You know me, Heather—how could you ever believe I would get drunk or cheat on you?"

Slowly, painfully, I realized from the cold, shuttered look on Heather's face that the damage was already done.

Sitting in front of twenty kids in homeroom while Tyler laughed in her face and told everyone her boyfriend was locked in a bedroom all night with another girl had humiliated her to the core—and once that bitter poison had taken root in her chest, no amount of truth about a cardboard box of dry cereal was going to undo it.

"Save it for somebody gullible enough to buy it, Nathaniel," Heather said coldly, stepping back from our two wheelchairs as the five-minute warning bell rang overhead. "I stuck up for you in front of everybody because I thought you were different from every other guy at this school, and you made me look like a complete fool. Don't call my house, don't wait for me at the bus loop, and don't ever talk to me again. We're done."

Before I could get another word out of my throat, Heather spun on her heel, walked briskly down the hallway past Tyler—who flashed me one last triumphant, nasty grin before disappearing into his classroom—and vanished around the corner toward the science wing.

## Monday, January 6, 2003, 3:45 PM — Roles Reversed Under the Blue Butterfly

All through first period, lunch, and afternoon classes, I felt like I was moving underwater.

What hurt more than anything wasn't just that my very first relationship had ended in less than sixty seconds on a Monday morning—it was the crushing, helpless injustice of being condemned for the exact opposite of who I was. I had followed every rule my father had laid down; I had protected my twin sister from a living room full of reckless drunks; I had sworn off alcohol for the rest of my life; and yet because one angry liar wanted revenge for having a bedroom door closed in his face, the girl I had been head over heels in love with for a year and a half now hated my guts without ever knowing I was innocent.

By the time the CVTA Route 15 Beacon V bus lowered us down the Elevate-U hydraulic stairwell lift onto the sidewalk along Military Highway at three-thirty that afternoon and we rolled up the aluminum ramp into our house, the knot in my throat was so tight I could barely breathe.

Without saying a word, Katie steered her power chair ahead of me down the short hallway into her bedroom—the exact same room where we had hidden six nights earlier—and patted the edge of her mattress beneath the framed blue butterfly print on the wall.

Just like we had done on that afternoon in seventh grade when Chad had dumped Katie in the cafeteria of Bayside Middle School—except with our roles completely reversed—we locked our wheelchair brakes side by side and transferred onto the edge of her bed.

As soon as I sat down, I buried my face in my hands, my shoulders shaking as the bitter sting of Heather's final words replayed in my ears.

Beside me, Katie didn't lecture me or tell me to tough it out. Instead, she wrapped both of her arms tightly around my shoulders, pulled my head down against her shoulder, and rested her cheek against my blonde hair while her hand rubbed slow, steady circles across my back.

"It isn't fair, Katie," I choked out against the sleeve of her sweater, my voice breaking in the quiet bedroom. "I didn't even know what she was talking about when I rolled up to her locker. We were sitting on this exact bed eating dry cereal out of a box to stay away from Tyler's booze, and she thinks I betrayed her."

"I know, Nate. I was right there with you every single second," Katie whispered fiercely, holding me with every ounce of strength in her seventeen-year-old arms. "Tyler is a vindictive jerk who couldn't stand that you wouldn't act like an idiot with him, and if Heather threw away everything she knew about your character over one drunk liar's story without even listening to the two of us, then she didn't deserve my brother's heart in the first place."

I lifted my head, wiping my eyes with the back of my wrist as I looked at my twin sister.

In seventh grade, when Chad had walked out on Katie and left her crying in the middle school hallway, I had held her right under that same framed blue butterfly print and promised her that I would never leave her behind. Now, five years later at seventeen—with both of our power wheelchairs parked side by side at the foot of the bed—Katie reached out, laced the fingers of her left hand tightly through my right hand, and gave me the exact same unbreakable anchor.

"Remember what you told me in seventh grade when Chad broke my heart, Nate?" Katie said softly, her blue eyes locked onto mine. "Boyfriends and girlfriends can walk away over a rumor in a sixty-second hallway conversation. Twin Sisters never walk away. You and I know the truth about who you are, and we've got each other for life."

Taking a long, steadying breath, I squeezed Katie's hand back and leaned my head against her shoulder until the shaking in my chest finally subsided—knowing beyond a shadow of a doubt that as we headed toward high school graduation and our adult lives together, the only person in the world I would ever completely trust was sitting right beside me.
