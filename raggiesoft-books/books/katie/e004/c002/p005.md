## Saturday, June 14, 2003, 11:52 AM — Two Squeezes, Cautious Ground Rules, & Wesley's Cheer Under the Canopy

![Outside the Virginia Beach Pavilion under the white steel space-frame canopy and blooming pink crape-myrtle trees after Northwood High's June 2003 Commencement, eighteen-year-old Katie and Nathaniel Prairie sit side by side in their two compact black SwiftChair power wheelchairs in royal-purple caps and gowns as Heather Collins kneels warmly hugging Nate with Katie's supportive hand on his shoulder while sixteen-year-old Wesley holds his blue silicone ring and pumps his right fist high in the air cheering.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b004/jun_2003_pavilion_accepted_apology_wesley_cheering_hug.jpg)

Katie gave my left hand two slow, questioning squeezes across our padded inner armrests.

Before answering a single word out loud, I turned my head toward my twin sister beneath the dappled pink shade of the crape-myrtle blossoms and looked straight into her blue eyes. Five months of sitting in the back corner of Room 204 had taught both of us how deeply a slammed metal locker door could shake our nervous systems—and neither Katie nor I was naive enough to pretend five months of hurt vanished with the snap of a finger. Yet standing three feet in front of our footrests wasn't the proud, defensive teenager who had panicked in front of twenty gossiping classmates in January; standing in front of us was someone who had owned every word of her mistake without a single excuse. And right beside her purple sleeve stood sixteen-year-old Wesley, rocking up onto the balls of his dark sneakers with his smooth blue silicone ring held between both palms and his whole heart shining out of his brown eyes—hoping against hope he wasn't about to lose the first real guy friend he had ever had.

Even if nothing else in the world had moved us, neither Katie nor I had it in us to break Wesley's heart.

Katie's protective grip softened by half an inch, and she squeezed my fingers twice again—steady, cautious, and clear: *Let's give her a careful chance, Twin Brother—for Wesley's sake and yours, with firm ground rules from minute one.*

Taking a deep breath of warm June salt air, I squeezed Katie's hand twice right back and looked up into Heather's wet hazel eyes.

"What happened at that locker in January really scared both of us, Heather," I said quietly and plainly, keeping my left hand anchored in Katie's while setting the boundary out in the open. "Katie and I never lie to each other, and we never lied to you. We're willing to take this slow and give this a careful second chance—for Wesley's sake, and to see if we can rebuild trust step by step—on one strict condition. There can never be another morning where somebody's rumor makes you raise your voice or slam a door before letting us finish a sentence. If something ever confuses you or worries you, you come straight to Katie and me and ask us calmly, face to face."

"Every single time—I swear on everything I care about, Nate, and to you too, Katie," Heather breathed out, a fresh tear slipping past her lower lashes as she nodded so earnestly her purple mortarboard tassel swung across her cheek. "Slow and cautious is more than I earned. Never again will I yell over either of you. I come straight to both of you first, and I listen."

Beside me, Katie offered Heather a guarded, genuine smile across my shoulder. "Starting slow from today—with calm voices, zero yelling, and Saturday pretzels with Wesley back on the calendar—we'd both like that, Heather. Welcome back."

"Yeah," I said, feeling five months of tight chest armor loosen as a real smile broke through. "Let's start over, Heather."

Just behind us across the sunlit concrete plaza, the heavy glass lobby doors of the Virginia Beach Pavilion swung wide for the departing symphonic band, sending one last bright, brassy swell of *Pomp and Circumstance* rolling out beneath the white steel space-frame canopy.

Sixteen-year-old Wesley didn't flinch at the horns—he exploded with pure, unfiltered joy.

Letting out a booming, triumphant three-note cheer from deep in his chest that turned half a dozen smiling parents' heads along the colonnade, Wesley shifted his blue silicone ring into his left hand, pumped his right fist straight up toward the canopy rafters, and bounced twice on his sneakers with the biggest ear-to-ear grin I had ever seen on a human being. Setting her purple leatherette diploma folder gently down onto the clean concrete beside my right wheel so she wouldn't bump either joystick, Heather knelt right beside my SwiftChair and wrapped both purple-robed arms warmly around my shoulders while Katie reached across to rest her left hand over my shoulder in quiet support.

Closing my eyes for five full seconds with my forehead resting against Heather's temple, Katie's warm palm steady on my collarbone, and Wesley's happy humming blending right into *Pomp and Circumstance* overhead, I hugged Heather back. When she eased back wiping her cheeks and smiling through relieved tears, Wesley stepped straight up beside my right armrest and held out his fist; I bumped the back of my knuckles twice against his—firm, unhurried, and unbroken.

## Sunday, June 22, 2003, 4:10 PM — Moving Boxes for Saint Paul, Aunt Linda Staying in Virginia, & Ten Campuses for Rising-Junior Wesley

"Read subsection B one more time for your father, Heather, so I make sure I'm looking at the real state tuition math," Mr. Collins said mildly across our round oak kitchen table on Arvin Road eight days later, adjusting his wire-rimmed reading glasses over two yellow legal pads and a printed copy of the Community College of Hampton Roads tuition schedule.

Outside our kitchen window, late-June cicadas hummed in the backyard maples while sixteen-year-old Wesley—freshly finished with his sophomore year at Northwood High—sat happily beside my right wheel at the end of the table, lining up warm cinnamon-sugar pretzel bites on a paper napkin and tapping my knuckles whenever a blue-and-silver bus commercial flashed across our muted living-room television. Across from Heather's parents—whose Kempsville house was closing next Monday as Mr. and Mrs. Collins retired back home to suburban Saint Paul, Minnesota—sat Wesley's mother, Aunt Linda, and our dad, with mugs of iced tea sweating on cork coasters between them.

"It's Section 23-7.4 of the Code of Virginia—continuous one-year in-state college domicile," eighteen-year-old Heather explained clearly, sliding the highlighted sheet across the oak grain so her parents, Aunt Linda, and my dad could all read the columns side by side. "Because we lived right here in Hampton Roads all through Northwood High School, I already hold full Virginia in-state domicile right now—both for starting my education core at CCHR this fall and for transferring into a Special Education teaching degree in Virginia. If I pack up with Mom and Dad next week, I pay out-of-state rates or lose continuous Virginia residency right when Aunt Linda and Wes need me here."

Aunt Linda looked thoughtfully from the tuition sheet over to sixteen-year-old Wesley—whose shoulders were completely relaxed six inches from my elbow—and spoke with quiet, maternal resolve to her sister and brother-in-law.

"You two worked thirty years to go home to Minnesota, and you deserve every quiet morning by the lake," Aunt Linda told Heather's parents warmly. "For Wesley and me, though, staying right here in Virginia makes all the sense in the world. Moving Wes thirteen hundred miles right before eleventh grade would pull him out of the Special Education team and developmental-services caseload he already has in place here in Virginia Beach, drop him to the bottom of a brand-new state's multi-year waiting list right before he turns eighteen, and tear him away from the only guy friend he has ever had. Heather moves into the second bedroom of my apartment ten minutes down Kempsville Road, keeps her in-state tuition, and helps me with Wes while he finishes junior and senior year at Northwood High."

"And while there's zero deadline pressure right now between Wesley's sophomore and junior years," Heather added earnestly, glancing from Aunt Linda to Katie and me, "Aunt Linda and I want to use late July and early August—right after Katie and Nate get unpacked in their new Norfolk apartment—to show Wes ten different campuses: nine across Virginia, plus North Star State University when all of us fly up to Minnesota in August so Katie and Nate can meet the rest of our extended family for the first time. That way, by the time Wes graduates high school in two years, he'll already know inside and out where he feels safe."

Dad leaned back in his wooden kitchen chair, nodding with genuine respect at Aunt Linda's foresight before looking squarely at Heather and me.

"If you four spend the summer scouting campuses from Tidewater to the Blue Ridge and up to Saint Paul—and if down the road Heather steps up as Wesley's legal guardian the way your family has planned—let's make sure the household rule stays crystal clear on every side," Dad said evenly.

Resting her left hand gently beside my right forearm without rushing my space, Heather looked across me straight into Katie's eyes first, and then down into mine.

"Wherever Nate goes, Katie goes too," Heather said simply. "That isn't a compromise—that's who the two of you are."

Katie smiled warmly across my shoulder, squeezed my left hand, and looked straight at sixteen-year-old Wesley before meeting Heather's and Aunt Linda's eyes.

"And wherever Heather goes, Wesley goes with her," Katie answered with equal certainty. "Nobody in our circle ever gets left behind."

"Two pairs, one team," I agreed, resting my right hand lightly over Heather's fingers while my left stayed anchored in Katie's—watching sixteen-year-old Wesley tap Heather's blank yellow legal pad twice with a bright grin as we wrote down Stop One for July.
