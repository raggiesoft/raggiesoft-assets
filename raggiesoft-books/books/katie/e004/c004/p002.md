## Saturday, July 26, 2003, 9:30 AM — Stop Five in Richmond & Stop Six in Fairfax: City Sirens at CVU & Traffic Jam Miles to George Wythe

"Watch the crosswalk countdown timer here—West Broad Street doesn't slow down even on a July Saturday," I called lightly over the rumble of six lanes of downtown Richmond traffic as Katie, Heather, Wesley, and I waited at the curb cut beside Monroe Park on the urban campus of Central Virginia University.

For Katie and me as dedicated transit nerds, rolling past a downtown James River Transit Commission Route 6 bus shelter every two blocks along West Broad Street had an undeniable metropolitan energy—wide city sidewalks, historic rowhouses converted into academic departments, and urban curb cuts at every corner.

For Wesley, however, Stop Five came with a wall of unbroken city noise.

Before we had even rolled two blocks from Monroe Park toward the student commons, a pair of city transit buses vented their air brakes with a sharp metallic hiss ten feet from the curb, a beverage delivery truck backed into an alley with a piercing reverse beeper, and two rescue ambulances wailed past at full volume heading east toward the downtown medical campus. Pressing his right palm flat over his right ear while gripping his smooth blue silicone ring tight inside his left shorts pocket, Wesley stepped in close against Heather's shoulder until we ducked inside the air-conditioned brick lobby of the education building. Even though the summer advisor inside was friendly and patient, Wesley kept glancing uneasily out the plate-glass doors at the honking West Broad Street traffic—tapping only a tentative sideways line next to Stop Five once we returned to the quiet cabin of the minivan.

Two hours of creeping bumper-to-bumper northbound up Interstate 95 through summer beach-traffic bottlenecks past Fredericksburg and Quantico brought us at one-fifteen that afternoon to Stop Six in Fairfax: George Wythe University.

Once Heather finally exited the highway onto the wooded suburban ring road circling George Wythe's sprawling campus, the physical grounds themselves were clean, modern, and thoughtfully ramped. Wide paved pathways wound beneath tall Northern Virginia oaks between contemporary brick lecture halls, and Wesley's shoulders relaxed noticeably compared to West Broad Street as we shared turkey sandwiches and cold apple juice from our cooler at a shaded courtyard table.

Yet when Katie unfolded our state highway map across her lap tray to check the mileage back to Tidewater, all four of us stared quietly at that long red artery of Interstate 95 stretching nearly two hundred miles south to Norfolk and Virginia Beach.

"Four hours each way in summer traffic whenever Aunt Linda wants to have Wesley home for Sunday dinner, or whenever Dad has a weekend off on Arvin Road," Heather murmured thoughtfully, tracing her finger past the Occoquan River bottleneck we had just crawled through at fifteen miles an hour. Looking over at Wesley, she asked gently, "Feels pretty far from Mom's kitchen and Emerald Creek Mall, doesn't it, Wes?"

Wesley looked down at the Tidewater corner at the bottom of the folded map, thought about sitting buckled into a van through four hours of brake lights just to hug Aunt Linda, and nodded once with quiet certainty before Heather turned west onto Interstate 66 and south down Interstate 81 toward Harrisonburg so we could catch the late-afternoon session of Stop Seven before checking into our accessible roadside hotel room for the night.

## Saturday, July 26, 2003, 4:05 PM — Stop Seven in Harrisonburg: Purple-and-Gold Balloon Arches & Cutting Shenandoah Valley University's Open House Short

"Uh-oh—Heather, look at all the charter buses lined up past the bluestone quad," Katie said softly as our minivan exited Interstate 81 onto the broad, green campus of Shenandoah Valley University in Harrisonburg five minutes past four.

When Heather had called Shenandoah Valley University's admissions office two weeks earlier to ask whether rising high-school juniors and transfer students could tour the campus and its College of Education and student-support center on the fourth Saturday of July, the student worker on the phone had cheerfully told us to come right over—forgetting to mention that today was SVU's biggest Summer Preview Open House of the entire season.

From the sweeping green lawn outside, the classic gray bluestone academic halls and Blue Ridge mountain backdrop looked genuinely welcoming—honestly catching my own eye right away as we rolled up the wide entrance ramp behind two families wearing purple-and-gold stick-on name tags while Wesley smiled with quiet curiosity beside my wheel.

Then two volunteers pulled both glass lobby doors wide open at once.

A wall of echoing sound slammed straight into our faces off the two-story tile atrium. Fifteen feet inside the lobby, a twenty-piece collegiate brass-and-snare pep band was blasting the SVU fight song at stadium volume beneath a giant purple-and-gold balloon arch. Two hundred prospective students and parents were packed shoulder-to-shoulder between folding brochure tables, shouting over the trumpets to hear student orientation leaders yelling into handheld megaphones—and right as Wesley's sneaker crossed the threshold, a stray gold latex balloon brushed a hot ceiling spotlight and popped like a firecracker overhead.

Wesley flinched from head to toe, clapped both hands hard over his ears, squeezed his eyes shut, and let out a sharp, distressed groan low in his throat.

None of the three of us waited half a second for politeness.

"Turn right back around—clear the ramp behind us, please," I said firmly over my shoulder as Katie and I spun our two SwiftChairs one hundred and eighty degrees in unison on the wide concrete landing while Heather immediately stepped between Wesley and the doorway, resting one steady, gentle hand at the center of his shoulder blades to guide him straight back out into the fresh summer breeze without anybody brushing his arms.

Two hundred yards away at the quiet far corner of the visitor parking lot beneath a row of shade maples—well out of earshot of the brass drums—Heather clicked the van's sliding doors open so Wesley could sit sideways on the cool bench seat with the air conditioning humming softly behind him. Without crowding him or peppering him with questions, Katie handed Heather a chilled juice box from the cooler, I kept my chair parked calmly beside his knee, and Heather sat quietly shoulder to shoulder beside him until Wesley slowly lowered both hands from his ears, took his blue silicone ring out of his pocket, and let out a long, shaky sigh.

"I'm so sorry about that racket, Wes," Heather said softly, squeezing his shoulder once he looked over at her. "Nobody told us there was going to be a pep band and megaphones crammed into one tile lobby. That tour is officially done for today—we don't have to take one more step inside."

Catching his breath in the shade, Wesley took a long sip of cold apple juice and looked back across the wide green quad toward the classic bluestone classroom buildings farther down the walk, where there weren't any balloon arches or megaphones at all. Reaching over to Heather's laminated clipboard on the seat between them, Wesley didn't draw an *X* through Stop Seven. Instead, he tapped his forefinger twice against the quiet bluestone quad out the window, held one hand flat over his ear and shook his head at the Open House crowd, and then pointed calmly back at the quiet classroom hall down the path.

"You actually liked the look of SVU outside—just not during a crazy Open House?" Katie smiled with instant understanding across our armrests.

Wesley nodded twice, giving a small, relieved half-smile and a quiet one-note hum.

"Honestly, I wouldn't mind coming back to learn more about SVU myself someday when it isn't a chaotic Open House," I agreed, looking back at the shaded bluestone quad against the mountains.

"Good thing we started looking early," Heather promised warmly, writing *Revisit SVU when it's not an Open House!* in neat blue ink beside Shenandoah Valley University on the sheet before buckling Wesley's seatbelt up front. "Whenever you two want a real look at Harrisonburg without a pep band within five miles, we'll come back on a quiet day when it's just ordinary sidewalks. Tonight we get a calm pizza dinner in our hotel room right here in Harrisonburg, get a full night's sleep, and tomorrow we finish the mountain loop down Interstate 81 at our last two Virginia stops."
