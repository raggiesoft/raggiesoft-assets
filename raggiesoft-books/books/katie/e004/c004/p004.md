## Saturday, August 2, 2003, 11:20 AM CDT — A Fresh Birch Ramp Outside Saint Paul & Meeting Heather and Wesley's Extended Family

"Hold the screen door wide, Grandpa—Nate and Katie made it all the way up from Virginia without the airline losing a single battery charger!" Heather laughed brightly as Aunt Linda eased her rented silver accessible minivan up to a freshly built, sturdy birch-plywood ramp bolted cleanly over the two front steps of a white clapboard farmhouse twenty minutes outside Saint Paul.

Before Katie and I had even finished rolling down the side ramp onto the level concrete driveway under tall northern white pines, Heather's gray-haired maternal grandfather—still brushing fresh birch sawdust off his flannel cuffs from building that porch ramp at seven o'clock this morning specifically for our chairs—stepped out onto the grass beside Heather's grandmother, Heather's newly relocated parents, two smiling aunts, and three cousins holding chilled pitchers of lemonade.

Nobody leaned over our armrests, patted our heads, or talked about us in the third person.

Instead, Grandpa shook my right hand and Katie's right hand firmly at eye level, Grandma set a plate of warm cinnamon rolls directly on the low patio table beside our chairs, and Wesley—positively glowing in his favorite blue polo shirt on his family's summer turf—proudly led Katie and me down the paved garden walk to show off the giant backyard oak tire swing he had played on every August since he was five, tapping his chest twice and pointing straight at me so every aunt, uncle, and cousin on the lawn knew beyond a shadow of a doubt that I was his guy friend from Virginia.

Later that afternoon around the shaded picnic tables, Aunt Linda and Heather's mother sat side by side watching Wesley share apple slices next to my wheel while Heather laughed quietly with Katie over our CCHR fall book list—not a trace of January's storm left in the air, just two sisters grateful that keeping Aunt Linda, Heather, and Wesley rooted back in Tidewater had given Wesley both sides of his world without losing either one.

## Monday, August 4, 2003, 10:30 AM CDT — Riding the Intercampus Transitway & Underground Winter Tunnels at North Star State University

On Monday morning, August fourth, Heather, Aunt Linda, Wesley, Katie, and I rode into the Twin Cities together to check off Stop Ten at the very bottom of Wesley's summer clipboard: North Star State University.

For two transit fans from Virginia Beach, boarding the wide, low-floor intercampus *NSSU Campus Connector* shuttle bus on its dedicated transitway bridge high above the sparkling Mississippi River—gliding smoothly from the bustling urban Minneapolis East Bank over to the leafy brick quads of the Saint Paul campus—was genuinely cool. Inside the education and inclusive-transition offices on the Saint Paul side, the advisors welcomed Wesley warmly, and the flat upper-midwestern walkways felt effortless under our SwiftChair wheels in the seventy-eight-degree August sunshine.

Then our student tour guide pointed overhead toward the enclosed glass second-story pedestrian skyways and led us down an elevator into the miles of fluorescent subterranean *North Star Way* tunnels linking the lecture halls underground.

"Once November hits and temperatures drop to fifteen or twenty below zero with two feet of lake-effect snow blowing off the river," the guide explained cheerfully, "almost nobody walks outside at street level until April—everybody uses the tunnels and the skyways!"

Down in that windowless concrete tunnel, Katie and I glanced once at the narrow double fire doors, thought simultaneously of what twenty-below-zero Minnesota January ice drifts would do to outdoor bus stops and twenty-four-volt wheelchair batteries—on top of forfeiting Heather's Virginia in-state tuition domicile and Wesley's Virginia Beach services—and saw Wesley tug gently on Heather's sleeve pointing back up toward the sunny Virginia beach-bus sticker on Katie's binder.

That evening back at Grandpa and Grandma's farmhouse dining table over homemade wild-rice soup, with all ten campuses across Virginia and Minnesota honestly seen ahead of Wesley's junior year of high school, nobody had to pretend or guess anymore. By the time Wesley walked across Northwood High's stage two Junes from now, everybody around that table knew Hampton Roads—starting at CCHR and looking right down Hampton Boulevard at Virginia Dominion University—was home, and hugging Heather's grandparents goodbye on the birch ramp Wednesday morning meant Katie and I flew home to Apartment A6 on Greenway Court with extended family cheering for us up north too.
