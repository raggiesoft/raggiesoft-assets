## Saturday, October 16, 2004, 1:15 PM — Empty Kitchen Cabinets, Unplugged Phone Cords, & The Mask Dropping Behind a Deadbolted Door

"Where's the dining-room table you told us about at the café, Brandon?" Nate asked quietly from his compact black SwiftChair just inside the narrow front hallway of Brandon Mercer's rented clapboard house two blocks off North Boundary Street.

I braked my own SwiftChair flush beside my twin brother's left armrest, feeling a sudden, cold prickle slide down the back of my neck.

Today was supposed to be a simple four-hour Saturday afternoon day visit—eating turkey sandwiches, watching an afternoon movie on DVD, and riding back down Interstate 64 in Brandon's rented side-ramp van so Nate and I would be home at Greenway Court before dark. Because this was my very first time visiting Brandon's house up in Williamsburg—while Heather was down in Virginia Beach helping Aunt Linda take Wesley to his Saturday afternoon sensory-gym appointment—Nate had come right along beside me as my chaperone, with both of our twenty-four-volt chargers, our CCHR laptops, our daily medication pouches, and our wallets holding Dad's laminated 1-800 calling cards and fresh thirty-day CVTA bus passes tucked securely inside our backrest backpacks.

Except the living room in front of us didn't look like someone hosting lunch. It looked like someone abandoning a lease in a hurry.

Every bookshelf along the plaster wall had been stripped completely bare. Two flattened cardboard boxes sat stacked beside the trash bin, the white modular telephone cord for the kitchen wall jack hung unplugged against the baseboard with no landline handset anywhere in sight, and propped right beside the half-open hall closet door sat an overstuffed black canvas duffel bag alongside two shiny red-and-white Canadian travel brochures for Ontario.

"Oh, my landlord's repainting next week, so I packed the clutter early," Brandon said, his voice losing half an octave of that patient Ghent café warmth as he clicked the front deadbolt shut behind us and slipped the rental-van keys deep into his trouser pocket. "Come look in the back study for one minute, Katie—Nate can wait right here by the television."

"Nate doesn't wait in another room—he's my chaperone today," I said evenly, keeping my right fingers laced through my brother's left hand across our armrests.

For the first time in five months of polite smiles, Brandon's jaw tightened with a flash of ugly, undisguised irritation.

"Drop the twin baby-blanket routine for five minutes, Katie—you're nineteen years old," Brandon snapped, stepping between the empty armchair and the hallway threshold. "Honestly, watching your brother shadow every breath you take down in Norfolk has been driving me up the wall since May, and my boss at the hospitality office is breathing down my neck over an audit Monday morning anyway. I've got a clean start lined up across the river in Windsor, Ontario—universal healthcare, nobody asking questions, and plenty of cash in my coat pocket to get you and me settled across the Canadian border by tomorrow afternoon."

My stomach dropped straight through the floor.

"Canada?!" I stared up at him in horror, backing my SwiftChair two inches closer to Nate's hub. "Brandon, have you lost your mind?! I came up here for Saturday lunch! I have classes at CCHR on Monday morning, I don't have a passport, and I am not going to Canada or anywhere else without my brother! Unlock the door and take Nate and me back to Norfolk right now!"

"I already turned the rental van back in at the agency lot at noon before walking home from the corner, sweetheart, so nobody's driving back to Norfolk," Brandon said coldly, stepping forward and clamping his right hand hard around my left wrist when I reached into my canvas lap pouch for my flip phone—twisting my arm sharp enough to leave five throbbing thumbprint bruises across my skin as he snatched both my phone and Nate's phone off our lap trays and shoved them into his coat pocket. "No calls to your dad, and no calls to your brother's loudmouth girlfriend down in Virginia Beach."

"Let go of my sister's wrist right now!" Nate barked, driving his front caster straight toward Brandon's shin until Brandon yanked his hand back with a sneer.

"Save the theatrics, Prairie," Brandon snapped, hoisting the heavy black canvas duffel bag onto his shoulder and checking his dark leather wristwatch. "The northbound afternoon train connects out of the Williamsburg depot two blocks down North Boundary Street at four-fifteen. Both of you are rolling quietly down the sidewalk with me right now while I buy our rail passes at the counter—and if either of you yells a single word on the street before I get my tickets printed, neither of you ever sees your phones again."

## Saturday, October 16, 2004, 3:25 PM — Two Silent Blocks Down North Boundary Street & Counting Every Second

"Keep your voice calm and don't fight him on the porch ramp," Nate whispered barely loud enough for my ears alone while Brandon turned to yank the front door open and check the quiet residential street. "Our wallets are still inside our zippered backpack pockets—he only took our cell phones. We still have Dad's 1-800 calling cards, crisp dollar bills, and our thirty-day CVTA passes."

My left wrist throbbed beneath my royal-blue sweater cuff, yet hearing Nate's steady voice right beside my shoulder snapped my racing autistic brain straight out of panic and into pure survival math.

Brandon thought taking two flip phones and turning in his rental van meant two nineteen-year-olds in power wheelchairs forty-five miles from Norfolk were trapped at his mercy. He had zero clue that Dad had drilled payphone calling-card numbers into our heads seven days ago, and even less clue what two lifelong transit riders looked for the second they rolled within sight of a bus loop.

Keeping our faces completely blank and obedient so Brandon wouldn't grab our rear push-handles or check our backpacks, Nate and I eased down the wooden porch ramp side by side and rolled two blocks down the brick sidewalk of North Boundary Street beside Brandon's hurried footsteps—watching the colonial red-brick roof of the Williamsburg Transportation Center rise straight ahead of us through the autumn trees.
