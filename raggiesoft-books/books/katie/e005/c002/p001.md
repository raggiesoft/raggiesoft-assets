## Friday, December 24, 2004, 3:30 PM — Eighteen Stops to Tonomy Hill & Wesley Meeting Cousin Chelsea for the First Time

"Nate! Katie! Welcome to Newport!" cried a slurred, overjoyed voice as the blue-and-white Ocean State Transit Authority Route 63 bus hissed away from the curb on Tonomy Hill in the crisp Rhode Island Christmas Eve wind and our twenty-two-year-old paternal cousin Chelsea hurried down her low wooden entry ramp with her familiar uneven gait, hugging a plush green stuffed sea turtle under one arm before throwing both arms warmly around Katie's shoulders and mine. "I'm s-so happy you came for Christmas!"

Because our Aunt Elizabeth had died earlier in 2004 from lifelong smoking not long after our Grandma Ruth passed away, Chelsea was facing her very first Christmas without her mama. With Dad stuck on holiday server cutover duty in Virginia Beach and Aunt Linda spending Christmas week with her sister's family, Dad had bought four round-trip Pathfinder tickets so nineteen-year-old Heather, seventeen-year-old Wesley, Katie, and I could ride north together and make sure Chelsea never spent Christmas Eve crying alone.

Getting two compact SwiftChairs plus two walking companions from Norfolk to Newport had been an eighteen-stop Eastern Seaboard bus marathon across four terminal transfers—Norfolk across the harbor to Hampton, Williamsburg, and Richmond; changing for Fredericksburg, Springfield, and Washington; crawling off Interstate 95 onto Interstate 395 through Downtown Baltimore traffic lights on West Fayette Street up through Newark, Delaware and Philadelphia into Manhattan's subterranean Port Authority Bus Terminal; changing up the Connecticut coast through Bridgeport, New Haven, Braxton, and Providence into Boston's South Station; and riding down through Fall River into the Newport Gateway Center to catch local OSTA Route 63.

Wiping a happy tear from the corner of her eye and taking one quick, practiced puff from her rescue inhaler in the December chill, Chelsea paused on the ramp and looked up with bright, curious eyes at nineteen-year-old Heather and tall, sandy-haired seventeen-year-old Wesley standing respectfully right behind my right wheel.

"Chelsea, this is my girlfriend Heather, and her cousin Wesley—he's my best guy buddy," I introduced them warmly. "Wesley doesn't talk with spoken words, Cous, and he loves buses and animals just like you."

For two seconds, twenty-two-year-old Chelsea looked straight at seventeen-year-old Wesley, and Wesley looked straight at the soft green plush sea turtle tucked under Chelsea's elbow.

Without a shred of shyness or adult awkwardness, Chelsea held her green plush turtle out toward Wesley with both hands. "This is S-Shelly Turtle! You wanna say hi?"

Wesley's whole face lit up like a Christmas tree. Letting out a gentle, delighted three-note vocal hum, Wesley reached one finger forward to tap Shelly Turtle's soft green plush flipper twice—and then held his smooth blue silicone ring up in his open palm so Chelsea could see his favorite treasure right back. Chelsea giggled with pure joy, tapped Wesley's knuckles gently in return, and ushered all four of us up the interior ramp into her cozy ground-floor apartment where paper snowflakes hung taped across the front window.

## Saturday, December 25 – Monday, December 27, 2004 — Hot Cocoa Around Shelly Turtle & Chelsea's West Main Road Shortcut Home

Across Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, and Sunday on Tonomy Hill, watching Heather, Wesley, and Chelsea together in that little apartment warmed something deep in Katie's chest and mine.

Never once did Heather blink or act uncomfortable around Chelsea's developmental delays, her chronic asthma inhalers, or the innocent, childlike way Chelsea hugged everybody in the family like we were still nine years old. When Chelsea came padding down the short hallway in an oversized cotton T-shirt looking for extra marshmallows for our hot chocolate, Heather helped her slip on her fuzzy pink fleece cardigan and warm socks with the exact same calm, matter-of-fact kindness she showed Wesley every morning—while out on the braided living-room rug, Wesley and Chelsea spent hours sitting side by side paging through Chelsea's picture books of sea turtles and lining up Wesley's miniature die-cast city bus beside Shelly Turtle without needing a single spoken sentence between them.

When Monday morning, December 27 arrived and all four of us zipped up our winter coats to head home to Virginia, Chelsea glanced down at our printed Pathfinder return tickets—and even with her developmental disability, spotted a timetable trap immediately. Our itinerary started at the Newport Gateway Center on a local run to Fall River, Massachusetts with only a five-minute transfer window to catch the connecting coach to Providence. Unloading two powerchairs on a hydraulic lift in Fall River in five minutes was physically impossible; we would miss the Providence bus cold.

"D-don't take Fall River bus!" Chelsea told us earnestly at the top of her wooden ramp, hugging Katie, me, and Heather tight before sharing one last knuckle-tap goodbye with Wesley and Shelly Turtle. "Take OSTA Sixty right out on West Main Road—goes straight over bridge to Providence Kennedy Plaza, whole hour early!"

Sure enough, rolling out to West Main Road and boarding <led>60 | PROVIDENCE | KENNEDY PLAZA</led> delivered all four of us straight over the Mount Hope Bridge into downtown Providence forty-five minutes early—sending Katie, Heather, Wesley, and me rolling smoothly down the Eastern Seaboard back to Tidewater with a quiet promise tucked in all four of our hearts that Chelsea would never be forgotten up north.
