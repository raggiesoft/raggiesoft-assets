## Saturday, July 16, 2005, 11:00 AM — Plenary Guardianship, Four Names on One Lease, & Keeping Heather's Door Open for Wesley

"Hold the drill steady right on that pencil mark, Nate—once this coat rod goes twenty-eight inches higher up the studs, you and Katie have the entire carpeted closet floor clear end to end," our forty-seven-year-old dad said cheerfully inside the sunlit corner bedroom of our new ground-floor three-bedroom Norfolk apartment five blocks south of Virginia Dominion University.

Two busy years since our cautious reunion outside the Virginia Beach Pavilion had brought all four of us—now twenty-year-old Katie, twenty-year-old Heather, eighteen-year-old Wesley, and me—to the exact household milestone we had promised around Dad's kitchen table in 2003.

In June, Katie, Heather, Dad, and I had cheered from the accessible row as eighteen-year-old Wesley walked across the graduation stage to celebrate finishing his high-school classes at Northwood High before entering the public school division's age-eighteen-to-twenty-one community transition program right here in Tidewater. Two weeks later, with Aunt Linda's arthritis making heavy daily lifting harder and her own retirement waiting beside her sister outside Saint Paul, twenty-year-old Heather—freshly admitted into Virginia Dominion University's Special Education Adapted Curriculum bachelor's program after completing her CCHR general-education credits alongside Katie and me—had stood calmly before a judge at the Circuit Court with Aunt Linda's full blessing to be appointed eighteen-year-old Wesley's plenary legal guardian and conservator.

Instead of Katie and me renewing our tiny single-room studio back on Greenway Court while Heather and Wesley rented a separate place down the street, the four of us had put all four names onto one wide, zero-step ground-floor three-bedroom corner apartment right between the Route 2 stop on Hampton Boulevard and the crosstown bus toward Kempsville.

Every room in the apartment honored how all four of us actually lived—with zero pretense and zero judgment.

Up front near the living room sat our shared study and coding table. Down the hallway, Heather had her own sunny bedroom, with eighteen-year-old Wesley's bedroom right next door—decorated with his oak-framed November 2001 *Annie's Pretzels* photo on his nightstand and its door steps from Heather's unlatched door so that whenever thunder or a bad dream scared Wesley in the middle of the night, he could walk straight next door, climb right into Heather's bed, and let his Safe Person cuddle him until his breathing steadied back into sleep.

And across the hall inside Katie's and my room stood a wide, carpeted three-by-six walk-in closet where Dad had just raised the wire shelving high overhead for our shared navy-blue sleeping bag and four pillows. Because ever since Brenda's footsteps in the Greenway Court back alley two Augusts ago Katie's and my autistic nervous systems still hid inside closets at night—curled up together inside our shared sleeping bag in the pitch dark—Heather smiled, kissed my cheek goodnight every evening, and insisted from Day One that nobody under our roof ever had to apologize for sleeping where their nervous system felt safest. Two pairs, four best friends, one unbreakable roof.
