## Thursday, November 24 – Friday, November 25, 2005 — Thanksgiving Dinner on Arvin Road & Matching Closet Shelving for Weekend Visits

"Smell that roasting bird!" Dad smiled from the stove of our single-story brick ranch on Arvin Road off Military Highway and Indian River Road as Katie and I rolled our compact SwiftChairs down the hallway into the warm, cinnamon-and-sage-scented kitchen at one o'clock on Thanksgiving afternoon while Heather carried in a warm bakery box of soft salted pretzel rolls for Wesley.

![Inside David Prairie's cozy brick ranch dining room on Arvin Road in Virginia Beach on Thanksgiving Day, November 24, 2005, twenty-year-old twins Katie and Nathaniel Prairie sit side by side in their black motorized SwiftChair power wheelchairs holding hands across their armrests and clinking tall glasses of iced tea over roast turkey and pumpkin pie with their beaming forty-seven-year-old father David.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b005/nov_2005_thanksgiving_dinner_dads_house_katie_nate_david.jpg)

Spending the whole four-day Thanksgiving holiday together at Dad's house—after calling Aunt Linda and Heather's parents on speakerphone up in snowy Saint Paul and calling Cousin Chelsea up on Tonomy Hill so Wesley could hum hello into the receiver—filled the brick ranch with the steadiest warmth any of us could remember. Twenty minutes later, with platters of carved roast turkey, herb stuffing, mashed potatoes and gravy, cranberry sauce, pretzel rolls, and green beans spread across the tablecloth, Dad raised his tall glass of iced tea and looked proudly around the table at Katie, me, Heather, and Wesley.

"Watching the four of you build a home together over in Norfolk—taking care of Wesley, acing your CCHR and VDU classes, and looking out for each other the way you did bringing Katie and Nate across the water last autumn—is the best Thanksgiving gift a father could ever ask for," Dad said softly behind his glasses.

"Nobody's ever putting a closed door between this crew, Dad," Katie smiled, squeezing my left hand across our armrests while Wesley tapped my right knuckles twice before clinking his apple-juice cup against Dad's glass.

The very next morning—Black Friday, November 25—Dad walked into our old back bedroom on Arvin Road carrying his yellow tape measure, spirit level, cordless drill, and white vinyl-coated wire shelving, raising the closet coat rod twenty-eight inches higher up the wall studs to match our Norfolk apartment so that whenever all four of us stayed over in Virginia Beach for holidays and weekends, Katie's and my three-by-six carpeted closet sanctuary stayed completely clear underneath while Heather and Wesley had the sunny guest bedrooms right across the hall.

## Sunday, November 27, 2005, 7:30 PM — The Seattle Transit Blog & Quietly Launching Ideas for Coastal Virginia Transit

"Look at this site I just found on OmniSearch, Katie," I said two evenings later on Sunday night of Thanksgiving weekend, angling our seventeen-inch CRT monitor slightly toward my sister's wheelchair along the wide computer desk on Arvin Road while Heather graded Special Education observation notes at the dining table behind us alongside Wesley. "It's called the Seattle Transit Blog."

Katie set her cold can of ginger ale on a coaster, released her brake, and rolled right up flush against my left armrest, resting her chin lightly on my shoulder as we read the screen together.

Out in Washington State, everyday bus riders, programmers, and wheelchair commuters who rode TAPS—the Transit Authority of the Puget Sound—had built a public blog dissecting King County and Pierce County bus corridors, regional Cascade Card smart-card fares, low-floor fleets, and timed light-rail feeder pulses with the exact kind of logical, map-by-map precision that made both of our autistic minds light up.

Katie looked from the glowing Seattle Transit Blog masthead down to the stack of folded paper Coastal Virginia Transit Authority pocket timetables spread across our desk: Route 12 along Indian River Road, Route 15 down Military Highway, Route 20 along Virginia Beach Boulevard, Route 25 and Route 29 into the CCHR Virginia Beach Campus, Route 1 up through Norfolk, and Route 61 across the Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel.

"Why doesn't Hampton Roads have something like the Seattle Transit Blog for CVTA?" Katie asked softly, a bright, unmistakable spark catching in her blue eyes. "Think about it, Nate. Look at today—it's Sunday here on Arvin Road, and we literally couldn't roll out to catch a bus anywhere in Kempsville without Heather driving the van because Virginia Beach shuts down almost its entire bus grid on Sundays!"

"And look at what happened to us last October coming down from Williamsburg," I added, the pieces locking cleanly together in my head. "It took six buses across two transit agencies to get from the Williamsburg train station down to Military Highway and Indian River Road—and if we hadn't caught that very last 6:45 PM Route 61 Expressways bus out of Newport News Transit Center to meet Heather and Wesley on Monticello Avenue, two powerchairs would have been stranded on the wrong side of the harbor all night!"

"Let's start our own blog tonight and jot our ideas down in public," Katie said, pulling her laptop closer across the desk. "Simple, constructive, and honest—*Ideas for Coastal Virginia Transit*."

Checking Omni Corporation's hosted BlogSphere service—which handled the post database, permalinks, RSS feed, and reader comments automatically while letting us switch into raw HTML mode—we registered *Ideas for Coastal Virginia Transit* under both of our first names and spent the next hour hand-coding a crisp, fast-loading coastal-navy, white, and teal two-column HTML and style-sheet template from scratch, linking the Seattle Transit Blog right at the top of our sidebar blogroll.

> Ideas for Coastal Virginia Transit<br>
> A Rider-Written Blog Exploring What Public Transit Across Hampton Roads Could Be<br>
> Written by Katie & Nate

Without naming Brandon Mercer, Katie typed and published our opening Sunday-night post laying out the six-bus Peninsula-to-Southside gauntlet—asking why a metropolitan region of one and a half million people severed its cross-harbor Route 61 lifeline at 6:45 PM on a Saturday night and left riders waiting inside transit centers with broken restroom stalls—followed immediately by our second post dissecting the glaring service cliff at the Norfolk–Virginia Beach city line on Routes 1 and 25, Mayor Myra Overton's infamous Newtown Road "riff-raff" turnaround story, and why Virginia Beach's last 5:33 PM Route 33 bus stranded transit riders twenty-seven minutes before 6:00 PM City Council meetings even began.

When I clicked Publish on that second entry at nine o'clock on Sunday night, our sidebar visitor counter read zero.

Katie laced her fingers warmly through mine across our wheelchair armrests while Heather walked over to rest a proud hand on my shoulder and Wesley tapped the glowing navy header on the monitor with a cheerful two-note hum.

"In 2008, Virginia Beach holds its next Mayor's Race and City Council elections," Katie murmured quietly, reading the exact math in my head. "If you and I keep riding every route together and writing two or three honest, schedule-backed posts every single week while we finish college, three years from now *Ideas for Coastal Virginia Transit* won't have zero readers anymore—and maybe the people running our city will finally have to listen to the people who ride the bus."
