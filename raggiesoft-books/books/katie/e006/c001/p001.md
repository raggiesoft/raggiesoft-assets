## Sunday, March 4, 2007, 6:30 PM — Four Brass House Keys & Unofficial Homeowners on Arvin Road

"Before I get on Interstate 64 tonight to head up to Alexandria for my first Monday morning in Tysons Corner, let's go over the house binder so nobody under this roof ever worries about a bill," our forty-nine-year-old dad said warmly at six-thirty on Sunday evening, setting four newly cut brass house keys and a blue leather household binder on our round dining-room table on Arvin Road.

Outside the kitchen window, the neighborhood streetlamps of Kempsville were flickering on in the March dusk while twenty-year-old Wesley happily arranged his framed *Annie's Pretzels* photo and miniature city bus on the shelf of his sunny back bedroom and Heather finished unpacking her VDU Special Education student-teaching binders down the hall.

"The mortgage on this house, the City of Virginia Beach real-estate taxes, the homeowner's insurance, the electric bill, the water and sewer, and the high-speed cable internet are all set to automatic payment straight out of my main checking account up on Sunset Drive in Alexandria," Dad explained, tapping the two reloadable Meridian debit cards sitting in front of Katie and me. "Until your own software work pays every dime you want it to—and while Heather finishes her final semester of student teaching at Virginia Dominion University before starting her classroom contract in the fall—as far as day-to-day life goes, the four of you are the unofficial homeowners of Arvin Road."

Katie reached into her crossbody bag, unzipped her wallet, and set her laminated Virginia DMV identification card down on the oak table beside her brass key.

"Look at the address line, Nate," Katie smiled, tapping the card with her fingernail.

I pulled my own wallet out of my jeans pocket and laid my vertical State ID card right beside hers. Printed across the front of both cards in crisp black DMV block letters was the exact Arvin Road address in Virginia Beach where all four of us were sitting right now.

"Neither of us ever bothered changing our DMV address during our student leases over in Norfolk, because Arvin Road was always home base," I grinned across at Dad and Heather. "And since Katie and I registered to vote right here at this table when we turned eighteen in April 2003, our Kempsville precinct registrations have been anchored to this exact house for four straight years."

"And look how our four rooms down the hallway fit together without a single threshold bump," Heather smiled, resting a warm hand on my shoulder as Wesley walked back in and tapped my knuckles twice.

Along the front window sat our dedicated twelve-by-twelve dual-desk office where Katie and I rolled our two black SwiftChairs side by side beneath twelve feet of laminate worktables. Down the hallway, Heather had her own bedroom right next to twenty-year-old Wesley's bedroom—keeping her door unlatched every single night so whenever Wesley got scared in the dark, he could walk straight next door and climb into bed beside Heather—while across the corridor inside Katie's old room stood our unobstructed, carpeted closet where Katie and I still hid every night inside our shared sleeping bag beneath Dad's raised shelves.

Seeing Dad off at the top of our aluminum porch ramp at quarter to ten as his 1991 Crew Cab pickup backed down the driveway toward Interstate 64, all four of us rolled and stepped back inside, clicked the brass deadbolt shut, and listened to the peaceful hum of our own home in Kempsville.
