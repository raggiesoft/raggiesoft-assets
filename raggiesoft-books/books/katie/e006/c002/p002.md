## Tuesday, March 13, 2007, 4:15 PM — Emergency at the Therapeutic Pool & The Towel Showdown

"I told you to leave her alone!" I shouted across the ninety-two-degree water of the indoor therapeutic pool at Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center, projecting my voice intentionally loud so the recreation staffer sitting behind the office glass would hear me.

Across the chest-deep water from where our two joystick-outfitted wheelchairs sat parked by the stainless-steel ladder, Chris—an aggressive student from the IT class cohort who had been pestering Katie since Monday morning and refused to accept that my twin sister had zero interest in dating him—smirked and wading-stepped closer.

"Come on, Nate, let's swim up to the shallow end," Katie said tightly, turning in the warm water to get away from him.

We pushed off toward the shallow end together, and Chris plowed straight through the water right behind us. Before I could pivot between them, he lunged forward and slammed both hands down into the water right in front of Katie's face—striking her hard across the bridge of her nose and cheek while his flailing fingers caught and tore the front piece of her two-piece swimsuit top.

Katie gasped, clutched onto my left forearm with one hand while clapping her other hand over her face, and burst into sobs.

My heart dropped into my stomach. Katie never cried over splashes or arguments; if Katie was crying, she was hurt.

Wrapping my arm around her waist to take her weight off her weak legs in the water, I guided her immediately to the stainless-steel pool ladder and helped her climb out onto the wet tile deck beside our parked wheelchairs. When I gently eased her hand back half an inch to check her face, I saw two things at the exact same second: bright red blood was pouring steadily from my twin sister's nose, and the front piece of her swimsuit top had been ripped away in the water, leaving her chest bare while both of her hands had to stay pressed tightly over her bleeding nose.

"Get me Student Health and Campus Security!" I roared at the top of my lungs across the natatorium. "My sister's nose is bleeding!"

Since Katie was in no condition to speak with both of her hands occupied holding her bleeding nose, I stepped right in front of her and used my own hands to cover up my sister's bare breasts to shield her modesty on the open pool deck while we waited for someone to bring a towel.

Instead of grabbing a towel from the rack by the wall, the poolside staffer walked over with a clipboard in his hand and frowned at me.

"You know I'm going to have to report you for inappropriate contact," the staffer said, staring straight at my hands covering my sister's chest while blood dripped between her fingers onto the wet tile.

"Either report me or get a towel to cover my sister's dignity!" I retorted angrily, not giving a damn what he thought while my sister stood bleeding and exposed in front of the pool.

Staring at the blood running down Katie's wrists and her torn swimsuit top floating by the gutter, it finally dawned on the staffer why my hands were covering her chest. He hurried over to the rack and brought back a thick white bath towel just as the Recreational Therapy supervisor rushed out with a walkie-talkie crackling that both Student Health and RRC Campus Police were on the way.

Only when that dry towel was wrapped securely around Katie's chest and shoulders did I let go.

Seconds later, two Student Health nurses pushed through the double doors and immediately pressed a sterile gauze pack over Katie's bleeding nose and swollen cheek, while a Campus Police officer pulled out his notepad to take a formal written statement from me and three other students who had witnessed Chris lunge at her in the water.

Over the edge of the bloody gauze pack, Katie fixed the poolside staffer who had threatened to write me up with a furious glare.

"You're going to write up my brother for trying to cover my boobs while no one wanted to get a towel or something?" she stammered thickly past the bandage.

The staffer looked down at his shoes without a word as the Recreational Therapy supervisor ordered everyone out of the water, closing the natatorium so maintenance could scrub the tile coping with a one-to-ten bleach solution, shock-chlorinate the ninety-two-degree water to four parts per million, and run a full sixty-minute filtration turnover hold.

While Student Health escorted Katie across the walkway to the clinic in the Bates-Harding Building, one of Katie's female friends from our class hurried into the women's locker room and brought all of Katie's dry clothes and backpack out to me. Stacking her bag on the seat of her wheelchair, I rolled both chairs over to the Student Health waiting room and sat stubbornly outside the exam door until evening—refusing to be more than ten feet away from my sister while she was hurt.

## 7:30 PM — The Rarely Used Back Storage Closet, Accused at the 11:00 PM Curfew Check, & Calling the Night Supervisor's Bluff

"I don't want to leave your side," Katie whispered at seven-thirty that evening as the Student Health clinic door opened and she rolled out wearing her swimsuit bottom and a worn-out oversized loaner T-shirt, with her right cheek bright red and swollen and fresh white tape holding the gauze dressing across the bridge of her nose. Reaching across our armrests for my hand as we rolled toward Barton Hall in the gathering dusk, she started sobbing again. "I don't give a damn about the Level 2 dorm rule... please don't leave me tonight, Nate."

"We used to live together!" she stammered as the automatic doors of Barton Hall slid shut behind us and we turned into the quiet side corridor between the wings, where no one was within earshot. "We used to share a bed! Hell, we would sleep in closets together!"

Looking at the fresh white gauze taped across my trembling sister's swollen nose after eight straight nights of forced separation in Barton Hall, I made up my mind on the spot. If we tried to hide in one of the regular hallway linen closets, a night staffer grabbing towels would find us in five minutes—and if we left our wheelchairs parked outside a closet door in the corridor, our chairs would give us away immediately. We needed a closet that was big enough to roll both wheelchairs all the way inside and that nobody ever opened.

"Remember that big, dusty seasonal-storage closet at the dead end of the back utility corridor behind the recreation lounge—the one stacked with artificial Christmas trees, folded ping-pong tables, and spare vinyl mattresses that nobody ever uses?" I whispered, squeezing her fingers. "Go into your room just long enough to put on dry pajamas and grab your spare blanket and two pillows. I'll grab my pillow and my jacket from my bed and meet you at the back utility corridor in five minutes."

Five minutes later, after checking both ways to make sure the rear corridor was completely empty, we turned the brass knob of the heavy solid-wood door to the seasonal-storage closet. It was unlocked, smelling faintly of cardboard boxes and old cedar. Rolling both of our joystick-outfitted wheelchairs all the way inside behind a stack of boxed holiday wreaths and folded banquet tables so nothing was visible in the hallway, we pulled the solid-wood door shut from the inside, spread our thick blanket and pillows across the clear square of linoleum floor at the back of the closet, and curled up tightly in each other's arms in the pitch dark.

Against my chest, Katie's ragged breathing finally slowed down. With both of our arms wrapped securely around each other in a close, soothing embrace—confident that nobody would ever think to look inside a dusty seasonal-storage closet at the back of the building—both of us drifted off into a deep sleep.

At eleven o'clock sharp, however, the Barton Hall night staff conducted the mandatory Level 2 curfew bed check down the separate men's and women's wings—and discovered at the exact same time that neither Nathaniel Prairie on the men's wing nor Katie Prairie in Room 157 on the women's wing was in bed, and that both of our wheelchairs were gone.

For the next twenty-five minutes, muffled footsteps, jangling master keys, and crackling walkie-talkies echoed faintly through the building as the night staff searched Barton Hall from end to end—checking the TV lounge, the vending alcove, the laundry room, the breezeway doors, and every study room and linen closet along the main wings.

Finally, at twenty-five past eleven, heavy footsteps stopped right outside the dead-end utility corridor. The brass handle rattled, the solid-wood door of the seasonal-storage closet was yanked wide open, and a blinding flashlight beam swept past our two parked wheelchairs and shone straight into our eyes.

"Aha! Found them!" the night supervisor barked into his walkie-talkie before glaring down at the two of us curled up together under the blanket on the floor. "What the hell do you two think you're doing sneaking off into a back storage closet after curfew to have sex?! Do you know how many couples we catch hooking up in closets around here?! Sneaking off after curfew to have sex is an automatic expulsion—people get kicked out of Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center and sent home for this!"

Neither Katie nor I shrank back an inch. Sitting bolt upright in our pajamas under the flashlight beam as we transferred off the closet floor into our wheelchairs, both of us went straight back at the night supervisor at full volume.

"Have sex?!" I roared in disgust, staring at him like he had lost his mind. "Are you out of your sick mind?! She's my twin sister, and we're both wearing full pajamas! We came in here to sleep in a quiet closet holding each other because she's terrified after getting assaulted in your pool today!"

"Look at my face!" Katie snapped furiously, pointing straight at the swollen purple bruise on her cheek and the white medical tape across her broken nose. "My nose was broken open in your therapeutic pool this afternoon by a creep in our class, your poolside staffer tried to write my brother up for covering my dignity when nobody brought a towel, and when your stupid Level 2 dorm rules wouldn't even let twin brother and sister stay together after I got hurt, you barge in here and accuse us of having sex?!"

"I don't care what excuse you give me—a male client and a female client hiding under a blanket in a dark closet after eleven-o'clock curfew is a major violation!" the night supervisor barked, shaking his clipboard in our faces. "Keep yelling at me like that, both of you, and I will have you kicked out of this Center and sent home first thing in the morning!"

Katie and I called his bluff on the spot.

"Do it! Kick us out right now!" I fired right back at him without blinking, unlocking my wheelchair brakes and rolling straight out of the closet threshold. "Don't wait until morning—walk over to the front-desk telephone and call our father in Alexandria right now! Tell him to get in his pickup truck and come pick us up tonight!"

"Go on, call our Dad in Alexandria right now!" Katie dared him at the top of her lungs, pointing down the corridor toward the main lobby desk. "Tell him you're kicking us out, and then explain to him why my nose is bandaged! We're ready to leave this place right now—we'll go pack our suitcases this second!"

The night supervisor froze dead in his tracks with his pen in the air.

He stared from my face to Katie's bandaged nose, realizing in an instant that we weren't scared teenagers begging not to get expelled—we were dead serious and ready to roll to our rooms and pack our bags that very minute. Within three seconds, every ounce of bluster drained out of his face, and he backed all the way down.

"Nobody is calling your father tonight, and nobody is packing any suitcases," the night supervisor muttered stiffly, lowering his clipboard and refusing to take a single step toward the lobby telephone. "Your vocational counselor is off work tomorrow on Wednesday. I am putting a residential write-up in the overnight log, and your counselor will speak to the two of you about this on Thursday morning when she returns. Now go back to your assigned beds."

Only when we were back in our separate rooms ten minutes later with our laptop webcams open on our nightstands so we could watch over each other through the screen did Katie speak quietly into her microphone.

"Wait a second, Nate," Katie whispered, frowning thoughtfully past the white tape across her nose. "Everybody in the dorms says that when staff catch two students sneaking off together after curfew and accuse them of having sex, they kick them out of the Center automatically. Why did he back down the very second we told him to call Dad in Alexandria and said we were ready to leave tonight?"

"Because he realized it wasn't a threat to us—we actually wanted to leave," I said quietly.

"I think it's more than that, Nate," Katie said slowly, her sharp eyes narrowing on the screen. "Think about how hard Voc Eval pushed to keep us here for two full weeks, and how fast that supervisor just panicked when he saw two full-time Web Applications students ready to pack their bags in Week Two. I bet the Center's funding from the state depends on how many beds and classroom seats they keep filled. If he kicks us out tonight—and Dad finds out what happened in the pool—they lose two students from their headcount."

"Whether it's their headcount or covering their own behinds over the pool, we now know their threats are hollow," I whispered back.

"I just need to be hugged," Katie murmured toward the webcam, her bandaged nose and swollen cheek catching the glow of her screen.

"I know," I said softly, keeping my hand resting beside the camera lens until her eyelids drooped, "and I'll be waiting right outside your suite door first thing in the morning. Goodnight, Katie."

## Wednesday, March 14, 2007, 8:30 AM — The Bates-Harding Office & "I LET HIM COVER MY DIGNITY!"

"I'm still so upset," Katie whispered in my ear at quarter to eight on Wednesday morning as we met right outside her women's-wing suite door and I pulled her into a tight morning hug across our armrests.

"I understand," I whispered back, rubbing her shoulder. Hoping to coax even a tiny smile out of her, I asked, "You ready to go eat some more garbage?"

Katie giggled just a bit against my collar, though when I started to ease back from the hug, she held on tightly to my jacket sleeves and refused to let go—making it crystal clear without words: *please don't let go*.

As we finally rolled side by side past the Barton Hall front desk toward the dining hall breezeway, the morning desk clerk looked up from the overnight binder and tapped a pink slip of paper. "Nathaniel, Katie—just so you know, your regular vocational counselor has her scheduled day off today, so your disciplinary write-up meeting for getting caught in the hallway closet at eleven o'clock last night is on her calendar for Thursday morning. Also, check the lobby board after dinner tonight—housing is running the mid-month dormitory room reassignments this evening."

Neither of us cared about Thursday's closet write-up meeting—and as soon as we finished breakfast at eight-thirty, an administrative runner paged both of us over to the student-services duty supervisor's office in the Bates-Harding Building regarding Tuesday afternoon's incident at the pool.

Rolling through the wood-paneled office door beside Katie, I took one look at the printout sitting on the duty supervisor's desk—the morning Chickenshit Report—and saw the poolside staffer's entry from Tuesday afternoon literally accusing me of *"fondling Katie's bare breasts while she was in distress, after having been assaulted and her swimsuit top destroyed by the person who assaulted her."*

Katie rolled her eyes and growled across the desk, "Delete that from the record! I told him to cover my boobs while we waited for someone to get a damn towel! None of this shit would've happened if that boy didn't cause me a nosebleed in the first place!"

My sister was furious—Katie never cussed like that in front of staff—and she kept right on going: "And I don't care if you call our Dad! He would tell you himself that my brother acted protectively in covering them!"

Having riled herself all the way up on top of Tuesday's pool assault and getting dragged out of the closet at eleven o'clock the night before, Katie's nervous system overloaded all at once, her voice cutting out as she descended straight into a full autistic meltdown.

Instinctively, I steered my wheelchair flush against hers, locked my brakes, and held her tightly in my arms while she trembled against my shoulder.

The duty supervisor looked into Katie's blank, overloaded eyes, seeing immediately that the only reason she had gone completely silent was because her autistic brain was in a full sensory shutdown.

"Let me try something," the supervisor said quietly, pressing the intercom button on his desk phone. "Could we please get a cup of water brought in for Katie?"

Turning back to the two of us while I held my trembling sister, he spoke in a calm, steady voice. "You really care about her, don't you, Nathaniel? No, I don't think you were touching her inappropriately. I think you were acting on instinct and shielding her from further humiliation while you waited for a towel to cover her up, and while she was busy trying to stop her nose from bleeding until help could arrive. I have the medical report from the nurses in Student Health right here on my desk that says the exact same thing."

When the secretary brought in a small paper cup of cool water and Katie slowly took a few sips, her breathing steadied. Reaching across the wooden desk, Katie pulled a yellow legal pad toward her wheelchair, picked up a thick black marker, and wrote down in firm block letters:

*I LET HIM COVER MY DIGNITY!*

Your regular counselor will still see the two of you tomorrow morning regarding last night's eleven-o'clock Barton Hall closet write-up, since she's out of the office today, but as far as yesterday afternoon at the pool goes, you did the right thing looking out for your sister, Nathaniel."

We are not used to being apart—and we are not staying somewhere that treats keeping my sister safe like a rule violation."
