## Tuesday, March 13, 2007, 4:15 PM — Emergency at the Therapeutic Pool & The Towel Showdown

"I told you to leave her alone!" I shouted across the ninety-two-degree water of the indoor therapeutic pool at Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center, projecting my voice intentionally loud so the recreation staffer sitting behind the office glass would hear me.

Across the warm water from where our two joystick-outfitted wheelchairs sat parked at the top of the sloped therapeutic pool entry ramp, Chris—an aggressive student from the IT class cohort who had been pestering Katie since Monday morning and refused to accept that my twin sister had zero interest in dating him—smirked and wading-stepped closer.

"Come on, Nate, let's swim up to the shallow ramp," Katie said tightly, turning in the warm water to get away from him.

We pushed off through the buoyant water toward the shallow ramp together, and Chris plowed straight through the water right behind us. Before I could pivot between them, he lunged forward and slammed both hands down into the water right in front of Katie's face—striking her hard across the bridge of her nose and cheek while his flailing fingers caught and tore the front piece of her two-piece swimsuit top.

Katie gasped, clutched onto my left forearm with one hand while clapping her other hand over her face, and burst into sobs.

My heart dropped into my stomach. Katie never cried over splashes or arguments; if Katie was crying, she was hurt.

Wrapping my arm around her waist in the buoyant water, I guided her immediately up the submerged shallow ramp to our parked wheelchairs at the edge of the wet tile deck and helped her transfer into her seat while I pulled myself into mine right beside her. When I gently eased her hand back half an inch to check her face, I saw two things at the exact same second: Chris's hand had split the thin skin right across the bony bridge of her nose while rupturing the blood vessels inside her nostrils so bright red blood was pouring steadily from her nose, and the front piece of her swimsuit top had been ripped away in the water, leaving her chest bare while both of her hands had to stay pressed tightly over her bleeding nostrils.

"Get me Student Health and Campus Security!" I roared at the top of my lungs across the natatorium. "My sister's nose is bleeding!"

Since Katie was in no condition to speak with both of her hands occupied holding her bleeding nose, I angled my wheelchair right in front of hers and leaned forward to use my own hands to cover up my sister's bare breasts and shield her modesty on the open pool deck while we waited for someone to bring a towel.

Instead of grabbing a towel from the rack by the wall, the poolside staffer walked over with a clipboard in his hand and frowned at me.

"You know I'm going to have to report you for inappropriate contact," the staffer said, staring straight at my hands covering my sister's chest while blood dripped between her fingers onto the wet tile.

"Either report me or get a towel to cover my sister's dignity!" I retorted angrily, not giving a damn what he thought while my sister sat bleeding and exposed in front of the pool.

Staring at the blood running down Katie's wrists and her torn swimsuit top floating by the gutter, it finally dawned on the staffer why my hands were covering her chest. He hurried over to the rack and brought back a thick white bath towel just as the Recreational Therapy supervisor rushed out with a walkie-talkie crackling that both Student Health and RRC Campus Police were on the way.

Only when that dry towel was wrapped securely around Katie's chest and shoulders did I let go.

Seconds later, two Student Health nurses pushed through the double doors and immediately pressed a sterile gauze pack over Katie's bleeding nose and swollen cheek, while a Campus Police officer pulled out his notepad to take a formal written statement from me and three other students who had witnessed Chris lunge at her in the water.

Over the edge of the bloody gauze pack, Katie fixed the poolside staffer who had threatened to write me up with a furious glare.

"You're going to write up my brother for trying to cover my boobs while no one wanted to get a towel or something?" she stammered thickly past the bandage.

The staffer looked down at his shoes without a word as the Recreational Therapy supervisor ordered everyone out of the water, closing the natatorium so maintenance could scrub the tile coping with a one-to-ten bleach solution, shock-chlorinate the ninety-two-degree water to four parts per million, and run a full sixty-minute filtration turnover hold.

While Student Health escorted Katie across the walkway to the clinic in the Bates-Harding Building, one of Katie's female friends from our class hurried into the women's locker room and brought all of Katie's dry clothes and backpack out to me. Stacking her bag on the seat of her wheelchair, I rolled both chairs over to the Student Health waiting room and sat stubbornly outside the exam door until evening—refusing to be more than ten feet away from my sister while she was hurt.

## 7:30 PM – 11:25 PM — Closing Time at the Rec Hall, Locking the Seasonal Closet From the Inside in Street Clothes, & Accused of Having Sex

"I don't want to leave your side," Katie whispered at seven-thirty that evening as the Student Health clinic door opened and she rolled out wearing her swimsuit bottom and a worn-out oversized loaner T-shirt. Once the clinic nurses had finally gotten the heavy bleeding inside her nostrils to clot and stop, they had had to pull the split skin across the bony bridge of her nose together with sterile MedStrip wound-closure strips and tape a protective white gauze-and-paper-tape dressing across the bridge of her nose and swollen right cheek, instructing her to keep the MedStrips and white tape bandage dry and in place across her nose for a full seven days so the split bridge wouldn't reopen. Reaching across our armrests for my hand as we rolled toward Barton Hall in the gathering dusk, she started sobbing again. "I don't give a damn about the Level 2 dorm rule... please don't leave me tonight, Nate."

"We used to live together!" she stammered as we paused outside the Barton Hall entrance so she could run into her room just long enough to change out of her damp swimwear into dry street clothes—blue jeans, sneakers, and a zip-up fleece hoodie. "We used to share a bed! Hell, we would sleep in closets together!"

Looking at the fresh white gauze taped across my trembling sister's swollen nose after eight straight nights of forced separation in Barton Hall, I made up my mind on the spot. We weren't going to try rolling down the dorm hallways in pajamas carrying pillows and blankets off our beds—that would be a dead giveaway to every staff member on duty. Instead, once Katie came back out in her dry jeans and hoodie, the two of us rolled over to the campus Rec Hall to spend the evening together in a corner booth where no one could separate us.

At ten-forty that night, the Rec Hall staffer flicked the overhead lights twice and called out across the game tables that it was five minutes until ten-forty-five—the mandatory departure time for all Level 2 clients to head back to Barton Hall ahead of the eleven-o'clock curfew check, while Level 3 and Level 4 clients from Cantor Hall were allowed to stay until the Rec Hall closed at eleven sharp.

Still dressed in our full daytime street clothes and shoes, Katie and I looked at each other and nodded without saying a word. First, both of us went to the bathroom one last time so neither of us would have to open a door in the middle of the night.

When we rolled back out of the restrooms at ten-forty-four, while the staffer at the Rec Hall front counter was busy collecting billiard balls and board games and the other Level 2 students were filing out the front glass doors toward Barton Hall, Katie and I checked over our shoulders to make sure no one was looking—and slipped quietly down a darkened hallway off the rear of the Rec Hall.

At the end of that darkened hallway stood the heavy solid-wood door to the seasonal storage closet—a walk-in closet big enough to fit both of our wheelchairs inside out of sight.

I reached out and tried the brass doorknob. To our relief, it turned smoothly—it was unlocked!

Immediately, Katie and I rolled both of our joystick-outfitted wheelchairs all the way inside the dark closet, pulled the door shut behind us, and twisted the interior thumb-turn lock until it clicked, locking the door from the inside.

In the quiet darkness, smelling of cedar and cardboard boxes, we clicked off our wheelchair power switches and felt around the shelves and floor for anything soft. Packed along the back wall were lumpy canvas storage bags stuffed with spare winter coats, folded fabric stage curtains, and foam cushions. Still wearing our full street clothes, jeans, hoodies, and sneakers, we pulled two lumpy bundles onto the linoleum floor to use as makeshift pillows, spread a thick folded stage curtain over our shoulders as a makeshift blanket, and transferred out of our wheelchairs onto the floor.

Wrapping both of our arms tightly around each other in the dark, Katie tucked her head beneath my chin with a long, shaky exhale. Confident that nobody would ever find us locked inside a back seasonal closet, we started to drift off to sleep in our street clothes.

Across the courtyard in Barton Hall, however, the eleven-o'clock Level 2 curfew bed check arrived. Because clients who weren't on Level 1 disciplinary restriction didn't have to sign in or out when moving between buildings on campus—only needing a yellow paper Pass on Level 2 (or signing up at the Rec Hall desk for a Center-sponsored trip to places like The Spot or ValueMart) if they were actually leaving the RRC campus—there was no paper trail showing where we had gone after supper. When the dorm aides discovered that neither Nathaniel Prairie on the men's wing nor Katie Prairie in Room 157 on the women's wing was in bed, and verified that neither of us had an off-campus Pass on file, they called over to the Rec Hall as the building was locking up—where the evening staffer remembered seeing the two of us sitting together in our corner booth all night and realized he had never seen our two wheelchairs roll out the front doors at ten-forty-five.

At eleven-twenty-five, just as I was sliding into sleep with my arms around my sister, the sound of footsteps echoed down the darkened Rec Hall hallway outside our closet.

Both of us went completely still in the dark.

The footsteps stopped right outside our door. Someone grabbed the outer brass doorknob and tried to turn it—*rattle, rattle*—finding it locked from the inside.

Katie and I held our breath and remained dead quiet, not moving a muscle in the dark.

Then, right outside the wood, we heard the metallic jingle of a master key ring. A key scraped into the outside lock cylinder, the bolt clicked back as someone unlocked the door, and the solid-wood door swung wide open as a blinding flashlight beam swept past our two parked wheelchairs and shone straight down onto the two of us huddled in our street clothes on the floor.

"Aha! I knew it!" the night supervisor barked, glaring down at us while the Rec Hall staffer stood behind him in the hallway. "What the hell do you two think you're doing locking yourselves inside a dark closet to have sex?! You know the Center rule—if two clients are caught having sex anywhere on this campus, at any time, both of you get kicked out of Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center!"

We had already heard plenty of stories from other students during our first week and a half about clients sneaking off to have sex being a rampant thing all over campus, and the second the supervisor barked *"I knew it!"* without even pausing to look at us, we figured this back closet in the Rec Hall was probably one of the places where clients got caught hooking up.

Neither Katie nor I shrank back an inch. Pushing the makeshift curtain off our shoulders as we transferred back up into our wheelchairs in our full street clothes and sneakers, both of us went straight back at the night supervisor at full volume.

"Have sex?!" I roared in disgust, staring at him like he had lost his mind. "Are you out of your sick mind?! First of all, she's my twin sister! Second, look at us—we're wearing our full street clothes, jeans, hoodies, and shoes! We locked ourselves in here to sleep using lumpy bags as pillows because my sister got her nose broken open in your pool this afternoon and your stupid Level 2 dorm rules wouldn't let us stay together!"

"Look at my face!" Katie snapped furiously, pointing straight at the swollen purple bruise on her cheek and the white medical tape across her broken nose. "My nose was broken open in your therapeutic pool five hours ago by a creep in our class, your poolside staffer tried to write my brother up for covering my dignity when nobody brought a towel, and when we just wanted a quiet closet where my brother could hold me so I wouldn't be alone all night, you unlock the door and accuse twin brother and sister of having sex?!"

"I don't care what story you give me—locking yourselves inside a dark seasonal closet together and skipping the eleven-o'clock curfew check is grounds for expulsion!" the night supervisor barked, shaking his clipboard in our faces. "Keep yelling at me like that, both of you, and I will have you kicked out of this Center and sent home right now!"

Katie and I called his bluff on the spot.

"Do it! Kick us out right now!" I fired right back at him without blinking, unlocking my wheelchair brakes and rolling straight out of the closet into the Rec Hall corridor. "It's eleven-twenty-five at night—don't wait until morning! Walk right down the hall to the Rec Hall's front-desk telephone and call our father in Alexandria right now! Tell him to get in his pickup truck and come pick us up tonight!"

"Go on, walk over to the Rec Hall desk phone and call our Dad in Alexandria right now!" Katie dared him at the top of her lungs, rolling right out beside me and pointing down the hallway toward the front counter. "Wake him up at eleven-twenty-five, tell him you're kicking us out, and then explain to him why my nose is bandaged! We're ready to leave this place right now—we'll go back across to Barton Hall and pack our suitcases this second!"

The night supervisor froze dead in his tracks with his pen in the air.

He stared from my face to Katie's bandaged nose, realizing in an instant that we weren't scared teenagers begging not to get expelled—we were dead serious and ready to roll across to our rooms and pack our bags that very minute. Within three seconds, every ounce of bluster drained out of his face, and he backed all the way down.

"Nobody is calling your father tonight, and nobody is getting kicked out," the night supervisor muttered stiffly, lowering his clipboard and refusing to take a single step toward the Rec Hall desk telephone. "Your vocational counselor is off work tomorrow on Wednesday. I am putting a residential write-up in the overnight log for missing eleven-o'clock bed check, and your counselor will speak to the two of you about this on Thursday morning when she returns. Now get across to Barton Hall and go to your assigned beds."

Only when we were back in our separate rooms in Barton Hall ten minutes later with our laptop webcams open on our nightstands so we could watch over each other through the screen did Katie speak quietly into her microphone.

"Wait a second, Nate," Katie whispered, frowning thoughtfully past the white tape across her nose. "Everybody on campus knows the rule: if two students are caught having sex anywhere at any time, both of them get kicked out of the Center automatically. He automatically assumed that's what we were doing and threatened to kick us out on the spot—so why did he back down the very second we told him to call Dad in Alexandria and said we were ready to leave tonight?"

"Because he realized kicking us out wasn't a punishment to us—we actually wanted to leave," I said quietly.

"I think it's more than that, Nate," Katie said slowly, her sharp eyes narrowing on the screen. "Think about how hard Voc Eval pushed to keep us here for two full weeks, and how fast that supervisor just panicked when he saw two full-time Web Applications students ready to pack their suitcases in Week Two. I bet the Center's funding from the state is tied to how many dorm beds and classroom seats they keep filled. They only kick people out when they think you'll beg to stay—the second he saw we'd gladly walk out the door tonight, he backed down so they wouldn't lose two seats."

"Whether it's their headcount funding or covering their own behinds over the pool, we now know their threats are hollow," I whispered back.

"I just need to be hugged," Katie murmured toward the webcam, her bandaged nose and swollen cheek catching the glow of her screen.

"I know," I said softly, keeping my hand resting beside the camera lens until her eyelids drooped, "and I'll be waiting right outside your suite door first thing in the morning. Goodnight, Katie."

## Wednesday, March 14, 2007, 8:30 AM — The Bates-Harding Office & "I LET HIM COVER MY DIGNITY!"

"I'm still so upset," Katie whispered in my ear at quarter to eight on Wednesday morning as we met right outside her women's-wing suite door and I pulled her into a tight morning hug across our armrests.

"I understand," I whispered back, rubbing her shoulder. Hoping to coax even a tiny smile out of her, I asked, "You ready to go eat some more garbage?"

Katie giggled just a bit against my collar, though when I started to ease back from the hug, she held on tightly to my jacket sleeves and refused to let go—making it crystal clear without words: *please don't let go*.

As we finally rolled side by side past the Barton Hall front desk toward the dining hall breezeway, the morning desk clerk looked up from the overnight binder and tapped a pink slip of paper. "Nathaniel, Katie—just so you know, your regular vocational counselor has her scheduled day off today, so your disciplinary write-up meeting for getting caught in the hallway closet at eleven o'clock last night is on her calendar for Thursday morning. Also, check the lobby board after dinner tonight—housing is running the mid-month dormitory room reassignments this evening."

Neither of us cared about Thursday's closet write-up meeting—and as soon as we finished breakfast at eight-thirty, an administrative runner paged both of us over to the student-services duty supervisor's office in the Bates-Harding Building regarding Tuesday afternoon's incident at the pool.

Rolling through the wood-paneled office door beside Katie, I took one look at the printout sitting on the duty supervisor's desk—the morning Chickenshit Report—and saw the poolside staffer's entry from Tuesday afternoon literally accusing me of *"fondling Katie's bare breasts while she was in distress, after having been assaulted and her swimsuit top destroyed by the person who assaulted her."*

Katie rolled her eyes and growled across the desk, "Delete that from the record! I told him to cover my boobs while we waited for someone to get a damn towel! None of this shit would've happened if that boy didn't cause me a nosebleed in the first place!"

My sister was furious—Katie never cussed like that in front of staff—and she kept right on going: "And I don't care if you call our Dad! He would tell you himself that my brother acted protectively in covering them!"

Having riled herself all the way up on top of Tuesday's pool assault and getting dragged out of the closet at eleven o'clock the night before, Katie's nervous system overloaded all at once, her voice cutting out as she descended straight into a full autistic meltdown.

Instinctively, I steered my wheelchair flush against hers, locked my brakes, and held her tightly in my arms while she trembled against my shoulder.

The duty supervisor looked into Katie's blank, overloaded eyes, seeing immediately that the only reason she had gone completely silent was because her autistic brain was in a full sensory shutdown.

"Let me try something," the supervisor said quietly, pressing the intercom button on his desk phone. "Could we please get a cup of water brought in for Katie?"

Turning back to the two of us while I held my trembling sister, he spoke in a calm, steady voice. "You really care about her, don't you, Nathaniel? No, I don't think you were touching her inappropriately. I think you were acting on instinct and shielding her from further humiliation while you waited for a towel to cover her up, and while she was busy trying to stop her nose from bleeding until help could arrive. I have the medical report from the nurses in Student Health right here on my desk that says the exact same thing."

When the secretary brought in a small paper cup of cool water and Katie slowly took a few sips, her breathing steadied. Reaching across the wooden desk, Katie pulled a yellow legal pad toward her wheelchair, picked up a thick black marker, and wrote down in firm block letters:

*I LET HIM COVER MY DIGNITY!*

"Katie, I'm agreeing with you," the supervisor said gently, tapping his keyboard and deleting the Tuesday afternoon poolside entry from my record on the spot. "That pool-deck entry is gone. Your regular counselor will still see the two of you tomorrow morning at ten o'clock regarding last night's eleven-o'clock seasonal closet write-up, since she's out of the office today, but as far as yesterday afternoon at the pool goes, you did the right thing looking out for your sister, Nathaniel."

"Back home in our apartment, we are at each other's side twenty-four hours a day," I told him bluntly before we unlocked our brakes to roll over to Mr. Alderman's classroom. "In case there's a meltdown or an emergency in the middle of the night, the other one is right there. We are not used to being apart—and we are not staying somewhere that treats keeping my sister safe like a rule violation."

Fifteen minutes later, when we rolled into the Web Applications Developer lab and told Tina, Sheila, and Jim that the poolside report had been deleted—while our ten-o'clock disciplinary meeting over getting locked in the seasonal closet was still on our counselor's calendar for Thursday morning—Tina immediately flipped open her sticky-noted Student Handbook to the residential discipline section and lowered her voice behind her glasses.

"Watch yourselves at ten o'clock tomorrow morning, you two," Tina warned us seriously. "Up until last night, the two of you were driving the staff crazy by bending the rules and lawyering the handbook without technically breaking a single written policy. Skipping the ten-forty-five Rec Hall departure and locking yourselves inside that seasonal closet after curfew, though, is the first time you actually broke a written rule. And since the night supervisor already saw last night that threatening to kick you out doesn't scare you, they're going to try to hit you tomorrow morning with a Level 1 restriction."

"What's a Level 1 restriction?" Katie asked, frowning past the white tape across her nose.

"Level 2 is where every new student starts in Barton Hall—you can go anywhere on campus freely without signing out, and if you leave campus you either need a signed yellow paper Pass or you sign up at the Rec Hall desk for a Center-sponsored trip to places like The Spot or ValueMart, while Level 3 over in Cantor Hall only has to sign out to leave campus and Level 4 can come and go off campus as they please without any pass or sign-out," Tina explained, tapping page eighteen of her handbook. "Level 1 is the disciplinary punishment tier. If they demote you to Level 1 tomorrow morning, they strip away all of your off-campus Pass and trip privileges for up to thirty days so you can't even ride the VATS bus or leave the campus gates, lock you into your separate dorm wings right after supper with flashlight bed checks every thirty minutes, and force you to sign a written Behavioral Contract ordering mandatory separation during meals, breaks, and recreation hours. And under Center rules, if you refuse to sign a Level 1 Behavioral Contract, they threaten to expel you for non-compliance and call your family to come pick you up."

Katie and I looked at each other across our wheelchair armrests, and the exact same fire lit up in both of our eyes.

"We will never sign a Level 1 restriction or a separation contract," I promised Tina flatly. "Not a single word of it."

"I dare anyone in that counseling office to try to make us sign a piece of paper ordering my brother and me to stay apart or locking us into separate wings after supper!" Katie said fiercely, her hand gripping mine across our armrests. "And if they tell us that refusing to sign their Level 1 contract means we're kicked out of the Center and they're calling our father in Alexandria to come pick us up, we'll look them straight in the eye and say, *'Go ahead!'* Dad will be in his pickup truck heading down Interstate 81 before they even hang up the phone."
