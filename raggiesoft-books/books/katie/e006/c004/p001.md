## Thursday, June 14, 2007, 2:15 PM — "Not Quite the Right Skill Match" & Six Polite Corporate Brush-Offs

"Your HTML layouts and your Associate of Applied Science transcripts from the Community College of Hampton Roads are certainly impressive on paper," the human-resources director said in a smooth, carefully measured voice at two-fifteen on Thursday afternoon inside a second-floor office park off Virginia Beach Boulevard, folding her hands neatly atop our printed resumes without quite meeting our eyes. "However, after reviewing our enterprise pipeline this afternoon, we feel your background isn't quite the right skill match for what our development team is looking for right now—particularly when it comes to formal, accredited server-side PHP and SQL relational database coursework."

Sitting side by side in our black SwiftChairs across the glass conference table, Katie and I exchanged a single, quiet glance.

We didn't need a translator to know what had actually changed between eleven o'clock that morning—when the company's lead developer had practically begged us over the phone to come in as soon as possible—and two-fifteen that afternoon. At eleven o'clock on the telephone, we were two twenty-two-year-old developers with honors associate degrees from CCHR. At two o'clock, when the elevator doors opened and Katie and I rolled into the reception lobby side by side in two motorized power wheelchairs after riding CVTA Route 15 and Route 20 to the office park, we watched the lead developer's smile freeze on his face before he hurriedly called the human-resources director into the room.

It was our sixth traditional job interview across Virginia Beach and Norfolk since returning from our twenty-second birthday week in Alexandria and cheering for Heather as she walked across the commencement stage at Virginia Dominion University with her bachelor's degree in Special Education.

In all six interviews, the exact same legally sanitized script had played out: *not quite the right skill match*, *looking for formal server-side PHP and SQL credentials*, *decided to go in another direction*.

"Thank you for your time this afternoon," Katie said politely, closing her leather portfolio binder across her lap as we turned our wheelchairs around, rolled out to the elevator, and headed back down to the bus stop on Virginia Beach Boulevard.

Back home on Arvin Road at four o'clock over cold ginger ale with Heather and Wesley at the kitchen table, Katie tapped the HR director's business card thoughtfully against her notebook.

"We know ninety percent of what happened in that glass room was our wheelchairs, Nate," Katie said quietly. "At the same time, CCHR really didn't offer an intensive server-side PHP and SQL relational database track, and we want those skills anyway—both to rebuild *Ideas for Coastal Virginia Transit* onto a real dynamic database and to launch client projects under *Prairie Twin Interactive, LLC*. Let's call Mrs. Evans over at CCHR Disability Support tomorrow morning and ask if there's an accredited summer PHP and SQL intensive we can knock out before fall."
