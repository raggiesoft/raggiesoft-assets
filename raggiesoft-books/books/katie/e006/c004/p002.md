## Friday, June 22, 2007, 1:30 PM — "Have Neither of You Heard of DARS?" & Giving Fishersville a Second Look

"Wait a minute, Katie, Nathaniel," Mrs. Evans said across her cluttered oak desk inside the Disability Support Services office at the CCHR Virginia Beach Campus after we told her about Tuesday's corporate brush-off and our search for an accredited summer course in server-side PHP and SQL. "Have neither of you ever met with a counselor over at DARS—the Virginia Department for Aging and Rehabilitative Services?"

"Only once from the hallway four summers ago," I admitted honestly, glancing across at Katie. "Back in July of 2003, when we toured ten campuses with Heather and her non-verbal cousin Wesley before his junior year of high school, we checked in at the Bates-Harding desk at Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center in Fishersville—and walked right back out five minutes later when the guide told us Wesley would be locked inside Redmond Hall away from Heather at night."

Mrs. Evans nodded sympathetically and slipped open a crisp state curriculum binder. "Oh, Redmond Hall is the medical residential unit—that's a completely different world from the Business and Information Technology division," she assured us earnestly, sliding a glossy course sheet across the blotter. "Look right here at track BIT-204: Web Applications Developer. Self-paced instruction in server-side PHP, SQL relational database management, and dynamic web architecture, 100% tuition-sponsored by the Commonwealth, with college-style student rooms over in Barton Hall across the courtyard—and Fishersville is barely two hours down I-66 and I-81 from your dad's house in Alexandria."

Looking down at *Server-Side Scripting (PHP) & Relational Database Management (SQL)*—the exact credential six HR offices had claimed we lacked—Katie and I traded a thoughtful twin look across our armrests.

## Thursday, June 28, 2007, 6:30 PM — Four Names on an Extended-Stay Hotel Reservation & Our Naive Dorm Plan

Six evenings later around our dining-room table on Arvin Road, Katie and I spread open the glossy Barton Hall welcome packet from the Newtown Road DARS office while twenty-two-year-old Heather—enjoying her summer break before starting her first full-time classroom contract in August teaching High School Special Education Adapted Curriculum—held up a printed hotel confirmation page beside twenty-year-old Wesley.

"Look at how the standard schedule works at Roosevelt, you guys," Heather smiled warmly, tapping the top of her confirmation printout. "Typically at RRC, incoming students go up for a two-week Vocational Evaluation first, then head back home to await their formal evaluation results, and only return to Fishersville later on to begin their actual classroom program. Since Wes and I are wide open on summer break, I just booked an accessible first-floor extended-stay hotel room with two queen beds and a kitchenette right on Jefferson Highway in unincorporated Augusta County between Fishersville and Waynesboro—literally right along the Route 250 Connector bus line—for the entire two weeks of your Voc Eval!"

Wesley rocked happily in his chair and tapped my knuckles twice at the thought of a two-week summer mountain trip together.

"Even better," Heather added brightly, having zero clue—just like Katie and me—about any off-campus pass rules inside Barton Hall, "when I reserved the suite for two weeks, I put all four of our names right on the hotel reservation: Heather Collins, Wesley Collins, Nathaniel Prairie, and Katie Prairie! Whenever you two finish for the afternoon or want real food instead of cafeteria trays, you can hop on the Route 250 Connector or call my cell and come over to our hotel room anytime you want! And make sure you list me right beside your dad as your local emergency contact on your intake forms since Wes and I will be five minutes down Route 250."

"And for sleeping on campus during the week while we knock out Voc Eval," Katie smiled confidently at me once Dr. Carter's seating clinic tuned up our two collapsible manual travel wheelchairs with their right-armrest joystick drives, tapping the glossy photograph of a two-bed Barton Hall dorm room, "we already know regular colleges only separate guys and girls so dating couples don't have messy breakups. Since you and I are twin brother and sister—actual family who have never spent a single night under separate roofs in twenty-two years—of course they'll let us share one of those two-bed rooms, and at bedtime we'll just unroll our sleeping bag inside the dorm closet like we always do!"

With zero inkling of the institutional brick wall waiting behind that glossy tri-fold brochure, all four of us packed our summer bags for the Shenandoah Valley—convinced two easy weeks of career skill-building were waiting right up Interstate 64.
