## Friday, March 23, 2007, 9:40 AM — Crossing Both Roads to Catch the Southbound Route 12

"Look at how baby-faced we were in these pictures," Katie laughed on Friday morning as we sat side by side in the hallway by the front door, holding up our two vertical Virginia State identification cards before slipping them into our lanyard pouches.

Both of us had gotten those original vertical Under-21 State ID cards back in October 2001, right after we turned sixteen and moved with Dad into this house on Arvin Road when he was first renting it from the landlord—years before he eventually bought the place outright. Across the top of each vertical card ran the bright red banner announcing the date we would turn twenty-one, and printed underneath our names was our exact street address here on Arvin Road.

Crucially, neither of us had ever bothered telling the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles about a single one of our temporary moves over the past three and a half years—not when Katie went up to Williamsburg for her freshman year of college and then down to Fishersville, and not when I moved into our Norfolk apartments on Greenway Court and West Ocean View Avenue before heading to Fishersville with her. As far as the DMV's computer database in Richmond was concerned, Katie and I had lived continuously at this exact house on Arvin Road since we were sixteen years old.

Now that we were twenty-one—and only sixteen days away from turning twenty-two—we wanted to trade in those worn vertical Under-21 cards for proper adult horizontal State ID cards before heading over to Fairlawn Recreation Center on Monday to sign up for resident memberships. And since the nearest DMV Customer Service Center was over on Buckner Boulevard in Virginia Beach, getting there meant taking CVTA Route 12.

At nine-forty that morning, we rolled out our front door and down the aluminum porch ramp into the sunny March air.

Getting from our driveway to the Route 12 stop heading toward the Community College of Hampton Roads required a specific two-stage crossing. Exiting the house, we made a right onto Arvin Road and another right onto the north sidewalk of Indian River Road, rolling west down the block to the roaring multi-lane intersection of Indian River Road and Military Highway.

Because the bus stop for southbound routes sat on the far southwest corner of the intersection, crossing one street wasn't enough—we had to cross both Indian River Road and Military Highway without a single painted crosswalk stripe on either roadway.

"Indian River first while the through-lanes on Military have the green," Katie called out over the rumble of a passing dump truck as we lined our SwiftChairs up side by side at the concrete curb cut.

The moment the turning traffic cleared, we pushed our joysticks forward in tandem across the unmarked asphalt of Indian River Road to the median island and the opposite corner, pivoted ninety degrees at the curb cut, waited through the next signal cycle while eight lanes of Military Highway traffic thundered past our footplates, and then rolled straight across Military Highway to the southbound CVTA bus stop sign.

That southbound stop on Military Highway was a workhorse corner for our end of Kempsville: it served CVTA Route 12 heading east toward the Community College of Hampton Roads Virginia Beach Campus, as well as CVTA Route 15 heading south toward either Emerald Creek Mall or Robert Hall Boulevard in Chesapeake on an alternating basis.

Seven minutes later, a low-floor CVTA Route 12 coach with *12 CCHR* glowing across its amber front destination sign pulled up to the curb, kneeled its front suspension, and unfolded its metal ramp onto the sidewalk so Katie and I could roll aboard and lock down our chairs for the ride over to Buckner Boulevard.

## 10:35 AM — Two Tickets (`B042` and `B043`), The DMV Waiting Rite of Passage, & Horizontal Cards Printed on the Spot

Rolling through the double glass doors of the Buckner Boulevard DMV Customer Service Center at ten-thirty-five, Katie and I wheeled side by side up to the check-in podium near the entrance.

"Good morning," Katie told the information clerk. "My twin brother and I live together at the same address, and we both need to trade our vertical Under-21 State ID cards for horizontal cards now that we're over twenty-one. Can we just use one ticket number together?"

"Sorry, honey—DMV policy is one ticket number per person, one transaction per ticket," the check-in clerk replied without missing a beat, tapping the touchscreen on the electronic queue ticket dispenser twice and handing Katie a paper slip printed with **B042** and me a second slip printed with **B043**—the **B** prefix routing us into the queue for driver's license and identification card renewals and reissues.

"Two separate ticket numbers for twins living in the same house," I grumbled under my breath as we rolled over to the open space beside the waiting room chairs.

"At least they're right next to each other," Katie whispered back, glancing up at the red LED matrix board where **B039** was flashing. "How long could three numbers possibly take?"

Famous last words.

Over the next ten minutes, the robotic female voice on the overhead ceiling speakers chimed through **B040** and then called out:

*"Ding-dong... Now serving... Customer Number... B-zero-four-one... at Window... four."*

Katie and I both sat up straight in our SwiftChairs and rested our right hands on our joysticks, ready to roll the second **B042** was called.

Instead, the DMV's automated queue computer decided that **B041** was the end of the **B** batch—and immediately launched into a twenty-five-minute marathon of every other letter in the alphabet.

*"Ding-dong... Now serving... Customer Number... T-one-one-eight... at Window... nine,"* the robotic voice droned, routing vehicle title and registration paperwork down the hall.

*"Ding-dong... Now serving... Customer Number... C-zero-two-seven... at Window... two,"* it chimed two minutes later for the compliance and suspension desk, followed by a string of **A** tickets for original learner's permits, **D** tickets for combined multi-step transactions, and **E** tickets for the express window.

Every time the two-tone chime echoed across the acoustic ceiling tiles, Katie and I looked up expectantly at the red LED board—only to groan in unison as another **T** or **C** flashed on the display while **B042** and **B043** sat frozen in limbo.

Finally—just when I was starting to wonder if the computer had forgotten the letter **B** existed—the speaker chimed again:

*"Ding-dong... Now serving... Customer Number... B-zero-four-two... at Window... six."*

"Finally!" Katie exhaled, and both of us pushed our joysticks forward to roll side by side up to the lowered accessible counter at Window 6.

"Good morning to you both," the DMV representative behind Window 6 smiled as we pulled up.

"Good morning, ma'am," Katie said, sliding her **B042** ticket and her 2001 vertical card across the laminate counter while I tried to slide **B043** and my vertical card right beside hers. "My brother and I both need new horizontal State ID cards now that we're over the age of twenty-one."

The representative glanced down at my **B043** slip and shook her head with an apologetic smile. "I can't open **B043** in the computer until I close out **B042**, young man—you'll have to wait your turn until **B043** is called."

I let out a quiet sigh and backed my SwiftChair half a foot to the right to wait my turn while the representative processed Katie's renewal first.

Typing Katie's identification number into her terminal, the representative glanced at her screen. "Are you still at the same address on Arvin Road here in Virginia Beach that we've had on file since October 2001?"

"Yes, ma'am—still the exact same house on Arvin Road," Katie smiled, confirming that her Virginia Beach voter registration in the Kempsville precinct was still current as well before rolling two feet over to the adjustable camera post in front of the pull-down sky-blue backdrop to have her new photo snapped.

The second Katie's photo registered in the system and the representative closed out **B042** to send Katie's card to the thermal printer behind the counter, the representative pressed the queue button on her keyboard.

Right above our heads, the ceiling speaker chimed:

*"Ding-dong... Now serving... Customer Number... B-zero-four-three... at Window... six."*

"Now it's officially your turn," the representative chuckled as I rolled my SwiftChair six inches forward to the exact spot Katie had just vacated and slid **B043** and my 2001 vertical card across the counter.

After confirming that I also still lived at the exact same Arvin Road address on file since October 2001, verifying my voter registration, paying our two card fees with our joint debit card, and having my own picture snapped in front of the blue screen, I rolled right back beside Katie's chair at Window 6 while the thermal card printer behind the counter whirred through its second pass.

## 11:45 AM — Dropped Off by the Bank on Northbound Military Highway

By eleven-forty-five, after riding the westbound CVTA Route 12 bus back from Buckner Boulevard, I reached up and pulled the yellow chime cord as the bus approached Indian River Road.

On the return trip home, Route 12 dropped us off on the northbound side of South Military Highway right in front of the brick Piedmont Bank branch at the southeast corner of the intersection.

Once the bus ramp folded back inside and the coach pulled away through the traffic light, Katie and I rolled up to the corner curb cut in front of the bank, waited for a clean break in the turning cars, and crossed the unmarked lanes of Indian River Road to the northeast sidewalk.

From there, we made a right onto the sidewalk of Indian River Road—rolling east against the flow of westbound traffic with our new horizontal Virginia State ID cards tucked safely inside our lanyards—and then made a left onto quiet Arvin Road, where our brick ranch house sat waiting for us on our left.
