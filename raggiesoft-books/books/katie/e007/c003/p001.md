## Wednesday, July 18, 2007, 9:15 PM — Scrambled Keycards, Leaving Campus Without Yellow Passes, & Daring RRC to Say a Word

"What are you doing in here, Tina?" I asked in surprise at nine-fifteen on Wednesday night as the door to my men's dormitory room in Barton Hall clicked open and in walked our short, petite classmate with her glasses and straight jet-black hair, holding a newly issued plastic room keycard and an overnight bag.

Tina pushed her glasses up the bridge of her nose and held up the white plastic keycard with a look of sheer disbelief. "I thought something was fishy when the front desk handed me this keycard ten minutes ago and told me my room had been reassigned over here to Barton Hall."

Sitting in my wheelchair at my desk with my laptop webcam connected to Katie's room on the women's wing, I swiveled my laptop screen toward the doorway so the camera faced Tina. Before Tina could say another word, Jim and Amanda stepped into our hallway holding their own reassigned keycards—the housing computer printout had scrambled the men's and women's room numbers across the entire campus, and the front-desk red tape had handed out the keys blindly anyway!

Rolling out to the Barton Hall lobby counter with Tina and Jim to talk to evening staffer Herb, I glanced down at my live laptop screen—and froze cold.

On my webcam window, the door to Katie's dorm room on the women's wing swung open—and standing in her doorway waving a reassigned plastic keycard was Chris, the exact guy who had broken Katie's nose open and torn her swimsuit top in the therapeutic pool the afternoon before.

"Katie: pack an overnight bag right now," I spoke into the microphone, before looking straight into the lens at Chris: "You touch my sister again and I will personally murder you."

With Jim, Bryan, and Sean striding right beside my wheelchair down the women's corridor as Chris bolted out the side exit—right after Katie heard terrified crying further down the women's wing and whispered in horror, *"Nate, I think he hurt Natalie!"* while Herb's walkie-talkie crackled *"Campus Police to Barton Hall"*—I made our decision on the spot.

"Call Heather's cell phone, Katie," I said flatly, zipping our laptops, chargers, and our rolled-up closet sleeping bag onto our lap trays. "Both of our names have been on that two-queen extended-stay hotel reservation five minutes down Route 250 since Day One. Neither one of us is sleeping another single night inside Barton Hall."

"What about yellow Level 2 off-campus passes?" Jim asked quietly as we rolled back into the Barton Hall lobby at quarter to ten, right as Heather's silver accessible van pulled right up outside the front glass doors with twenty-year-old Wesley sitting up front turning his blue silicone ring in fierce concern.

"Watch us walk right out without one," Katie said past the white MedStrip and gauze bandage taped across her swollen nose, rolling hub to hub beside me straight past the front desk without touching the yellow pass pad or signing a single line on the clipboard.

The night supervisor who had walked in behind the counter stared straight at our wheelchairs heading out the automatic glass doors at nine-forty-eight at night with zero passes—opened his mouth half an inch—saw the white medical bandage across Katie's broken nose from Tuesday's pool assault and Heather's van idling at the curb with local emergency contact Heather stepping out—and snapped his jaw shut without uttering a syllable.

Twenty minutes later inside Room 114 of the quiet extended-stay hotel down Jefferson Highway—where Heather's credit card had already covered the full two weeks with all four of our names registered on the room folio since Sunday morning, and where Katie and I tapped straight through the side entrance using the two white Room 114 keycards we had kept in our pockets since Day One—Heather warmed up soup on the kitchenette range while Wesley gently tapped my knuckles twice, and at bedtime Katie and I unrolled our familiar sleeping bag across the clean carpet of the suite's walk-in closet while Heather and Wesley settled into the two comfortable queen beds right outside.

## Thursday, July 19, 2007, 7:05 AM — Rolling Back Onto Campus Without Passes, Natalie's Tears, & Sweeping a Predator Out the Front Gate

At five minutes past seven on Thursday morning—since the Route 250 Connector didn't begin its weekday runs until seven-thirty and didn't turn onto the RRC campus loop until quarter to five in the afternoon—Heather pulled her accessible van up outside the front gates on Roosevelt Avenue on her morning drive with Wesley, lowered the side ramp, and watched Katie and me roll straight onto the grounds of Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center having never picked up a pass to leave campus on Wednesday night and never signed back in, with twin looks on our faces that openly dared any staff member on property to say a single word about Level 2 passes or eleven-o'clock bed checks.

Not one administrator or dorm clerk dared open their mouth. Inside Barton Hall and Bates-Harding, the housing database was still locked up from Wednesday night's scrambled room reassignments, our ten-o'clock disciplinary meeting over Tuesday night's Rec Hall storage closet was quietly canceled—and everyone in administration knew Katie had been assaulted by Chris in their pool forty hours earlier.

Right outside the cafeteria doors, Katie gasped softly and pointed toward the concrete bench by the bushes, where Tina and Sarah were walking over toward Natalie as three of Natalie's friends held her while she sobbed inconsolably and rocked back and forth.

"That thing... he pees from..." Natalie stammered through her tears, pressing one hand below her waist and rubbing at her eyes like a hurting seven-year-old child who had zero adult comprehension of what had been done to her—understanding only that Chris had hurt her and that it hurt real bad below her tummy.

"I think... well... I don't even want to consider it," Katie whispered in horror beside me, her hand shaking in mine as Tina, Sarah, and Jim exchanged grim, sickened looks behind us. Every single one of us knew right then what Chris had done to Natalie during Wednesday night's keycard chaos.

Less than forty minutes later, at seven-fifty on the walkway toward Mr. Alderman's classroom, we stopped dead by the curb and watched in stunned, furious silence as two RRC staffers escorted Chris out to a waiting passenger van by the front gate carrying his duffel bag—with not a single state trooper cruiser, sheriff's deputy, or pair of handcuffs anywhere in sight.

"He assaulted Katie in the pool on Tuesday, he attacked Natalie in the dorms last night during their own keycard mix-up, and instead of calling the State Police, the administration is just putting him in a van and sweeping the whole thing under the rug?!" Tina said aloud beside Katie's wheelchair, her voice shaking with anger behind her glasses.

That afternoon after class—and again on Friday—Katie and I worked shoulder to shoulder with Tina, Sheila, Jim, and Sarah in Mr. Alderman's lab until every single PHP 5 and SQL course module, textbook exercise, and database schema sat copied cleanly onto both of our personal laptop hard drives, before rolling straight out to the campus shelter at five o'clock, dropping two quarters apiece into the farebox of the eastbound <led>250 | WAYNESBORO</led> Connector bus—since neither SVT's website nor RRC's website advertised that the Center even had bus tokens, and we certainly weren't staying long enough to find out—and riding back down Jefferson Highway to Heather and Wesley's hotel suite without ever touching a yellow Hall Pass again.
