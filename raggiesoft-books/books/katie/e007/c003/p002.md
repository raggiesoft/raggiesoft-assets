## Sunday, July 22, 2007, 12:30 PM — Dad Arriving From Alexandria, Dropping Our Keys Forever, & Keeping Week Two in the Hotel

"I couldn't be prouder of both of you for standing up for your dignity and walking away from a place like this," Dad said fiercely at twelve-thirty on Sunday afternoon, July 22, 2007—exactly fourteen days after our arrival—backing his pickup up to the Barton Hall curb just as Heather parked her accessible van right behind him with Wesley stepping out beside us.

The second Dad saw the white MedStrip and gauze bandage across the bridge of his daughter's nose in person, his jaw set into solid granite as he wrapped Katie and me in a fierce hug and thanked Heather and Wesley for giving us a safe harbor down Route 250 since Wednesday night. Within twenty minutes, Dad, Heather, and classmate Jim had cleared every remaining suitcase and laundry bag out of our Barton Hall dorm rooms and loaded our heavy dorm trunks beneath the locked fiberglass cap of Dad's pickup bed.

Rolling side by side up to the Barton Hall front counter without waiting for an exit interview, Katie and I dropped our brass room keys onto the wooden blotter simultaneously.

"We're checking out permanently," Katie told the startled Sunday desk clerk before spinning her wheelchair around alongside mine and rolling out the automatic glass doors into the July sunshine for good.

Out by the tailgate of Dad's pickup before the five of us headed into Waynesboro for a celebratory Sunday lunch, Heather held up the plastic hotel keycard sleeve with a bright grin.

"Remember when I booked our two-queen extended-stay room along Route 250 thinking you two were only coming up for a two-week Voc Eval before heading home?" Heather laughed, looking from Dad to Katie and me. "Wait—today is the end of Week One on my second-week receipt because I booked fourteen nights through next Sunday! The room is already one hundred percent paid for! Mr. Prairie, if you haul their heavy dorm suitcases back with you, what do you two say to staying right here in the hotel with Wes and me all week and turning Week Two into a real Shenandoah Valley vacation?"

Katie and I looked at each other across our armrests, and twin grins broke across our faces while Wesley pumped his fist once beside my elbow.

"No eleven-o'clock flashlights, no yellow passes, and a whole paid-for week in the mountains with our favorite people?" Katie beamed. "Try stopping us!"

## Tuesday, July 24 – Sunday, July 29, 2007 — Beef Stew With Tina in Luray, Blue Ridge Overlooks, & Coming Home Whole

![Outside the flat-roofed red-brick buildings of Roosevelt Rehabilitation Center in Fishersville, Virginia in July 2007, fraternal twins Katie (with a small white MedStrip and gauze bandage taped across the bridge of her nose from Tuesday's pool injury) and Nate Prairie sit side by side on the rear bench seat of Tina's silver four-door compact sedan holding hands through the open rear door as petite, jet-black-haired classmate Tina in glasses lifts their collapsed manual wheelchair frame into the trunk for a Shenandoah Valley drive up Route 340 to Luray]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b006/jul_2007_rrc_tina_compact_sedan_trunk_collapsing_wheelchairs_luray_trip.jpg)

That second week in the extended-stay hotel along Route 250 turned a twelve-day institutional nightmare into one of the happiest summer memories the four of us ever shared.

On Friday afternoon after her own week of classes ended at three-thirty, our fearless classmate Tina met Katie and me at the lot in her silver compact sedan—lifting our two collapsed manual travel frames effortlessly into her trunk while Heather and Wesley followed right behind us up Route 340 North into the green Page Valley so all four of us could have homemade beef stew around Tina's sunlit kitchen table in Luray and test our instant-messenger PHP and SQL study group with Sheila, Jim, and Sarah. Across the rest of Monday through Saturday, the four of us rolled along the paved mountain overlooks above Rockfish Gap, browsed historic brick storefronts in downtown Staunton, rode the fifty-cent Route 250 Connector with pocketfuls of quarters between the Waynesboro ValueMart, Augusta County Library, Staunton Mall, Statler Square, and the Staunton Hub on Lewis Street purely for fun like ordinary transit tourists, and slept nine peaceful hours every night at the hotel with zero clipboards anywhere near us.

When Heather eased the van up our driveway on Arvin Road in Virginia Beach on Sunday evening, July 29—where Dad had driven down from Alexandria to welcome all four of us home and check on the newly made navy-and-slate-blue queen bed and clean closet sanctuary waiting in our bedroom—Katie and I slid off our travel frames right back into our full-size black SwiftChairs, home in Kempsville and ready to build our own future on our own terms.
