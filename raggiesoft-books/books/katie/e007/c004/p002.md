## Saturday, August 18, 2007, 1:30 PM — Turning My Old Bedroom Closet Into Our Networking Closet

"All right, Chief Technology Officer—lower intake louver is screwed flush, upper exhaust cutout is clean, and every speck of drywall dust is in the shop-vac," Dad announced cheerfully at half past one on Saturday afternoon, setting his cordless drill onto a folded towel beside the open closet doorway of our twelve-by-twelve home office on Arvin Road before wiping his forehead with his sleeve. "I'm going to step out to the garage and blow the sawdust off the jigsaw blade before your sister catches a single flake near her keyboard. Don't wire the hot leg on that relay until you double-check the pinout diagram."

"Already pulling up the schematic, Dad," I grinned from my compact black SwiftChair at the right half of our twelve-foot span of light-oak desks, turning a small gray digital thermostat controller and a solid-state relay module over on my desk mat beside my precision screwdriver.

Three feet to my left, where neat Velcro bundles of blue Category-6 patch cord lay coiled across her lap tray, Katie clicked her label maker twice, peeled a crisp white strip reading *ETH-04 — STAGING LINUX*, and wrapped it cleanly around the boot of a patch cable.

"Listen to the difference in here already just having the server tower tucked behind the closet jamb, Nate," Katie said happily, pausing for two seconds to tilt her head toward the quiet room. "For five months we've had four case fans humming right beside our ankles eight hours a day, and neither of us realized how tired our ears were until today."

Looking over at the open three-by-six alcove three feet to my right made my whole chest swell with quiet, geeky satisfaction.

Because Katie and I slept every night across the hallway inside the carpeted three-by-six closet of Katie's old bedroom—leaving our navy-and-slate-blue queen bed out in the main room purely for afternoon reading and folding warm laundry—the matching three-by-six closet right here inside my old high-school bedroom had been sitting empty ever since we turned this room into our office back in March. As soon as our first three client checks for Prairie Twin Interactive, LLC cleared the bank the week before, Katie and I had drawn up a real small-business hardware upgrade on graph paper and waited for Dad's weekend drive down from Alexandria to cut the vents.

Mounted inside the open office closet on sturdy black wire shelving sat our Linux staging and database server, our cable modem, a twenty-four-port managed gigabit ethernet switch with neat rows of blinking green link lights, and a heavy black uninterruptible power supply resting on the floor slab so a summer thunderstorm over Kempsville couldn't drop an open SQL transaction before we saved our work.

The one piece of engineering neither Katie nor I was willing to cut corners on was heat. Shutting a running server and a twenty-four-port switch behind a hollow-core closet door in August in Tidewater Virginia without active ventilation would cook every hard drive on the shelf by suppertime. To solve it properly, we had ordered a quiet hundred-and-twenty-millimeter exhaust fan for the upper header alongside an industrial twelve-volt digital thermostat controller and external relay—planning to wire the relay in a strict fail-safe configuration so that if the temperature sensor wire ever got nicked, loosened, or unplugged, the relay contacts would default to spinning the exhaust fan continuously rather than letting the closet overheat in silence.

I unfolded the tiny tissue-thin instruction leaflet that had come inside the cardboard thermostat box, squinted at a blurry, postage-stamp diagram translated half into broken English where Terminals Three and Four weren't even labeled normally open or normally closed, and let out an amused snort.

"Hold off plugging in the twelve-volt wall adapter for two minutes, sis," I said, sliding my fingers onto my keyboard while Katie slipped one side of her cushioned headphones over her left ear to check vibration-damping fan mounts on her own screen. "Whoever printed this datasheet made the normally closed symbol three pixels wide. Let me see if somebody on StreamTube already bench-tested this exact controller with a multimeter."

Typing the eight-character alphanumeric model code stamped on the side of the gray thermostat housing into the StreamTube search bar brought up four hits right away. Clicking the top clip—ninety seconds of an older electronics hobbyist touching continuity probes across the green screw block on a workbench—gave me the exact answer I needed in under a minute: Terminal Three was common, and Terminal Four stayed closed whenever power or probe resistance dropped to zero, giving us a true fail-safe fan circuit.

Satisfied, I reached for my mouse to close the browser tab and pick up my wire strippers.

Half an inch before my cursor reached the corner *X*, my eye caught the second rectangular thumbnail generated automatically down StreamTube's right-hand *Related Videos* sidebar.

## Saturday, August 18, 2007, 2:10 PM — Gloved Hands, An Aquatic Turtle Habitat Build, & Thinking of Cousin Chelsea

![Inside Katie and Nathaniel Prairie's sunlit dual-desk home office on Arvin Road in August 2007 beneath their framed Prairie Twin Interactive, LLC certificate—with their open three-by-six office closet beside them newly converted into a ventilated networking closet holding a Linux server tower, blinking 24-port gigabit switch, blue patch cables, UPS battery backup, exhaust fan, and digital thermostat—twenty-two-year-old fraternal twins Katie and Nate sit side by side in matching black SwiftChair power wheelchairs holding inner hands as they smile warmly over their shoulders at an angled StreamTube monitor showing black-nitrile-gloved hands arranging basking stones for a painted turtle.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b006/aug_2007_arvin_road_networking_closet_thermostat_streamtube_turtle_video.jpg)

Nothing on earth except StreamTube's literal 2007 keyword matcher—pairing the exact eight-character thermostat part number in the electronics title to another upload carrying that same part number in its description—would ever have put those two videos on the same page. Sitting sandwiched between a home-brewing fridge build and an attic gable-fan clip was a standard-definition four-by-three thumbnail with barely three hundred views from an educational hobbyist channel called *Bluewater Terrapin & Habitat*:

*Indoor Painted and Box Turtle Habitat — Fail-Safe Thermostat Relay, Basking Dock, and Quiet Filtration*.

I froze with my hand on the mouse, staring at the little brightly patterned painted turtle resting on a flat gray river stone inside that tiny preview frame.

In half a heartbeat, before I even thought about electronics again, my chest gave a sharp, wistful squeeze—and all I could think of was our cousin Chelsea.

Nearly three years had passed since that crisp Christmas weekend on Tonomy Hill up in Newport, Rhode Island—Chelsea's first Christmas without Aunt Liz—when twenty-two-year-old Chelsea had beamed with pure delight introducing plush green *Shelly Turtle* to seventeen-year-old Wesley on our braided rug before teaching all four of us her Route 60 shortcut to Providence. After the Newport housing office closed Chelsea's old Tonomy Hill unit the following year, Dad had tried twice through Rhode Island's state disability agency to find out where the court-appointed institutional guardian who took over after Aunt Liz's passing had moved Chelsea; because Dad was an uncle rather than the named court fiduciary, the clerks wouldn't even confirm what town she lived in. Not a single month went by without Katie, Heather, Wesley, and me thinking of her and hoping somebody up north was being gentle with her and Shelly Turtle.

Smiling softly to myself, I clicked the thumbnail—and three minutes later burst out laughing when Katie looked up from searching quiet silicone fan grommets on her own monitor and discovered StreamTube's keyword matcher had dropped her onto the exact same quiet canister-filter turtle tank build, complete with properly punctuated closed captions! Calling Heather and Wesley in from the kitchen to watch the little olive-and-red painted turtle climb effortlessly onto its sunlit river-stone basking dock and nibble neat squares of romaine lettuce—while twenty-year-old Wesley tapped the glass screen gently twice with a happy three-note hum—Katie rested her cheek against my left shoulder.

"Can you imagine Chelsea's face if she and Wesley had a tank like that right in our living room beside Shelly Turtle?" Katie murmured softly, right as Dad stepped in from the garage with clean hands to help us finish buttoning up our brand-new networking closet.
