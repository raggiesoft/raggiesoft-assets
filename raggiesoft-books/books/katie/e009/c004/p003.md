## Tuesday, July 19, 2016, 9:00 AM — Certified Newport Birth Certificates & R.I. Gen. Laws Section 33-15.2 in Braxton Municipal Probate Court

"Docket PC-2008-0419, *In re Guardianship of Chelsea*—emergency motion to vacate plenary appointment for fraud upon the court, remove guardian for medical neglect under Section 33-15-18, and issue provisional interstate transfer to the Commonwealth of Virginia under Chapter 33-15.2," the courtroom clerk announced across the wood-paneled Braxton Municipal Probate courtroom at nine-oh-two on Tuesday morning.

At the petitioner's table, Katie and I sat braked side by side in our brushed-silver-and-black SwiftChairs next to our retained Braxton probate counsel, Eleanor Callahan-Vance, while Dad sat tall in his navy blazer holding a leather folder of raised-seal Newport vital records that he had driven across the Jamestown and Newport Pell Bridges at eight o'clock sharp to pick up directly from the Newport City Clerk, and Brittany sat right behind our chairs keeping watch on the courtroom doors.

Across the center aisle, Bradford H. Sterling, Esquire—dressed in a custom charcoal three-piece suit and a purple-and-gold striped King's College alumni tie—flipped open a calfskin briefcase looking annoyed that he had been pulled away from a trustees' breakfast over a Medicaid-waiver annex resident.

"Your Honor," Sterling began smoothly before he had even glanced at our exhibits, "the Regalis Community Living Foundation has served without incident as court-appointed plenary guardian for eight years. Whatever well-meaning out-of-state individuals appeared unannounced at our Mount Regalis practicum residence yesterday lack standing to disturb a final 2008 decree—"

Probate Judge Thomas Donahue held up one thick hand from the bench, peering down over his half-moon reading glasses at the three certified exhibits Ms. Callahan-Vance had just handed up through the bailiff.

"Hold your horses, Counselor," Judge Donahue said, his gravelly Rhode Island baritone dropping ten degrees colder with every page he turned. "I am looking at two raised-seal vital records issued sixty minutes ago by the City Clerk of Newport, Rhode Island: Certificate of Live Birth for David Prairie, born Newport Hospital, and Certificate of Live Birth for his full blood sister Elizabeth Prairie, born Newport Hospital, alongside your own sister-in-law's residential telephone log recording monthly calls from *Uncle David Prairie — Virginia Beach, VA* going back eight straight years."

Judge Donahue lifted his eyes from the Newport seals straight to Bradford Sterling's purple-and-gold tie.

"Now I am looking at Box Seven of Petition Form PC-2.2, signed under pains and penalties of perjury by *you*, Mr. Sterling, in October of 2008, swearing to this Court that this young woman had *No Known Living Next of Kin* after you mailed a single certified letter to a house Mr. Prairie sold in 2001—and right underneath it, I am reading a sworn overnight inspection affidavit from the Rhode Island Disability Law Center and a state licensing nurse confirming that at two-thirty-five yesterday afternoon, your foundation's house director had this severe asthmatic woman's rescue bronchodilator locked inside an office cart while she was actively wheezing over five hundred King's College alumni mailers."

Across the aisle, every trace of color drained out of Bradford Sterling's face.

"Your Honor, if there was an inadvertent clerical oversight in the 2008 genealogical search—" Sterling stammered, his fingers suddenly trembling on his calfskin latch.

"If you finish that sentence with anything other than an unconditional consent stipulation, Counselor, my clerk is walking copies of Docket PC-2008-0419 straight upstairs to the Rhode Island Attorney General's Medicaid Fraud Control Unit and the Disciplinary Counsel of the Supreme Court before lunch," Judge Donahue said flatly. He turned toward our table, nodding respectfully toward Katie's wheelchair and Dad. "Ms. Callahan-Vance, I understand Mr. Prairie's daughter here is a sitting member of the Virginia House of Delegates, and the family has a permanent, accessible home waiting in Virginia Beach?"

"Yes, Your Honor," Katie answered clearly and respectfully from her SwiftChair beside me. "Chelsea is our older first cousin and our dad's niece. My twin brother Nathaniel, our cousin Brittany, our father, and I share a fully accessible single-story brick ranch in Virginia Beach. Brittany works full-time for my brother's and my private software company—never a cent of public funds—so Chelsea will have loving twenty-four-hour family care, immediate access to both of her asthma inhalers at all times, and her own doctors five minutes down the road. Under Rhode Island General Laws Section 33-15.2-301, which took effect January 1 of this year, and Virginia Code Section 64.2-2100, our Virginia Beach counsel is standing by to file the mirror acceptance petition in Virginia Beach Circuit Court this week."

"We—the Foundation withdraws all opposition and stipulates to immediate vacatur, immediate discharge, and full release of the ward to her uncle, Your Honor," Bradford Sterling blurted out so fast his voice cracked, practically lunging for his pen before the words *Medicaid Fraud Control Unit* could echo a second time.

Ten minutes after the clerk had called the case, Judge Donahue brought his gavel down with a sharp, satisfying crack: vacating the 2008 institutional guardianship *ab initio* for defective notice, appointing Uncle David Prairie immediate temporary family guardian pending formal interstate transfer to Virginia under Chapter 33-15.2, ordering the Regalis Foundation to wire every penny of Chelsea's withheld personal-needs allowance and back Social Security accounting within seventy-two hours, referring Regalis Community House to state licensing receivership to protect the five remaining residents, and directing a uniformed Braxton County Sheriff's deputy to escort our family's vehicle onto Mount Regalis immediately to execute the release order.

## Tuesday, July 19, 2016, 10:45 AM — Raising the Wrought-Iron Royal Gate, Signing Chelsea Out Forever, & Crossing the Stub of Interstate 895 to Newport Harbor

![Just inside the raised black wrought-iron Royal Gate of King's College on Mount Regalis in Braxton, Rhode Island on a sunny July 2016 morning with a county sheriff's cruiser parked behind David Prairie's open-ramp accessible minivan, radiant 34-year-old Chelsea clutches her green plush sea turtle with her asthma inhalers tucked in her cardigan pocket as she hugs 31-year-old Nathaniel and Katie Prairie in their SwiftChair power wheelchairs while cousin Brittany in her wire-rimmed glasses and Uncle David load Chelsea's bags into the van.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b005/jul_2016_kings_college_royal_gate_court_order_chelsea_rescue_family.jpg)

At quarter to eleven under bright July skies, nobody on Mount Regalis told Dad to turn his Virginia minivan around and take the Scrub Bus.

Rolling straight up the cobblestone approach to King's College's main wrought-iron Royal Gate directly behind a black-and-tan Braxton County Sheriff's cruiser, we watched the exact same white-gloved campus gate guard who had sneered at our wheelchairs twenty-one hours earlier step hastily out of his booth, swallow hard at the sight of the Sheriff's deputy tapping the embossed probate seal against his palm, and hit the brass switch to raise the purple-and-gold barrier arm all the way up.

Two dozen King's College summer students crossing the upper quad stopped dead on the flagstone walk in stunned silence as the Sheriff's cruiser led our out-of-state wheelchair van right past their manicured 1755 bell tower and down the service drive to the front ramp of the brick annex.

Before Dad even finished unfolding the aluminum side ramp of the minivan, the annex screen door flew open—held wide by the smiling state licensing nurse—and out hurried thirty-four-year-old Chelsea in her sky-blue summer cardigan, her wavy chestnut-brown hair brushed smooth, both asthma inhalers tucked right inside her front pocket where her fingers could pat them whenever she wanted, and Shelly the green plush sea turtle hugged tight under one arm.

*"Uncle David! Nate! Katie! Brittany!"* Chelsea cried out with pure, radiant joy, hurrying down the wooden ramp straight into my open arms first and then Katie's while happy tears spilled down her cheeks. *"The nice nurse helped me pack all my clothes! Am I really going to Virginia right now? I don't ever gotta fold purple papers or ask for my puffers again?"*

"Never again for the rest of your life, sweetheart," Dad said thickly, handing the speechless interim Foundation supervisor the stamped copy of Judge Donahue's order before picking up Chelsea's battered suitcase in one hand while Brittany slung Chelsea's blue duffel bag warmly over her shoulder and gave Chelsea a gentle side-hug. "Say goodbye to Mount Regalis, Chelsea—you're coming home with your family."

Chelsea didn't even look back at the brick annex or the Gothic spires above the stone wall as Brittany helped her buckle securely into the wide passenger seat right behind Katie's and my locked-down SwiftChairs.

Once we rolled back out beneath the raised wrought-iron arch and descended Mount Regalis toward Interstate 95 South, however, Dad glanced into the rearview mirror at his niece hugging her plush sea turtle and tapped his right turn signal at Exit 1 instead of merging straight south toward Connecticut.

"Before we point the wheels down I-95 toward Virginia," Dad smiled over his shoulder as we curved onto the six-lane concrete flyover of Interstate 895—Braxton's famously quirky east-west stub superhighway that federal engineers had signed *NORTH* toward an unbuilt Aquidneck Island freeway back in the sixties, funneling down from three lanes to a grassy two-lane parkway across the Narrow River watershed before climbing high onto the Jamestown and Newport Pell suspension bridges over glittering Narragansett Bay—"your Aunt Liz and Grandma and I grew up looking at Newport Harbor, and I refuse to let Chelsea's last memory of Rhode Island be a padlocked pantry by a steam plant. Who votes for New England clam chowder and warm buttered rolls down by the boats at Perotti Park before we head home?"

*"Me! Me! Clam chowder at Perotti Park!"* Chelsea cheered, clapping her hands excitedly against Shelly Turtle as the white towers of the Newport Pell Bridge rose ahead of the windshield and dozens of white sails tacked across the bright blue water below, before leaning forward between our headrests to point proudly out the side window as we rolled off the highway stub toward downtown Newport. *"Look, Nate! There's the blue OSTA bus! Just like the Route 60 bus I showed you how to catch!"*

"You saved our whole trip home that Monday morning, Cous—you still know Newport buses better than anybody," I grinned back at her, reaching up to high-five her hand while Katie and Brittany laughed warmly beside us.

For one glorious, unhurried hour on the sunny waterfront boardwalk of Perotti Park—with salt spray sparkling off the harbor pilings, seagulls wheeling overhead, and zero shouting Tiffany or barking house managers anywhere within five hundred miles—the five of us ate cups of creamy New England clam chowder and warm rolls together around an accessible picnic table, letting Chelsea say a happy, peaceful goodbye to the island where she and Dad were born.

Then we rolled back up Dad's minivan ramp, buckled in together, took Interstate 895 West—signed *SOUTH*—back across both bay bridges to Interstate 95 South, and drove Chelsea all the way home to Virginia Beach.
