## Saturday, February 11, 2017, 9:30 PM — Rolling Up Our Closet Sleeping Bags & Moving All Six Full-Time Onto the Alaskan King

![Inside their warm lamp-lit bedroom on Arvin Road in Virginia Beach on the night of February 11, 2017, while the open white bedroom closet shows neatly rolled-up navy sleeping bags stored on the top shelf behind two parked black-and-silver SwiftChair power wheelchairs, all six adults relax happily side by side in modest flannel and knit pajamas atop the navy-and-cream patchwork quilt of their extra-wide solid-oak wheelchair-transfer-height Alaskan King platform bed: 29-year-old Wesley in navy flannel pajamas holding his blue silicone ring on the left, 31-year-old Heather Prairie in forest-green flannel leaning warmly between Wesley and her husband Nate, 31-year-old Nathaniel Prairie in grey plaid flannel holding hands with twin sister Katie in lavender knit pajamas, 34-year-old cousin Chelsea in cream waffle-knit sleepwear hugging plush green Shelly Turtle below her blue and purple asthma inhalers on the oak headboard shelf, and 31-year-old cousin Brittany in burgundy flannel pajamas and thin silver wire-rimmed oval glasses wrapping an affectionate arm around Chelsea.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b005/feb_2017_arvin_road_first_night_out_of_closet_six_person_alaskan_king.jpg)

"Okay, twenty-minute timer is officially done," Heather said softly at nine-fifty on the frosty Saturday night of February 11, 2017, clicking off the little brass timer and setting our warm chamomile-mint tea mugs onto the oak nightstand beside the lamp while winter wind rustled the pine needles outside our heavy blackout curtains on Arvin Road. "Do you four want the giant sleeping bag in the closet tonight while Wes and I head next door, or do we want five more minutes on the quilt?"

Lying right in the warm middle of the nine-foot orthopedic mattress in my grey plaid flannel pajamas—with Heather nestled cozily against my right shoulder in her forest-green flannel pajamas and Wesley stretched out peacefully right beside Heather in his navy-blue flannel holding his smooth blue silicone ring, while on my left Katie lay tucked close in her lavender knit pajamas lacing her fingers through mine beside Chelsea in her cream waffle-knit pajamas hugging Shelly Turtle beneath her blue and purple inhalers on the oak headboard shelf and Brittany in her burgundy flannel pajamas and silver wire-rimmed glasses—I stayed completely still and listened to my own ribs.

For thirteen and a half years—five thousand consecutive nights stretching back to Apartment A6 on Greenway Court in August 2003—lying down on a bed after dark had meant waiting for a cold iron fist to close around my lungs at the first creak of vinyl siding or distant set of headlights.

Outside our drawn curtains right then, a February gust brushed pine cones softly across the eaves, and down at the corner of Arvin Road a neighbor's sedan rolled quietly past toward Indian River Road.

Nothing rang inside my chest.

My shoulders were sunk two full inches into warm, supportive memory foam instead of pressing into compressed flannel over hard plywood subflooring, with my wife warm against one shoulder and my twin sister holding my hand on the other. Our front and back deadbolts were thrown, our home address was shielded behind our registered agent and commercial mailbox, Brenda had zero idea where Arvin Road was, Tyler was locked away inside a state medical prison ward, and King's College was six hundred miles north under a permanent probate receivership order.

Looking gently past Katie's shoulder toward Chelsea first, Katie spoke in a low, unhurried whisper. Over the past seven months—thanks to hundreds of gentle, loving, zero-shame reminders from Brittany, Heather, Katie, and me—Chelsea hadn't wandered down the chilly hall without clothes in weeks, proudly patting her warm chest and her puffer pockets every night after her bath. Yet nightfall had always been the hardest test for her fear of Mount Regalis too.

"What about you, Chelsea?" Katie asked gently. "Did you hear the wind outside? Are you scared Group Home Two-Face might try to come here tonight, or do you want the closet bag?"

Chelsea blinked her warm brown eyes slowly in the bedside lamplight, reached one hand up to tap her blue and purple puffers resting right within reach on the oak headboard shelf, and hugged Shelly Turtle contentedly under her chin while Brittany wrapped a protective arm around her shoulder.

"Door locked," Chelsea murmured steadily through her severe speech impediment, working each short, slurred phrase out without a hint of panic or wheezing in her throat. "Judge paper say Group Home Two-Face g-gone forever. Me got warm chest. Me got puffers. Me safe w-with all six."

On my other side, Heather turned gently toward Wesley—who would turn thirty next month—to make sure he wasn't feeling crowded out of his side room down the hall. "What about you, Wes? Do you want your own bed next door tonight, or do you want to sleep right here next to me on the big bed every night?"

Wesley didn't hesitate for half a second. Grinning ear to ear, he patted the navy-and-cream patchwork quilt right beside Heather's left arm twice with his open palm, tucked his blue silicone ring comfortably against his chest, and rested his head squarely back on his pillow—visibly relaxed down to his toes knowing Heather was right there beside him from bedtime till sunrise without ever having to wake up scared in a dark room alone.

Beside my left shoulder, Katie lifted her head half an inch off her pillow and looked straight into my eyes in quiet amazement.

"Nate?" Katie whispered. "My heart isn't racing at all."

"Mine isn't either," I whispered back, smiling slowly in the warm lamplight as I kissed Heather's temple on my right and squeezed Katie's fingers on my left. Looking past Katie at Brittany's hopeful eyes shining behind her thin silver wire-rimmed oval glasses, I nodded toward the open white bedroom closet just beyond our two parked SwiftChairs, where our zipped-together navy sleeping bags still lay spread across the floor. "Brittany, Heather... what happens if the two of you roll those sleeping bags up onto the top closet shelf tonight and Wes moves his pillow in here full-time?"

Brittany and Heather let out the softest, happiest gasps in the room at the exact same instant.

"Nobody move a muscle—we've been waiting all winter to hear those words!" Brittany whispered joyfully.

Slipping quickly off the smooth oak transfer edge together before any of us could second-guess ourselves, Heather and Brittany knelt inside the wide bedroom closet one last time. Together they unzipped the brass perimeter teeth joining our oversized navy flannel-lined sleeping bags, rolled each retired sleeping bag into a neat, tight cylinder, tied their cotton straps fast, and lifted the rolls side by side onto the top closet storage shelf—leaving the closet floor completely bare, open, and empty behind our two quietly charging wheelchairs.

Climbing right back onto the Alaskan King platform bed—Heather settling warmly between Wesley and my right side while Brittany settled in beside Chelsea and Katie on the right—Heather clicked the bedside lamp down to a soft golden glow as we pulled the oversized patchwork quilt up across all six sets of knees.

Katie let out a long, low, utterly shameless groan of pure physical relief as every vertebra in her spine finally melted all the way down into thick orthopedic memory foam for the very first full night since we were eighteen years old.

"Oh, my gosh, Nate," my twin sister breathed against my left shoulder. "Why did nobody tell us real mattresses felt *this* good?"

Brittany laughed softly into the quiet room. "I only spent eleven months sleeping on closet plywood—the last seven of them wedged against a drywall stud—trying to break the news gently."

"And look at Wes," Heather murmured fondly against my right shoulder, resting her hand gently over Wesley's forearm as his breathing slowed immediately into deep, tranquil sleep without a trace of nighttime anxiety. "He fell asleep faster than I've ever seen him fall asleep in his life."

"Bed is soft," Chelsea murmured sleepily on Katie's other side, snuggling Shelly Turtle beneath her chin where nobody in a group home could ever yell at her or take her puffers away again. "No closet. All six together."

And when Sunday morning sunlight filtered bright and gold around the edges of our curtains at eight-thirty the next day, all six of us woke up in that exact same unbroken line—out of the closet for good, and home together on our Alaskan King.
