## Saturday, March 25, 2017, 2:10 PM — Two Rescued Tidewater Painted Turtles & Chelsea's Favorite Hands-Only Tutorials

"L-look, Nate! Look, Katie! Shelly Turtle got real swimmy friends!" thirty-five-year-old Chelsea cheered from the center cushion of our tan living-room sofa, working happily around her thick speech impediment as she bounced her heels twice against the carpet in her soft, tagless sky-blue fleece loungewear—both asthma inhalers tucked safely inside the front hip pocket Heather and Brittany had sewn onto her pullover—and held her beloved plush green sea turtle two inches from the sparkling tempered glass of our brand-new sixty-gallon aquarium.

Parked hub to hub right beside the left arm of the sofa with our inner fingers laced warmly across our SwiftChair armrests, Katie and I grinned from ear to ear while Brittany sat beside Chelsea smoothing a stray chestnut curl off Chelsea's forehead, thirty-year-old Wesley stood happily beside the kitchen pass-through with Heather rinsing fresh heads of dark-green romaine lettuce in a colander, and Dad wiped a drop of conditioned aquarium water off his hands with a clean dish towel.

Six weeks after all six of us had rolled up our retired closet sleeping bags for good and woken up together on our solid-oak Alaskan King down the hall—turning Wesley's and Chelsea's freed-up side rooms into sunny hobby and art spaces—watching Chelsea and Wesley bond over every daily routine inside a house where nobody ever barked at either of them felt like watching spring thaw out ten winters at once.

Two Saturdays earlier, when Chelsea and Wesley had lingered together in front of an aquatic rescue display on Virginia Beach Boulevard mesmerized by two eastern painted turtles basking on wet cork bark, Heather, Katie, and I hadn't even needed to finish a sentence before agreeing to adopt a rescued Tidewater pair right into our living room. Using the exact twelve-volt fail-safe thermostat relay wiring Katie and I had first mastered back in August of 2007 when we cooled our office networking closet, along with Heather's eye for quiet sensory-friendly classrooms, we had curated a saved playlist of calm, zero-drama hands-only aquarium build tutorials on StreamTube—steady overhead tripod videos showing black-nitrile-gloved hands arranging smooth river slate, silicone canister-filter grommets, and cork ramps above clear closed captions with zero shouting hosts.

For both thirty-five-year-old Chelsea and thirty-year-old Wesley, those calm, predictable hands-only videos and their shared afternoon feeding routine turned out to be pure magic.

"P-put on turtle rock movie again, Nate?" Chelsea asked eagerly, tucking plush Shelly Turtle snugly into the crook of her elbow on the sofa and pointing toward the angled living-room television across the rug as Wesley carried out a clean cutting board of washed romaine leaves beside Heather. "S-show Sunny an' Pebble how we made their warm rock!"

"Coming right up, Cous," I laughed, picking up the living-room media remote from my lap tray and tapping knuckles twice with Wesley as he set the greens tray down on the coffee table.

## Saturday, March 25, 2017, 2:35 PM — Silicone Grommets, Fail-Safe Thermostats, & Wesley and Chelsea's Neat Squares of Romaine

![Inside the warm, sunlit living room on Arvin Road in March 2017 beside a low oak stand holding a crystal-clear sixty-gallon indoor painted-turtle habitat with basking stones and live plants, thirty-five-year-old Cousin Chelsea sits happily in modest sky-blue fleece loungewear on the tan sofa hugging plush green Shelly Turtle and pointing delightedly at an angled television playing a StreamTube video of black-nitrile-gloved hands arranging river slate with closed captions, while Brittany smiles beside a wax-paper plate of neat romaine squares and twins Katie and Nathaniel Prairie hold hands in their black SwiftChairs.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b005/mar_2017_arvin_road_chelsea_turtle_habitat_streamtube_hands_only.jpg)

Clicking open our saved *Indoor Painted Turtle Habitat* video on the angled widescreen television filled the quiet living room immediately with the gentle trickle of filtered water and calm closed captions reviewing basking-dock ramp angles, vibration-dampening silicone canister-filter grommets, and external fail-safe temperature probes.

On the sofa, Chelsea leaned happily against Brittany's shoulder, her eyes tracking the black-nitrile-gloved hands on the television and then darting proudly six feet to the left—checking off every single matching piece inside our own low, thirty-inch-high oak-stand tank that Dad and Heather had leveled right at wheelchair and sofa height so none of us ever had to crane our necks up over a high rim.

"W-we got quiet rubbers on the pump!" Chelsea announced proudly, pointing at the closed oak cabinet doors beneath our tank where the canister filter ran without a whisper of vibration. "N-no loud buzz for Wes an' me!"

"Zero buzz at all, Chelsea—Katie and Heather picked out the exact silicone grommets so the pump stays whisper-quiet," Dad chuckled, pulling a dining chair over beside my right wheel with a tall glass of iced tea.

"An' Nate put safe-box wire on the warm light so water never gets too hot!" Chelsea added, beaming over at me as our two rescued Tidewater painted turtles—*Sunny*, with bright yellow stripes along her neck, and *Pebble*, half an inch smaller with a smooth olive carapace—paddled side by side through the hornwort stems and hauled themselves effortlessly up the gentle cork-bark basking ramp under the golden lamp.

"Same exact fail-safe relay model keeping our Linux servers cool down the hall since 2007," I smiled back at her, watching Pebble stretch all four webbed feet out beneath the basking bulb in total contentment.

Then came Chelsea and Wesley's favorite five minutes of every afternoon. Sitting side by side at the coffee table with Brittany and Heather smiling proudly behind them, thirty-year-old Wesley demonstrated his methodical craft—tearing washed dark-green romaine leaves into tidy, uniform bite-sized squares on clean bakery wax paper—while Chelsea carefully copied every square right beside him until both sheets of wax paper matched row for row. Carrying their wax-paper tray together in their fleece socks over to the low open feeding hatch Dad slid back for them, Wesley and Chelsea set the greens gently onto the dry slate ledge inside our tank.

"Eat good greens, Sunny! Eat good, Pebble!" Chelsea whispered softly with a radiant, unhurried grin, kneeling comfortably on a thick foam cushion beside Wesley at eye level with the glass next to Katie's wheelchair and mine while Sunny walked right over to nibble the first green square and Wesley tapped two quiet, proud celebratory taps against his palm. "Wes an' Shelly Turtle say welcome home!"

Across our inner armrests, Katie squeezed my left hand twice while Heather rested her warm hand on my right shoulder—all of us watching Wesley and Chelsea beam side by side in front of their thriving turtle sanctuary, knowing our six-person home had finally found every piece of its heart.
