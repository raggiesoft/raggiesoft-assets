## Saturday, June 16, 2018, 3:15 PM CDT — Leaving North Lion Scales & Tails: One Last Earned Hug & Chelsea's First Hug Outside Our Family

![On the sunlit Saturday afternoon sidewalk plaza outside the red-brick-and-glass storefront of North Lion Scales & Tails in Maplewood, Minnesota on June 16, 2018 with the cut green Grand Opening ribbon halves tied to brass stanchions, thirty-six-year-old Chelsea in her lavender fleece set with plush Shelly Turtle tucked under one elbow wraps both arms warmly around thirty-one-year-old Staff Member Wesley Collins in his forest-green STAFF polo, tan khakis, and magnetic WESLEY badge—hugging someone outside her own family for the very first time in her life as Brittany covers her mouth in happy tears beside Katie and Nathaniel in their black SwiftChairs, and Heather, Dan, Jonas, Nora, and Molly smile warmly from the storefront.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b005/jun_2018_maplewood_north_lion_farewell_chelsea_wesley_magical_first_hug.jpg#fullwidth)

Warm golden mid-afternoon sunlight slanted across the concrete sidewalk plaza outside *North Lion Scales & Tails*, where the two halves of the cut forest-green *GRAND OPENING* satin ribbon fluttered gently from their brass stanchions and steady streams of Twin Cities families were still stepping off Twin Cities Transit Route 64 to tour the conservatory inside.

With over four hundred visitors having walked and rolled through the doors since eleven o'clock—and our evening Gateway Airlines flight back to Norfolk via Chicago Midway boarding out of Terminal 2 before six—Brittany rolled our two compact carry-on bags out through the automatic sliding glass doors while Dan, Heather, Staff Member Wesley, Jonas, Nora, and Molly walked out onto the sunny plaza to say goodbye.

"Safe flights back to Virginia, you four—and tell your dad that Sunny and Pebble better review that green shrimp ball five stars!" Dan grinned warmly, shaking my hand and Brittany's hand while respecting Katie's cheerful wave from her armrest, right beside twelve-year-old Jonas humming his happy three-note farewell and Nora—still linked inner-hand to inner-hand with her twin brother—reaching out gently with her free fingers to give Chelsea's lavender cuff two silent, loving squeezes.

Then thirty-three-year-old Heather knelt smoothly down on one knee beside my SwiftChair right there in front of the red-brick storefront she and Dan had spent a decade building toward.

Flipping my right armrest up without needing a single word, I leaned forward and shared one more long, steady, well-earned two-armed hug with Heather in the warm Minnesota sun—both of us smiling cleanly when we eased back, grateful down to our bones that neither seventeen-year-old heartbreak nor thirteen hundred miles had won in the end.

And three feet to our right beside the glass Route 64 bus shelter, something genuinely magical happened.

Ever since Dad, Brittany, Katie, and I had walked through a Rhode Island probate court order to bring thirty-six-year-old Chelsea home from eight years inside *Regalis Community House*—a place where cold-eyed staffers had grabbed residents by the forearms and punished anyone who spoke past seven-thirty at night—Chelsea had never once hugged a single human being on earth who wasn't a member of our immediate family. At home in Virginia Beach, she cuddled happily with Katie, Brittany, and me in our four-person oak nest and hugged Uncle David every morning in the kitchen; everywhere else in the world, from Capitol corridors to neighborhood grocery aisles, Chelsea kept plush green Shelly Turtle held flat against the center of her chest with both hands like a soft, polite shield.

Standing tall on the sunlit concrete in his forest-green collared *STAFF* polo, tan khakis, and magnetic *WESLEY* badge, thirty-one-year-old Wesley smiled warmly down at Chelsea and held his closed right hand steady at waist height—expecting one last friendly two-tap knuckle salute before our bus arrived.

Chelsea paused.

Looking straight up into Wesley's unhurried, completely gentle brown eyes—remembering how he had shared his laminated photo cards of Clover, Scooter, and Barnaby without rushing her by half a second, outfitted her in matching blue *nice glove man* gloves, and slipped their instant photograph into Sunny and Pebble's green paper treat bag looped around her wrist—thirty-six-year-old Chelsea slowly tucked plush Shelly Turtle under just her left elbow, leaving her front completely unguarded.

Opening both lavender fleece arms wide in the afternoon sun, Chelsea asked softly, "Can I give you a real big-brother turtle hug, Wes?"

Behind her wire-rimmed glasses, Brittany gasped without a sound, pressing four trembling fingers straight over her lips as hot, joyful tears sprang into her eyes. Beside my left wheel, Katie's hand gripped mine so tightly across our armrests I felt her pulse jump.

For half a heartbeat, thirty-one-year-old Wesley stood completely still in pure, radiant wonder. Then, with the gentlest care imaginable, he slipped his smooth blue silicone ring safely into his khaki pocket so both hands were free, leaned slightly down to Chelsea's height, and wrapped both arms steadily and respectfully around her shoulders—holding on warmly while Chelsea beamed cheek-to-cheek against his green *STAFF* collar, hugging someone outside her own family for the very first time in her adult life!

## Saturday, June 16, 2018, 3:24 PM CDT — Wesley Insists on Seeing Us Off, & Why We Booked Chicago Midway Instead of O'Hare or Detroit

Down White Bear Avenue rolled the blue-and-white nose of our southbound Twin Cities Transit Route 64 coach, braking smoothly toward the yellow curb strip.

Right as Chelsea eased happily back from hugging Wesley and Brittany reached down for the telescoping handles of our two carry-on bags, Wesley let out an unmistakable, booming two-note vocal protest!

Planting both sneakers squarely beside the bus shelter glass, thirty-one-year-old Wesley tapped the center of his own chest twice, pointed firmly at Heather, pointed at the kneeling city bus, and swept his flat right hand upward at a thirty-degree angle like a jetliner climbing into the Minnesota sky—shaking his head emphatically at the notion of saying goodbye on the sidewalk!

Heather burst out laughing, wiping a stray tear from the corner of her eye as she looked over at Dan in the storefront doorway.

"Don't even bother trying to talk him out of it, Nate," Heather grinned warmly. "Wes already made up his mind over lunch in the breakroom—the two of us are riding with you guys all the way to MSP Airport until you hit the TSA checkpoint!"

"Go for it!" Dan called back cheerfully, resting one arm around Molly's shoulders beside Jonas and Nora at the open doors. "Dr. Meyer, our afternoon crew, Molly, the twins, and I have the zoo running like clockwork—go see our Virginia VIPs all the way to security!"

Thirty seconds later—after Heather ducked behind the oak counter just long enough to grab her StarPass wallet while keeping her forest-green *North Lion* polo shirt, tan khakis, and magnetic *HEATHER* badge right on beside Staff Member Wesley—all six of us rolled and stepped up the kneeling ramp of Route 64 together, transferring effortlessly across the level concrete island at Maplewood Plaza onto limited-stop Route 54 and hopping the zero-step airport light-rail shuttle one stop straight into the quiet, sunlit lobby of MSP Terminal 2.

Rolling hub to hub with Katie up to the lowered accessible counter of Gateway Airlines while the friendly agent printed laminated manual-freewheel battery instruction tags for both of our SwiftChairs—so ramp crews in Chicago and Norfolk would store both powerchairs upright inside the full-height cargo hold of mainline PAC 737 jets without ever tipping a frame sideways—Heather glanced curiously at the three-letter airport codes printing onto our bag tags.

"MSP to MDW to ORF," Heather read aloud, smiling over at Katie and me as Wesley proudly stood guard beside Chelsea's carry-on tote. "You two are changing planes at Chicago Midway instead of O'Hare or Detroit?"

"By strict design," Katie grinned, patting her armrest decisively. "If we flew Meridian Airlines through Chicago O'Hare, changing between Concourse B and Concourse C means rolling underground through *The Prismatic Corridor*—eight hundred feet of flashing overhead rainbow neon tubes, mirrored glass walls, and echoing electronic chimes bouncing off every surface. Five minutes inside that tunnel and my sensory overload meter would be pegged straight through the roof before we even found our gate! Chicago Midway is one calm, flat, single-level X-shaped concourse with zero flashing neon tunnels and full-sized 737 cargo doors on both legs."

Then Katie's blue eyes flicked upward toward the overhead terminal departure screen—where, two rows above our five-fifty-five Gateway flight to Midway, an AeroSky flight to *DETROIT (DTW)* was blinking *BOARDING*—and a wicked, bone-dry laugh escaped my twin sister's lips right there in the middle of Terminal 2.

"And as for skipping Detroit," Katie added dryly, leaning an inch toward Heather and me with a glint of pure mischief, "if Nate and I had a ninety-minute layover in Detroit, I'd get us kicked out of the concourse laughing my head off picturing Brandon Mercer getting bounced right back across the Windsor tunnel by Canadian border control and hauled away in handcuffs!"

For two full seconds Heather blinked—remembering what we had just told her inside the Education Classroom four hours earlier—before Heather, Brittany, and I all lost it laughing right there in front of the luggage scale while Chelsea giggled happily hugging Shelly Turtle and Wesley chimed in with a cheerful two-note laugh of his own!

## Saturday, June 16, 2018, 4:38 PM CDT — Outside the Terminal 2 TSA Checkpoint: A Three-Way Hug?! & One Last Goodbye

![Inside the sunlit public departure concourse right outside the Security Checkpoint stanchions at Minneapolis–Saint Paul Airport Terminal 2 on Saturday late afternoon, June 16, 2018, thirty-three-year-old Katie Prairie and Nathaniel Prairie parked hub-to-hub in their black SwiftChairs and kneeling thirty-three-year-old Heather Lindstrom in her forest-green North Lion polo shirt and magnetic HEATHER badge share an emotional, unprecedented three-way hug on the left while thirty-one-year-old Staff Member Wesley Collins and thirty-six-year-old Chelsea with plush Shelly Turtle and her green treat bag hug goodbye one last time beside Brittany on the right.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b005/jun_2018_msp_airport_tsa_three_way_hug_katie_nate_heather_wesley_chelsea.jpg)

Ten paces ahead of our front caster wheels stretched the retractable blue belt stanchions beneath the clean overhead terminal sign reading *SECURITY CHECKPOINT — TICKETED PASSENGERS ONLY*—the exact line on the polished terrazzo floor where Minnesota had to stay on one side and Virginia had to roll through to the other.

Slowing to a stop five feet outside the entry lane so commuter travelers could pass cleanly around us, thirty-three-year-old Heather let out a long, steady breath, smiled down at me with bright hazel eyes, and knelt smoothly onto one knee right in front of my SwiftChair—still careful, just as she had been on the Maplewood plaza that morning, to angle herself squarely in front of *my* chair without crowding Katie's personal space beside me.

"Thank you for coming all this way, Nate," Heather said softly, opening her arms for one last goodbye hug. "Tell your dad hi from all of us—and keep an eye on your mailbox for Wes's zoo postcards."

Clicking my joystick power off and flipping up my outer right armrest, I leaned forward and wrapped my arms warmly around Heather's shoulders—expecting a quiet two-person goodbye before we rolled into the TSA queue.

Then a soft mechanical click sounded two inches to my left.

Across thirty-three years of life—and with zero exceptions outside Dad, Brittany, and Chelsea inside our own home since November of 2005—Katie Prairie does not let *anyone* touch her except me. Not campaign donors, not fellow delegates in Richmond, not reporters, not well-meaning strangers. Polite nods from six inches clear of her wheels, always; physical hugs with outsiders, *never*. Heather had known that boundary since we were sixteen years old at Northwood High, and she had honored it without a blink all day long.

Without saying a word of warning, thirty-three-year-old Katie switched her own SwiftChair power button off right beside me, flipped both of our shared inner armrests straight up out of the way, leaned sideways across the gap between our seats, and wrapped *both* of her arms all the way around Heather's shoulders and mine—pulling all three of us tightly together into a genuine, breathtaking three-way hug!

Against Katie's royal-blue sleeve, Heather's whole breath caught in a stunned, quiet gasp.

"Katie...?" Heather whispered, instant tears spilling over her lashes as she carefully, reverently rested one arm across Katie's back and one arm across mine—holding on as if we had just handed her the moon.

"You proved it for ten straight years when nobody was watching, Heather Lindstrom," Katie whispered fiercely and warmly right beside Heather's ear, giving both of us one firm, unmistakable squeeze before easing gently back into her seat and flipping our inner armrests down so she could link her fingers right back through mine. "Take good care of Wes—and don't you dare wait thirteen years to ride a bus with us in Virginia Beach."

Heather wiped both cheeks with the back of her wrist, laughing through tears she didn't even try to hide anymore, and nodded on her word of honor. "We won't. I promise."

Right beside us in front of the retractable TSA belt stanchions, thirty-one-year-old Staff Member Wesley and thirty-six-year-old Chelsea didn't hesitate for a second this time—stepping straight into one last warm, joyful two-armed goodbye hug of their own, with plush Shelly Turtle peeking snugly out of Sunny and Pebble's green paper treat bag alongside their blue-gloved instant photo looped safely on Chelsea's wrist!

Stepping gently back with a proud, beaming smile beneath his magnetic *WESLEY* badge, Wesley tapped Chelsea's knuckles twice, tapped Brittany's offered hand once, reached down to tap Katie's knuckles twice, and finally tapped the back of my right hand twice—no longer a heartbroken sixteen-year-old boy at a mall table trying to understand why he would never see his first guy friend again, just a proud thirty-one-year-old working man who knew down to his bones where his friends lived and that postcards and visits ran both ways now.

"See you soon, buddy," I smiled up at him, my throat full and my chest completely at peace.

Presenting our four boarding passes and IDs at the accessible TSA podium together, Katie, Chelsea, Brittany, and I rolled and walked side by side past the glass security partition toward our gate for Chicago Midway and Norfolk—looking back over our shoulders one last time to see Heather and Staff Member Wesley standing tall shoulder to shoulder in the sunlit Minnesota terminal, waving us all the way home to Virginia.
