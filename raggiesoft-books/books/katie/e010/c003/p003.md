## Monday, May 18, 2026, 11:30 AM — Roanoke's Third Street Station, The RVT Roanoke Express, & The Brooks Family Compound on Lee Highway

"Watch how fast the unified validator reads the card, Dad," forty-one-year-old Katie smiled at eleven-thirty on Monday morning, May 18, 2026, as the three of us detrained from ARTS *The Patriot* at Roanoke's Third Street Station and rolled across the bus plaza to the waiting forty-five-foot Roanoke Valley Transit coach.

Because the passenger rail extension to Christiansburg Cambria Station at 800 Depot Street NE wouldn't open for regular train service until the following year in 2027, Roanoke Valley Transit operated both the official *ARTS Train Connector* and *The Roanoke Express* out of Third Street Station—linking downtown Roanoke down Interstate 81 to the Christiansburg Transit Center at Falling Branch, New River Valley Mall, and the CPI Transit Center in Blacksburg.

As the operator deployed the coach lift and secured our two SwiftChairs in the front ADA bays, Katie, Dad, and I each tapped our blue-and-slate contactless Blue Ridge Go-Cards—printed with the regional slogan *One Card. Three Systems. The Entire Valley.*—against the electronic reader, deducting the standard three-dollar RVT Express fare with a crisp green beep.

Forty minutes later, our *Roanoke Express* coach glided off Interstate 81 at Exit 118 into the ground-level eight-bay bus terminal beneath VDOT's eight-level, six-thousand-space garage at the Christiansburg Transit Center at Falling Branch, exited directly onto the grade-separated buses-only flyover ramp onto the US-460 Bypass without touching a single local traffic light, made its intermediate regional stop at the New River Valley Mall Transit Center outside Hudson's and The Spot, and pulled into the multi-bay CPI Transit Center in Blacksburg at 12:30 PM.

For the next four hours, Katie, Dad, and I rode the entire New River Valley transit network alongside the students, faculty, and working families who used it every day.

Aboard a sixty-foot Prairie Coachworks articulated bus in the *Mountain Mist and River Ribbon* livery on Blue Ridge Transit Route 1—displaying <led>1 CBURG TRANS CTR</led> and <led>via S MAIN ST</led> on its amber Luminator sign—we rolled down Progress Street NE in Blacksburg, where Ryan and Cassidy O'Connell, their twin cousins Evan and Tyler Wright, and their youngest sister Holly O'Connell were waiting at the Progress Street bus stop after ten o'clock in the morning to wave and hand Katie a thermos of cold ginger ale for the road. Continuing south down Business 460 into downtown Christiansburg to the Pepper Plaza Transit Center right next door to the Montgomery County Courthouse across from 105 Pepper Street and Valu-Mart, we transferred onto a thirty-five-foot low-floor bus on BT Route 2 (*The Christiansburg Local*, displaying <led>2 NEW RIVER VALLEY MALL</led> / <led>via AQUATIC CTR & CAMBRIA</led>). Rolling past the two Ironwood Collective apartment complexes on Wades Lane and turning down Mill Lane NE past the Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center onto Depot Street NE, we inspected the newly paved off-street bus loop at 800 Depot Street NE—Christiansburg Cambria Station—where Katie recorded a campaign video pledging that when ARTS *The Patriot* passenger trains began pulling into Cambria Station in 2027, she would use her seat in the United States Senate to protect federal passenger rail and rural public transit funding all the way to Bristol.

After riding BT Route 4 east out Roanoke Street past the Exit 118C hotels and the Simmons Road NE deviation through the CPI Christiansburg Campus—universally called "the Law School" by every rider on the bus—out to the purpose-built VDOT sixty-foot turnaround loop at Woodland Drive, and taking a loop on 100-percent fare-free Radford Transit aboard *The Radford Connector* down Prices Fork Road and Peppers Ferry Road through Belview, Centerville, and Fairlawn across the New River Bridge into Radford's McConnell Transit Center, we boarded Blue Ridge Transit Route 3 (*The Radford Link*) heading west out of Pepper Plaza along U.S. Route 11.

Halfway between Christiansburg and Radford along Lee Highway, the Route 3 operator tapped his brakes for the privately funded, covered bus shelter sitting on the one-dollar perpetual public transit easement that Daniel Brooks had conveyed at the foot of his family's driveway back in 2014.

When the front ramp folded down onto the concrete pad and Katie, Dad, and I rolled up the heated, zero-grade brushed-concrete walkway onto the front porch, the front double doors swung wide open—and the real-life heroes of "The Accessibility Project" came out to meet us, led by the original trio from Bennett Hall Room 214.

Thirty-year-old Jordan Sinclair-Brooks rolled his custom manual chair straight up to Katie and me to shake our hands, wearing his rectangular prescription glasses and behind-the-ear hearing aids, flanked on either side by his wife Aubrie Jean Kelly and Madison Elizabeth Scott, along with Zoë Wren Brooks, Lauren Nicole Barnes, Hannah Parker Anderson, Mark Shawn Anderson, Isabel Claire Dawson, Jessamine "Jess" Mercer, Sarah Kristyn Braithwaite, Jessica Gallagher, and Daniel, Laura, and Megan Brooks—every single one of them wearing royal-blue *Katie Prairie for U.S. Senate* lapel buttons.

Rolling side by side down the sweeping forty-eight-inch hallways lined with recessed oak wall bumpers and through the zero-threshold pocket doors into the communal kitchen—where dual-height islands let Jordan, Katie, and me roll right underneath the counter beside Laura, Aubrie Jean, and Madison—Katie looked around the room in quiet awe.

"We've spent twenty-five years designing our own living spaces around our wheelchairs," Katie said warmly, looking from the heated concrete bus-stop walkway outside to the dual-height kitchen islands and the first-floor grandparents' suite. "Rolling off a regular fixed-route Route 3 bus on Lee Highway straight into a house built like this feels like seeing the exact future we're fighting for."

Beside us at the accessible island, Jordan tapped a sentence into NeoEdit on his Pinnacle Vanguard Pro laptop and triggered EchoType's warm "Sydney" voice:

<aac>When Daniel and Laura built this compound on Lee Highway, they proved that universal design isn't a medical burden—it's what makes a home belong to everyone. Now let's go show the rest of Virginia.</aac>

"And we've already registered over fourteen hundred new student voters across CPI and Radford University for the June sixteenth primary," Zoë added, pulling up her tablet angled toward her eyes to read off the campus precinct tallies she, Aubrie Jean, and Mark had organized with the student disability union at Callahan Hall. "Preston Holloway's campaign sent a surrogate to Blacksburg last week who parked in a hundred-and-fifty-dollar VIP lot and left after twenty minutes. Meanwhile, every student on the Route 1 articulated buses and the Radford Connector knows you and Nate actually ride the bus and play our song at every stop."

"And speaking of buses," thirty-year-old Madison Elizabeth Scott smiled, tapping the Blue Ridge Go-Card clipped to her lanyard as Aubrie Jean and Jordan grinned beside her, "the three of us from Room 214 are riding down to Carroll County with you today! Bluewater is my hometown—and the Bluewater Bunker where my parents, my brother Bob, and my cousin Emily live is forty-five minutes down Interstate 81 and Interstate 77 from here. The next southbound *Maroon Express* pulls into the Christiansburg Transit Center at 1:55 PM. Ready to go show Bluewater what a real Senator looks like?"

## Monday, May 18, 2026, 2:30 PM — Riding Bluewater Transit's Maroon Express Into Bluewater With the Room 214 Crew & Winning the June 16 Primary

*<pa>Bluewater Transit Maroon Express to Bluewater Transit Center via Christiansburg Transit Center,</pa>* the exterior ADA speaker of the gleaming forty-five-foot Sainte-Claire 45H3 over-the-road highway coach announced at the Christiansburg Transit Center bay while its two-screen amber LED sign alternated between <led>MAROON EXPRESS</led> and <led>BLUEWATER EXP via CBURG TC</led>.

Unlike the low-floor city transit buses on Blue Ridge Transit, Bluewater Transit's *Maroon Express* highway coaches were equipped with heavy-duty mid-coach hydraulic ADA wheelchair lifts mounted above the under-floor luggage bays and expanded multi-bay wheelchair securement rows inside the cabin. With the entire Room 214 trio—Jordan, Aubrie Jean, and Madison—riding right alongside us and Dad tapping our Blue Ridge Go-Cards for the five-dollar Express fare, Katie, Jordan, and I rode the hydraulic platform up into the coach cabin, locked into the floor tie-downs, and watched the Blue Ridge mountains rise outside the tinted coach windows as we cruised south down Interstate 81 past the Dublin Park and Ride at Justin's Station (Exit 98) and the Max Meadows Park and Ride (Exit 84), turned south onto Interstate 77 up the mountain grade past the Bluewater Park and Ride at Exit 14 (where the coach's GPS automatically dropped the Go-Card validator to one-dollar local mode for the final hop into town), and rolled into the Bluewater Transit Center at 2:35 PM.

Directly across the street stood Bluewater High School—and five minutes earlier, at 2:30 PM on the dot, the final dismissal bell had rung.

As the operator lowered Katie, Jordan, and me down the mid-coach hydraulic lift onto the sunny concrete transit plaza beside Aubrie Jean, Madison, and Dad, hundreds of high-school students, teachers, cafeteria workers, and parents were pouring across the crosswalk into the transit center for the 2:45 PM clockface pulse—where Madison's parents David and Diane Scott, her younger brother Bob in his wheelchair, and her cousin Emily were waiting by the shelter to wave and cheer us in. Surrounding the bays stood Bluewater Transit's twenty-nine-foot and thirty-five-foot low-floor buses waiting to depart in every direction across the Blue Ridge plateau: the hourly Purple Line heading west on US-58 and US-221 to Galax Community College; the Gold Line heading northeast up US-221 through Dugspur and Willis to the Floyd County Courthouse (where riders connected down State Route 8 to Riner and BT Route 8); the Emerald Line heading east on US-58 across the Blue Ridge Parkway at Meadows of Dan and down scenic Lovers Leap into Stuart and the Martinsville Transit Center (where riders transferred directly onto Piedmont Regional Transit to Danville); the Silver Line heading south down US-52 and Interstate 77 past Fancy Gap to the state-line transfer hub; and a thirty-five-foot low-floor Mountain Empire Transit *Highlands Express* coach waiting to carry riders west toward Wytheville, Marion, Abingdon, and Bristol.

"People in Washington look at a county map of Southwest Virginia and write Carroll County off as deep-red Restore the Republic territory without ever setting foot here," Madison told Katie and me proudly as she, Aubrie Jean, and Jordan greeted Bluewater High School teachers, working parents, and Galax Community College nursing students who gathered around our three wheelchairs on the plaza while "The Accessibility Project" played crisp and bright from our portable speaker. "What the out-of-town pundits never understand is that Bluewater is the largest incorporated town in the entire Commonwealth of Virginia—solely because the voters here in Bluewater have always refused to break away from Carroll County and become an independent city! If Bluewater were an independent city like Galax or Radford, it would show up as a solid Democratic blue circle on every election map in America."

Right there in front of the *Maroon Express* coach during the 2:45 PM pulse—with Katie, Jordan, and me parked side by side in our wheelchairs and Aubrie Jean and Madison handing out campaign literature and voter-registration clipboards beside Dad—Katie spent an hour talking eye-to-eye with Carroll County voters before we boarded Bluewater Transit's Emerald Line east across Lovers Leap through Meadows of Dan and Stuart into Martinsville, transferred onto Piedmont Regional Transit into Danville, and rode the evening bus back up to Bluewater to catch the northbound *Maroon Express*.

Four weeks later—on Primary Night, Tuesday, June 16, 2026—the political establishment in Washington and Richmond learned the hard way what happens when you dismiss the people who ride the bus.

Despite retiring Senator Martin Warren's handpicked corporate candidate Preston Holloway spending fourteen million dollars in Super PAC television ads, voters across all eleven Congressional Districts—powered by historic turnout in Hampton Roads, Richmond, the CPI and Radford corridors, Bluewater, Charlottesville, and working-class neighborhoods across Northern Virginia—delivered a seismic upset.

At 8:19 PM, the GNN Decision Desk called the Democratic Primary for State Senator Katie Prairie by an eleven-point landslide: fifty-five point six percent to forty-four point four percent.
