## Wednesday & Thursday, April 14–15, 2027 — Convicting Secretary Halverson and Vice President Vance

![Inside the United States Senate Chamber on April 16, 2027, 42-year-old U.S. Senator Katie Prairie sits at her custom-height mahogany floor desk in her black motorized SwiftChair power wheelchair casting her historic Guilty vote in the impeachment trial while her twin brother Nate (in his SwiftChair) and their father David watch proudly from the Senate Family Gallery.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b006/apr_2027_us_senate_impeachment_guilty_vote_katie_nate_david.jpg)

Two years earlier, in January of 2025, I had been sitting at my desk in the Senate of Virginia in Richmond when President Garrett Thorne nominated Bret Halverson—the slick, hair-sprayed weekend host of *Patriot Watch Weekend* on the Liberty First Network—to serve as United States Secretary of Defense, and when Vice President Julian Vance cast the tie-breaking fiftieth vote to put him in charge of the Pentagon over the objections of every military family in Hampton Roads.

That weekend in 2025, Halverson had gone back on his old cable network for a victory lap, laughing with his former co-hosts about *"that little wheelchair state senator down in Richmond who thought she could stop the Pentagon."*

He wasn't laughing anymore.

On Wednesday afternoon, April 14, 2027, Bret Halverson sat pale and sweating at the respondent's table in the well of the United States Senate Chamber while Chief Justice Jonathan Sterling presided from the marble rostrum and the Reading Clerk called the final roll on Article One and Article Two of his impeachment.

Up on the top tier of the Senate Floor beside the accessible upper doorway, I sat alone at my custom-height mahogany Senator's desk in my compact SwiftChair with my electromagnetic drive brakes locked firm and my red-tabbed impeachment evidence binder resting flat across my lap. Because Senate rules strictly limit the chamber floor during an impeachment trial to sworn United States Senators and officers of the court, my twin brother Nate sat in his matching SwiftChair beside our dad at the brass railing of the Senate Family Gallery directly across the chamber, his blue eyes locked steadily on mine.

Down in the well, Halverson's defense attorneys didn't even look up toward my desk. After the House managers had displayed the screenshots of Halverson texting classified Naval Station Norfolk carrier strike group deployment schedules over a commercial phone app alongside the four-hundred-million-dollar Thorne Global Holdings kickback ledger Nate's scripts had uncovered, seventeen institutional Republican Senators from defense and coastal states had announced they were crossing the aisle.

"Mr. Mendoza," the Reading Clerk called out.

"Guilty," Senator Mendoza answered from across the aisle.

The clerk's eyes moved down the alphabetical tally sheet and looked up toward my mahogany desk at the top of the chamber.

"Ms. Prairie."

For half a second, the entire Senate Chamber went dead silent. Down at the respondent's table, Bret Halverson finally raised his head, looking up the tiered steps of the chamber straight at the wheelchair he had mocked on national television two years ago.

I didn't raise my voice, and I didn't take my hands off my armrests. Looking straight down into the well on behalf of every sailor at Naval Station Norfolk whose operational safety he had treated like a cable-news segment, I gave him the vote I had promised from Richmond in 2025.

"Guilty," I said clearly across the quiet chamber.

By a vote of seventy-one to twenty-nine—four votes above the two-thirds constitutional threshold required under Article I, Section 3 of the Constitution—the Senate convicted and removed Bret Halverson from office, followed ten minutes later by a simple-majority vote permanently disqualifying him from ever holding any office of honor, trust, or profit under the United States again.

Twenty-four hours later, on Thursday afternoon, April 15, 2027, the second domino fell.

Confronted with the unredacted Treasury wire tables proving that Vice President Julian Vance had personally co-signed the offshore routing waivers for the Naval Station Norfolk logistics kickbacks and sat on the exact same unsecured messaging thread while our Fifth Fleet sailors' movements were compromised, sixty-eight Senators—including me—voted Guilty at four-thirty on Thursday afternoon to convict and remove Julian Vance from the Vice Presidency, followed immediately by a second vote permanently disqualifying him from future federal office.

With the Democratic majority in the House of Representatives and our fifty-four-seat majority in the Senate refusing to entertain any Twenty-Fifth Amendment replacement nominee from a disgraced White House, the office of Vice President of the United States stood completely vacant as Thursday night fell over Washington.

## Friday, April 16, 2027, 1:30 PM — The Words of George Mason, James Madison, and Abraham Lincoln

At three o'clock on Friday morning—less than eleven hours before the final Senate deliberation and roll-call vote on the Articles of Impeachment against President Garrett Thorne—the President had gone online from the residence of the White House to unleash one final, desperate tirade against Nate and me.

In all-capital letters, Thorne had called me a *"defective, bitter little cripple on wheels"* and a *"spinster who couldn't keep a man in her twenties and is trying to steal the Presidency from her wheelchair with her shut-in twin brother."*

Even at the very end, Garrett Thorne still didn't understand that every ugly, ableist insult he hurled at my wheelchair and my brother only disgusted more institutional Senators in his own party and cemented the two-thirds coalition against him.

At one-thirty on Friday afternoon, April 16, 2027, inside the closed marble chamber of the United States Senate during the final hour of deliberations before the roll call, I engaged the drive lock on my SwiftChair beside my top-tier mahogany desk, pressed my microphone button, and rested my hands on the edge of my lectern as all ninety-nine of my fellow Senators turned toward my seat.

"Mr. Chief Justice, and my colleagues of the Senate," I said evenly across the silent chamber. "Two hundred and forty years ago, right down the road from where my brother Nate and I grew up in Virginia, George Mason stood at the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia and warned that no man who holds the executive power should ever be placed above the reach of the law when he corrupts his office, asking the delegates: *Shall any man be above justice? Above all, shall that man be above it, who can commit the most extensive injustice?*"

Up in the Family Gallery above the marble columns, Dad rested his hand on Nate's shoulder as Nate looked down at me from his SwiftChair.

"And when another Virginian, James Madison, wrote *Federalist Number 51*," I continued, my voice calm and unhurried, "he didn't tell us to trust the personal goodwill of a President or a Cabinet Secretary. Madison wrote: *If men were angels, no government would be necessary. If angels were to govern men, neither external nor internal controls on government would be necessary. In framing a government which is to be administered by men over men, the great difficulty lies in this: you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself.* Madison and the framers gave this Senate the sole power to try all impeachments specifically to guard the republic against men who treat a public trust as a private business."

I looked down into the well toward the respondent's table where President Garrett Thorne's defense lawyers sat staring down at their legal pads.

"Abraham Lincoln once said that nearly all men can stand adversity, but if you want to test a man's character, give him power—and at Gettysburg, he charged every generation of Americans to ensure that government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth," I said. "We gave Garrett Thorne, Julian Vance, and Bret Halverson the highest powers under this Constitution. They used that power to trade tariff waivers and Naval Station Norfolk fleet contracts for four hundred million dollars in offshore kickbacks, to leak the movements of six thousand Virginia sailors on commercial phones, and to mock the disabled citizens they swore an oath to serve. Mason, Madison, and Lincoln left us an exact constitutional remedy for men who fail that test—and when the Clerk calls the roll, I will vote Guilty."

## Friday, April 16, 2027, 2:15 PM — Sixty-Eight Guilty Votes, Permanent Disqualification, & President Clara Morales

At two o'clock, the gallery doors were unlocked to the public and the press corps, and Chief Justice Jonathan Sterling rose in his black judicial robe at the marble rostrum.

"The Secretary will call the roll on Article One of the Articles of Impeachment exhibited by the House of Representatives against Garrett Thorne, President of the United States," Chief Justice Sterling announced.

This was the third time in American history that the House of Representatives had impeached Garrett Thorne—and during his first two impeachments back in his first term, he had escaped removal only because the Senate tally had fallen short of the two-thirds constitutional wall.

This third time, the wall broke.

One by one, as the Reading Clerk called the alphabetical roll, fourteen Republican Senators who had voted to acquit Thorne in his earlier impeachment trials rose from their mahogany desks and answered *"Guilty."*

"Mr. Mendoza," the Reading Clerk called out.

"Guilty," Senator Mendoza answered.

"Ms. Prairie."

I kept my hands resting calmly on the armrests of my SwiftChair, my electromagnetic drive brakes locked tight against the blue Senate carpet. Twenty-one years after Brandon Mercer had switched off my powerchair outside the Williamsburg train station in the pouring November rain and told me I was powerless—and thirteen days after rolling into Suite 756 at Timberline Shopping Center on Holland Road and seeing the shrines and unopened crates of *"3-0 UNDEFEATED"* and *"THORNE 2028"* merchandise my own estranged mother had chosen over her children—I looked straight down into the well of the United States Senate.

"Guilty," I said clearly.

Twenty minutes later, when the Reading Clerk handed the certified tally sheet up to the rostrum showing that sixty-eight Senators had voted Guilty on both Articles of Impeachment—exceeding the sixty-seven-vote two-thirds constitutional threshold required under Article I, Section 3—Chief Justice Sterling struck the marble rostrum once with his ivory gavel.

"The Senate having tried Garrett Thorne, President of the United States, upon two Articles of Impeachment exhibited against him by the House of Representatives, and two-thirds of the Senators present having found him guilty of high crimes and misdemeanors," Chief Justice Sterling declared across the hushed chamber, "it is therefore ordered and adjudged that the said Garrett Thorne be, and he is hereby, removed from office."

Before the defense table could even close their briefcases, our Senate Majority Leader rose and moved the second resolution under Article I, Section 3, Clause 7: permanent disqualification from ever holding any federal office again, which under settled Senate precedent required only a simple majority of fifty-one votes. When the clerk called *"Ms. Prairie"* on the permanent disqualification roll call, I delivered my second vote of the hour just as evenly as the first:

"Aye."

The disqualification resolution passed sixty-eight to thirty-two. With that gavel strike, Garrett Thorne, Julian Vance, and Bret Halverson were not only stripped of their offices—they were barred for the rest of their natural lives from ever putting their names on a federal ballot or holding any office of honor, trust, or profit under the United States again.

And because we had convicted and removed Vice President Julian Vance on Thursday afternoon and left the Vice Presidency vacant, the Presidential Succession Act of 1947—Title 3, Section 19 of the United States Code—took immediate constitutional effect.

Fifteen minutes after the Senate doors swung open, Nate and Dad took the elevator down from the Family Gallery and rolled down the marble corridor beside my wheelchair into the Mansfield Room just off the Senate Floor.

Standing beneath the crystal chandelier—having just signed her formal resignation as Speaker and Representative from the House—was Clara Morales, her left hand resting on a bound copy of the Constitution and her family Bible while she raised her right hand before Chief Justice Jonathan Sterling to take the constitutional oath of office as the new President of the United States.

Nate locked his SwiftChair brakes right alongside mine at the front of the room and reached across the two-inch gap between our armrests to squeeze my hand while Dad rested his hands proudly on our shoulders.

"Clean sweep, Senator," Nate whispered against my ear as the room erupted into applause for President Clara Morales. "All three of them gone for good."

"Every single one, Nate," I whispered back, squeezing my twin brother's hand just as tight.

Twenty minutes later, when Nate, Dad, and I rode the subway car back under Constitution Avenue and rolled into Dirksen Room 511, Sam, Mandy, and Sarah Hawkins were standing side by side around the reception television with tears in their eyes and smiles from ear to ear.

On the screen, live from his front porch in Danville with Aunt Marge Callaway standing beside him, former Republican House Majority Leader Richard Callaway was looking straight into a row of Roanoke and Richmond television cameras.

"Every conservative in the Commonwealth of Virginia who still believes in the United States Constitution owes Senator Katie Prairie and the sixty-seven Senators who stood with her today a debt of gratitude," Uncle Richard declared in his steady Southside baritone as the news chyron beneath him flashed *FORMER VA GOP MAJORITY LEADER RICHARD CALLAWAY ENDORSES SEN. PRAIRIE'S 'GUILTY' & 'AYE' IMPEACHMENT VOTES*. "When Senator Prairie voted Guilty to convict Garrett Thorne, Julian Vance, and Bret Halverson for selling out our Naval Station Norfolk sailors, and when she voted Aye to bar all three of those men from ever holding federal office again, she didn't cast a partisan vote. She cast the exact vote that George Mason and James Madison wrote into the Constitution to protect this Republic."

Before the television anchor could ask a follow-up question, the private direct line on Sarah's front desk chimed.

"It's Uncle Richard and Aunt Marge calling from the porch!" Sarah beamed, tapping the speakerphone button as Sam and Mandy leaned in beside our wheelchairs.

"Katie! Nate! David!" Uncle Richard's voice boomed warmly through the speakerphone once the television crew in his front yard had lowered their microphones. "Marge and I just watched President Clara Morales take the oath of office, and we wanted to tell you ourselves how proud we are of the two of you."

"Thank you, Richard—and thank you for going on the air in Danville to back both the Guilty vote and the Aye vote to bar them from office," I said warmly from my SwiftChair.

"Easiest endorsement of my life, Senator," Uncle Richard chuckled, before his voice took on the quiet, determined resolve of the old Majority Leader. "And now that President Morales has been sworn in and Garrett Thorne is barred from ever putting his name on a ballot again, the fever can finally break. Starting tomorrow morning, I'm rolling up my sleeves with every institutional conservative left in Virginia to clean up the Republican Party and bring it back to the honorable, problem-solving party I remembered when I served in the House of Delegates—so that one of these days, Marge and I can finally go back to voting for Republicans again."

Beside my wheelchair, Sam, Mandy, and Sarah exchanged a grin.

"Every Republican, Uncle Richard?" Mandy asked playfully.

Over the speakerphone from Danville, Uncle Richard let out a rich, warm laugh.

"Well, every Republican except one," the old lion of Southside Virginia corrected himself gently. "I already warned the state party committee this afternoon: I'm going back to voting Republican down the line—except when Katie Prairie runs for re-election to the United States Senate. As long as Katie's name is on that ballot, she has Marge's vote and mine."

Seconds after we thanked Uncle Richard and Aunt Marge and hung up the speakerphone, the breaking-news banner across the bottom of the reception television flashed bright red.

Down in Florida—stripped of the Presidency, stripped of his Secret Service successor authority, and barred for the rest of his life from ever putting his name on a ballot again—private citizen Garrett Thorne had just stormed out onto the driveway of his resort club in front of the cable cameras.

Even though sixty-eight United States Senators—including fourteen members of his own party—had just voted to convict and disqualify him, Garrett Thorne's narcissistic, ableist ego could not bear the humiliation that a forty-two-year-old autistic woman in a wheelchair and her twin brother had uncovered his four-hundred-million-dollar Naval Station Norfolk kickback scheme and delivered the closing constitutional case that ended his political life.

On live national television, his face purple with rage, Garrett Thorne launched a full-blown personal vendetta—blaming *"Broken Katie Prairie"* for his removal from office.

*"Broken Katie Prairie did this to me!"* Thorne screamed into the microphones, his voice cracking as he jabbed a finger toward the camera lenses. *"Sixty-eight weak, spineless cowards let Broken Katie Prairie in her little wheelchair and her shut-in brother's rigged computers in Virginia Beach steal my Presidency and ban me from running in 2028! Broken Katie Prairie is the reason I was removed from office—she destroyed our movement, and no real patriot in Restore the Republic should ever give Broken Katie Prairie a single moment of peace!"*

Uncle Richard hadn't even reached his front door in Danville before the impact of Thorne's tirade hit our fifth-floor suite.

Within sixty seconds, all eight external trunk lines on Sarah's front reception console began blinking frantic red at once as furious Restore the Republic extremists—incited by their deposed leader's live meltdown—started flooding our office switchboard.

Sarah's hand hovered over the receiver, her shoulders tensing, when Nate rolled his SwiftChair forward and gently rested his hand over the mute bar.

"Don't pick that up, Sarah," Nate said calmly.

Before the console could ring a third time, the double mahogany doors of Dirksen Room 511 swung open.

Stepping briskly into our reception room was the Senate Sergeant at Arms, flanked by Inspector Marcus Vance of the United States Capitol Police Threat Assessment Section and four plainclothes special agents wearing the gold lapel pins of the Capitol Police Dignitary Protection Division.

"Leave the phones on mute, Ms. Sarah—our telecom unit in the Capitol Police Command Center is cutting in on your external trunk lines right now," Inspector Vance said with calm, professional authority as two Senate telecommunications technicians stepped behind Sarah's desk and plugged a hardware trap-and-trace filter into the switchboard wall jack. "Every incoming call, voicemail, and IP packet threatening Senator Prairie, her family, or her staff is being routed directly to the Capitol Police Threat Assessment Section and the FBI Washington Field Office for felony prosecution under Title 18, Section 115 of the United States Code. None of the three of you will have to listen to a single one of those calls."

Sam, Mandy, and Sarah let out a collective breath as the blinking red lights on the console shifted to silent blue law-enforcement recording indicators.

Inspector Vance turned toward Dad, Nate, and me, his expression grave.

"Senator Prairie, we monitored former President Thorne's broadcast from Florida in real time," the Capitol Police Inspector said. "Because he singled you out by name and blamed you personally for his conviction and disqualification, our Threat Assessment Section has elevated your protective posture to Tier One. Uniformed Capitol Police officers are now posted outside Room 511 around the clock, and we've already coordinated twenty-four-hour residential patrols with the Alexandria Police Department on Sunset Drive and the Virginia Beach Police Department on Lord Nelson Drive. In addition, the Capitol Police Board has assigned a full-time plainclothes Dignitary Protection detail to accompany you and your brother everywhere you travel—and we have an armored black Capitol Police SUV waiting downstairs in the Dirksen courtyard to drive you, Nate, and Mr. Prairie home to Alexandria right now."

Inside Dirksen Room 511, the room went quiet.

Dad looked down at Nate and me, a knowing smile tugging at the corner of his mouth, while Sam, Mandy, and Sarah watched my wheelchair.

I kept my hands resting evenly on the armrests of my SwiftChair and looked up at Inspector Vance.

I wasn't the slightest bit afraid of Garrett Thorne—not after surviving Brandon Mercer switching off my wheelchair in the rain twenty-one years ago, and not after staring Thorne's lawyers down from the top tier of the Senate floor—but I took the protection of the United States Capitol Police very seriously, especially when it came to keeping Dad, Nate, and my staff safe.

"Inspector Vance, thank you—first and foremost for stepping in immediately to protect Sam, Mandy, and Sarah at the front desk, and for coordinating with Alexandria and Virginia Beach police," I said clearly. "We take your Threat Assessment team's protocols very seriously, and our office will follow every security procedure you set for Room 511 and our travel schedules. We will gladly accept your plainclothes Capitol Police Dignitary Protection detail, subject to two non-negotiable rules."

"Name them, Senator," Inspector Vance said.

"Rule one: nobody on the detail ever touches the push-handles of my wheelchair or my brother's wheelchair without asking our permission first," I said.

"Understood and already briefed to every agent on the team," Vance nodded. "And rule two?"

I glanced toward the framed purple-and-blue crayon drawing from Natalie Brooks hanging on our office wall, then over at Sam's desk—where Sam, Nate, and I had spent every spare hour between impeachment trial sessions drafting the federal transit appropriations rider to fund a public bus link across the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel—and finally at my twin brother Nate sitting in his SwiftChair beside me.

"Rule two, Inspector, is that you can send the armored SUV back to the garage," I said calmly. "Garrett Thorne is a disgraced private citizen who just got fired from the Presidency and permanently barred from ever holding public office again. I respect your officers' mission, and we will give your detail our exact train and bus times in advance so your agents can position themselves safely around us—but if I let Garrett Thorne's temper tantrum scare my brother and me off public transportation and lock us inside an armored truck, then Garrett Thorne wins. Plus, Sam, Nate, and I still have an hour of work left on our federal transit grant connecting Cape Charles on the Eastern Shore across the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel to the CVTA transfer point at Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road. At five-fifteen, Nate, Dad, and I—along with Sam, Mandy, and Sarah—are rolling down the elevator and out the accessible door to Capitol South Station to ride the Blue Line home to Alexandria, and tomorrow morning we are catching the ARTS train and the CVTA Route 24 bus home to Virginia Beach."

Nate grinned from his SwiftChair and held up his blue plastic transit card between two fingers.

"Your Dignitary Protection agents are more than welcome to ride with us, Inspector," my twin brother added cheerfully. "Just make sure every agent brings a loaded CapitalPass card—because on Senator Prairie's detail, everybody pays their fare and everybody rides the bus."

## Saturday, July 17, 2027, 10:15 AM — How Thorne's Vendetta Heat Died Down & Rolling Off the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel Bus at Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road

For the next ten weeks, we followed every protocol the United States Capitol Police put in place—and while the cable pundits wondered how long Garrett Thorne's post-conviction vendetta against *"Broken Katie Prairie"* would last, we kept our heads down and did the legislative work the people of Virginia had sent us to Washington to do.

When reporters outside the Capitol South elevator asked me to respond to Thorne's latest rant from his Florida driveway, I didn't give his ego a single drop of the oxygen he was starving for.

"I'm grateful to the United States Capitol Police officers riding the Blue Line with us today, and I have complete confidence in federal law enforcement," I told the cameras without looking up from my committee folder. "Mr. Thorne is an unemployed private citizen in Florida with a federal court date. Right now my brother Nate, my Legislative Correspondent Sam, and I are marking up the federal funding to connect ShoreLine Transit in Cape Charles across the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel to the CVTA transfer point at Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road—because working families on the Eastern Shore and in Hampton Roads still have to get to work and the hospital tomorrow morning."

Watched closely by Inspector Vance's Threat Assessment team, the heat of Garrett Thorne's vendetta collapsed faster than anyone in Washington had predicted—extinguished by five cold, unmistakable realities.

First, within seventy-two hours of Thorne's April sixteenth outburst, the United States Capitol Police Threat Assessment Section and the FBI used the hardware trap-and-trace logs from Dirksen Room 511 to arrest the first fourteen Restore the Republic extremists who had called our switchboard with threats. When those callers were led into federal courthouses in handcuffs on the six o'clock news facing ten-year felony sentences under Title 18, Section 115 of the United States Code—and suddenly realized that Garrett Thorne was no longer President of the United States, possessed zero constitutional pardon power to save them, and wasn't paying a single dime toward their defense lawyers—the nerve of the online mob shattered overnight. Within two weeks, the threatening calls to our office dropped to zero.

Second, under Article I, Section 3, Clause 7 of the United States Constitution, removal by the Senate stripped Garrett Thorne of every shred of presidential immunity and left him, as a private citizen, *"liable and subject to Indictment, Trial, Judgment and Punishment, according to Law."* On May 12, 2027, a federal grand jury returned a thirty-four-count felony indictment against Garrett Thorne, Julian Vance, and Bret Halverson for federal bribery, wire fraud, money laundering, and Espionage Act violations over the four-hundred-million-dollar Naval Station Norfolk kickback scheme, while the Department of Justice froze Thorne Global Holdings' offshore accounts. At Thorne's federal arraignment that afternoon, as a strict condition of pre-trial release under Title 18, Section 3142 so he wouldn't await trial behind bars, the United States District Judge imposed an ironclad gag and no-harassment order: if Garrett Thorne uttered or posted the name of Senator Katie Prairie, her family, her staff, or any congressional witness on television or online, his bail would be revoked immediately and United States Marshals would remand him straight into a federal detention cell. Terrified of spending a single night in a jail cell, Thorne shut his mouth on the spot.

Third, our sixty-eight-vote *Aye* tally permanently barring Thorne from ever holding federal office again drained every ounce of his political leverage. A movement built on personal fealty to one man cannot survive once that man is legally barred from ever appearing on a ballot again. Within a month, the cable cameras at LibertyMax and the Liberty First Network stopped cutting to his driveway in Florida, ambitious politicians started fighting over the wide-open 2028 nomination as if Thorne were already ancient history, and back home in Virginia, Uncle Richard and a coalition of institutional conservatives began methodically cleaning out the state Republican party apparatus.

By Monday, July 12, 2027—after sixty consecutive days without a single credible threat logged against our office or our homes—Inspector Marcus Vance walked into Dirksen Room 511 with a warm smile, unplugged the trap-and-trace unit from Sarah's reception console, and informed Dad, Nate, Sam, Mandy, Sarah, and me that the Capitol Police Threat Assessment Section had officially downgraded our posture back to routine baseline and stood down our round-the-clock protective detail.

Five days later—on Saturday morning, July 17, 2027—all six of us celebrated getting our normal, quiet everyday lives back in the only way that felt right.

Having seen President Clara Morales sign our bipartisan Federal Rural & Bridge-Tunnel Transit Connectivity appropriation into law the month before—establishing dedicated Federal Transit Administration Section 5311(f) intercity operating funds to cover the seventeen-point-six-mile cross-bay span and the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel District's nominal Class 8 public-transit toll of one dollar per crossing via the agency fleet transponder, together with a mandatory statutory stipulation requiring a reciprocal cross-honoring agreement for all fares and passes between CVTA and ShoreLine Transit—Nate, Dad, Sam, Mandy, Sarah, and I rode the inaugural southbound revenue run of the ShoreLine Transit / CVTA Bay Bridge-Tunnel Express south from Cape Charles.

Without a single armored SUV or protective detail trailing us, our low-floor bus glided through the electronic Class 8 toll lane at the North Toll Plaza for a single dollar, rolled out across the high trestles, and plunged through the two mile-long underwater tunnels of the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel—where the silver-green waves of the Chesapeake Bay met the open Atlantic Ocean—before climbing onto the south toll plaza at Wise Point and turning onto Shore Drive in Virginia Beach.

"Approaching Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road—Coastal Virginia Transit Authority Transfer Point," the automated interior chime announced at ten-fifteen in the morning as our driver pulled smoothly into the concrete transfer bay just west of the bridge-tunnel approach, right beside a waiting low-floor CVTA bus with its amber LED destination sign glowing in the July sunshine.

As the driver kneeled the air suspension and deployed the front hydraulic ramp onto the Virginia Beach sidewalk at Pleasure House Road, Sam, Mandy, and Sarah stepped down onto the concrete with Dad, turning back to smile as Nate and I rolled our two SwiftChairs side by side down the ramp into the warm coastal breeze.

Up at the CVTA bus shelter, commuters in navy shipyard shirts, Eastern Shore hospital nurses, and summer beachgoers with backpacks were already stepping off our coach and walking ten feet across the sidewalk—where, thanks to the cross-honoring stipulation in our bill, the CVTA farebox accepted their ShoreLine Transit passes and transfers without charging them a single extra dime to continue into Norfolk and Virginia Beach.

Dad rested one hand on my shoulder and the other on Nate's, looking back toward the shimmering span of the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel stretching out to the northern horizon.

"No cameras, no guards, and a bus running right across the Bay to Pleasure House Road," Dad said softly. "How does it feel to have your normal Saturday back, Senator?"

I looked over at Sam, Mandy, and Sarah chatting happily with the CVTA operator by the open front door, then up at Dad, and finally across the two inches between our armrests at my twin brother Nate.

"It feels like home, Dad," I smiled, unlocking my joystick as Nate and I rolled side by side toward the open ramp of the waiting CVTA bus with our cross-honored passes in hand. "Come on—let's tap our passes before we miss the transfer."
