## Saturday, July 17, 2027, 9:40 AM — How Thorne's Vendetta Heat Died Down & Rolling Off Route 913 at Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road

![At the inland coastal-suburban intersection of Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road in Virginia Beach on a sunny Saturday morning in July 2027—framed by tall Virginia loblolly pines, blooming pink crape myrtles, overhead mast-arm traffic signals, and the brick Bayside Pharmacy across the four-lane divided highway—42-year-old U.S. Senator Katie Prairie and her twin brother Nate sit side by side in their black motorized SwiftChair power wheelchairs holding their cross-honored transit passes on the concrete sidewalk beside low-floor CVTA bus #2514 with its yellow wheelchair ramp deployed and 913 CAPE CHARLES glowing on its amber LED headsign for its next cross-bay departure, surrounded by their father David, cousin Brittany Fletcher in her wire-rimmed glasses, 45-year-old cousin Chelsea smiling warmly in her floral cardigan beside Brittany's canvas tote bag with Shelly the plush sea turtle peeking out, and 25-year-old Hawkins triplets Sam, Mandy, and Sarah by the glass transit shelter.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b006/jul_2027_cbbt_bus_pleasure_house_road_transfer_family.jpg)

For ten weeks after former Speaker Clara Morales took the presidential oath in the Mansfield Room, we followed every protocol the United States Capitol Police put in place—and while the cable pundits wondered how long Garrett Thorne's post-conviction vendetta against *"Broken Katie Prairie"* would last, we kept our heads down and did the legislative work the people of Virginia had sent us to Washington to do.

When reporters outside the Capitol South elevator asked me to respond to Thorne's latest rant from his Florida driveway, I didn't give his ego a single drop of the oxygen he was starving for.

"I'm grateful to the United States Capitol Police officers riding the Blue Line with us today, and I have complete confidence in federal law enforcement," I told the cameras without looking up from my committee folder. "Mr. Thorne is an unemployed private citizen in Florida with a federal court date. Right now my brother Nate, my Legislative Correspondent Sam, and I are marking up the federal funding to connect ShoreLine Transit in Cape Charles across the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel to the CVTA transfer point at Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road—because working families on the Eastern Shore and in Hampton Roads still have to get to work and the hospital tomorrow morning."

Watched closely by Inspector Vance's Threat Assessment team, the heat of Garrett Thorne's vendetta collapsed faster than anyone in Washington had predicted—extinguished by five cold, unmistakable realities.

First, within seventy-two hours of Thorne's April sixteenth outburst, the United States Capitol Police Threat Assessment Section and the FBI used the hardware trap-and-trace logs from Dirksen Room 511 to arrest the first fourteen Restore the Republic extremists who had called our switchboard with threats. When those callers were led into federal courthouses in handcuffs on the six o'clock news facing ten-year felony sentences under Title 18, Section 115 of the United States Code—and suddenly realized that Garrett Thorne was no longer President of the United States, possessed zero constitutional pardon power to save them, and wasn't paying a single dime toward their defense lawyers—the nerve of the online mob shattered overnight. Within two weeks, the threatening calls to our office dropped to zero.

Second, under Article I, Section 3, Clause 7 of the United States Constitution, removal by the Senate stripped Garrett Thorne of every shred of presidential immunity and left him, as a private citizen, *"liable and subject to Indictment, Trial, Judgment and Punishment, according to Law."* On May 12, 2027, a federal grand jury returned a thirty-four-count felony indictment against Garrett Thorne, Conrad Drayton, and Bret Halverson for federal bribery, wire fraud, money laundering, and Espionage Act violations over the four-hundred-million-dollar Naval Station Norfolk kickback scheme, while the Department of Justice froze Thorne Global Holdings' offshore accounts. At Thorne's federal arraignment that afternoon, as a strict condition of pre-trial release under Title 18, Section 3142 so he wouldn't await trial behind bars, the United States District Judge imposed an ironclad gag and no-harassment order: if Garrett Thorne uttered or posted the name of Senator Katie Prairie, her family, her staff, or any congressional witness on television or online, his bail would be revoked immediately and United States Marshals would remand him straight into a federal detention cell. Terrified of spending a single night in a jail cell, Thorne shut his mouth on the spot.

Third, our sixty-eight-vote *Aye* tally permanently barring Thorne from ever holding federal office again drained every ounce of his political leverage. A movement built on personal fealty to one man cannot survive once that man is legally barred from ever appearing on a ballot again. Within a month, the cable cameras at LibertyMax and the Liberty First Network stopped cutting to his driveway in Florida, ambitious politicians started fighting over the wide-open 2028 nomination as if Thorne were already ancient history, and back home in Virginia, Uncle Richard and a coalition of institutional conservatives began methodically cleaning out the state Republican party apparatus.

By Monday, July 12, 2027—after sixty consecutive days without a single credible threat logged against our office or our homes—Inspector Marcus Vance walked into Dirksen Room 511 with a warm smile, unplugged the trap-and-trace unit from Sarah's reception console, and informed Dad, Nate, Brittany, Chelsea, Sam, Mandy, Sarah, and me that the Capitol Police Threat Assessment Section had officially downgraded our posture back to routine baseline and stood down our round-the-clock protective detail.

Five days later—on Saturday morning, July 17, 2027—all eight of us celebrated getting our normal, quiet everyday lives back in the only way that felt right.

Having seen President Clara Morales sign our bipartisan Federal Rural & Bridge-Tunnel Transit Connectivity appropriation into law the month before—establishing dedicated Federal Transit Administration Section 5311(f) intercity operating funds to cover the seventeen-point-six-mile cross-bay span and the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel District's nominal Class 8 public-transit toll of one dollar per crossing via each agency's fleet transponder, together with a mandatory statutory stipulation requiring complete reciprocal cross-honoring of all fares and passes between CVTA and ShoreLine Transit—the two transit agencies had pooled one vehicle apiece from opposite shores into a joint hourly express numbered Route 913 along U.S. Route 13: one forty-foot low-floor CVTA Stalwart Advantage coach pulling out northbound from Virginia Beach on the even hours and southbound from Cape Charles on the odd hours, paired with one highway-geared ShoreLine Transit dual-rear-wheel cutaway bus departing southbound on the even hours and northbound on the odd hours. Even smarter, rather than forcing forty-foot transit coaches and through-riders on a five-mile detour off the highway into historic downtown Cape Charles, the planners terminated Route 913 right on U.S. Route 13 and State Route 184 at the Cape Center Market Square plaza—timed to arrive at forty minutes past every hour so riders heading north to Eastville, Exmore, or Onley could step right across the plaza curb onto ShoreLine Transit's northbound Red Route cutaway van at forty-five minutes past the hour as it came out of downtown Cape Charles, while the Purple and Red cutaway loops handled the short shuttle into town.

At nine o'clock sharp that morning, having just transferred across the Market Square plaza curb on U.S. Route 13 without an armored van or protective detail anywhere in sight, Nate, Dad, Brittany, Chelsea, Sam, Mandy, Sarah, and I boarded the nine o'clock southbound Route 913 departure aboard CVTA Coach 2514, its full-width amber LED headsign showing a giant *913* beside the two-line split *VA BEACH / SHORE & PLEAS HSE* and *VA BEACH / via CBBT*. Gliding through the electronic Class 8 lane at the North Toll Plaza for a single dollar, our forty-foot coach rolled out across the sun-drenched concrete trestles with Chelsea beaming out the wide tinted window beside Brittany. At nine-twenty, halfway across the open water between Fisherman Island and the Thimble Shoal channel, our driver tapped a friendly two-note horn salute as ShoreLine Transit's northbound cutaway bus hummed past us in the opposite lane—its narrow single-line amber LED forehead sign above the cab windshield flipping crisply between *CAPE CHARLES* and *913 via CBBT* on its way to meet the nine forty-five northbound Onley connection—before we plunged through the two mile-long underwater tunnels where the silver-green waves of the Chesapeake Bay met the open Atlantic Ocean, climbed onto the south toll plaza at Wise Point, and turned west onto tree-lined Shore Drive into Virginia Beach.

"Approaching Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road—Coastal Virginia Transit Authority Transfer Point," the automated interior chime announced at nine-forty in the morning as our driver pulled smoothly up to the right-hand concrete curb just past the Pleasure House Road mast-arm traffic signals, across the four-lane divided suburban corridor from the brick Bayside Pharmacy beneath tall Virginia loblolly pines and blooming pink crape myrtles, and tapped his overhead keypad so Coach 2514's front amber LED destination sign flipped cleanly to *913 CAPE CHARLES* and *913 via CBBT* ahead of its ten o'clock northbound run.

As the driver kneeled the air suspension and deployed the front yellow hydraulic ramp onto the sunny Virginia Beach sidewalk at Pleasure House Road, Dad, Brittany—setting her canvas tote bag with Chelsea's green plush sea turtle Shelly beside the shelter—Chelsea, and twenty-five-year-old Sam, Mandy, and Sarah Hawkins stepped down onto the concrete, turning back to smile as Nate and I rolled our two SwiftChairs side by side down the ramp into the warm Bayside breeze.

Up at the glass CVTA bus shelter, commuters in navy shipyard shirts, Eastern Shore hospital nurses, and summer riders with backpacks were already stepping off our coach and walking across the sidewalk toward the connecting local stop—where, thanks to the cross-honoring stipulation in our bill, CVTA fareboxes accepted their ShoreLine Transit passes and transfers without charging them a single extra dime to continue across Virginia Beach and Norfolk, while early passengers bound for Onley and the Eastern Shore lined up with their CVTA passes ready for ten o'clock.

Dad rested a proud hand behind my chair beside Brittany and Chelsea, glancing east down tree-lined Shore Drive toward the Wise Point approach we had just rolled in from as everyday Saturday traffic hummed past the brick median.

"No cameras, no guards, and a public bus running all the way across the Bay to Shore Drive and Pleasure House Road," Dad said softly. "How does it feel to have your normal Saturday back, Senator?"

I looked over at Sam, Mandy, and Sarah smiling by the glass shelter, then up at Dad, Brittany, and a beaming Chelsea behind us, and finally across the two inches between our armrests at my twin brother Nate.

"It feels like home, Dad," I smiled, holding up my cross-honored transit pass right alongside Nate's in the warm July sunshine. "Come on, everybody—let's catch our connection."
