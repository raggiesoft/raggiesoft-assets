## Tuesday, June 20, 2028, 8:45 AM — Sunset Drive: Our 1991 Crew Cab Pickup, Dual Pull-Out Stereo Caddies, & Fifty-Seven Yeas

"Wait a second, Uncle David—before we walk down to King Street for the Yellow Line, show Heather and Wes which stereo caddy has *Our Own Frequency* loaded in it!" Brittany laughed behind her thin silver wire-rimmed glasses, leaning past the hand-cranked window handle of Dad's pickup in the sunlit driveway on Sunset Drive.

Behind his eyeglasses, our dad grinned like an engineer showing off his favorite lifelong project, holding up the heavy steel pull-out carrier caddy housing his aftermarket AM/FM stereo auto-reverse cassette receiver before sliding the matching second steel carrier caddy—housing his separately wired AM/FM stereo CD receiver—smoothly down the permanently mounted dashboard dock rails until its standardized fourteen-pin rear connector block seated with a solid mechanical *thunk-click*.

"Two different bare head units never shared the same factory plug back in the nineties, so I bought a universal pull-out dock kit and two matching steel slide-out carrier trays at Circuit Barn," Dad explained warmly to Heather and Wesley, patting the clean square-shouldered dashboard right above the horizontal metal HVAC sliders and the twin floor shifters for the cast-iron four-speed manual gearbox—with its compound creeper first gear—and manual four-wheel-drive transfer case. "I soldered and heat-shrunk the cassette receiver's harness permanently inside Caddy One and wired the CD receiver's harness permanently inside Caddy Two, so both trays mate to the exact same fourteen-pin dock block in the dash and I never had to give up thirty-five years of Stardust Engine cassette tapes. Bought this one-ton Highland Crew Cab brand-new in 1991 when Katie and Nate were six—the very last year Crestline built the classic square-fendered four-door body with opening triangular wing windows, back when Roy Slater was singing *Steady as Stone* on every television break. And when Katie and Nate turned sixteen in the fall of 2001 and moved into their SwiftChairs, we didn't get rid of their childhood truck—we changed the truck. Thirty-seven years of new clutches, fresh hoses, and cold air conditioning later, she hasn't missed a trip yet."

Standing right beside the gleaming chrome rear bumper and Virginia disabled license plate in his forest-green collared shirt, Wesley tapped the smooth flank of Dad's pickup twice in enthusiastic approval while Chelsea—holding Wesley's other hand tightly beside Heather and Brittany on the brick front walk—giggled happily at the warm eighties synth chord Dad tested through the cab speakers before clicking the ignition back off so all seven of us could walk one level block east under the King Street railroad bridge to catch the Yellow Line into Washington together.

Less than fourteen hours earlier, at seven-fifteen on Monday evening, Katie's landmark legislation—the Supported Companions, Waiver Portability, and Social Security Solvency Act—had cleared its final Senate conference vote fifty-seven to forty-three.

Thanks to Majority Leader Chris Van Deren's steady command of the fifty-five-seat Democratic caucus—plus Alaska's newly seated Democratic Senator bringing the majority aisle to fifty-six and two moderate Republican Senators crossing the aisle after Uncle Richard Callaway went live on *Beacon News* from his front porch in Danville to ask what kind of conservative defends billionaire payroll-tax loopholes while sending government clerks with clipboards to interrogate disabled adults over a four-dollar silicone ring—every single line of Katie's bill had survived intact:

Starting at midnight on New Year's Day, the fifty-six-year-old Supplemental Security Income and Disabled Adult Child marriage, cohabitation, in-kind meal, and Form SSA-4178 holding-out penalties were repealed from the United States Code forever. Disabled adults on state Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services waivers gained an ironclad federal right to carry their waivers across state lines with zero waiting-list resets and full dual-state reciprocity. Dual-guardian Supported Companion Declarations now carried automatic federal hospital and intensive-care bedside visitation rights, airline and ARTS rail companion passes, and Fair Housing zoning protection for family-connected companion suites while preserving every plenary guardianship and conservatorship shield so neither partner could ever be exploited by mortgage brokers, predatory lenders, or commercial contracts. And by repealing the Social Security payroll tax income cap on high earners, taxing billionaire fortunes above one billion dollars, and taxing corporate windfall profits alongside Pentagon contractor fraud clawbacks, Social Security stood fully solvent into the twenty-second century with twelve billion dollars already flowing toward accessible public school roofs and elevators back home in Virginia.

## Tuesday, June 20, 2028, 10:30 AM — First Time Inside the White House Gates & Blooming Roses Where Concrete Used to Sit

As the wrought-iron Northwest Gate on Pennsylvania Avenue swung open after the uniformed Secret Service officers checked our seven Virginia state IDs against the White House visitor manifest, Chelsea stopped two paces inside the curved driveway, hugged plush green Shelly Turtle to her lavender cardigan with one arm, and let out an awestruck gasp while Wesley's eyes went wide beside her.

"Nate! Katie! Unca David!" Chelsea whispered excitedly through her thick speech impediment, squeezing Wesley's hand with all her might as Heather and Brittany gazed up at the gleaming white columns of the North Portico in quiet wonder. "We inside the real White House!"

Rolling side by side with Katie down the smooth accessible walkway beside Dad and Heather, I felt a warm wave of memory wash straight back twenty-one springs. Back in April of 2007, on our twenty-second birthday right before our summer in Fishersville, Dad, Katie, and I had come through the East Wing visitor entrance on the ordinary public White House tour—and over the past fourteen months since Clara Morales had taken the presidential oath, Katie had visited the West Wing in her constitutional role as United States Senator while I accompanied her under two clean, ethics-compliant hats: my Office of Congressional Workplace Rights ADA disability-support companion credential and, today, an official Presidential Guest invitation issued directly by the White House Office of Public Engagement recognizing my independent open-data research and my family role as Chelsea's cousin and standby co-guardian.

For Heather, Wesley, Brittany, and Chelsea, this sunlit June Tuesday was the very first time in their lives any of the four of them had ever set foot inside the White House gates. Watching Heather squeeze my left hand while Wesley and Chelsea stood shoulder to shoulder on the White House colonnade carpet—twenty-five years after Graduation Day at the Virginia Beach Pavilion when we had cautiously welcomed Heather back for Wesley's sake and kept our family together in Virginia—felt like watching twenty-five years of unbroken devotion shine in the open sunlight.

Then the tall French doors beside the West Wing colonnade opened, and an usher gestured all of us out into the restored White House Rose Garden.

Katie and I both paused at the top of the flush bluestone ramp just to take in the view.

Three years earlier, before Congress had impeached and removed Garrett Thorne from office, Thorne had ordered bulldozers into this historic garden, ripping out the classic 1962 green grass lawn and tearing back the heirloom flowerbeds to pave the center of the Rose Garden over in glaring, sterile stone slabs like a private country-club patio. One of the quietest, happiest gifts Clara Morales had given the American people during her first spring in office—working with the National Park Service and horticulturalists without spending a dime on gold-plated vanity—was jackhammering every square inch of Thorne's concrete patio slab out of the ground and restoring the living Rose Garden to its full natural beauty, reinforced underneath the roots with invisible permeable turf-grid engineering so power and manual wheelchairs could roll straight out onto the real green grass without sinking a millimeter.

Everywhere we looked under the warm June sky, lush emerald-green grass stretched from boxwood border to boxwood border, framed by hundreds of fragrant, blooming pink, crimson, ivory, and golden-yellow heirloom roses beneath white-and-pink flowering crabapple trees.

"Good morning, everyone—and welcome to the People's House!" President Clara Morales called out warmly in her tailored burgundy blazer, stepping across the living green turf ahead of Majority Leader Chris Van Deren, Senator Tess Dunmore, and Senator Josh Novak before shaking hands directly with Wesley and Chelsea first and clasping hands warmly with Heather, Dad, Brittany, Katie, and me.

Positioning our four wheelchairs—Katie's and my compact motorized SwiftChairs flush on the left of the lowered oak signing desk with its carved wooden American eagle crest, and Tess Dunmore's and Josh Novak's ultralight manual chairs flush on the right—while Wesley and Chelsea stood beaming directly behind President Morales's shoulders flanked by Heather, Dad, Majority Leader Van Deren, and Brittany, we watched Clara Morales uncap the first black-and-silver fountain pen.

"Fifty-six years is fifty-six years too long to punish disabled Americans simply for loving one another, visiting family across state lines, or sharing a safe home under their guardians' care," President Morales said into the gentle breeze rustling the crabapple blossoms, before signing her name cleanly across the bound parchment enrollment of the Supported Companions, Waiver Portability, and Social Security Solvency Act.

Turning smilingly in her chair on the green grass, the President of the United States didn't hand the first two ceremonial pens to the Senators around the desk. She handed Pen Number One straight up into Wesley's gentle palm beside his smooth blue silicone ring, and Pen Number Two straight into Chelsea's hand right beside Shelly Turtle.

"Fank you, Madam Pwesident!" Chelsea beamed from ear to ear while Wesley gave a radiant, unguarded smile and tapped his chest twice in gratitude, before Dad and Heather stepped forward at the corner of the oak table—once the official statutory signing concluded—to add their guardian signatures beneath Wesley's printed mark and Chelsea's careful block letters on the very first voluntary Federal Supported Companion Declaration in the United States.

Standing right there on the living green grass among the blooming roses—watching Wesley wrap a calm, deep-pressure arm around his Turtle Girl's shoulders while Chelsea rested her head happily against his chest—Dad looked across at Heather, Brittany, Katie, and me and grinned. Before we even walked back out the Northwest Gate onto Pennsylvania Avenue, all of us had shaken hands on the exact gift we were filing with the city permit offices the moment the law took effect on New Year's Day: two identically mirrored zero-step ground-floor companion suites—one attached to our home in Virginia Beach and a second attached to Sunset Drive in Alexandria—so Wesley and Chelsea had their own connected daytime sanctuary and turtle room right off our kitchen while still curling up beside us every single night on our solid-oak Alaskan King.
