## Saturday, March 10, 2029, 6:30 PM — Mirrored Inch for Inch in Virginia Beach and Alexandria & Keeping Our Alaskan King Every Night

![Inside their warm, zero-step ground-floor companion suite living room with an open solid-oak connecting pocket door to the main family kitchen on the left, a 75-gallon basking turtle habitat on the right-hand oak cabinet, and a cozy afternoon reading daybed visible behind them, forty-two-year-old Wesley Sorenson in his forest-green Northwood Lion tee and plaid flannel lounge pants with his blue silicone ring wraps a gentle deep-pressure arm around forty-seven-year-old Chelsea in modest lavender fleece loungewear holding plush Shelly Turtle on their oatmeal loveseat.]({{CDN}}/raggiesoft-books/images/scenes/katie/b006/mar_2029_three_mirrored_inlaw_suites_wesley_chelsea_sanctuary.jpg)

"Okay, Turtle Girl—slide the oak door open and tell Uncle David and Heather if we got every single inch in the right place!" Brittany beamed behind her silver wire-rimmed glasses, stepping back beside Katie's and my parked SwiftChairs in our warm Kempsville kitchen while Heather held my hand with a proud smile.

Standing right at the threshold in her soft lavender fleece loungewear with plush green Shelly Turtle tucked beneath her left arm and Wesley holding her right hand in his favorite forest-green Northwood Lion tee and navy plaid flannel lounge pants, Chelsea slid the solid-oak interior pocket door smoothly into the wall cavity on its whisper-quiet brass rollers.

Chelsea gasped softly, her whole face lighting up like a sunrise over the Chesapeake Bay, while beside her Wesley let out a deep, contented two-note hum and tapped his smooth blue silicone ring twice against the oak jamb in pure recognition.

"Wes! Look!" Chelsea cheered happily, stepping zero-threshold across the warm hardwood floor without a single bump or saddle strip anywhere beneath their socks. "Our couch! And Sunny and Pebble's big tank! And a quiet nap room!"

Behind our wheelchairs in the main kitchen doorway, Dad clinked his coffee mug gently against Brittany's ginger ale glass while Heather reached down to squeeze Katie's shoulder and mine with bright, grateful eyes.

Nine months earlier, on the living green grass of the restored White House Rose Garden, watching President Clara Morales hand Pen Number One to Wesley and Pen Number Two to Chelsea as Dad and Heather co-signed Supported Companion Declaration Number One had inspired the exact home expansion we were celebrating tonight.

Once Katie's Supported Companions, Waiver Portability, and Social Security Solvency Act took legal effect at midnight on New Year's Day—permanently repealing the Supplemental Security Income and Disabled Adult Child marriage, in-kind shelter, and Form SSA-4178 holding-out penalties and protecting family-connected accessible companion suites under federal Fair Housing law—we wanted Wesley and Chelsea to have a dedicated daytime couple's retreat of their very own whenever our main living room was busy with Prairie Twin Interactive code deploys or constituent briefings, without ever breaking their weekly rhythm between Tidewater and Northern Virginia.

To honor both Wesley's and Chelsea's autistic and developmental need for unbroken visual and spatial routine as our six-person family rode ARTS Business Class between Kempsville (Thursday through Sunday) and Alexandria (Monday through Wednesday during Senate session weeks), our barrier-free architect built two identically mirrored five-hundred-and-twenty-square-foot ground-floor companion suites—one attached here to our shaded garden wing in Virginia Beach, and a second attached to the ground-floor garden wing of Sunset Drive in Alexandria one block from King Street–Old Town.

In Virginia Beach and in Alexandria alike, the unlocked solid-oak pocket door opened on the exact same left-hand wall ten steps from the main family kitchen. Directly ahead beneath two warm framed woodland prints stood an identical plush oatmeal-linen loveseat. Against the right-hand wall sat a matching solid-oak cabinet supporting a seventy-five-gallon filtered basking habitat—housing Sunny and Pebble here in Virginia Beach and a calm second pair of rescued painted turtles up in Alexandria so no animal ever had to be stressed in transit. In the corner sat a cool-touch, roll-under kitchenette with an auto-shutoff electric tea kettle and zero open burners or scorching coils, and through the wide step-free archway sat a quiet afternoon reading-and-nap alcove with a wooden stand for Chelsea's spare inhalers and a carved cedar dish for Wesley's blue silicone ring.

And best of all, nobody had to give up their nighttime anchor for a single night. Every evening at nine-thirty—whether we were home in Virginia Beach or up on Sunset Drive in Alexandria—once Wesley and Chelsea switched their turtle tank over to its soft amber night lamp, both of them strolled right back through that open oak pocket door into our master bedroom to climb onto our nine-foot solid-oak six-person Alaskan King platform bed, where Wesley slept peacefully and securely right next to Heather, Heather and Katie flanked me in the center, and Chelsea and Brittany cuddled happily beside Katie until morning.

Even better, because Title Three of Katie's law preserved every single plenary guardianship and conservatorship protection while recognizing their Dual-Guardian Supported Companion Declaration, our family kept the real-world guardrails crystal clear and loving. Neither Wesley nor Chelsea ever had to comprehend a forty-page mortgage, property-tax escrow, utility contract, or predatory credit mailer—because the homes stayed one hundred percent owned, insured, and maintained by our household while Heather managed Wesley's graphic-design earnings and ABLE account, Dad and Brittany managed Chelsea's protected benefits and ABLE account with zero federal benefit cuts, and loving family hands stayed ten seconds away on the other side of that unlocked oak pocket door twenty-four hours a day.

Walking hand in hand into the warm lamplight of their new living room, Wesley guided Chelsea gently down onto the oatmeal loveseat, settled right beside her hip, and wrapped his steady left arm warmly around his Turtle Girl's shoulders while his right hand closed gently over hers atop Shelly Turtle.

Chelsea let out a long, blissful sigh, resting her curly chestnut head right against Wesley's chest as both of their shoulders dropped in unison.

Quietly, smiling across our wheelchair armrests at Heather, Dad, and Brittany in the kitchen glow, Katie and I backed our SwiftChairs two feet clear of the threshold, leaving the oak pocket door wide open so the smell of warm cinnamon applesauce drifted gently in while we gave Wesley and Chelsea a peaceful hour on their loveseat before all six of us headed down the hall to our Alaskan King for the night.
