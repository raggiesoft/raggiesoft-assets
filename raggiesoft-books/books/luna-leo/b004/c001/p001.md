The Northwood High cafeteria during 5th period lunch was a symphony of controlled chaos, a noisy, echoing cavern filled with the clatter of trays and the roar of hundreds of teenage conversations. Fourteen-year-old Luna Porter, leaning slightly on the armrest of her twin brother Leo’s Quantum XT wheelchair for support as they scanned for a relatively calm table, felt a familiar wave of freshman overwhelm. Leo, beside her, his own Quantum Communicator XT resting on his tray, observed the scene with his usual quiet intensity.

They'd heard the upperclassmen's "Unofficial Orientation" earlier that morning. Mark and that girl with the pink hair had spun wild tales about the cafeteria supervisor, Mrs. DeMarco: her encyclopedic memory for pranks, her fanatical defense of the mystery meat, and her alleged fluency in every language known to humankind. Luna had exchanged a skeptical look with Leo. Every language? Really? Leo had typed back a succinct: HIGHLY IMPROBABLE. They’d figured most of it was just freshman hazing, designed to terrify.

They found a table, and as they settled in with their lukewarm pizza, the relative peace was shattered.

"PENIS!"

The shout, loud and arrogant, came from a few tables over. Luna nearly choked on her milk.

"Seriously?" she muttered to Leo, her eyes wide. "First day?"

Leo was already typing, his expression a mixture of disbelief and grim anticipation: JOSH RILEY. OF COURSE. THIS WILL BE... EDUCATIONAL.

They watched as a formidable-looking woman – that must be Mrs. DeMarco, Luna realized with a jolt – began to walk with slow, deliberate steps towards Josh Riley’s table. The cafeteria hummed, then quieted. Josh, still smirking, was clearly oblivious to the doom approaching.

Then, Mrs. DeMarco's voice cut through the air, cool and precise, carrying effortlessly across the large room. "Mr. Riley, I presume?"

Luna's jaw dropped. She stared at Leo, who looked equally stunned. The upperclassmen hadn't been exaggerating about her presence.

They watched as Josh's grin faltered. He tried to maintain his cool, offering a weak, "Yeah? What's up?"

"What's 'up,' Mr. Riley," Mrs. DeMarco stated, her voice a low, dangerous thrum, "is your apparent inability to comprehend simple social decorum. This particular display of puerile exhibitionism is, if my records are correct, the 253rd time I have had the distinct displeasure of encountering it in my tenure."

Luna whispered to Leo, her eyes wide, "Did she just say she keeps a tally?"

Leo typed, a new level of profound awe in his synthesized voice: THE LEGENDS ARE TRUE. SHE IS A TACTICAL GENIUS.

Mrs. DeMarco wasn't finished. "Therefore, Mr. Riley," she announced, "allow me to be the first to officially welcome you to Northwood High's most exclusive after-school enrichment program. You, and your two equally misguided associates, will report to me, here in the cafeteria, promptly at 2:15 PM this afternoon, and every afternoon for the remainder of this week. You have all earned yourselves an intensive, four-day seminar in advanced sanitation techniques, commonly known as trash duty."

The sentencing of Josh and his friends to a week of trash duty felt swift and absolute. Mrs. DeMarco leaned in slightly, delivering the final blow. "And believe me, Mr. Riley, after a week with our dumpsters, the only four-letter words you'll be contemplating will be 'soap' and 'work'. Am I understood?"

Josh, pale and sweating, managed a strangled, "Y-yes, Mrs. DeMarco."

"Excellent," she said. "And one more thing. Any more outbursts, in any language – and yes, Mr. Riley, I do mean *any* language – and we will be discussing an extension of your apprenticeship. Perhaps into the fascinating world of grease trap maintenance."

With a final, withering glare, Mrs. DeMarco turned and resumed her patrol. She addressed the entire cafeteria, her voice deceptively calm, asking if anyone else wanted after-school trash duty.

The silence was absolute. Not a single student dared to breathe too loudly.

Luna let out a breath she hadn’t realized she was holding. "Okay," she whispered to Leo, who was using his Quantum Communicator XT with a grin. "Mrs. DeMarco is officially my new hero. Did you see Josh's face? He looked like he was going to wet his pants!"

Leo typed, his own amusement evident: SHE IS VERY… EFFECTIVE. GOOD TO SEE BULLIES GET EXACTLY WHAT THEY DESERVE. THE TRASH DUTY PART IS BEAUTIFUL JUSTICE.

Luna snickered. "Totally. I'm never, ever getting on her bad side."

She glanced across the cafeteria to another table where a boy in a wheelchair and his friends were sitting. They looked just as relieved as everyone else. The first day of freshman year, and they had already witnessed a legend in action. Northwood High was going to be an adventure, and clearly, the cafeteria was its most unpredictable and educational stage.
