Back in the apartment later that day, the energy from the classic rock discoveries in the car still lingered. Luna and Leo seemed magnetically drawn to the turntable and Stella's box of records. Seeing their continued interest, Leo used his device to type, *"More music?"* while Luna looked hopefully at Stella.  

Stella smiled. "Still hooked, huh?" She went back to her box of LPs, thoughtfully flipping through them. She paused, pulling out an album with a simple, somewhat abstract cover featuring figures holding lights. "Okay," she said, holding it up. "This one is special to me. Origin, '...And Only Three Remained...' This was the very first record album I ever bought with my own allowance money. Saved up for weeks."

She slid the vinyl out. "I showed you how to start at the beginning of a side," she said, placing the record on the turntable. "But sometimes you want to play a specific song that's not the first one. See these smoother, wider bands between the groups of grooves? Those usually separate the songs."

She flipped the record over to Side B and pointed towards the outer edge of the record, then carefully moved her finger inwards, counting the bands visually. "The song I want to play is the last one on this side. So, I need to find the space *before* that last track." She carefully lifted the tonearm with the lever, positioned the needle precisely over the silent band just before the final set of visible grooves, and gently lowered it.

After a brief moment of silence, the gentle, instantly recognizable keyboard intro and mellow beat of "Wherever You Go" began to play. Stella leaned back, a nostalgic expression on her face, lost in the memory associated with her first album purchase. Luna and Leo listened intently, watching the needle track the grooves, absorbing both the music and the new technique Stella had just shown them. It was another piece of the vinyl puzzle clicking into place, connecting them further to the music and to Stella's own history.
