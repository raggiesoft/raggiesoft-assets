After twenty minutes of quiet recovery in the Zenith Tower lobby, the twins' tear-streaked faces had dried, and the acute panic had receded into a dull, manageable exhaustion. 

"Okay," Stella said, pushing herself off the marble wall. "We need a reset. We need something heavy, warm, and distinctly ground-level."

A quick conversation with a sympathetic security guard pointed them toward Russo's, a hole-in-the-wall pizzeria a few blocks away. It wasn't one of the flashy, tourist-trap chains with neon signs; it was a dimly lit, brick-walled establishment tucked down a side street, smelling powerfully of garlic, oregano, and baking dough. 

They snagged a corner booth, parking Leo's wheelchair at the end of the table. The ambiance was cozy and wonderfully cavernous—the exact opposite of a glass box suspended in the sky. 

When the waitress brought their order out thirty minutes later, it didn't look like a pizza. It looked like a cast-iron pie pan filled to the brim with a literal mountain of bubbling cheese, chunky tomato sauce, and thick, savory sausage. 

"Oh my god," Luna breathed, her eyes widening as the waitress used a heavy steel spatula to lift the first slice. Thick ropes of mozzarella stretched and pulled, refusing to let go of the pan. 

Stella stared at the monstrous slice as it was deposited onto her plate. The crust was nearly two inches deep. "I am going to need a nap after just looking at this," she muttered, picking up her fork. 

Olivia cut Leo's slice into manageable, bite-sized pieces for him. He used his fork to spear a chunk of the thick, buttery crust covered in cheese and sauce. The moment it hit his tongue, his eyes closed in sheer bliss. It was incredible. It was heavy, rich, and incredibly comforting—exactly the kind of grounding sensory input his fried nervous system craved. 

"THIS IS SO GOOD," Leo typed, reaching eagerly for his second bite. 

"It really is," Luna agreed, her mouth full. The terrifying ordeal at the Zenith Tower seemed a million miles away, replaced by the warm, buzzing atmosphere of the restaurant and the unbelievable weight of the food. 

Stella managed exactly one slice before she had to lay her fork down in defeat, pressing a hand to her stomach. "It's amazing," she admitted, looking at the massive, half-eaten pizza still sitting in the center of the table. "But I think my stomach just waved a white flag. That is an obscene amount of cheese."

Olivia, possessing the legendary metabolism of a tall, athletic nineteen-year-old, happily reached for her third slice. 

Leo watched his best friend eat, a quiet, contented warmth settling in his chest. The heavy food, the dimly lit brick walls, and the lively chatter of the pack were successfully drowning out the ticking clock, at least for the moment. He took another bite of his own pizza, letting the rich flavors ground him firmly to the earth.
