The *Western Pioneer* didn't just glide out of Denver; it fought for every inch of altitude. 

For the first hour, the massive double-decker train aggressively wound its way up the sheer face of the Front Range. The tracks bent into impossible, sweeping horseshoes—the infamous "Big Ten Curve"—where Leo could look out his window and see the very back of their own train trailing far below them. The urban sprawl of Denver shrank into a hazy grid on the plains, replaced by towering walls of granite and dense, dark pine forests.

But as the train climbed higher into the sky, the air inside the sleeper car began to change. 

Denver had been a mile high. By 10:00 AM, the train was approaching nine thousand feet above sea level. 

Leo was sitting on the edge of the lower bunk, staring out the window, when the first wave of lethargy hit him. It felt like an invisible weight pressing down on his chest. His breathing grew shallow and rapid, his lungs instinctively trying to pull oxygen out of air that was growing aggressively thin. 

Across the tiny compartment, Luna was slumped against the wall, her eyes half-closed and her chest rising and falling in quick, uneven hitches. 

"Okay, oxygen check," Stella announced, her voice cutting through the heavy hum of the diesel engines. She was sitting in the corner jump seat, completely unaffected by the altitude. 

Olivia immediately dropped her book and moved into action. She leaned over Luna first, checking her pulse and gently tilting her sister's head back. "Deep breaths, Lu. In through your nose, out through your mouth. You're just panting. You have to pull the air all the way down."

Luna reached clumsily for her own NovaVox resting beside her. She tapped a single, pre-programmed button, lacking the breath and focus to type out a full sentence. 

*I'm trying. It feels empty,* her device announced, the calm, synthesized female voice contrasting sharply with Luna's erratic, wheezing breaths. 

"It's just the elevation," Olivia promised, rubbing Luna's shoulders with a steady, grounding pressure. "We're almost at the peak. Once we cross the divide, we start going back down."

Stella shifted her attention to Leo. She didn't ask how he was feeling; she could see the pale, grayish tint creeping into his cheeks. She reached into her medical bag and pulled out a small, portable pulse oximeter, clipping it smoothly onto his index finger. 

The little digital screen blinked to life. *88%.*

"Yeah, you're not loving this mountain, are you?" Stella murmured, unzipping a secondary compartment in her bag. "Just relax, Leo. Don't fight it. Let your body figure it out." 

She pulled out a small, pressurized oxygen canister—a standard precaution she had bought at a pharmacy in Denver specifically for this leg of the trip. She fitted the plastic mask over Leo's mouth and nose, pressing the trigger. 

A rush of cool, concentrated oxygen flooded his lungs. The relief was instantaneous. The crushing weight on his chest lifted, and the dark edges of his vision cleared. He took a long, deep breath, the plastic mask fogging up with his exhalation. 

"Better?" Stella asked. 

Leo nodded weakly, keeping the mask pressed to his face. He reached out and pulled his NovaVox—his dedicated AAC tablet—onto his knees on the edge of the bunk. His fingers moved quickly over the thick, rugged touchscreen. 

*Where are we going?* the synthesized voice from the NovaVox asked, cutting through the low rumble of the train. *After the mountains.*

"We're on this train until tomorrow afternoon," Stella said, checking his oxygen levels again as they ticked back up into the high nineties. "Non-stop to Emeryville. Then we catch a bus across the bay into San Francisco."

Leo's fingers tapped the screen again. *Emeryville. Is that near Jack London Square?*

Olivia looked up from Luna, surprised. "Yeah, it is. How do you know about Jack London Square?"

*We read The Call of the Wild in middle school,* the electronic voice replied. Leo looked back out the window at the rugged, unforgiving wilderness passing by. *Mr. Harrison told us about him. The author. He said Jack London loved the cold. He loved the wild. I always wondered what his city looked like.*

Before Olivia could answer, the bright, mid-morning sun vanished. 

The train plunged into absolute, pitch-black darkness. The sudden absence of light was so absolute it felt physical. The rhythmic clatter of the wheels violently echoed off close, invisible stone walls. 

"Moffat Tunnel," Stella announced in the dark, her voice echoing in the cramped compartment. "Six point two miles straight through the Continental Divide. We're at the top of the world, kids." 

Leo sat in the dark, the oxygen mask hissing softly against his face. He felt the immense, millions of tons of solid mountain pressing down on the roof of the train. He thought about wolves, and wilderness, and escaping into the cold. 

For the first time in his life, he was going to see the ocean on the other side.
