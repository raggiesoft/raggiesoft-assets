By early afternoon, the vast, pale scrub brush of the Nevada desert finally began to give way to rising terrain. 

The *Western Pioneer* had left Reno behind and was now beginning its final, grueling climb of the journey: scaling the Sierra Nevada mountain range to cross into California.

Leo was sitting in his manual wheelchair in the Family Bedroom, having finally transferred out of the bunks for the afternoon. He had his NovaVox mounted securely on its metal swing-arm, typing idly as he watched the scenery change. The desert faded into thick, towering forests of incredibly tall pine trees. The train twisted and turned, carving its way up the side of the mountain range. 

"We're crossing the border right now," Stella announced, looking at the route map she had picked up in the Sightseer Lounge earlier. "Welcome to California, everyone."

"Finally," Olivia sighed, stretching her legs out across the lower bunk. "My back is starting to permanently mold to the shape of these Amtrak beds."

The train plunged into a long, dark snow shed—a massive, wooden tunnel built over the tracks to protect them from winter avalanches. When they burst back out into the sunlight, they were at the summit of Donner Pass. 

Leo stared out the window. Unlike the Rocky Mountains, which were jagged and terrifyingly steep, the Sierras felt immensely heavy and ancient. He could see Donner Lake sparkling like a sapphire far below them in the valley. 

They were over 7,000 feet high again, but this time, the altitude didn't bother him. His body had already run the grueling gauntlet of the Rockies; this climb felt completely manageable in comparison. 

*How much longer?* Leo typed, his NovaVox echoing in the cabin. 

"We've hit the peak," Stella said, tracing her finger down the map. "From here, we start the long descent into the Sacramento Valley. We'll hit Emeryville by late afternoon. Then we just have to hop on the Thruway bus across the Bay Bridge into San Francisco."

Leo looked over at Luna. She was sitting on the floor, leaning against his wheelchair, completely engrossed in watching the towering pine trees blur past the glass. She caught him looking and reached up, giving his hand a quick, excited squeeze. 

They had crossed the entire North American continent. They had survived the Midwest, the Rockies, the deserts, and the Sierras. 

In just a few short hours, they were going to see the ocean.
