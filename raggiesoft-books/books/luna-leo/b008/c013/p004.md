The *Western Pioneer* finally hissed to a halt at the concrete platform of the Emeryville station late in the afternoon. 

The air outside the train felt fundamentally different. The dry, thin chill of the mountains and the desert was gone, replaced by a cool, heavy, salt-tinged breeze rolling in off the San Francisco Bay. 

"End of the line for the train, folks," the conductor announced as he lowered the metal bridge to the platform. 

The pack spilled out onto the concrete. After two and a half days confined to the narrow, swaying carriages of the train, the stationary ground beneath their feet felt almost strange. Olivia and Stella were both carrying two massive, heavy duffel bags, burdened with all the supplies they needed for a month-long stay on the West Coast. 

Leo pushed the handrims of his manual wheelchair, rolling easily down the long platform towards the station building. He looked around the bustling transit hub. He knew from his obsessive research before the trip that Emeryville was a major connection point for the East Bay. 

He stopped and tapped quickly on his NovaVox. 

*Why aren't we taking BATS?* the device asked, referencing the heavy-rail Bay Area Transit System that ran directly under the bay into the city. 

"Two reasons," Stella answered, adjusting the strap of a heavy duffel bag on her shoulder. "One: we have way too much luggage to be dragging it up and down the escalators in the BATS stations. And two: our train tickets actually go all the way into the city. ARTS operates a dedicated Thruway bus that connects right here."

She pointed toward the front of the station, where a massive, silver, wheelchair-accessible motorcoach was already idling at the curb. 

"The bus goes straight across the Bay Bridge," Stella explained as they walked toward it. "And it drops us off directly at the San Francisco Ferry Building. Our hotel is literally right across the Embarcadero. It's the flattest part of the entire city."

That sounded perfect to Leo. He expertly maneuvered his wheelchair onto the motorized lift of the coach bus, and a few minutes later, they were merging onto the highway. 

However, they immediately slammed into a wall of cars. It was five o'clock in the evening, and the legendary Bay Area rush hour was in full swing. 

"Well, we're not going anywhere fast," Olivia said, leaning her head against the bus window as they crept inch-by-inch through the massive concrete maze of the toll plaza. 

But Leo didn't mind the gridlock at all. Because they were heading westbound into San Francisco, their bus was routed onto the **upper deck** of the Bay Bridge. Sitting high up in the motorcoach on the top level of the bridge gave them a completely unobstructed, sweeping view of the choppy, dark blue water of the bay. In the distance, the iconic, fog-draped skyline of San Francisco rose up like a city of glass and steel, framed by the rolling green hills of the peninsula. 

It took them almost an hour to crawl across the bridge, but when the bus finally pulled up to the curb outside the historic Ferry Building, the sun was just beginning to dip low over the city. 

Leo rolled down the ramp and onto the wide, paved sidewalk of the Embarcadero. Stella was right. After navigating the uneven bricks, steep curbs, and narrow hallways of the East Coast for his entire life, the Embarcadero was a revelation. It was incredibly wide, perfectly paved, and completely flat. 

He didn't have to fight his wheelchair. He didn't have to scan the ground for obstacles. He could just roll. 

"Hotel is right there," Olivia pointed, hoisting her duffel bags again as she gestured to an elegant, towering building just across the wide boulevard with the glowing words *The Sovereign Regency San Francisco* affixed to the stone facade. 

Leo took a deep breath of the heavy, salty sea air. They were thousands of miles away from the intense humidity and quiet isolation of their small house in Virginia. They were on the edge of the Pacific Ocean. And for the next month, this was going to be their basecamp.
