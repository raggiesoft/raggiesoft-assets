The next morning, the pack stepped out of the sprawling atrium of The Sovereign Regency and onto the bustling, sun-drenched sidewalk of the Embarcadero. 

It was their first official day on the West Coast, and the city of San Francisco was roaring to life around them. Just a few blocks away, near the edge of the Financial District, Leo caught sight of an iconic, wooden San Francisco Cable Car slowly ratcheting its way up an impossibly steep hill, its bell clanging loudly over the traffic. 

He immediately stopped his wheelchair, reaching eagerly for his NovaVox. 

*I want to ride that,* his device announced. 

Stella stopped walking, turning to look at the distant cable car. She offered Leo a gentle, sympathetic smile, kneeling down next to his chair. 

"I'm sorry, buddy," Stella said softly. "But those cars were built in the 1870s. They are incredibly narrow, you have to climb up onto the running boards to get in, and they have absolutely zero ramps or tie-downs. There is no physical way to get a wheelchair on board safely."

Leo slumped back into his seat, his excitement instantly deflating. The Cable Cars were the single most famous thing about San Francisco transit, and the fact that he was locked out of experiencing them because of his body was a bitter pill to swallow. 

"Hey," Olivia said, stepping in front of his wheelchair and pointing down the wide boulevard of the Embarcadero. "Don't pout yet. Look at that."

Rolling smoothly down the center of the street was a stunning, vintage streetcar painted in a vibrant, two-tone classic transit livery. It wasn't a cable car—it drew power from overhead wires—but it was undeniably historic and beautiful. 

"That's the SFMA F-Line," Stella explained, standing back up. "They run a massive fleet of fully restored vintage streetcars from all over the world right down the center of the Embarcadero. And unlike the cable cars, the city built elevated concrete boarding islands with wayside ramps at every single stop. They are one hundred percent wheelchair accessible."

Leo sat up a little straighter, watching the beautiful vintage trolley glide toward a station just a block away. 

"Come on," Stella grinned, pulling her jacket tight against the cool bay breeze. "I booked us tickets for a boat ride at Pier 33. The F-Line goes right to it."

Ten minutes later, the pack was rolling onto the vintage streetcar. The boarding island's ramp aligned perfectly with the trolley's middle doors, allowing Leo to drive straight on without a single bump. He parked in the designated tie-down area, watching through the massive windows as the waterfront blurred past. 

"Where does the boat take us?" Luna asked, her hands resting firmly on the push-handles of Leo's wheelchair. She walked and stood independently, but her Cerebral Palsy made her balance unsteady, so she frequently used the back of Leo's heavy manual wheelchair like a walker to keep herself grounded. 

"Alcatraz," Stella said, hanging onto a leather hand-strap as the streetcar rumbled along the tracks. 

Luna's eyes went wide. "Al Capone," she said out loud, her voice ringing with excitement. 

Olivia laughed. "Yep. The most famous mobster in American history. The government couldn't nail him to the wall for all the murders, the bootlegging, or the racketeering. They just couldn't prove it."

"So they got him for cheating Uncle Sam out of taxes," Luna finished with a massive grin.

"Exactly," Stella nodded. "They locked him in the inescapable island fortress right out there in the bay. And we're going to go stand right in front of his cell."
