The SEAT tram hummed quietly as it made the incredibly steep, winding climb up the rock face of the island, bypassing what would have been a grueling hike. It deposited the pack directly in front of the heavy, imposing steel doors of the Main Cellhouse. 

Inside, the temperature immediately dropped. The thick concrete walls absorbed the chill of the bay, and the air smelled faintly of salt and old iron. 

Stella grabbed four heavy yellow headsets from the Audio Tour desk. She placed one over Leo's ears, adjusting the volume dial on the side, before handing the rest out to the pack. 

"Alright," Stella said, looking around the massive, cavernous room. "The narrator says we start down Broadway."

Broadway was the wide, central corridor running between B Block and C Block. As promised, the floor was perfectly flat, poured concrete. Luna grabbed the push-handles of Leo's manual wheelchair, stabilizing her footing as she pushed him easily down the wide corridor. 

The scale of the prison was staggering. Three tiers of stacked, iron-barred cells rose up on either side of them, echoing with the faint shuffling of other tourists and the dripping of water. 

Leo listened to the deep, gritty voice of a former guard on the audio tour as Luna pushed him slowly down the row. Finally, they came to a stop in front of a completely unremarkable cell on the bottom tier of B Block. 

Luna pulled her headset down around her neck. "This is it," she whispered, her voice echoing slightly in the cold corridor. "Cell 181. Inmate eighty-five."

Leo stared through the heavy iron bars. The cell was terrifyingly small—measuring only five feet by nine feet. It contained a narrow steel cot folded down from the wall, a small sink, and an exposed toilet. That was it. There were no windows, just the concrete walls and the bars facing the corridor. 

"It's so small," Olivia murmured, standing next to Leo's wheelchair. "The man who practically owned the entire city of Chicago, who lived in penthouses and had police chiefs on his payroll, was locked in a concrete closet."

"No special treatment," Stella added quietly. "That was the entire point of this place. Once you stepped off that boat, you weren't a mob boss anymore. You were just a number."

Leo reached for his NovaVox, his fingers cold against the screen. 

<aac>It feels like a tomb,</aac> he typed, the synthesized voice cutting through the damp, heavy air of the cellhouse. 

"It basically was," Luna agreed, staring into the dark cell. "He spent four and a half years in this exact spot. And the worst part?"

She pointed toward the heavy doors at the end of the corridor. 

"On New Year's Eve," Luna said softly, "if the wind was blowing in the right direction, the guards said the inmates could hear the music and the laughter from the yacht club parties carrying across the water from the city. They could hear the freedom right outside, but they couldn't reach it."
