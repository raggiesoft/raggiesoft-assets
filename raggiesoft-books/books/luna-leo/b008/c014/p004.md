Luna pushed Leo's wheelchair further down the flat concrete corridor, following the directions of the gritty narrator in their yellow headsets. 

"Oh, this is the good one," Olivia said, pointing to the next cluster of cells as the audio track seamlessly transitioned. "The nineteen sixty-two escape."

They stopped in front of a row of cells. Unlike Capone's empty, sterile cell, these cells looked like they had been frozen in time in the middle of the night. Tucked under the woolen blankets on the steel cots were incredibly realistic-looking human heads. 

Luna pulled her headset down to her neck. "Papier-mâché," she said out loud, leaning over the handles of Leo's chair to get a closer look. "Frank Morris and the Anglin brothers built dummy heads out of soap, toilet paper, and concrete dust. They even swept up real human hair from the prison barbershop and glued it to the tops."

"That is wildly creepy, but you have to respect the craftsmanship," Stella muttered, staring at the dummy head resting on the pillow. 

"Look under the sink," Olivia pointed out. 

Leo leaned forward in his wheelchair. The small, rectangular ventilation grate under the sink had been completely removed, leaving a dark, jagged hole leading into the utility corridor behind the cells. 

"They chipped away the concrete around the vent using stolen spoons from the dining hall," Luna explained, her voice bouncing excitedly off the cell walls. "It took them months. They played the accordion during music hour to cover up the sound of the scraping. Then they climbed up the plumbing pipes, got onto the roof, and paddled into the bay on a raft made of fifty stolen raincoats."

*Did they survive?* Leo typed on his NovaVox, looking at the dark hole in the wall. 

"Nobody knows," Olivia said, pulling her headset off. "The official FBI report says they drowned in the bay. The currents are incredibly fast, and the water is freezing."

"They survived," Luna said instantly, with absolute, unwavering conviction. "They were smart enough to build a raft out of raincoats and dummy heads out of soap. You don't spend a year chipping through concrete with a spoon just to jump into the water without a plan."

Leo looked back at his sister, then turned to the window at the end of the cell block, which looked out over the dark, violently choppy, freezing water of the San Francisco Bay. 

He tapped his NovaVox screen. 

*They drowned,* his device announced flatly. 

"You have no imagination," Luna huffed, playfully shoving his shoulder. 

"I'm with Leo on this one," Stella said, shuddering as she looked out the window. "I wouldn't last five minutes in that water. Come on, let's go see D Block. The tour says there are still bullet holes in the walls from the Battle of Alcatraz."
