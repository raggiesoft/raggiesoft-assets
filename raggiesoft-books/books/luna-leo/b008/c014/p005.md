The ferry ride back to the mainland was significantly quieter than the ride out. The heavy, oppressive atmosphere of the Alcatraz cellhouse had left the entire pack feeling somber and exhausted. 

By the time they rolled off the gangway at Pier 33, it was one o'clock in the afternoon, and the waterfront was completely packed with tourists. 

Luna pushed Leo's wheelchair along the wide concrete sidewalks of the Embarcadero, following Stella and Olivia as they headed north toward Fisherman's Wharf. The air was thick with a chaotic mix of smells: sea salt, roasting nuts, frying seafood, and the overwhelming, yeasty scent of baking sourdough bread. 

"Okay, I am starving," Olivia announced, stopping in front of a massive waterfront bakery where dozens of tourists were standing in line. She pointed at a large picture on the menu board. "We are in San Francisco. We have to get the famous clam chowder in the sourdough bread bowls."

Stella took one look at the picture of the thick, creamy white soup spilling over the edges of a hollowed-out loaf of bread. Instantly, her thick, working-class Newport, Rhode Island accent—which usually only appeared when she was incredibly passionate or angry—flared to life. 

"That is absolutely not chowda," Stella said, crossing her arms defensively. "That is a bowl of heavy cream and potatoes pretending to be seafood. Real chowda has a clear broth."

"Amen to that!" a voice called out from the front of the line. 

An older man in a windbreaker stepped out of the queue, grinning as he pointed at Stella. He had an accent just as thick and unapologetic as hers. "You can keep your heavy cream! Tell me you're from the Ocean State."

"Newport," Stella smiled, her defensive posture dropping instantly at the sound of home. She gestured to the twins. "My brother and sister and I were born there."

"Tiverton," the man beamed, tapping his chest. "I've been trying to tell my wife for three days, you can't get real seafood out here on the West Coast! Anyway, have a great trip, Newport!"

"You too!" Stella called back, waving as the man rejoined his family and walked away. 

Luna leaned over the back of Leo's wheelchair, staring at the picture on the menu board. The thought of thick, lumpy, creamy soup making the inside of a bread bowl soggy sent a visible shudder down her spine. It was a texture and sensory nightmare. 

"It looks like paste," Luna said out loud, wrinkling her nose in disgust. 

Leo completely agreed. He hated soggy textures more than almost anything else. He reached for his NovaVox, tapping the screen quickly. 

<aac>Burgers?</aac> the device announced. 

"Thank you, Leo," Stella said, looking incredibly relieved as she turned away from the chowder stand. "Yes. Burgers. No soggy bread, no heavy cream. Let's go find a place that just serves a normal piece of meat on a dry bun."

Olivia sighed dramatically, mourning her lost tourist experience, but she dutifully fell into step beside Leo's wheelchair. "Alright, Rhode Island. Lead the way to the burgers."
