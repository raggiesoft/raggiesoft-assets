The shadows of the Financial District were stretching long across Market Street by the time the pack finally rolled away from the Crown Point Memorial. 

"Alright," Stella said, stretching her arms over her head with a tired groan. "It has been a massive day. I think we need to head back to the Sovereign Regency, order some room service, and completely crash."

Leo immediately slammed his hand down onto his armrest, shaking his head rapidly. He pulled his NovaVox into his lap, his fingers flying across the screen. 

*BATS,* the device announced. *Transbay Tube. East Bay. EBT bus.*

Luna, who was leaning heavily on the push-handles of his chair, let out a long, exhausted sigh. "Leo, you have been sitting upright in this chair since nine o'clock this morning. You are running on pure adrenaline. If you don't go lay down, you are going to crash so hard you won't be able to move tomorrow."

Leo shook his head stubbornly. He pointed down Market Street toward the massive concrete entrance of the underground Montgomery Street station. The BATS heavy-rail trains were a major special interest of his, and the idea of riding one entirely underwater through the famous Transbay Tube to the East Bay was simply too intoxicating to pass up. 

"He wants a complex transit route," Olivia smiled, accurately reading his intense focus. "He wants to transfer."

*Dinner in the East Bay,* Leo typed, looking up at Stella with wide, pleading eyes behind his thick glasses. 

Stella rubbed her temples. She knew exactly what was going to happen. Leo was going to push himself past his physical limits out of sheer transit-induced excitement, and he was going to pay a heavy, painful price for it later tonight. But looking at the desperate excitement on his face, she couldn't bring herself to say no. 

"Fine," Stella relented. "We take BATS under the bay, we transfer to an EBT bus, and we find somewhere to eat in Oakland. But the second we are done, we are coming straight back."

Leo grinned triumphantly. 

Ten minutes later, the pack was underground. They fed their paper magnetic-stripe tickets into the automated turnstiles and took the elevator down to the BATS platform. When the heavy-rail train arrived, the doors slid open perfectly level with the platform, allowing Leo to roll straight on. 

As the train accelerated out of the city and plunged into the massive, dark concrete tunnel of the Transbay Tube resting at the bottom of the bay, the roar of the tracks echoed loudly in the cabin. Leo watched the dark tunnel walls blurring past the window, completely enthralled. 

By the time they emerged into the sunlight in the East Bay, transferred at MacArthur station, and boarded an EBT bus rumbling through the streets of Oakland, the adrenaline was finally starting to wear off. 

Leo sat in the tie-down area of the bus, listening to the pneumatic hiss of the brakes and the rattle of the windows. He had successfully orchestrated a three-agency, cross-bay transit route. It was a logistical masterpiece. 

But as the bus bounced over a pothole, a sharp, deep ache radiated up his spine. His eyelids felt impossibly heavy. Luna was right. He had pushed himself way too far, and the crash was coming fast.
