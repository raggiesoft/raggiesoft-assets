The EBT bus pulled over to the curb in a relatively quiet, tree-lined commercial district in North Oakland, the pneumatic brakes hissing loudly as it kneeled to the pavement. 

Leo let out a heavy breath as he unclipped his chair from the tie-downs and rolled down the ramp onto the sidewalk. The late afternoon air in the East Bay was noticeably warmer than the freezing wind whipping across the San Francisco waterfront, but Leo was too exhausted to enjoy it. His spine felt like it was made of lead, and every bump on the sidewalk sent a dull ache radiating up his back. 

"Okay, emergency dinner protocol," Stella said, taking one look at Leo's pale, drooping face. She scanned the street rapidly. "We need something quiet, and we need a booth where you can get out of that chair and take the weight off your back."

"There," Olivia pointed down the block. 

It was a classic, completely unremarkable American diner. The neon sign in the window was buzzing faintly, but looking through the glass, the restaurant was mostly empty and quiet. 

Luna pushed Leo's chair the rest of the way down the block. As soon as they got inside, the hostess led them to a wide, vinyl booth in the back corner. 

"Help me up," Leo typed weakly on his NovaVox. 

Luna immediately locked the brakes on his manual chair. She grabbed him securely under the arms, and with a coordinated, practiced heave, she helped him slide out of the wheelchair and into the soft, padded vinyl of the booth. 

Leo instantly slumped over, resting his forehead against the cool, smooth surface of the diner table. The relief on his spine was immediate, but his energy reserves were completely empty. 

"I'll order," Luna said softly, sitting in the booth next to him. She looked up at the waitress. "Two grilled cheese sandwiches, plain, with french fries. And two large chocolate milks."

"I'll take a turkey club," Olivia said. 

"And I need the largest black coffee you have, please," Stella added with a sympathetic smile. 

As the waitress walked away, Stella reached across the table and gently rubbed Leo's shoulder. He didn't lift his head from the table, but he turned his face slightly to look at her through his thick glasses. 

"You orchestrated a brilliant transit ride today, buddy," Stella said softly. "BATS through the Transbay Tube and a flawless EBT bus connection. It was a masterpiece."

Leo managed a small, exhausted smile. 

"But you're paying for it now," Stella continued gently. "So here is the plan. We are eating dinner, I am calling a minivan cab to pick us up at the curb, and they are driving us straight across the Bay Bridge to the hotel. And tomorrow is a zero-impact day. No ferries, no mountains, no complex transfers. We are staying on flat ground."

<aac>Thank you,</aac> Leo tapped slowly on his NovaVox, closing his eyes as he waited for his grilled cheese.
