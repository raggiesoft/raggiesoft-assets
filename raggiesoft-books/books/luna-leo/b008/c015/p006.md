The sub-basement of the Sovereign Regency was an absolute fortress. 

Unlike the sprawling, glass-enclosed atrium upstairs, Conference Room D was a windowless, concrete box buried two stories below sea level. The air down here was thick and heavily air-conditioned, carrying the faint, sterile smell of industrial carpet cleaner. Thick, sound-dampening acoustic panels lined the walls, rendering the room completely dead to outside noise. 

It was, in a word, perfect.

Luna unrolled her blue sleeping bag across the patterned carpet, patting it flat. Across the room, Isabel and Eleanor were busy dismantling a massive, polished mahogany boardroom table, pushing the heavy wooden segments against the walls to open up the floor space. 

"Okay, perimeter secured," Isabel announced cheerfully, wiping her hands on her jeans. "We officially have a bunker."

Stella walked through the heavy double doors carrying two massive canvas tote bags overflowing with snacks. Olivia followed right behind her, carefully balancing a cardboard tray holding six large, paper-wrapped burritos from a local taqueria. 

"Rations have arrived," Stella smiled, dropping the tote bags onto the carpet. 

Leo was parked near the front of the room in his manual wheelchair, wearing his thickest, most comfortable sweatpants. He looked incredibly relaxed. Next to him, Oliver sat in his massive motorized wheelchair, a sleek pair of heavy-duty noise-canceling headphones resting loosely around his neck. 

<aac>I told you it was completely soundproof,</aac> Oliver's MagnaByte laptop announced in its deep, synthesized voice. <aac>My engineering professor actually helped design the foundation of this building. He used specialized seismic dampeners that completely isolate the sub-basement from acoustic vibrations.</aac>

Leo tapped rapidly on his NovaVox tablet. <aac>We should live down here.</aac>

Sophia, who was meticulously arranging a pile of sensory-friendly fidget toys onto one of the displaced tables, let out a soft laugh. "I know, right? It's so quiet. Upstairs, the lobby is an absolute madhouse right now. Half the city is out on the Embarcadero waiting for the fireworks."

"Well, they can have the fireworks," Eleanor said, dropping onto a green sleeping bag and cracking open a can of soda. "I would take this over a loud, crowded pier any day of the week."

Luna smiled, settling down cross-legged on her sleeping bag next to Leo. She looked around the room, taking in the scene. It was incredibly rare for her and Leo to be in an environment where they didn't have to apologize for their needs or feel like they were inconveniencing anyone. Being surrounded by other neurodivergent young adults who completely understood the necessity of a 'bunker picnic' felt profoundly validating.

For the first time since they had boarded the train in Chicago, Luna felt her baseline anxiety truly begin to melt away. 

"Alright, movie time," Olivia declared, pulling a portable DVD player and a thick binder of discs out of one of the tote bags. "What's the consensus? We have *The Matrix*, *Shrek*, or *Jurassic Park*."

<aac>Jurassic Park,</aac> Leo and Oliver's text-to-speech engines announced in unison. 

The two young men looked at each other, a shared, silent grin passing between them. 

"Dinosaurs it is," Isabel laughed. 

As Olivia set up the DVD player and Stella began passing out the burritos, the first faint, distant thud of a firework echoed from somewhere high above them. 

Down in the sub-basement, insulated by tons of concrete and seismic dampeners, it sounded like nothing more than a muffled, heavy footstep. Leo didn't even flinch. He just took a bite of his burrito, his eyes glued to the small screen as the opening credits of the movie began to roll. 

Luna leaned back against the wall, pulling her knees to her chest. She looked over at Stella, who caught her eye and offered a warm, knowing smile. 

They had made it. The Fourth of July, the most notoriously stressful night of the year for their sensory-sensitive pack, was going to be just fine.
