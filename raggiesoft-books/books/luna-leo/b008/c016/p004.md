The warm, fuzzy feeling of having made a new friend lasted exactly ten minutes before Leo's body completely betrayed him. 

As the EBT bus rolled off the Bay Bridge and began navigating the foggy, darkened streets of downtown San Francisco, a sudden, violent cramp ripped through Leo's lower abdomen. 

He gasped, his hands flying off his NovaVox tablet and gripping the armrests of his manual wheelchair. His knuckles turned stark white. 

"Leo?" Luna asked instantly, leaning forward in her seat. Her twin radar was flawlessly tuned to his physical state. "What is it? Muscle spasm?"

Leo squeezed his eyes shut, shaking his head. He didn't even have the core control to reach for his tablet. He just looked at Stella, his eyes wide with absolute, raw panic. The heavy, greasy Mexican food from Oakland was hitting his incredibly sensitive digestive system like a freight train, and it was moving fast. 

Stella understood the look immediately. All the color drained from her face. 

"Bathroom," Stella commanded, her voice dropping into a low, deadly serious register. She stood up, grabbing her backpack. "Olivia, get ready. We're hitting the ground running the second this bus stops."

"Copy that," Olivia said, instantly matching Stella's intensity. She moved into position behind Leo's wheelchair, grabbing the push handles. 

Because Leo lacked bowel and bladder control, gastrointestinal emergencies were a horrifying reality of their daily lives. The pack had clinical, military-grade protocols for dealing with it, but that didn't make the physical urgency any less terrifying for Leo. He squeezed his eyes shut, using every ounce of his willpower to clench his muscles and hold on. 

The bus finally pulled into the massive, echoing concrete cavern of the Transbay Terminal. 

The air brakes hissed, and the front doors swung open. Before the driver could even lower the accessibility ramp, Olivia popped Leo's wheelchair back into a wheelie and expertly bumped him down the two steep steps onto the concrete platform. 

"Go, go, go!" Stella ordered, sprinting ahead of them. 

"Sorry guys, emergency!" Olivia yelled back over her shoulder to Oliver and his cousins, who were still waiting for the driver to deploy the ramp for Oliver's heavy motorized chair. 

Olivia pushed Leo at a dead sprint across the dimly lit terminal concourse. Stella was twenty feet ahead of them, frantically scanning the overhead signs. 

"Family restroom, three o'clock!" Stella shouted, pointing at a large blue sign down a side hallway. 

They tore down the corridor. Stella slammed her hand against the metal push-plate on the wall, and the heavy door of the accessible family restroom slowly swung open. Olivia shoved Leo's wheelchair through the gap before the door was even fully open. 

Stella hit the lock button on the inside, instantly securing the room. 

There was no time for privacy or modesty. This was a clinical emergency. Olivia ripped the brakes on the wheelchair, grabbing Leo under the arms. Stella grabbed him by the waist of his sweatpants. 

"One, two, three!" Olivia shouted. 

With a massive, unceremonious heave, they lifted Leo out of his chair, stripped his sweatpants down, and slammed him onto the toilet seat. 

They were exactly one second too late. 

Leo let out a miserable, exhausted groan, leaning forward and burying his face in his hands as a violent wave of diarrhea evacuated from his body. The pain in his stomach was sharp and relentless, and he felt profoundly humiliated, even though his sisters and Olivia had seen it a thousand times before. 

"It's okay, buddy," Stella said softly, her intense military demeanor dropping instantly as she rubbed his back. "You made it. You're safe. We've got you."

"Mexican food was a bad idea," Olivia sighed, grabbing a massive wad of paper towels from the dispenser and wetting them in the sink. "My bad. I totally should have known the grease would wreck his system."

"It's not your fault," Luna said, who had quietly slipped into the bathroom right before the door locked. She knelt down next to the toilet, gently prying Leo's hands away from his face and wiping the cold sweat off his forehead with her sleeve. "He wanted tacos too."

Leo kept his eyes glued to the tiled floor, his stomach still cramping violently. The physical reality of his disability was exhausting, humiliating, and completely inescapable. But as Luna rubbed his arm and Stella prepared the clean-up supplies with zero hesitation or judgment, the intense wave of shame began to recede. 

He didn't have a normal body, but he had the best pack in the world.
