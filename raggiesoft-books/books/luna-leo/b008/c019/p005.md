"I am not letting you skip it," Leo typed aggressively on his NovaVox, holding the screen up for Luna to read. "We have to stop the bully from altering the timestream continuum. This is heavy."

They were standing outside the massive Institute of Future Technology building, home to the Upper Lot's flagship motion simulator: **Timeline Paradox: The Ride**. 

"Leo, it's a motion simulator," Luna argued, looking at the warning signs. "It's violent, it jerks you around, and it's basically a roller coaster in a box. You hate roller coasters."

<aac>It's not a real roller coaster. It's hydraulics. And I love the movies. I'm riding it.</aac>

Olivia cracked her knuckles. "He's the boss. Let's go save the timestream."

Because of the tight loading garages, Leo had to leave his manual wheelchair parked out on the main concourse with an attendant. Olivia effortlessly deadlift carried him through the short queue and into the small, dark garage, depositing him securely in the back seat of the modified, silver sports car. She sat tightly next to him, and Stella pulled the gull-wing door shut.

"Commencing temporal jump," the video screen on the dashboard announced as the car suddenly rumbled to life. 

The garage doors opened, and the entire car lifted eight feet into the air, pushing forward into a massive, multi-story wraparound projection dome. The simulated wind blasted their faces as the car banked sharply, chasing the villain through the prehistoric ice age. Leo gripped the armrests, laughing at the sheer sensory overload. 

And then, everything went black. 

The massive projection screen died. The hydraulic pistons under the car let out a loud, whining hiss. The car dropped abruptly and violently back down into the garage with a heavy *thud*, rattling everyone's teeth.

The bright, harsh fluorescent work lights clicked on overhead. 

"Attention time travelers," a bored voice announced over the intercom. "We have experienced a technical fault. Please remain seated. An attendant will assist you with an evacuation."

"Well," Stella sighed, rubbing her neck. "That was anti-climactic."

A minute later, a park attendant manually popped the gull-wing door open. "Sorry folks, ride's down. We have to evacuate the building immediately through the emergency exits. I can go grab the wheelchair, but it's parked all the way on the upper concourse—"

"Don't worry about it," Olivia said, immediately unbuckling her seatbelt. 

She leaned into the tight back seat, slipped her arms under Leo's back and knees, and hoisted him out of the sports car in one fluid, effortless deadlift. She carried him out of the garage, down a flight of concrete maintenance stairs, and out into the blazing California sun, barely breaking a sweat. They retrieved his wheelchair from the concourse attendant just a minute later. 

Because of the e-stop, a deeply apologetic manager handed them four golden "Front-of-the-Line" re-ride coupons. 

They killed the next thirty minutes taking a bathroom break and wandering through the sci-fi gift shop, letting the adrenaline of the sudden drop fade. Leo bought a replica license plate before proudly leading the pack back to the ride entrance. 

They handed over their golden coupons, loaded back into a fresh sports car, and this time, the timestream continuum was saved without a single hitch.
