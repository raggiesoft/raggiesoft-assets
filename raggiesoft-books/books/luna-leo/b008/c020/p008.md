Even though *Buccaneer's Bounty* had reopened, their day at *The Enchanted Kingdom* had ultimately been cut short. 

The cascading effect of the park-wide shutdown meant that as rides slowly came back online, they were instantly swarmed by tens of thousands of displaced, frustrated guests. The sensory environment of the park rapidly deteriorated. The walkways became gridlocked, the standby wait times skyrocketed into the multi-hour range, and the overall volume of the crowd reached an overwhelming, chaotic pitch. 

Recognizing that the environment was no longer safe or enjoyable for the twins, Stella had made the executive decision to pull the plug. 

The journey back to Hollywood had been long and exhausting. They had navigated the massive crowds leaving the park, boarded an **SCR Orange County Line** train back up to LA Union Station, and then transferred to the underground **LATA Red Line** subway to get back to their hotel. 

Now, they were safely back in the quiet, air-conditioned sanctuary of Room 412. 

Leo lay flat on his back on his bed, his eyes closed, listening to the hum of the mini-fridge. He was disappointed, of course. He had wanted to see the haunted mansion again, or maybe try the jungle cruise. But unlike the catastrophic meltdown triggered by *Cosmic Peak*, he wasn't spiraling. He understood logic. An earthquake was an act of nature, a massive, undeniable force that broke the rules for everyone, not just him. 

"Okay, let's see what the locals have to say about this," Stella said, dropping onto the edge of her bed and grabbing the television remote. She flipped the TV to a local Los Angeles news channel. 

The screen flared to life, showing an anchor sitting at a brightly lit news desk. Behind him, a graphic of a seismograph was splashed across the screen with the bold, red text: **AFTERNOON JOLT**. 

*"...and while it certainly sent a scare through tourists and locals alike, seismologists confirm there is no reported damage from this afternoon's earthquake,"* the anchor reported in a crisp, professional tone. *"The United States Geological Survey has officially upgraded the tremor to a magnitude 3.1, centered just off the Newport-Inglewood Fault Zone."*

The broadcast cut to B-roll footage of the exact plaza they had been in just hours ago. News helicopters showed sweeping aerial shots of massive crowds milling around the entrance to *The Enchanted Kingdom* and *Golden State Adventure*. 

*"The minor tremor did prompt immediate, park-wide safety protocols at major Southern California theme parks,"* the anchor continued over the footage. *"All attractions were temporarily halted while maintenance crews conducted mandatory track inspections, leading to massive crowds and early exits for many frustrated vacationers. The transit authority also ordered all regional SCR commuter trains to slow to restricted speeds for about an hour, though schedules are now fully back to normal."*

"Well, at least we weren't the only ones who tapped out early," Olivia remarked, sitting cross-legged on the floor near Leo's bed. She reached over and gently rubbed his shoulder. "Are you doing okay, Lee?"

Leo opened his eyes and reached for his NovaVox, which was resting on the nightstand. 

<aac>I am okay,</aac> he typed, the synthesized voice filling the quiet room. <aac>I am sad we left early. But the ground broke the rules. Not the park.</aac>

"That's a really good way to look at it," Luna said from her spot by the window. She was staring out at the hazy Los Angeles skyline, the fading evening sun casting a warm, golden glow over the city. "The Earth just had to stretch its legs."

<aac>Yes,</aac> Leo agreed. <aac>Next time, the Earth needs to ask for a turn.</aac>

Stella burst out laughing, the tension of the long, chaotic day finally breaking. "I'll be sure to pass that along to the geological survey, buddy. Now, who's ready to order some outrageously overpriced room service?"
