The walk back to The Heritage House from the pizzeria was significantly slower than their outbound journey. The adrenaline crash from the Zenith Tower, combined with the sheer, undeniable gravity of Chicago deep-dish pizza, had turned the pack into a slow-moving caravan.

But nobody was struggling quite as much as Stella. 

"I think I made a terrible mistake," Stella groaned, a hand pressed firmly against her stomach as they waited at a crosswalk. She looked slightly pale, her usual commanding posture replaced by a slight, pained hunch. 

"You only had one slice, Stel," Luna pointed out, though she was rubbing her own stomach sympathetically. 

"That wasn't a slice, Luna. That was a brick of solid dairy wrapped in dough," Stella muttered, wincing as a sharp cramp hit her abdomen. "My body is actively rejecting it. We need to get back to the room. Now."

Olivia chuckled softly, easily pushing Leo's wheelchair over the curb as the light changed. "The guardian bear has been defeated by cheese."

"Laugh it up," Stella wheezed, picking up her pace despite her obvious discomfort. "Just wait until you hit your mid-twenties. Your iron stomach will betray you too."

By the time they reached the opulent lobby of their hotel, Stella was speed-walking. The moment she unlocked their door, she tossed the keycard onto the nearest bed, bypassed her luggage entirely, and made a beeline straight for the bathroom. 

The door shut with a solid *click*, followed immediately by the sound of the locking mechanism sliding into place. 

"I claim this territory!" Stella called out from behind the door, her voice echoing slightly against the tile. "No one is allowed in here for the foreseeable future. Save yourselves!"

Luna snorted, collapsing back-first onto one of the queen beds. "I think she's dying."

<aac>She will be okay,</aac> Leo typed, a small, amused smile playing on his lips. It was rare to see their indestructible older sister so thoroughly sidelined by something as mundane as lunch. 

"Yeah, she'll survive," Olivia agreed, dropping her own bags near the desk and stretching her arms over her head. "But I think our sightseeing is officially done for the day."

"I'm fine with that," Luna sighed, closing her eyes. The heavy food had successfully anchored her frayed nerves, but the emotional exhaustion of the panic attack was finally demanding its due. "I just want to lie here and not think about falling from the sky."

With Stella completely occupying the bathroom and Luna sinking into a food coma on the bed, Olivia walked over to Leo's wheelchair. She knelt down in front of him, resting her hands lightly on his knees. 

"You doing okay?" she asked softly, her green eyes searching his face. "That was a pretty rough afternoon."

Leo nodded slowly. The phobia was gone, safely locked away behind the memory of solid ground and heavy food. But sitting here in the quiet hotel room, with the late afternoon sun slanting through the windows, the other, quieter ache returned. 

He reached out and gently laid his hand over hers. 

<aac>I know,</aac> his communicator said, though he hadn't typed it recently. It was a saved phrase he kept readily available. He knew she had to go. He knew the clock was ticking. 

Olivia's expression softened, a deep, empathetic sadness in her eyes. She squeezed his knee. "I know too, Leo," she whispered. 

She stood up, stepped around to the side of his chair, and carefully lifted him into her arms. But instead of carrying him to the empty queen bed, she carried him over to the bed where Luna was currently dozing. Olivia knew that while Leo desperately needed her warmth, he still needed the physical anchor of his twin sister to fully regulate his nervous system after the panic attack. 

Olivia gently laid him down on the mattress right beside Luna. Luna stirred, blindly reaching out to wrap an arm around her brother before sinking back into her food coma. Olivia kicked off her own shoes and climbed into the bed on his other side, wrapping her arms around his torso to complete the hold. Together, they formed a quiet, three-person knot of absolute security, listening to the distant, muffled groans of their oldest sister in the bathroom.
