When the time finally came to officially go to sleep, the pack naturally gravitated toward their established, unspoken formations. Even though there were two spacious queen beds in the hotel room, Luna didn't want to sleep alone. She climbed into the bed where Leo was resting, squeezing herself onto the mattress so that Leo was sandwiched securely between her and Olivia. 

It was a tight fit, but the deep, grounding pressure of having a warm body on either side of him was exactly what Leo's exhausted, altitude-stressed nervous system needed to finally shut down. 

Sometime around 2:00 AM, however, the exhaustion curdled into a nightmare. 

It wasn't a normal dream. It was a visceral, suffocating night terror—a delayed psychological reaction to the massive influx of stress, the timezone shifts, and the crude, triggering joke outside the tavern. In the dark theater of his sleeping mind, Leo was surrounded. Faceless, aggressive shapes were closing in on him, grabbing at his chair, taunting him. He was trapped, unable to speak, unable to defend himself. 

In the real world, Leo let out a sharp, choked gasp. He rolled violently onto his stomach, his contracted arms suddenly lashing out in blind, panicked swings against the mattress as he fought desperately against the invisible attackers. 

"Leo?" Olivia's groggy voice cut through the dark. She sat up instantly, alarmed by the sudden violence of his thrashing. "Leo, hey, wake—"

She didn't get to finish the sentence. 

Before Olivia could even fully orient herself, Luna was already moving. Operating on pure, deeply ingrained muscle memory, the eighteen-year-old girl scrambled over the tangled bedsheets. She didn't hesitate or try to gently wake him up. Instead, she threw her entire body directly on top of him. 

Luna landed face-down on his back. She instantly locked her long legs around his waist and snaked her arms tightly under his thrashing ones, wrapping her arms completely around his torso. With a fierce, practiced motion, she clamped her arms shut, brutally pinning his swinging arms flush against his own sides. She buried her face firmly into the crook of his neck, effectively turning herself into a human straitjacket. 

Olivia lunged forward, her protective instincts flaring. "Luna, what are you doing? Let him—"

"Stand down, Liv!" Stella's voice snapped sharply from the other bed. 

The bedside lamp clicked on, flooding the room with dull yellow light. Stella was sitting up, her eyes wide but her posture completely calm and authoritative. 

Olivia froze, her hands hovering inches from Luna's back. "He's having a seizure. She's crushing him!"

"He's not seizing, he's having a night terror," Stella corrected, her voice steady and commanding. "If you try to wake him up now, he'll accidentally hit you, or he'll hurt himself. Luna knows exactly what she's doing. She's been pulling him out of these for years. Just stand down and give her a minute."

Olivia slowly lowered her hands, her chest heaving as she watched the chaotic scene on the mattress. 

Underneath the crushing, immovable weight of his twin sister, Leo was still fighting. His lungs burned as he tried to buck her off, his mind still trapped in the terrifying hallucination. But Luna didn't budge. She held onto him with a fierce, desperate strength, her body providing a massive, inescapable wave of deep proprioceptive pressure. 

"I've got you," Luna whispered fiercely into his ear, her voice tight with exertion. "I'm right here. You're safe. I've got you, Leo. Come back to me."

Slowly, the heavy, grounding weight of his twin sister began to break through the panic. The invisible attackers in his mind started to dissolve, replaced by the physical reality of the mattress beneath him and Luna's familiar, rhythmic breathing against his neck. 

The frantic thrashing began to subside into violent tremors, and then, finally, into heavy, exhausted sobs. 

Leo went limp against the mattress. He was awake. He was safe. 

"There you go," Luna murmured. 

But she didn't let go, and she didn't climb off of him. As the fight left his body, the brutal, clinical restraint seamlessly transitioned into a profound, prolonged hug. She simply stayed lying face down on top of him, adjusting her arms so they were wrapped lovingly around his torso rather than pinning him. 

Olivia watched, still hovering near the edge of the bed. "Is she going to get up?" she whispered to Stella. "She's squishing him."

"No, she's not. Leave them be," Stella said softly, leaning back against her own headboard. "That's the second part of the protocol. Once the terror breaks, it turns into a hug. She'll stay on top of him until she is absolutely satisfied that he's fully back to reality. And then she'll probably just stay there because they both like it."

Olivia looked at Stella, awe mixing with the lingering adrenaline. "Have you ever...?"

"Tried to pull him out of one?" Stella nodded slowly. "Yeah. Plenty of times. But I don't have the magic touch. Luna mastered the technique years ago. She knows exactly how much pressure to apply." Stella offered a small, bittersweet smile. "Plus, when his world has completely fallen apart and he's terrified, he'd much rather realize his twin sister is the one holding him together than his older sister."

Olivia watched the two of them, the steady rise and fall of their backs moving in unison. "Does she do this every time?"

"Every time," Stella confirmed quietly. "And if Luna ever has an autistic meltdown or a terror of her own, Leo does the exact same thing for her. They are each other's weighted blankets. It's how they survive."

Olivia sat silently on the edge of the mattress, the last of her adrenaline fading into a profound realization. She possessed the raw, athletic strength to physically carry Leo across the country, but in the deepest, darkest moments of his psychological terror, it was Luna—the seemingly fragile, anxious twin—who was his ultimate, unbreakable shield.
