The sun rose over the Mile High City, pouring bright, harsh light through the gap in the blackout curtains. 

Leo was already awake. He was still lying on his stomach, completely pinned to the mattress by Luna. She hadn't moved all night. She was still draped heavily across his back, her legs tangled with his and her arms securely wrapped around his torso. 

He didn't try to wake her. The crushing pressure of his twin sister felt like a physical anchor tethering him to the real world, keeping the dark, chaotic terrors of the night firmly locked away. 

Eventually, Stella’s alarm went off across the room. 

"Alright, fortress, time to move," Stella groaned, slapping the alarm clock on the nightstand. "We have an eight o'clock train to catch."

Luna stirred, letting out a muffled sigh against Leo's neck. She slowly unhooked her arms and rolled off of him, rubbing her eyes behind her glasses. She didn't say anything about the night terror, and neither did he. It was an unspoken, lifelong protocol. She had pulled him out of the darkness, and now it was morning. 

Olivia quickly helped Leo transfer back into his motorized wheelchair. He still had a lingering headache from the altitude, but it was vastly overshadowed by the adrenaline of knowing they were finally leaving the thin air behind. 

They packed up their bags, checked out of The Horizon Suites, and rolled quickly down the 16th Street pedestrian mall to Union Station. 

The historic, Beaux-Arts train station was bustling with morning commuters, but the pack moved with practiced, military precision. Stella led the way, brandishing their updated tickets like a shield. They bypassed the crowds, navigating the accessible ramps directly to the boarding platform where the massive, silver Superliner cars of the *Western Pioneer* were waiting. 

"Family Bedroom, straight through to Emeryville," the conductor announced, checking Stella's tickets and lowering the metal bridge to the train. "You folks are in for a spectacular ride today. We're going straight through the Rockies."

Olivia pushed Leo onto the lower level of the sleeping car, navigating the tight corner into the familiar, cramped space of the Family Bedroom. 

It was exactly the same claustrophobic layout they had endured across Nebraska—two tiny adult bunks on one side, two child-sized bunks on the other, spanning the entire width of the train car with windows on both sides. But this time, nobody complained about the lack of space. 

"Okay, beds are stowed, bags are under the seats," Stella said, dropping heavily onto the lower couch and letting out a long breath. "We did it. We survived Denver."

At exactly 8:05 AM, the massive diesel engines of the *Western Pioneer* roared to life. The train gave a violent, metallic lurch, and then slowly began to pull away from the concrete platform of Union Station. 

Leo sat perfectly still in the narrow space between the bunks, watching the brick buildings of downtown Denver slowly begin to slide backward past the window. 

As the train picked up speed, the towering, snow-capped peaks of the Rocky Mountains filled the forward windows. It was a massive, intimidating wall of rock that seemed to entirely block the horizon. 

They were officially leaving the Midwest behind. The final, massive leg of their cross-country journey had begun.
