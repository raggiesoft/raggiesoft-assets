The ferry ride from Pier 33 was shockingly smooth. The gangway leading onto the massive boat was a wide, ridged metal ramp, making it incredibly easy for Leo to push his manual wheelchair directly onto the main lower deck. 

Once they were out on the open water of the San Francisco Bay, the biting, salt-laced wind whipped fiercely across the deck. The water was dark and choppy, crashing aggressively against the hull of the boat. 

Luna was standing right next to Leo, her hands gripping the push-handles of his wheelchair tightly to keep herself balanced against the rocking of the ferry. She was staring out at the rocky, isolated island looming in the distance. 
 
"United States versus Sullivan," Luna said, her voice loud over the roar of the boat's engines and the wind. "Nineteen twenty-seven... Supreme Court ruling."

Olivia leaned against the railing next to them, pulling her jacket tight. "What about it?"

"The Supreme Court ruled that all income, from whatever source derived, is taxable," Luna continued, stumbling slightly over the legal jargon but pushing through with sheer excitement. "They ruled that the government doesn't care if you make your money legally or illegally. You still have to pay... income tax on it. Even on stolen money."

"Wait, seriously?" Olivia laughed. "You're legally required to report stolen money on your taxes?"

"Yes. That was the silver bullet," Luna said, leaning heavily onto the back of Leo's chair. "The Feds couldn't prove Al Capone was a murderer. But because of the Sullivan ruling, they could prove he didn't pay taxes on his bootlegging money. It's the only reason he ended up on that island."

"That is the most aggressively bureaucratic way to take down a mob boss," Stella said, shaking her head in amusement as she held onto the railing. "Only Uncle Sam could take down the Chicago Outfit with an IRS audit."

Leo looked out at the island. As they got closer, the sheer, vertical rock cliffs of Alcatraz became intimidatingly clear. The massive concrete cellhouse sat at the very top of the island, looming like an inescapable fortress over the freezing, treacherous waters of the bay. 

He tapped his own NovaVox. <aac>How do I get up there?</aac> 

Stella pointed to the dock rapidly approaching the front of the ferry. "The Park Service has it completely covered. The hike from the dock to the cellhouse is the equivalent of climbing a thirteen-story building. But they run an accessibility tram called the SEAT. It's waiting right there at the dock, and it will drive us all the way up the hill to the prison doors."

"And once we're inside?" Olivia asked. 

"The main cellhouse is perfectly flat," Stella smiled, patting Leo on the shoulder. "You'll be able to roll right down the middle of Broadway and look straight into Capone's cell."
