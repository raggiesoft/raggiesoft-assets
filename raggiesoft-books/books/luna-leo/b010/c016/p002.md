By the time the NBT bus dropped the pack off at the Embarcadero, Leo was thoroughly sick of the freezing, damp San Francisco air. Stella had forced all of them to wear thick winter coats over their t-shirts that morning, and the heavy layers felt stifling and restrictive against the straps of his manual wheelchair. 

They met up with the cousins at the entrance to the Embarcadero BATS station. The eight of them took the elevator down into the massive, echoing concrete cavern beneath the city. 

"Alright, we want an East Bay-bound train," Sophia instructed, looking up at the glowing digital destination signs. "Anything heading to Richmond or Pittsburg/Bay Point."

Within minutes, a sleek, silver BATS train roared into the station, the aerodynamic cars looking more like an airplane fuselage than a traditional subway. The doors slid open with a distinctive chime. 

Stella and Luna maneuvered Leo into the first car, parking his manual chair directly across from Oliver's motorized chair. It was a tight fit with all eight of them clustered into the accessible space, but they made it work. 

The train doors closed, and the BATS train accelerated out of the station with a smooth, powerful surge. 

"We are now entering the Transbay Tube," Isabel announced dramatically, gripping a silver stanchion pole as the train plunged into the darkness. "We are currently traveling at seventy miles per hour inside a concrete tube sitting directly on the floor of the San Francisco Bay."

Leo looked out the dark window. The thought of being completely submerged under billions of gallons of ocean water was terrifying, but the sheer speed and smoothness of the train ride was incredibly soothing to his spastic muscles. He looked across the aisle at Oliver. 

Oliver was staring right back at him, his thick glasses reflecting the fluorescent lights of the train car. The older boy tapped his MagnaByte. 

<aac>I hate tunnels,</aac> Oliver's laptop announced. 

Leo grinned, quickly typing on his NovaVox. <aac>Me too. Too much water.</aac>

Oliver nodded in profound, silent agreement. It was the first time Leo had ever really interacted with another young man who fundamentally understood his perspective. Surrounded entirely by the fierce, fiercely protective women in his life, Leo realized he was actually starting to enjoy the quiet, unassuming solidarity of having another guy around. 

Ten minutes later, the train burst out of the tunnel and began a steep climb up an elevated concrete viaduct. 

The transition was physically staggering. When they had boarded the train in San Francisco, the city was buried under fifty-five-degree fog. As the train doors opened at the West Oakland BATS station, a wave of eighty-degree, bone-dry, blindingly sunny heat blasted into the train car. 

"Oh my god," Olivia gasped, instantly ripping off her heavy winter coat as they stepped out onto the outdoor platform. "It's literally a completely different season over here."

"Welcome to the East Bay," Eleanor laughed, shedding her own jacket and tying it around her waist. "The East Bay Hills block the marine layer. It's basically a desert over here compared to the city."

The eight of them took the elevator down to the street level and immediately boarded an EBT bus. The bright green bus drove them a few miles north, pulling to a stop along a wide, relatively quiet stretch of Mandela Parkway. 

They disembarked onto a wide, beautifully landscaped pedestrian median that ran directly down the center of the boulevard. It was peaceful and completely bathed in warm sunlight. 

"So, why are we here?" Stella asked, looking around the quiet neighborhood. 

"Because this is where the Cypress Street Viaduct used to be," Sophia explained, adjusting her glasses as she looked at the wide expanse of the parkway. "Until 1989, this was a massive, double-decker concrete freeway holding up Interstate 880."

Oliver maneuvered his motorized wheelchair closer to the group, his fingers flying across the keys of his MagnaByte. 

<aac>The Loma Prieta earthquake hit on October 17th, 1989,</aac> Oliver's deep, synthesized voice echoed across the quiet parkway. <aac>It was a 6.9 magnitude quake. The Cypress Viaduct was built on soft mud, and the columns holding up the top deck didn't have enough steel reinforcement. When the shaking started, the supports failed. The entire top deck collapsed directly onto the bottom deck, crushing dozens of cars.</aac>

Leo looked down the long, peaceful stretch of the parkway. It was hard to imagine a terrifying, catastrophic collapse happening right where they were standing. 

"It was a massive engineering failure," Isabel added softly, her usual cheerful demeanor completely gone. "They prioritized cost-cutting and speed over structural integrity. They knew the soil was unstable, but they built it anyway."

"Which is the exact thesis of our paper," Eleanor concluded, crossing her arms. "Engineering ethics. The responsibility to prioritize human life over vanity and budgets. Arthur Sterling understood that when he built his sanctuary tower. The Crown Point developers didn't."

Leo stared at Oliver. The older boy was looking down the parkway, his face set in a look of quiet, intense concentration. Leo realized that Oliver wasn't just studying engineering because he was smart. He was studying it because he cared deeply about keeping people safe. 

Leo tapped his tablet. <aac>You are going to be a good engineer, Oliver.</aac>

Oliver looked up, clearly surprised by the compliment. A slow, genuine smile spread across his face, and he gave Leo a small, appreciative nod.
