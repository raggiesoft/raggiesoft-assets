"Ladies and gentlemen, we are arriving in San Luis Obispo," the conductor's voice crackled over the intercom. "This will be a ten-minute fresh air break. If you step off the train, please do not stray far from your car."

Luna wrinkled her nose. "Fresh air break? Don't they just mean a smoking stop?"

"Technically yes," Stella said, standing up and stretching her arms over her head. "But they don't want to encourage it over the intercom. And frankly, after sitting in this cabin for six hours, I want actual fresh air. Let's go stretch our legs."

Because they had booked the accessible seating on the lower level, getting off the train was surprisingly easy. They didn't have to navigate the steep, terrifying stairs. A conductor simply dropped a heavy metal bridge plate over the small gap between the train door and the concrete platform, and Leo rolled right out. 

The moment they hit the platform, the smell of cheap tobacco smoke hit them. A dozen passengers from the upper levels had already spilled out onto the concrete, desperately lighting cigarettes the second their feet touched the ground.

Leo coughed, aggressively waving a hand in front of his face. 

<aac>Disgusting.</aac>

"Gross," Luna agreed, immediately grabbing the handles of his wheelchair. "Let's get up to the front of the train. Away from the chimneys."

Olivia and Stella followed as Luna pushed Leo quickly down the length of the platform, putting as much distance as possible between themselves and the heavy cloud of smoke hovering near the boarding doors. 

San Luis Obispo was beautiful. The station was nestled in a valley surrounded by golden, sun-baked hills, and the afternoon air was warm and distinctly Californian. Unlike the salty, damp breeze of Oakland, the air here felt dry and crisp. 

"Okay, this is better," Stella said, leaning against a large brick pillar near the front locomotive. She took a deep breath, looking around at the quiet, sunlit platform. "Ten minutes of actual fresh air. Enjoy it, because once we hit Los Angeles tonight, the air is just going to taste like smog and exhaust."

Leo locked his brakes and tilted his head back, letting the warm California sun hit his face. The deep, rumbling vibration of the train tracks was finally gone, replaced by the quiet rustling of nearby palm trees. 

He closed his eyes. The July 4th fireworks and the sensory overload of San Francisco finally felt like they were a hundred miles away. 

"Five minutes!" a conductor yelled from down the platform. "All aboard in five minutes!"

Luna sighed, resting her arms on the back of his wheelchair. "Well, it was nice while it lasted. Back into the metal tube we go."
