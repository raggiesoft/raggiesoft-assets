By the time the massive ARTS Coastliner finally screeched to a halt at Los Angeles Union Station, it was completely dark outside. The twelve-hour journey had drained whatever residual energy the pack had left. 

As the train doors hissed open, the cool, conditioned air of the cabin was immediately replaced by the heavy, warm, exhaust-choked night air of downtown LA. 

"Alright," Stella said, her voice tight with exhaustion as she hoisted her heavy duffel bag over her shoulder. "We are not out of the woods yet. Stay close, keep your bags secured, and don't lose sight of Leo."

Olivia deployed Leo's manual wheelchair on the platform, and he quickly transferred into it, his body stiff and aching from sitting all day. Luna dropped her heavy backpack onto the back of his chair, using it as an anchor.

They moved as a tight, cohesive unit through the massive, echoing transit hub. Union Station was beautiful, with its towering vaulted ceilings and art deco architecture, but it was also chaotic. The sheer volume of people, the echoing announcements, and the clattering of wheeled suitcases made Leo wince. He pulled his noise-canceling headphones tighter over his ears and kept his eyes focused squarely on the back of Olivia's shirt.

"Red Line," Stella muttered, staring at the overhead signs and pointing toward a long, sloping pedestrian tunnel. "We need the LATA Red Line subway."

They navigated the elevators down into the cavernous, dimly lit underground transit station. Unlike the relatively clean BART stations they had used in San Francisco, the LATA subway felt gritty and industrial.

When the silver subway train finally rattled into the station, the doors slid open to reveal a mostly empty car. They dragged their luggage inside, securing Leo's wheelchair near the doors. 

Luna slumped into a hard plastic subway seat, her eyes half-closed. "Tell me the hotel is right next to the station."

"It's a Voyage Inn Express about four blocks from the Hollywood/Highland stop," Stella said, looking at her printed transit map. "It's standard, not too expensive, and we have two queen beds for the four of us. Just like the Sovereign Regency, but without the luxury price tag."

"I don't care if it's a cardboard box," Olivia groaned, leaning her head back against the subway window. "As long as it's horizontal and I can close my eyes."

Leo gripped the wheels of his chair as the subway lurched forward, plunging deeper into the dark tunnels beneath Los Angeles. They were almost there.
