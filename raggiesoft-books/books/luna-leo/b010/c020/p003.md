The heavy, soundproof door of the Medical Pavilion closed, plunging the small clinical room into dim, safe quiet. But the noise inside the room didn't stop. 

Luna was curled into a tight ball on the pristine linoleum floor, her hands clamped over her ears as she sobbed hysterically. Leo was violently rocking back and forth in his wheelchair, letting out sharp, high-pitched vocalizations of pure distress. 

Stella and Olivia immediately dropped to the floor, ready to intervene. But as the seconds ticked by, a terrifying realization washed over both of them. 

They had both seen the twins go into autistic meltdowns before. It was a known, manageable part of their lives. But they had never seen *this*. They had never seen one twin's meltdown act as a catalyst for the other, trapping them both in a synchronized, escalating feedback loop of pure sensory overload. 

"Leo, it's okay, I'm here," Olivia pleaded, grabbing his hands to keep him from hitting his head. 

But Leo couldn't process her words. He could only hear Luna crying. And across the room, Luna couldn't hear Stella's soothing voice. She could only hear Leo screaming. 

"We can't stop it," Stella said, her voice shaking slightly as she wrapped her arms around Luna's trembling shoulders. She looked up at Olivia, her eyes wide with helplessness. "We just have to wait for the storm to blow itself out."

Olivia swallowed hard, her heart aching as she looked at Leo's tear-streaked face. "Okay," she whispered. "We hold on."

For the next forty-five minutes, that was all they could do. 

They rode out the storm. Stella held Luna tightly against her chest, providing constant, grounding deep pressure to the sobbing girl. Olivia knelt in front of Leo's wheelchair, holding his shaking hands, murmuring soft, repetitive reassurances that he couldn't hear, but hoping the vibration of her voice offered some comfort. 

About ten minutes into the meltdown, the door clicked open. The First Aid nurse quietly stepped inside. True to the park's strict medical protocol for sensory emergencies, she didn't try to intervene, speak, or offer unsolicited medical assessments. She simply placed a tray with four ice-cold bottles of water and a heavy, weighted medical blanket onto the counter, gave Stella a deeply sympathetic nod, and slipped back out to give them total privacy. 

Slowly, agonizingly, the storm began to pass. 

The sharp screams dissolved into exhausted, ragged breathing. The violent rocking slowed to a gentle, rhythmic sway. The uncontrollable sobbing faded into quiet, heavy hiccups. 

By the time the room finally fell silent, both twins were completely, utterly drained. Leo slumped forward in his wheelchair, his head resting heavily against Olivia's shoulder. Luna lay exhausted on the floor, her face buried in Stella's lap. 

The door clicked open again. The nurse peeked her head in, offering a warm, professional smile. 

"How are we doing?" she asked softly, keeping her voice low. 

"We're okay," Stella whispered, gently stroking Luna's messy hair. "Just exhausted."

"Take as much time as you need," the nurse assured them. "There's no rush to leave. Since you already have wheelchair access for the ride queues, I can authorize VIP seating for the shows and parades instead. It will give you a dedicated, uncrowded space so you don't have to fight the dense crowds. Or, if you feel it's best to head back to your hotel, I can arrange for a quiet, backstage transport vehicle to take you directly to your shuttle bus."

Stella looked at Olivia, then looked down at the exhausted twins. The day had barely started, and they were already completely shattered.
