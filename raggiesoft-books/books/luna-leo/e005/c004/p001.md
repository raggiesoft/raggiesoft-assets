## 4:00 PM — Difficult News

It was a Friday afternoon, the air buzzing slightly with the anticipation of the evening's home football game and halftime performance. Stella was making an early dinner, while Luna and Leo were likely running through music mentally or relaxing before the pre-game rush. Her maternal grandparents had stopped by for a visit, wanting to see the twins and wish them luck before heading to the stadium later themselves. Stella poured them coffee at the kitchen table.

There was a comfortable lull in the conversation about band preparations before Grandmother sighed softly, reaching across the table to place her hand gently over Stella's. "Stella, honey," she began, her voice quiet and laced with a familiar sadness, "there's something your grandfather and I need to tell you."

Stella looked up, meeting her grandmother's gaze, sensing the weight behind the words. "What is it, Grandma?"

"It's about your mother," Grandmother said gently. "We saw her doctor last week... well, we were listed as contacts since... since your father left. It's not good news, honey. The years of heavy drinking... and the smoking she took up after the divorce... it's done significant damage. Her liver, her lungs... the doctor says it's... very advanced. She doesn't have much longer."

Stella absorbed the news quietly, her expression somber but not shocked. It felt like the inevitable conclusion to a long, self-destructive path. There was a pang of sadness, not for the relationship they had, but perhaps for the one they never could have had.

Grandfather cleared his throat. "We visited her briefly," he added, his voice gruff with emotion. "Tried to talk to her. But..." He trailed off, shaking his head slightly.

"She's still the same, Stella," Grandmother finished sadly. "Still blaming everyone else – us, your father, you... for everything. No acceptance of her own part in... in any of it. Still insists she was a good mother."

Stella nodded slowly, a deep weariness settling over her. "I'm sorry you both have to deal with that, Grandma, Grandpa," she said sincerely. "Truly."

There was a moment of silence, then Grandmother asked gently, "We know there's the restraining order... but given the circumstances... have you thought about...?"

Stella knew what she was asking. She met her grandmother's gaze directly, her own expression clear and resolved, though not unkind. "No, Grandma," she said softly but firmly. "Thank you for telling me, I appreciate knowing. But no. I won't be going to see her. Nothing has changed. She hasn't changed. Seeing her now... it wouldn't bring closure, it wouldn't heal anything. It would just reopen wounds for me, and potentially for Luna and Leo down the line. She made her choices, over and over again. We made ours – to build a safe life away from that."

Her grandfather reached over and squeezed her hand. "We understand, Stella. Completely. We don't blame you one bit."

"We just had to let you know," Grandmother added quietly. "Whatever peace you can find with this, honey, you hold onto it. You've earned it."

Stella gave them a small, grateful smile. It was sad news, the end of a difficult chapter finally approaching, but her path forward, alongside Luna and Leo, remained clear. There would be no deathbed reconciliations built on denial. Their life was here now, built on truth and mutual support.

## 5:00 PM — The Final Phone Call

The phone call came on a Tuesday evening. Stella answered, recognizing her grandmother's number.

"Stella, honey," her grandmother's voice was heavy, weary. "It's... it's over. Your mother passed away peacefully about an hour ago. It was... faster than the doctors predicted."

Stella closed her eyes for a moment, leaning against the kitchen counter. There wasn't a surge of grief, more a quiet, profound sense of an ending. A final, irrevocable closing of a painful chapter. "Okay, Grandma," she said softly. "Thank you for letting me know."

"She... she didn't change her mind about anything, Stella," her grandmother added sadly. "Right up to the end, she... well. She was herself."

"I know," Stella replied, the resignation clear in her voice. "I didn't expect she would." There was a pause. "Are you and Grandpa okay?"

"We'll be alright, dear. We knew this was coming. We'll handle the... arrangements. You don't need to worry about any of that."

"Thank you, Grandma. Really." Stella's decision, made weeks ago when she first heard the prognosis, remained firm. There would be no visits, no deathbed vigil, no funeral attendance. Her mother's refusal to ever acknowledge the harm she'd caused had severed that possibility long ago. "Please take care of yourselves."

After hanging up, Stella took a few deep breaths before going into the living room where Luna and Leo were working side-by-side at their MagnaByte Opus stations, the familiar click-clack of the keyboards filling the otherwise quiet room. They looked up as she entered.

Stella sat down on the sofa nearby, gathering her thoughts. She needed to tell them directly. "Guys," she began gently, waiting until they both gave her their full attention, turning slightly from their screens. "I just got off the phone with Grandma."

She saw their slight tensing, associating calls from the grandparents sometimes with news of the past. "Grandma called to let me know that... that Mom passed away this evening."

She watched them closely. There were no tears, no gasps of shock. Luna simply nodded slowly, her expression somber but calm, absorbing the information. Leo looked at Luna, then back at Stella, his face unreadable for a moment before he gave a single, almost imperceptible nod. They understood death; they had processed the finality of the separation long ago, thanks to the restraining order and their mother's continued absence and silence.

"Are you okay, Stella?" Luna asked quietly, her concern directed towards her sister. Leo echoed the sentiment with a questioning look.

Stella managed a small smile. "I'm okay, guys. Sad in a way, maybe, for what could have been, but... okay. We're okay." She hesitated, then asked gently, "How are you two feeling?"

Luna shrugged slightly. "Okay," she repeated Luna's word, perhaps the only one that fit. It wasn't grief, but simply the acknowledgment of a fact, the confirmation of an ending. Leo reached over and patted Luna's hand, then looked back at Stella and gave a thumbs-up, not a happy one, but one signifying acceptance and readiness to move on. They weren't sorry she was gone; they were simply acknowledging she was no longer a factor, a distant source of pain finally silenced.

"Alright," Stella said, accepting their quiet strength. "Grandma and Grandpa are handling everything. We don't need to do anything. We just... keep going." She stood up. "Homework finished?"

They both nodded, turning back to their computers, the quiet click-clack resuming, a familiar rhythm anchoring them in the safe, stable present they had built together, away from the chaos that had finally consumed its source.
