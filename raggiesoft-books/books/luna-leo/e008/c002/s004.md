After finishing their lunch at Rocket Burger, Stella called for a tactical break. "Okay, team," she announced, "everyone use the restroom before we leave."

They found a facility with three doors: a men's, a women's, and a single, accessible family restroom. The plan was immediate and unspoken. "Luna and I will use the women's," Stella said. "Liv, you and Leo take the family restroom. We'll meet back at this bench."

Inside the spacious family restroom, the heavy door clicked shut, plunging the small room into a sudden, insulated quiet. The constant, echoing hum of the museum crowds vanished. It was in this abrupt silence, alone with the one person he trusted implicitly with his most vulnerable self, that the brave facade Leo had been maintaining all morning finally fractured.

The joy of the exhibits, the thrill of the new city—it had all been a desperate, exhausting performance to pretend this trip wasn’t just a beautiful ticking clock. But in the quiet isolation of the tiled room, the crushing reality of Olivia's impending departure fell upon him with a suffocating physical weight. It was too much to hold back anymore.

A ragged, choking gasp escaped him as his chest heaved. He didn't scream or thrash; instead, the grief manifested as a profound, overwhelming system failure. His body began to tremble violently, a deep, silent tremor that shook his entire frame. Tears spilled over his glasses, hot and fast, as he squeezed his eyes shut, trying to physically brace against the agonizing sorrow of losing his best friend. He couldn't articulate the depth of the void opening up inside him; his nervous system simply redlined under the emotional load.

Olivia didn't hesitate. She knew, with a gut-wrenching, absolute certainty, what this was about. This wasn't a sensory overload from the museum. This was Seattle. This was *her*. The silent agony radiating from him was a direct reflection of the goodbye he couldn't bear to face. She knew perfectly well that there was nothing she could say or do to erase the pain—she was the source of it.

"Hey, hey, I've got you," she said, her voice a low, steady anchor in the storm. She immediately knelt on the tile floor in front of his chair, ignoring the cold surface, and pulled him forward into a fierce, protective embrace. She wrapped her arms securely around his back, pressing her forehead against his shoulder.

"I've got you, Leo. I'm right here," she murmured softly into his ear, her own body providing the deep physical pressure he needed to stay tethered. "Just breathe with me. You're safe. I'm not leaving you in this room. I'm right here. Right now."

The subtle truth in her words was everything. She wasn't making empty promises about the future; she was promising *now*. He let out a long, shuddering breath, the tension leaving his shoulders as he collapsed against her. The violent trembling slowly subsided into quiet, heavy sobs. He clung to the fabric of her shirt, his anchor in a sea of overwhelming grief, holding onto the present moment she had promised him.

As she held him tightly, feeling his tears soak into her collar, the full weight of his agony settled like lead into her own heart. The seeds of a profound, terrible doubt began to take root in her mind. *Is this worth it?* she thought, her own tears now silently falling. *Is starting a new life at Northwest Pacific University worth breaking his heart? Is this the right choice?*

When she finally pulled back slightly to check on him, his face was tear-streaked and pale, but the overwhelming panic in his eyes had receded, replaced by a raw, heartbreaking exhaustion. He looked at her with a vulnerability that required no words.

When they emerged a few minutes later, Stella and Luna were waiting by the bench. They took one look at Leo's exhausted face and Olivia's own red-rimmed eyes and knew instantly what had transpired. No questions were asked. Stella simply reached out and squeezed Olivia's shoulder—a gesture of profound, unspoken gratitude for carrying him through it. Luna moved to her brother's other side, her hand finding his. The fortress wordlessly closed ranks, stronger and more necessary than ever.
