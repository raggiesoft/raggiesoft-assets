After the devastating realization at the river's edge, Leo was physically and emotionally spent. The walk back from Point State Park was a blur. Olivia pushed his chair, her hands steady and reassuring, while Luna and Stella flanked them, creating a tight physical barrier between Leo and the busy downtown sidewalks. 

They didn't have far to go. Downtown Pittsburgh—the "Golden Triangle"—was incredibly compact. In less than fifteen minutes, they were back in the quiet, air-conditioned lobby of The Three Rivers Grand Hotel. 

This was where Stella’s meticulous, fiercely protective planning revealed its true brilliance. A standard hotel checkout was at 11:00 AM. If they had followed the rules, they would be sitting in the lobby right now with all their luggage, staring down another nine hours before their midnight train. 

But Stella had simply bought another night. She had explicitly noted to the front desk that she was paying for a full extra night just so they could have a private, accessible sanctuary to crash in until 10:30 PM. The front desk staff, charmed by her upfront honesty and perhaps impressed by her unwavering dedication to her family, had gladly extended their keys.

As soon as the hotel room door clicked shut behind them, the outside world ceased to exist. 

Stella closed the heavy blackout curtains, plunging the room into cool, soothing darkness. Luna quietly turned down the blankets on Leo and Olivia's queen bed. Without a word, Olivia helped Leo transfer out of his chair and onto the mattress. 

He curled up on his side, burying his face in the pillows. The sensory deprivation of the dark, quiet room was an immediate balm to his overloaded nervous system. Olivia climbed into the bed beside him, wrapping her arms around him and pulling him flush against her chest. She provided the heavy, consistent deep pressure he needed, anchoring him to the mattress. 

"I've got you," she whispered into the dark. "Just rest."

For the next six hours, the hotel room was a tomb of recovery. Stella and Luna ordered quiet room service, eating by the dim light of the bathroom door so as not to disturb Leo. They read books and spoke in hushed whispers. There were no expectations, no masks to maintain, no ticking clocks to watch. There was only the fortress, doing what it did best.

By 10:00 PM, Leo had recovered enough spoons to sit up and eat a few bites of cold toast. The crushing panic of the afternoon had ebbed into a dull, manageable ache. He was still heartbroken about the journey west, but the intense physical paralysis had passed. 

"Alright, team," Stella said softly, checking her watch. "It's time. The station is only a five-minute bus ride from here. Let's get packed up."

They moved with practiced efficiency. By 10:45 PM, they had checked out and were standing at the 3RTA bus stop outside the hotel. The city was quiet, the streets mostly empty under the glow of the streetlights. 

A short bus ride dropped them off at Pittsburgh Union Station. It was a beautiful, historic building, but completely deserted at this hour, echoing with the soft hum of the ticketing kiosks. They navigated their way to the platform, the cool midnight air a stark contrast to the muggy afternoon. 

At exactly 11:45 PM, the massive silver Superliner cars of the ARTS *Capitol Limited* rolled into the station. 

Booking accommodations on the train had been a calculated compromise by Stella. Real-world ARTS wheelchair-accessible sleeper bedrooms only held two people. There was absolutely no way Leo would ever accept being separated from the rest of the pack in a different room. Therefore, Stella had deliberately booked the "Family Bedroom" on the lower level. It spanned the entire width of the train car and had enough beds to sleep all four of them together, but it came with a massive drawback: it was not wheelchair accessible, and it did not have an en-suite bathroom. 

They had to rely entirely on brute strength and zero modesty. While Stella and Luna normally had to work together to lift Leo, Olivia was incredibly strong. She effortlessly deadlifted Leo out of his manual chair and carried him straight down the public hallway to the tiny communal combination toilet-and-shower room. 

Because the door opened into the public corridor, it had to be firmly locked shut. This meant Olivia, Stella, and Leo were all crammed together inside a space barely larger than a closet. It was a chaotic, physically demanding process. They all stripped down so Stella could wash Leo, carefully maneuvering around the tiny sink and toilet, before Stella and Olivia quickly showered themselves. 

It was cramped and far from glamorous, but it meant they all got to sleep in the same room. As the train pulled out of Pittsburgh, heading into the dark expanse of Ohio and Indiana, Leo lay in the lower bunk of the Family Bedroom. The rhythmic rocking of the train was instantly soothing. He was heading the wrong way, deeper into the Midwest, but as Olivia’s hand reached down from the bunk above him to brush against his arm, he closed his eyes and let the train carry him into the night.
