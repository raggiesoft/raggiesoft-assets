## 8:30 AM — The BRAT Diet

The next morning, the heavy curtains of their room at The Heritage House were pulled back to reveal a bright, cloudless Chicago day. But inside the room, the pace was significantly slower than the morning before. 

Stella was sitting propped up against the headboard, looking a little better but still noticeably fragile. The color hadn't quite returned to her cheeks, and she was moving with a stiff, careful deliberation to avoid aggravating her sore abdominal muscles. Luna was sitting at the foot of the bed, keeping a watchful, protective eye on her older sister.

The heavy hotel door clicked open, and Olivia backed into the room, pulling Leo's wheelchair inside. She had understood implicitly that Leo didn't want to be separated from her for even a ten-minute errand, so they had gone down to the lobby together while Luna stayed upstairs. 

Olivia set a plastic bag from a nearby convenience store down on the desk and began pulling items out with the brisk, no-nonsense efficiency of a triage nurse. 

"Alright, Stella. Breakfast is served," Olivia announced. She handed Stella a bruised yellow banana, a small plastic cup of unsweetened applesauce, and two slices of dry, unbuttered toast they had snagged from the hotel’s continental breakfast downstairs. 

Stella looked at the grim spread, her nose wrinkling in distaste. "Olivia, I'm feeling better. I don't need to eat like a toddler."

"You threw up yesterday," Olivia countered, crossing her arms over her chest and raising a single, challenging eyebrow. "You pushed your body past its lactose breaking point. You are on the BRAT diet today. Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, and Toast. Non-negotiable."

"I am the adult here," Stella muttered, though she didn't make any move to put the banana down. 

"And I'm the one who usually carries Leo when things go sideways, which means my vote counts double," Olivia replied smoothly. "Eat the toast, Stel. It's for the best."

Luna, who was happily munching on a chocolate chip muffin on the other bed, giggled. "Just listen to Dr. Olivia, Stella. You're the one who always tells us to respect our biological limits."

Stella let out a long, defeated sigh and picked up the dry toast. "Fine. But I am filing a formal grievance against Chicago's culinary scene."

Leo smiled from his wheelchair, his communicator already swung into place. <aac>What are we doing today?</aac> he typed, hitting the play button. 

"Well," Stella said, taking a cautious, joyless bite of her toast. "We are definitely not going anywhere high up. And given that my stomach is still sore, we are not doing anything that requires running or a massive amount of walking. Luckily, The Heritage House is right in the middle of everything."

She pulled a city map from the nightstand. "Right across the street is Lakeside Park. It's flat, it's on the ground, and it's right on the edge of Lake Michigan. We can walk down to the Grand Fountain, and if we're feeling up to it, the Lakefront Aquarium is just south of there. It's fully wheelchair accessible, completely air-conditioned, and has lots of fish. Does that sound safe and reasonable to everyone?"

<aac>Fish are good,</aac> Leo typed immediately. The aquarium sounded perfectly safe. It was on the ground, it was indoors, and there were zero glass boxes suspended in the sky. 

"I'm in," Luna agreed, finishing her muffin. "But if you need to stop and sit down, Stella, you have to tell us."

"Deal," Stella said, looking mournfully at her applesauce. "As soon as I finish this incredibly depressing breakfast, we'll head out."

***

## 10:00 AM — Fountains and Fishes

The morning sun reflected brilliantly off the surface of Lake Michigan as the pack slowly made their way through Lakeside Park. After the terrifying verticality of the Zenith Tower, the sprawling, manicured green space was exactly the kind of massive, horizontal grounding the twins needed. 

They started at the Grand Fountain, a colossal, ornate water feature that shot massive jets of water high into the air. Leo loved the thundering sound of the water crashing back into the tiered marble basins. It was chaotic but rhythmic, a predictable kind of sensory input that felt safe. 

"Okay, this is infinitely better than looking down from a glass box," Luna declared, sitting on the stone retaining wall and letting the fine, cool mist spray across her face. 

"Agreed," Stella said, sitting down heavily next to her. She was moving at half her usual speed. 

From the fountain, it was a short, paved walk south to the Lakefront Aquarium. The massive, classical stone building sat right on the edge of the water. For the next two hours, they wandered through the cool, dimly lit, air-conditioned halls. 

For Leo, the aquarium was a sanctuary. The slow, graceful movements of the stingrays, the mesmerizing glow of the jellyfish tanks, and the quiet, bubbling hum of the massive filtration systems were incredibly soothing. He sat parked in front of a massive, multi-story Caribbean reef tank, completely captivated by a sea turtle lazily swimming past the glass. 

Olivia stood right behind his chair, resting her hands on the push-handles. Every so often, she would lean down and point out a particularly colorful fish, her voice a low, comforting murmur in his ear. 

"I like the turtle," she whispered. "He looks like he's got nowhere to be."

<aac>He is very relaxed,</aac> Leo typed back, a small smile on his face. He leaned his head back against her arm, perfectly content. 

A few yards away, Stella was sitting on a padded bench in front of a penguin exhibit. She had her elbows resting on her knees, rubbing her temples. Luna was sitting next to her, watching her older sister with a hawk-like intensity. 

"You're done, aren't you?" Luna asked quietly. 

Stella let out a long, exhausted sigh. "I think the guardian bear needs to tap out. My stomach isn't actively hurting anymore, but my energy reserves are completely shot. I am running on fumes and applesauce."

She pulled up the sleeve of her jacket and checked her watch. It was 12:15 PM. 

"Alright," Stella announced, raising her voice slightly to get Olivia and Leo's attention. "We need to wrap this up. We have to grab our bags from the hotel bell desk and get to Union Station."

"When does our train leave?" Olivia asked, turning Leo's chair away from the reef tank. 

"The *Western Pioneer* departs exactly at 2:00 PM," Stella said, slowly pushing herself up from the bench. "And since I am moving at the speed of a wounded turtle today, we need to leave plenty of buffer time."

"Next stop: Denver," Luna said, a hint of excitement returning to her voice. 

The mention of the next city sent a familiar, dull ache through Leo's chest. Chicago had been chaotic and exhausting, but leaving it meant boarding another train. It meant moving further West. The clock was still ticking, and with every mile they covered on the *Western Pioneer*, Olivia’s departure in Seattle drew closer. 

But as Olivia pushed his wheelchair toward the exit, she squeezed his shoulder. He reached up and patted her hand. They were still together. The clock was ticking, but the alarm hadn't gone off yet.

***

## 1:30 PM — Boarding the Pioneer

Chicago Union Station was a cavernous, echoing cathedral of transit. Even with the mid-day crowds, the sheer scale of the Great Hall made the pack feel small. 

They retrieved their bags from The Heritage House bell desk and made their way to the boarding platforms. Stella was moving slowly, her face pale, letting Olivia take the lead. The deep dish pizza incident had completely depleted her energy reserves, and she was currently operating on pure stubborn willpower. 

"Okay," Olivia said, turning to face the pack as they waited near the track for the *Western Pioneer*. She had naturally slipped into the commander role. "Stella, your only job is to get on the train and sleep. I've got the bags, I've got Leo, and I've got the elevation protocols."

"Elevation protocols?" Luna asked, tilting her head. 

"We are heading to Denver," Olivia explained, slipping effortlessly into "triage nurse" mode. "It's the Mile High City. We are currently sitting at sea level. By tomorrow morning, we'll be at five thousand, two hundred and eighty feet. That means less oxygen, which means we all need to be aggressively hydrating starting right now, and we need to pace ourselves when we get there."

Leo listened carefully. His cardiovascular system had never been tested at a high altitude before. The prospect of thin air was slightly intimidating, but hearing Olivia take charge of the medical logistics was incredibly reassuring. 

<aac>Hydrate,</aac> his communicator stated, and he obediently reached for the water bottle attached to the frame of his wheelchair. 

"Exactly," Olivia smiled, giving his shoulder an affectionate squeeze. "Drink up. The air on the train is dry anyway."

At 1:30 PM, the boarding call for the sleeper cars echoed through the station. 

Just like their journey from Pittsburgh, Stella had booked the Family Bedroom on the lower level of the sleeper car. The real-world logistics of rail travel meant it was the only room large enough to keep the four of them together, even though it wasn't officially wheelchair accessible and lacked a private bathroom. 

Once they navigated the narrow hallway, the brutal, cramped reality of the Family Bedroom became apparent. To get all four of them in one room, sacrifices had to be made. 

Stella, pale and clutching her stomach, immediately called dibs on the Adult Upper bunk. It was narrow, but it was six feet long, allowing her to stretch out her cramping abdomen. She climbed the small ladder, kicked off her shoes, and pulled a blanket over her head. "Goodnight, Chicago," her muffled voice came from above. "Wake me up when we hit the mountains."

"Get some rest, Stel," Olivia murmured, turning to the remaining beds. 

Luna looked at the remaining two beds on the opposite wall: a tiny upper and lower bunk meant for small children, both under five feet long. With a resigned sigh, Luna claimed the 4'9" Child Lower bunk, immediately drawing her knees to her chest and curling into a tight fetal position to fit. 

That left the Adult Lower bunk. At 40 inches wide, it was exactly the size of a standard twin bed. It was technically meant for one adult, but it was the only option left. 

Olivia effortlessly lifted Leo from his wheelchair and settled him into the Adult Lower bed, then squeezed in right beside him, her back pressed against the wall. She wrapped her long arms around him, pulling him tightly against her chest to keep him from rolling off the narrow mattress. 

Normally, Leo loved this kind of extreme closeness and deep pressure from any member of his pack. But right now, he specifically wanted Olivia. He didn't know if he would ever get to see her again after this summer. The ticking clock was a constant, terrifying drumbeat in his mind, and being held this tightly by her was the only way to silence it. 

Because Stella was out of commission in the top bunk, the physical burden of the journey had shifted entirely to Olivia's athletic shoulders. She would be the one carrying him down the hall to the tiny, cramped shower room tonight. She would be the one lifting him back into his chair tomorrow morning. 

It was a stark, unignorable preview of what was going to happen on the return trip. When they boarded the train in Seattle to head back East, Olivia wouldn't be with them. And if Stella or Luna were compromised, the entire caregiving infrastructure of their fortress would instantly collapse. 

A sharp spike of anxiety hit his chest, completely unrelated to the impending altitude change. He pushed the terrifying thought away, forcing himself to focus on the gentle hum of the train's massive diesel engines spinning up. 

At exactly 2:00 PM, a long, low horn blast echoed through the station. The *Western Pioneer* lurched forward, slowly rolling out of the dark station and into the bright Midwest afternoon. The great journey West had resumed.

***

## 8:00 PM — Cramped Quarters

By 8:00 PM, the *Western Pioneer* was rolling steadily through the darkening cornfields of Iowa. Inside the Family Bedroom, the air was close and warm. Stella was still fast asleep in the Adult Upper bunk, having barely moved since they left Chicago. 

"Alright," Olivia said, sitting up carefully so she wouldn't bump her head on the low ceiling. "Bathroom and shower time. Let's get this logistical nightmare over with."

Luna enthusiastically unfolded herself from the Child Lower bunk, wincing as her joints popped. "Yes, please. If I stay in the fetal position any longer, I'm going to permanently fuse into a ball."

Because their cabin didn't have an en-suite, the evening routine required a public excursion down the narrow hallway of the lower level to the communal combination toilet-and-shower room. 

Olivia effortlessly deadlifted Leo out of the narrow bed and carried him out the cabin door. Luna followed closely behind, carrying the clean clothes and the shower bag. The rocking of the train made the walk down the corridor a wobbly, bouncing affair. 

When they reached the communal bathroom, the three of them crowded inside and locked the door behind them. The space was incredibly cramped—barely larger than a standard closet—with a toilet on one side and a tiny shower nozzle on the other. Because the door opened directly into the public hallway, there was no option to leave it propped open for space. They had to make it work with the door shut. 

Olivia carefully undressed Leo and lowered him onto the small, fold-down plastic shower seat. Once he was secure, the two girls began undressing to take their own showers and use the facilities, navigating the tiny space with the casual, practiced modesty of the pack. 

Luna grabbed the sponge and the body wash, stepping into the narrow spray of the shower to begin washing her brother. Olivia stood nearby, bracing herself against the fiberglass wall as the train swayed, waiting to help rinse him. 

"Make sure you get his back first, it's easier while he's sitting up straight," Olivia suggested casually. 

Luna paused, the sponge hovering over Leo's shoulder. She looked up at Olivia, her mouth opening as if she was about to correct her, to explain the exact, methodical routine that she and Stella had perfected over the years. She wanted to say: *No, you always start with his arms so you don't overstimulate his core too quickly.* She wanted to teach Olivia how to do it perfectly. 

But the words died in her throat. 

Leo watched his twin's face fall. He saw the sudden, sharp heartbreak in her eyes as the realization hit her. There was no point in teaching Olivia the routine. There was no point in passing on the intricate, specialized knowledge of how to bathe him, because in just a few days, Olivia was going to get off a train in Seattle and never come back. 

Luna swallowed hard, looking quickly down at the sponge so Olivia wouldn't see the tears welling in her eyes. "Right. The back," she whispered, her voice thick with unshed emotion. 

Leo felt a heavy, crushing ache in his own chest. He reached out a wet hand and gently grasped Luna's wrist. He couldn't speak, but he squeezed her wrist twice—their silent twin code for *I know. I feel it too.*

Luna gave him a watery, devastated smile, gently resting her forehead against his wet shoulder for just a second before she resumed washing him. 

Olivia, blissfully unaware of the silent heartbreak passing between the twins, hummed quietly over the rumble of the train tracks. They finished the cramped, chaotic showering process in silence, toweling off and getting dressed in the tiny space before Olivia lifted Leo once more to carry him back to their room.

***

## 9:00 PM — Crossing the Plains

When Olivia carried Leo back into the Family Bedroom, the small cabin felt noticeably different. The heavy, oppressive silence of Stella's sleep had lifted. 

Stella was sitting up in the Adult Upper bunk, her legs dangling over the edge. She still looked tired, but the pallor had faded from her cheeks. 

"Look who's alive," Luna said, tossing the damp shower bag into the small closet and sinking gratefully back onto her tiny mattress. 

"I am resurrected," Stella declared, her voice still a bit gravelly from sleep. She rubbed her stomach carefully. "The nausea is completely gone. My abdominal muscles feel like I did a thousand crunches, but I think the cheese has finally vacated the premises."

"Good, because you need to eat," Olivia said, gently depositing Leo back into his spot on the lower bunk. She reached into her own duffel bag and pulled out a sleeve of saltine crackers and a bottle of ginger ale she had bought before they boarded. "You're off the BRAT diet, but you're on the cracker protocol tonight. Small bites. We need to get some calories in you."

Stella didn't argue. She accepted the crackers and the soda, taking a cautious, grateful bite. "Thank you, Liv. Honestly, you've been a lifesaver today."

Olivia waved off the compliment, unscrewing the cap of a large water bottle. "Don't thank me yet. I'm about to become a tyrant again. Where are we?"

Luna leaned over and peered out the window into the pitch-black night. Occasional lights from distant farmhouses whipped past the glass. "I think we're almost to Omaha. We're about to cross into Nebraska."

"Perfect," Olivia said, handing the water bottle to Luna, then pulling out a second one with a straw for Leo. "We are about to start a massive, steady climb. Omaha is barely a thousand feet above sea level. By the time we wake up tomorrow, we'll be in Denver at over five thousand feet. I need everyone to drink a full bottle of water before we go to sleep to prep for the altitude."

Leo gladly took the straw. He always found it easier to sleep when he felt like there was a structured plan in place. Olivia’s clinical, take-charge attitude was exactly what the pack needed with Stella recovering. 

Up in the top bunk, Stella munched on her crackers, watching Olivia manage her siblings with a quiet, appreciative smile. She knew she could always rely on Luna for emotional support and meticulous routine management, but Olivia had the raw physical strength and the unshakeable, practical endurance that kept the fortress moving when things broke down. 

"Drink up, Stella," Olivia ordered, pointing a finger up at the top bunk. "You lost a lot of fluids yesterday. And the air on this train is only going to get thinner."

"Yes, ma'am," Stella chuckled, taking a sip of her ginger ale. 

The mood in the cabin had noticeably lightened. With everyone clean, dressed in soft clothes, and actively preparing for the geographic shift, the cramped dimensions of the Family Bedroom felt less like a prison and more like a cozy, rolling bunker. 

Leo settled deeper into his pillows on the Adult Lower bunk. A few minutes later, Olivia climbed in beside him, maneuvering her tall frame into the narrow space between him and the wall. She wrapped an arm over his chest, her steady breathing syncing up with the rhythmic *clack-clack-clack* of the train wheels against the steel tracks. 

Outside the window, the flat, dark expanse of the Great Plains rushed by as the *Western Pioneer* began its long, invisible climb into the Rocky Mountains.
