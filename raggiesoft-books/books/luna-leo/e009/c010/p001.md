## 6:30 AM — The Mile High City

Sometime during the deep, pitch-black hours of the night, while the *Western Pioneer* rattled across the high plains of western Nebraska, the train silently crossed a geographical threshold. They had officially left the Central Time Zone and entered Mountain Time. 

When Leo woke up in the cramped, narrow confines of the Adult Lower bunk, the first rays of morning light were just beginning to filter through the curtains. He was still wrapped tightly in Olivia's arms, her even breathing a steady rhythm against his back. 

He blinked, reaching awkwardly with a contracted arm to tap the button on his digital watch. The screen illuminated in the dim cabin: **8:30 AM**. 

Because it was a standard 2003 digital watch, it hadn't automatically updated. It was still anchored to the time in Virginia Beach. 

Leo stared at the glowing green numbers, doing the math in his head. They had crossed into Mountain Time overnight. It was only 6:30 AM locally. 

A sharp, familiar pang of anxiety immediately flared in his chest. Looking at the literal, mathematical difference between where he was and where he lived was a geographical reality check. It was undeniable proof of exactly how far West they had traveled, and a terrifying reminder that the Pacific Time Zone—and Olivia's departure—was looming just beyond the mountains. 

He let out a quiet, shuddering breath, trying to push the panic down. He shifted slightly, and Olivia's arms instantly tightened around him in her sleep, a subconscious, protective reflex. The deep pressure helped ground him, keeping the timezone anxiety at a manageable simmer. 

"Morning," a soft, raspy voice whispered from the top bunk. 

Leo looked up. Stella was peering over the edge of her mattress, her hair a messy bird's nest. She looked significantly better than she had the night before. The pallor was completely gone, and her eyes were bright and alert. The long, horizontal stretch of the Adult Upper bunk had done exactly what she needed it to do. 

<aac>How is your stomach,</aac> Leo typed on his communicator, making sure the volume was turned down low so he wouldn't wake Luna, who was still curled tightly in her fetal position in the child's bed across the aisle. 

"A hundred percent better," Stella whispered, carefully climbing down the small ladder. "The guardian bear has officially resumed her post."

She stretched her arms over her head, cracking her back, then reached over to peel back the heavy curtain covering the large window. 

The view outside was staggering. 

The flat, endless expanse of the Great Plains was gone. In its place, rising like a massive, impenetrable wall of dark rock and snow-capped peaks against the western horizon, were the Rocky Mountains. They were colossal, beautiful, and incredibly intimidating. 

"Wow," Stella breathed, staring out the window as the train began to slow. The sprawling suburban grid of Denver was beginning to rise up around the tracks, nestled right against the foothills of the mountain range. 

Olivia stirred behind Leo, letting out a sleepy yawn. She blinked her green eyes open, resting her chin on his shoulder to look out the window. "We made it," she murmured, her voice rough with sleep. "Denver."

"And we're at five thousand feet," Stella noted, rolling her shoulders. "I can definitely feel the air is thinner. It feels crisp."

Luna groaned from the tiny Child Lower bunk, slowly uncurling her stiff limbs. "I feel like a pretzel," she complained, rubbing her lower back as she sat up. "I don't think my spine is supposed to bend that way for eight hours."

"We'll get you stretched out as soon as we're off the train, Luna," Stella promised, reaching over to playfully ruffle her sister's messy hair. "We've got a two-day layover here. Lots of time to stretch."

A low chime echoed through the train's PA system, followed by the conductor's voice. *"Good morning, passengers. We are currently on approach to Denver Union Station. We anticipate an on-time arrival at 7:15 AM. Please make sure you have all your belongings..."*

The pack moved into action. The overnight train ride had been cramped, logistically brutal, and emotionally taxing, but they had survived it. Stella was back to full health, the twins had survived the altitude climb in their sleep, and the majestic, imposing wall of the Rocky Mountains was waiting for them just outside the station doors. 

Leo watched the mountains draw closer, the sheer scale of them making him feel incredibly small. The great journey West was officially entering its most beautiful, and most daunting, phase.

## 7:30 AM — Denver Regional Transit

Stepping off the *Western Pioneer* onto the concrete platform of Denver Union Station was a physical shock to the system. 

The air was noticeably thinner, incredibly crisp, and lacked the heavy, suffocating humidity they were used to on the East Coast. It felt clean, pulling sharply into their lungs with every breath. 

"Okay," Stella said, pausing on the platform and taking a deep breath of the mountain air. "I feel completely revived. The air here is amazing."

"It's dry," Luna noted, immediately digging into her duffel bag for a tube of chapstick. "But I'll take this over Chicago humidity any day."

Olivia pushed Leo's wheelchair along the platform, easily keeping pace with the flow of departing passengers. "So, what's the logistical plan, Commander? Do we have a shuttle?"

"Better," Stella grinned, pointing toward the end of the station complex. "We have the DRT."

The Denver Regional Transit (DRT) system was a sprawling network of buses and light rail trains that crisscrossed the city. More importantly, it was fully wheelchair accessible. 

For Leo, the prospect of taking a local commuter train was thrilling. It combined two of his favorite things: predictable rail travel and an environment built to accommodate his wheelchair without requiring a massive logistical struggle. 

They followed the clear signage out of the historic train station and over to the modern light rail platform. The morning commuter rush was just starting to build, a mix of business professionals in suits and locals in outdoor gear. 

<aac>This is very convenient,</aac> Leo typed on his communicator as they waited by the bright yellow tactile strip on the edge of the platform. 

"It really is," Stella agreed, checking the transit map she had printed out before they left Virginia. "Our hotel is just a few stops away on the main line. We don't have to worry about finding an accessible cab, and we don't have to deal with loading and unloading all this luggage into a trunk."

A few minutes later, a sleek, modern light rail train glided into the station with a quiet, electric hum. The doors hissed open, perfectly level with the platform. 

Olivia pushed Leo straight on board, parking his wheelchair in the designated accessible space near the doors. She locked his brakes and grabbed a nearby handrail, while Stella and Luna piled the luggage into the adjacent row of seats. 

The train doors closed, and the DRT accelerated smoothly out of the station. Through the large windows, the sprawling cityscape of Denver rolled by, backed by the ever-present, majestic wall of the Rocky Mountains. 

Leo watched the mountains pass, feeling the steady, rhythmic vibration of the light rail train beneath his wheels. The thin air was exhilarating, the transit system was flawless, and for the first time since they left Pittsburgh, the heavy dread of the ticking clock was pushed aside by genuine, unbridled excitement for the new city.

## 8:00 AM — The Horizon Suites

The DRT light rail carried them smoothly out of the downtown core and into a quieter commercial district. They got off at a modern, concrete station platform and rolled two blocks down the sidewalk to their destination: The Horizon Suites. 

After the opulent, historic grandeur of the Chicago hotel, The Horizon Suites was refreshingly mundane. It was a mid-tier budget hotel, the kind of modern, cookie-cutter building favored by traveling salespeople and frugal road-trippers. 

"I know it's not exactly five stars," Stella said, pushing open the glass front doors to the lobby. "But after I paid Chicago prices for deep dish pizza and Skydeck tickets, the fortress budget needed a breather."

"It's perfect," Olivia said, looking around the clean, brightly lit lobby. 

It wasn't luxurious, but it was top-of-the-line for a budget brand. Everything was new, the floors were spotless, and most importantly, the ADA accessibility was completely standardized. 

They checked in at the front desk and took the elevator up to the third floor. When Stella unlocked their room, they found a clean, spacious layout with two standard queen beds, a generic faux-wood desk, and a large, accessible bathroom equipped with grab bars and a roll-in shower. 

For Leo, the lack of historic charm was a massive feature, not a bug. Historic usually meant cramped, unpredictable, and full of architectural barriers. The Horizon Suites was predictable. He knew exactly where the light switches would be. He knew the bathroom would have a wide turning radius. The standardization was deeply soothing to his nervous system. 

"Okay, this is actually really nice," Luna said, dropping her duffel bag onto one of the beds and bouncing on the mattress to test it. "It's clean, and the TV is huge."

"And the bathroom is fully accessible," Olivia noted, peaking into the brightly lit washroom. "No more cramped ARTS closets for the next two days."

"Exactly," Stella smiled, dropping her own bags near the desk. She walked over to the window and pulled back the curtains. Their third-floor room didn't have a view of the mountains, but it looked out over a quiet, tree-lined street. "Alright, pack. We have a two-day layover. The first order of business is getting unpacked. The second order of business is taking a nap in a bed that isn't moving."

"And stretching," Luna groaned, rolling her shoulders. "I am going to do yoga on this carpet for an hour. My spine is still shaped like an ARTS child's bed."

Leo drove his wheelchair over to the window, parking next to Stella. The room felt incredibly safe. It was quiet, the air conditioning hummed with a steady white noise, and the deep pressure of the overnight train ride was fading. 

<aac>This is a good base camp,</aac> he typed on his communicator. 

"I'm glad you think so," Stella said, resting a hand affectionately on his shoulder. "We're going to take it easy today. We need to let our bodies adjust to the altitude before we do any serious sightseeing."

Leo nodded. He liked that plan. The ticking clock was still there, quietly counting down the hours until they had to board the train to California, but for now, they were safely anchored in a clean, quiet room a mile above sea level.

## 5:00 PM — The MallRide

By late afternoon, the pack had successfully napped, unpacked, and stretched out their various ARTS-induced aches and pains. Stella's stomach was officially back to normal, and she was eager to get them out of the hotel room to enjoy the famously crisp Colorado evening. 

"Okay, I have a plan," Stella announced, unfurling a tourist map on one of the queen beds. "It's a beautiful Friday evening. We are going to head downtown to a district called LoDo. We can ride the shuttle, find a quiet place for dinner, and just enjoy the ground-level mountain air."

"Sounds perfect," Olivia smiled, grabbing her light jacket. "How do we get there?"

"That's the best part," Stella grinned. "We take the Free MallRide."

Ten minutes later, they were back out on the street. The Free MallRide was a fleet of distinctive, low-floor shuttle buses that ran constantly up and down the 16th Street pedestrian mall. Because they were designed to be incredibly easy to board, Leo didn't even have to use a complicated mechanical lift. The bus simply kneeled, and Olivia rolled him right on board. 

The shuttle was pleasantly full of locals and tourists enjoying the summer evening. Leo parked his wheelchair in the center bay, enjoying the cool breeze blowing through the open windows of the bus as it crawled past shops, restaurants, and street performers. 

"This is infinitely better than navigating East Coast traffic," Stella said, holding onto a hand strap as the bus glided along the pavement. 

As they neared the Lower Downtown (LoDo) district, the massive, red-brick structure of Summit Stadium loomed into view at the end of the street. It was a beautiful, towering building designed to look like a classic, old-school ballpark. 

"Wow," Luna breathed, looking out the bus window at the massive floodlights. "It's huge."

"That's where the Denver Peaks play," Stella noted, referencing her map. "They're actually playing an away series in Pittsburgh this weekend. Which is ironic, since we just came from there. Otherwise, I might have suggested we go to a game."

Leo looked out the window at the sheer scale of the massive, empty stadium. A sudden, undeniable wave of relief washed over him. As he imagined what it would look like full—forty thousand screaming people, blaring PA systems, and suffocating crowds—he realized his carefully regulated nervous system would have absolutely short-circuited. 

He glanced over at Luna. She was staring at the stadium, too, her shoulders visibly relaxing as she came to the exact same realization. 

"You know what?" Luna said, a small, grateful smile playing on her lips. "I think I'm okay with the team being in Pittsburgh. Forty thousand people sounds like a sensory nightmare."

<aac>Agreed,</aac> Leo typed, hitting the play button emphatically. 

Olivia chuckled, patting Leo on the shoulder as the bus slowed for their stop. "Alright, no baseball for the fortress. Let's just go find some dinner."

## 6:00 PM — Bison and Oysters

The pack got off the Free MallRide in the heart of LoDo, the historic Lower Downtown district of Denver. The streets were lined with beautifully restored Victorian brick buildings that had been converted into lofts, galleries, and restaurants. 

"Okay, so what is traditional Colorado food?" Luna asked, looking around at the various awnings. "I feel like we should eat something authentic."

"Well, there's always Rocky Mountain Oysters," Olivia suggested, pointing to a rustic-looking tavern across the street with a menu posted in the window. 

Leo wrinkled his nose. He liked some seafood, but oysters seemed a bit slimy. <aac>Oysters in the mountains?</aac> he typed, eyeing Olivia skeptically. The ocean was a thousand miles away. 

Olivia grinned, a mischievous glint in her green eyes. "Oh, they aren't from the ocean, Leo. They're bull testicles. Deep fried."

Olivia meant it as a lighthearted, gross-out joke, expecting a theatrical gag from the twins. But she miscalculated. 

Because of his profound intellectual disability, Leo did not understand sex or reproductive anatomy. What he did understand—what he remembered with vivid, terrifying clarity—were the crude, vicious taunts of middle school bullies like Josh Riley. Boys who would corner him, using biological words like weapons to humiliate him and make him feel small, broken, and unsafe. 

Hearing that specific word didn't just gross him out; it triggered a deep, visceral spike of panic. 

Leo's hand dropped away from his communicator. His breathing hitched, shallow and fast. He stared at the tavern window, his eyes wide and unblinking, his body suddenly rigid as the phantom echoes of locker-room bullying flooded his nervous system. 

Olivia's smile vanished instantly. She saw his chest seizing, saw the raw, unadulterated fear replacing the curiosity in his eyes, and she immediately realized her mistake. 

"Hey. Hey, Leo, look at me," Olivia said, her voice dropping into a low, firm, incredibly gentle register. She stepped right in front of his wheelchair, blocking his view of the tavern window, and placed both of her strong hands firmly on his shoulders. 

He flinched slightly, but her grip was steady and safe. 

"I'm sorry," she said clearly, holding his gaze. "That was a stupid joke. We aren't eating that. We aren't going in there. You're safe. I've got you."

She applied deep, downward pressure on his shoulders, forcing his highly sensitive nervous system to process the grounding physical contact. 

Luna stepped up beside him, her own disgust instantly evaporating as she recognized her twin's distress. She placed a hand over his on the armrest. "We're going to get burgers," Luna promised softly. "Just normal, boring burgers."

Leo let out a long, shuddering breath, his chest rising and falling heavily as the panic slowly receded. He looked at Olivia's hands, then up at her face. There was no teasing there—only fierce, protective focus. He nodded once, slowly. 

"Okay," Stella said quietly, stepping in to cleanly break the tension. "I just survived the Chicago deep dish incident anyway. I am finding us a nice, normal burger place right now."

They eventually settled on a lively, accessible brewpub a few blocks down that specialized in another Colorado staple: Bison burgers. 

The pub had a wide, flat entrance and plenty of space between the heavy wooden tables, allowing Olivia to park Leo comfortably at the end of a booth. 

When the food arrived, it was hearty and deeply satisfying. The bison meat was leaner and slightly sweeter than regular beef, served on thick, toasted buns with crispy fries. Stella, still playing it safe with her stomach, had opted for a simple grilled chicken sandwich, but she looked genuinely happy as she ate. 

"This is amazing," Luna mumbled through a mouthful of bison burger. "Way better than the oysters."

Leo took a bite of the burger Olivia had cut up for him. It was delicious. The lively, conversational hum of the pub was a perfect volume—enough background noise to feel energetic, but nowhere near the overwhelming roar of a stadium. 

He looked around the table. Stella was laughing at a joke Olivia had just made, and Luna was happily dipping her fries in ketchup. The crisp mountain air blowing in through the open front doors felt clean and refreshing. 

The timezone shift was still a looming reality, and the ticking clock was still quietly counting down the days until Seattle. But here, eating bison burgers in a brick tavern in the shadow of the Rocky Mountains, the fortress felt incredibly strong. For the first time since they left Virginia, Leo felt like they were actually on an adventure, rather than just surviving a geographical relocation.
