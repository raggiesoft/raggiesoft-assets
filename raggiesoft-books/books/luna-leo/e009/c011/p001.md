## 10:00 AM — The Centennial Botanic Gardens

The next morning, Stella announced a strict, low-exertion itinerary. They had survived the timezone anxiety, the cramped ARTS closet, and the near-miss at Summit Stadium, but they still needed to let their bodies fully acclimate to the 5,280-foot elevation. 

"We are going to be outside, but we are not hiking," Stella declared, making sure everyone had a full bottle of water before they left the hotel room. "We are going to find flat ground, paved paths, and lots of shade. We are going to the Centennial Botanic Gardens."

After another easy ride on the DRT, the pack arrived at the massive, sprawling urban oasis of the Centennial Botanic Gardens. 

It was exactly what they needed. The gardens were a labyrinth of flawlessly paved, ADA-accessible paths winding through dozens of specialized, highly manicured biomes. It was the exact opposite of the chaotic sensory nightmare of a baseball stadium. It was quiet, fragrant, and profoundly peaceful. 

Olivia pushed Leo's wheelchair along a wide concrete path that snaked through a massive alpine garden, past cascading water features and delicate, brightly colored mountain flowers. The crisp, dry mountain air felt incredibly clean, and the sun was warm on their shoulders. 

"This is amazing," Luna said, stopping to inspect a patch of vivid purple columbines. "It feels like we're in the mountains, but I don't have to climb anything."

"That's the magic of Denver," Olivia smiled, resting her hands lightly on the push handles of Leo's chair. "You get all the scenery with half the effort."

Leo let his head rest back against his headrest, closing his eyes for a moment as they rolled into the dappled shade of a massive cottonwood tree. The deep, rumbling stress of the long train ride was finally melting out of his muscles. The environment here was perfectly structured. There were clear paths to follow, quiet places to sit, and no sudden, loud noises to startle him. 

<aac>This is very nice,</aac> he typed on his communicator, turning the volume up just enough to cut through the quiet splashing of a nearby fountain. 

"I'm glad you like it, buddy," Stella said, dropping onto a wooden bench near the fountain and taking a long drink of water. She looked completely relaxed, the guardian bear finally able to drop her defensive posture in the safe, enclosed environment. 

They spent the next two hours slowly winding their way through the massive complex. They explored a climate-controlled tropical conservatory—a massive glass pyramid that felt comfortably warm—and wandered through a serene, carefully raked Japanese garden. 

For the first time since they left Virginia Beach, the ticking clock in Leo's mind fell completely silent. 

He didn't think about the fact that they were in the Mountain Time Zone. He didn't think about the terrifying Pacific Time Zone waiting for them across the Rockies. He just sat in his chair, feeling the sun on his face, listening to Luna happily identify different types of ferns, and letting Olivia push him safely through the beautiful, highly structured world. 

It was a perfectly functional, joyous, and fiercely protective display of the fortress operating at peak efficiency.

***

## 2:00 PM — Thin Air

Despite the peaceful, low-exertion environment of the Botanic Gardens, the geographical reality of the Mile High City eventually caught up with them. 

By early afternoon, the physical toll of being 5,280 feet above sea level began to manifest in the twins. Their carefully regulated bodies, built and conditioned for the dense, humid air of the East Coast sea level, were struggling to process the thin, dry oxygen of the Rockies. 

Leo felt a dull, persistent throbbing at the base of his skull. No matter how much water Olivia made him drink through his straw, his mouth felt like cotton, and an overwhelming wave of lethargy made it difficult to even keep his eyes open. 

Beside him on a shaded bench, Luna wasn't faring any better. She had abandoned her enthusiastic identification of local flora and was currently resting her head on her knees, her eyes closed. 

"My head hurts," Luna mumbled, her voice muffled against her jeans. "And I feel like I just ran a marathon, but all we did was walk past some orchids."

Stella frowned, crouching down to press the back of her hand against Luna's forehead. "No fever. Just altitude sickness. You guys are dehydrated, and your bodies are working overtime just to pull oxygen into your blood."

<aac>This sucks,</aac> Leo typed slowly, his finger feeling heavy and uncoordinated. 

"I know, buddy," Olivia sighed, rubbing the back of his neck gently. "I can feel it too. The air is just too thin for our East Coast lungs."

Stella stood up, making a swift, executive decision. The guardian bear was back in full logistical mode. "Alright. We are cutting the Denver layover short. We aren't doing another day of this."

"Can we change the train tickets?" Olivia asked, looking concerned. "We were supposed to stop in Salt Lake City on the way to California."

"Salt Lake City is over four thousand feet," Stella countered, shaking her head. "It's basically the same altitude as here. If they're struggling in Denver, they are going to struggle in Utah, and the train doesn't even get into Salt Lake until almost midnight. We aren't doing it. I'll call ARTS when we get back to the hotel. We're going to bypass Utah entirely."

"Where are we going?" Luna asked weakly, finally lifting her head. 

"We are booking a straight-through sleeper cabin," Stella said firmly. "We are going to ride the train up over the Rockies, across the Nevada desert, and straight down into Emeryville. We are getting you guys back to sea level as fast as geographically possible."

Leo felt a massive wave of relief wash over him. The thought of getting back to sea level—to dense, breathable air and the familiar smell of the ocean—sounded like heaven. He didn't care that they were skipping Salt Lake City. He just wanted to stop feeling like he was suffocating in slow motion. 

"Let's get back to the hotel," Olivia said, unlocking the brakes on Leo's wheelchair. "We'll get you guys into bed with the AC running while Stella works her magic on the phone."

The pack moved out of the gardens with renewed, singular purpose. The Rocky Mountains were beautiful, but the fortress had officially reached its vertical limit. It was time to head back to the coast.

## 3:00 PM — One More Night

Stella was on her cell phone the moment they got back to the quiet, air-conditioned sanctuary of their room at The Horizon Suites. 

Olivia quickly helped Leo transfer out of his wheelchair and into one of the queen beds, propping him up with pillows so he could rest his aching head. Luna had immediately face-planted onto the other bed, asleep almost before her head hit the mattress. 

"Yes, the Western Pioneer," Stella said into her phone, pacing near the window. "I need to change our itinerary. We want to cancel the stopover in Salt Lake City and book straight through to Emeryville. Four passengers, one wheelchair accessible... yes, the Family Bedroom again if you have it."

Leo closed his eyes, focusing on the deep, rhythmic hum of the hotel's air conditioning unit. The cool air was a massive relief after the bright sun of the Botanic Gardens. 

"Wait, 8:05 AM?" Stella's voice spiked in surprise. She checked her watch. "There's only one train a day? Okay. No, that's fine. We'll take it tomorrow morning."

She finished the transaction, gave the agent her confirmation number, and flipped her cell phone shut with a sharp snap. 

"Good news and bad news," Stella announced, turning back to the room. 

Olivia looked up from where she was pulling a blanket over Leo's legs. "Give me the bad news first."

"The bad news is that the westbound ARTS train leaves Denver precisely at 8:05 in the morning," Stella said, rubbing her temples. "Which means we've completely missed it for today. We are stuck at five thousand feet for one more night."

Leo groaned softly from his pile of pillows. 

"And the good news?" Olivia asked, gently brushing the hair out of Leo's eyes. 

"The good news is that I got us the Family Bedroom all the way to California starting tomorrow morning," Stella grinned, waving her phone like a trophy. "We don't have to get off in Utah. Once we board that train tomorrow, we are on a straight shot back to sea level. The air is going to get thicker by the hour."

"Thank God," Olivia sighed. "We need to get out of the mountains."

"So, here is the new itinerary," Stella declared, walking over to pull the heavy blackout curtains closed over the window, plunging the room into a cool, soothing darkness. "We are officially in survival mode. Nobody moves. Nobody exerts themselves. We lie in these beds, we drink water, and we order room service until it is time to get on the DRT tomorrow morning."

<aac>Best itinerary ever,</aac> Leo typed, the text-to-speech voice sounding appropriately enthusiastic in the dark room. 

"I thought you'd like that," Stella chuckled, kicking off her shoes and climbing into the bed next to the sleeping Luna. 

Leo settled back into his pillows, feeling Olivia crawl into the bed beside him. She wrapped an arm around his waist, grounding him with the familiar, heavy pressure he needed. The dull ache in his head was still there, but knowing that they were leaving tomorrow—that they were abandoning the thin air and heading for the ocean—made it bearable. 

They were going to survive one more night in the Mile High City.
