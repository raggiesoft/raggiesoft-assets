## 2:00 AM — Night Terrors

When the time finally came to officially go to sleep, the pack naturally gravitated toward their established, unspoken formations. Even though there were two spacious queen beds in the hotel room, Luna didn't want to sleep alone. She climbed into the bed where Leo was resting, squeezing herself onto the mattress so that Leo was sandwiched securely between her and Olivia. 

It was a tight fit, but the deep, grounding pressure of having a warm body on either side of him was exactly what Leo's exhausted, altitude-stressed nervous system needed to finally shut down. 

Sometime around 2:00 AM, however, the exhaustion curdled into a nightmare. 

It wasn't a normal dream. It was a visceral, suffocating night terror—a delayed psychological reaction to the massive influx of stress, the timezone shifts, and the crude, triggering joke outside the tavern. In the dark theater of his sleeping mind, Leo was surrounded. Faceless, aggressive shapes were closing in on him, grabbing at his chair, taunting him. He was trapped, unable to speak, unable to defend himself. 

In the real world, Leo let out a sharp, choked gasp. He rolled violently onto his stomach, his contracted arms suddenly lashing out in blind, panicked swings against the mattress as he fought desperately against the invisible attackers. 

"Leo?" Olivia's groggy voice cut through the dark. She sat up instantly, alarmed by the sudden violence of his thrashing. "Leo, hey, wake—"

She didn't get to finish the sentence. 

Before Olivia could even fully orient herself, Luna was already moving. Operating on pure, deeply ingrained muscle memory, the eighteen-year-old girl scrambled over the tangled bedsheets. She didn't hesitate or try to gently wake him up. Instead, she threw her entire body directly on top of him. 

Luna landed face-down on his back. She instantly locked her long legs around his waist and snaked her arms tightly under his thrashing ones, wrapping her arms completely around his torso. With a fierce, practiced motion, she clamped her arms shut, brutally pinning his swinging arms flush against his own sides. She buried her face firmly into the crook of his neck, effectively turning herself into a human straitjacket. 

Olivia lunged forward, her protective instincts flaring. "Luna, what are you doing? Let him—"

"Stand down, Liv!" Stella's voice snapped sharply from the other bed. 

The bedside lamp clicked on, flooding the room with dull yellow light. Stella was sitting up, her eyes wide but her posture completely calm and authoritative. 

Olivia froze, her hands hovering inches from Luna's back. "He's having a seizure. She's crushing him!"

"He's not seizing, he's having a night terror," Stella corrected, her voice steady and commanding. "If you try to wake him up now, he'll accidentally hit you, or he'll hurt himself. Luna knows exactly what she's doing. She's been pulling him out of these for years. Just stand down and give her a minute."

Olivia slowly lowered her hands, her chest heaving as she watched the chaotic scene on the mattress. 

Underneath the crushing, immovable weight of his twin sister, Leo was still fighting. His lungs burned as he tried to buck her off, his mind still trapped in the terrifying hallucination. But Luna didn't budge. She held onto him with a fierce, desperate strength, her body providing a massive, inescapable wave of deep proprioceptive pressure. 

"I've got you," Luna whispered fiercely into his ear, her voice tight with exertion. "I'm right here. You're safe. I've got you, Leo. Come back to me."

Slowly, the heavy, grounding weight of his twin sister began to break through the panic. The invisible attackers in his mind started to dissolve, replaced by the physical reality of the mattress beneath him and Luna's familiar, rhythmic breathing against his neck. 

The frantic thrashing began to subside into violent tremors, and then, finally, into heavy, exhausted sobs. 

Leo went limp against the mattress. He was awake. He was safe. 

"There you go," Luna murmured. 

But she didn't let go, and she didn't climb off of him. As the fight left his body, the brutal, clinical restraint seamlessly transitioned into a profound, prolonged hug. She simply stayed lying face down on top of him, adjusting her arms so they were wrapped lovingly around his torso rather than pinning him. 

Olivia watched, still hovering near the edge of the bed. "Is she going to get up?" she whispered to Stella. "She's squishing him."

"No, she's not. Leave them be," Stella said softly, leaning back against her own headboard. "That's the second part of the protocol. Once the terror breaks, it turns into a hug. She'll stay on top of him until she is absolutely satisfied that he's fully back to reality. And then she'll probably just stay there because they both like it."

Olivia looked at Stella, awe mixing with the lingering adrenaline. "Have you ever...?"

"Tried to pull him out of one?" Stella nodded slowly. "Yeah. Plenty of times. But I don't have the magic touch. Luna mastered the technique years ago. She knows exactly how much pressure to apply." Stella offered a small, bittersweet smile. "Plus, when his world has completely fallen apart and he's terrified, he'd much rather realize his twin sister is the one holding him together than his older sister."

Olivia watched the two of them, the steady rise and fall of their backs moving in unison. "Does she do this every time?"

"Every time," Stella confirmed quietly. "And if Luna ever has an autistic meltdown or a terror of her own, Leo does the exact same thing for her. They are each other's weighted blankets. It's how they survive."

Olivia sat silently on the edge of the mattress, the last of her adrenaline fading into a profound realization. She possessed the raw, athletic strength to physically carry Leo across the country, but in the deepest, darkest moments of his psychological terror, it was Luna—the seemingly fragile, anxious twin—who was his ultimate, unbreakable shield.

## 7:00 AM — The Western Pioneer

The sun rose over the Mile High City, pouring bright, harsh light through the gap in the blackout curtains. 

Leo was already awake. He was still lying on his stomach, completely pinned to the mattress by Luna. She hadn't moved all night. She was still draped heavily across his back, her legs tangled with his and her arms securely wrapped around his torso. 

He didn't try to wake her. The crushing pressure of his twin sister felt like a physical anchor tethering him to the real world, keeping the dark, chaotic terrors of the night firmly locked away. 

Eventually, Stella’s alarm went off across the room. 

"Alright, fortress, time to move," Stella groaned, slapping the alarm clock on the nightstand. "We have an eight o'clock train to catch."

Luna stirred, letting out a muffled sigh against Leo's neck. She slowly unhooked her arms and rolled off of him, rubbing her eyes behind her glasses. She didn't say anything about the night terror, and neither did he. It was an unspoken, lifelong protocol. She had pulled him out of the darkness, and now it was morning. 

Olivia quickly helped Leo transfer back into his motorized wheelchair. He still had a lingering headache from the altitude, but it was vastly overshadowed by the adrenaline of knowing they were finally leaving the thin air behind. 

They packed up their bags, checked out of The Horizon Suites, and rolled quickly down the 16th Street pedestrian mall to Union Station. 

The historic, Beaux-Arts train station was bustling with morning commuters, but the pack moved with practiced, military precision. Stella led the way, brandishing their updated tickets like a shield. They bypassed the crowds, navigating the accessible ramps directly to the boarding platform where the massive, silver Superliner cars of the *Western Pioneer* were waiting. 

"Family Bedroom, straight through to Emeryville," the conductor announced, checking Stella's tickets and lowering the metal bridge to the train. "You folks are in for a spectacular ride today. We're going straight through the Rockies."

Olivia pushed Leo onto the lower level of the sleeping car, navigating the tight corner into the familiar, cramped space of the Family Bedroom. 

It was exactly the same claustrophobic layout they had endured across Nebraska—two tiny adult bunks on one side, two child-sized bunks on the other, spanning the entire width of the train car with windows on both sides. But this time, nobody complained about the lack of space. 

"Okay, beds are stowed, bags are under the seats," Stella said, dropping heavily onto the lower couch and letting out a long breath. "We did it. We survived Denver."

At exactly 8:05 AM, the massive diesel engines of the *Western Pioneer* roared to life. The train gave a violent, metallic lurch, and then slowly began to pull away from the concrete platform of Union Station. 

Leo sat perfectly still in the narrow space between the bunks, watching the brick buildings of downtown Denver slowly begin to slide backward past the window. 

As the train picked up speed, the towering, snow-capped peaks of the Rocky Mountains filled the forward windows. It was a massive, intimidating wall of rock that seemed to entirely block the horizon. 

They were officially leaving the Midwest behind. The final, massive leg of their cross-country journey had begun.

## 10:00 AM — The Climb to the Divide

The *Western Pioneer* didn't just glide out of Denver; it fought for every inch of altitude. 

For the first hour, the massive double-decker train aggressively wound its way up the sheer face of the Front Range. The tracks bent into impossible, sweeping horseshoes—the infamous "Big Ten Curve"—where Leo could look out his window and see the very back of their own train trailing far below them. The urban sprawl of Denver shrank into a hazy grid on the plains, replaced by towering walls of granite and dense, dark pine forests.

But as the train climbed higher into the sky, the air inside the sleeper car began to change. 

Denver had been a mile high. By 10:00 AM, the train was approaching nine thousand feet above sea level. 

Leo was sitting on the edge of the lower bunk, staring out the window, when the first wave of lethargy hit him. It felt like an invisible weight pressing down on his chest. His breathing grew shallow and rapid, his lungs instinctively trying to pull oxygen out of air that was growing aggressively thin. 

Across the tiny compartment, Luna was slumped against the wall, her eyes half-closed and her chest rising and falling in quick, uneven hitches. 

"Okay, oxygen check," Stella announced, her voice cutting through the heavy hum of the diesel engines. She was sitting in the corner jump seat, completely unaffected by the altitude. 

Olivia immediately dropped her book and moved into action. She leaned over Luna first, checking her pulse and gently tilting her sister's head back. "Deep breaths, Lu. In through your nose, out through your mouth. You're just panting. You have to pull the air all the way down."

Luna reached clumsily for her own NovaVox resting beside her. She tapped a single, pre-programmed button, lacking the breath and focus to type out a full sentence. 

<aac>I'm trying. It feels empty,</aac> her device announced, the calm, synthesized female voice contrasting sharply with Luna's erratic, wheezing breaths. 

"It's just the elevation," Olivia promised, rubbing Luna's shoulders with a steady, grounding pressure. "We're almost at the peak. Once we cross the divide, we start going back down."

Stella shifted her attention to Leo. She didn't ask how he was feeling; she could see the pale, grayish tint creeping into his cheeks. She reached into her medical bag and pulled out a small, portable pulse oximeter, clipping it smoothly onto his index finger. 

The little digital screen blinked to life. *88%.*

"Yeah, you're not loving this mountain, are you?" Stella murmured, unzipping a secondary compartment in her bag. "Just relax, Leo. Don't fight it. Let your body figure it out." 

She pulled out a small, pressurized oxygen canister—a standard precaution she had bought at a pharmacy in Denver specifically for this leg of the trip. She fitted the plastic mask over Leo's mouth and nose, pressing the trigger. 

A rush of cool, concentrated oxygen flooded his lungs. The relief was instantaneous. The crushing weight on his chest lifted, and the dark edges of his vision cleared. He took a long, deep breath, the plastic mask fogging up with his exhalation. 

"Better?" Stella asked. 

Leo nodded weakly, keeping the mask pressed to his face. He reached out and pulled his NovaVox—his dedicated AAC tablet—onto his knees on the edge of the bunk. His fingers moved quickly over the thick, rugged touchscreen. 

<aac>Where are we going?</aac> the synthesized voice from the NovaVox asked, cutting through the low rumble of the train. <aac>After the mountains.</aac>

"We're on this train until tomorrow afternoon," Stella said, checking his oxygen levels again as they ticked back up into the high nineties. "Non-stop to Emeryville. Then we catch a bus across the bay into San Francisco."

Leo's fingers tapped the screen again. *Emeryville. Is that near Jack London Square?*

Olivia looked up from Luna, surprised. "Yeah, it is. How do you know about Jack London Square?"

*We read The Call of the Wild in middle school,* the electronic voice replied. Leo looked back out the window at the rugged, unforgiving wilderness passing by. *Mr. Harrison told us about him. The author. He said Jack London loved the cold. He loved the wild. I always wondered what his city looked like.*

Before Olivia could answer, the bright, mid-morning sun vanished. 

The train plunged into absolute, pitch-black darkness. The sudden absence of light was so absolute it felt physical. The rhythmic clatter of the wheels violently echoed off close, invisible stone walls. 

"Moffat Tunnel," Stella announced in the dark, her voice echoing in the cramped compartment. "Six point two miles straight through the Continental Divide. We're at the top of the world, kids." 

Leo sat in the dark, the oxygen mask hissing softly against his face. He felt the immense, millions of tons of solid mountain pressing down on the roof of the train. He thought about wolves, and wilderness, and escaping into the cold. 

For the first time in his life, he was going to see the ocean on the other side.

## 12:30 PM — The Canyons of the Colorado

The darkness inside the Moffat Tunnel felt like it lasted a lifetime. 

For six long miles, the *Western Pioneer* rumbled through the heart of the Continental Divide, completely cut off from the outside world. Leo sat quietly in the lower bunk, his oxygen mask still strapped to his face, listening to the rhythmic, echoing clatter of the train wheels against the solid stone walls. Next to him, Luna's breathing had finally steadied into a normal rhythm, the initial shock of the extreme altitude slowly wearing off.

And then, just as suddenly as the sun had vanished, the train burst back into the light. 

The transition was violent and breathtaking. They didn't emerge into another dark pine forest or a sprawling plain. They shot out of the rock face and immediately found themselves clinging to the side of a massive, sun-drenched gorge. 

"Whoa," Olivia breathed, pressing her forehead directly against the glass. 

They were deep inside the Rocky Mountains, descending into Gore Canyon. The walls of the canyon were steep, jagged cliffs of red and gold rock that plummeted straight down. At the very bottom of the gorge, a raging river carved its way through the stone—the mighty Colorado River, winding its way west. 

The train tracks hugged the very edge of the water, tracing the impossible, winding path of the river canyon.

Stella unclipped the pulse oximeter from Leo's finger. The screen read a comfortable 98%. "Alright, oxygen mask comes off. We're back below eight thousand feet and dropping fast."

Leo reached up and pulled the plastic elastic band over his head, taking a deep, unfiltered breath. The air still felt incredibly fresh, but the crushing weight on his chest was completely gone. 

He looked over at his twin sister. Luna was leaning over Olivia's shoulder, completely mesmerized by the churning whitewater rapids of the Colorado River just a few dozen feet outside their window. She reached for her NovaVox, her fingers tapping out a quick, excited thought. 

<aac>It looks like another planet,</aac> the calm, synthesized female voice noted. 

Leo smiled, pulling his own NovaVox onto his lap. <aac>It looks like freedom,</aac> his device replied.

"It looks like it's time for lunch," Stella declared, standing up and stretching her arms over her head. The low ceiling of the Family Bedroom meant she couldn't stand up completely straight, but she didn't seem to mind. "They just called for the first dining car service. We have a reservation in fifteen minutes. And the best part is, we don't even have to leave this canyon to eat."

The train tilted aggressively into another tight curve, following the contour of the river. The sun caught the mist rising off the rapids, creating brief, shimmering rainbows against the red canyon walls. 

Leo secured his NovaVox in his lap, feeling a profound sense of peace settle over him. The terrifying darkness of the tunnel was behind them, and the vast, golden expanse of the American West was pulling them inexorably toward the ocean.

## 12:45 PM — The Observation Deck

The Family Bedroom was located on the lower level of the sleeper car. To get to the dining car, they had to go upstairs and cross through two swaying, rattling train carriages. 

Earlier that morning, when the Lead Service Attendant had walked through the car to take lunch reservations, she had noticed Leo's folded wheelchair and politely offered to have their meals delivered directly to the room on trays. It was standard protocol for passengers with mobility issues. But Leo had reached for his NovaVox and typed out a quick question to Olivia: <aac>Will you carry me?</aac>

Olivia hadn't even hesitated. *Absolutely,* she had smiled. Stella had immediately turned back to the attendant and booked a table for four at 12:45. 

"Alright, wheelchair stays here," Olivia said now, cracking her knuckles as the time arrived. She stepped into the narrow space between the bunks and slid her arms securely under Leo's back and behind his knees. With a smooth, practiced deadlift, she hoisted him effortlessly into her arms. 

As she lifted him, Leo leaned heavily into the carry. He wrapped his arms securely around her neck, pulling himself into a tight, affectionate hug against her chest. He had complete trust in her. She carried him around their small, single-story house in Virginia all the time; a moving train was just a fun new balancing challenge. 

"Lead the way, Captain," Olivia said, shifting Leo's weight against her chest to keep her center of gravity low. 

Stella opened the sliding door and stepped out into the narrow corridor, followed closely by Luna, who was carrying both of their NovaVox tablets. 

They climbed the narrow, twisting staircase to the upper level of the sleeper car. The train was aggressively swaying side-to-side as it carved through the winding canyon, but Olivia’s footing was absolute. She moved with the sway of the train, absorbing the shocks through her legs, carrying Leo as easily as if he weighed nothing at all. 

They crossed through the heavy, sliding metal doors connecting the carriages, stepping over the loud, rattling metal plates that bridged the gap between the cars. 

"Whoa," Luna breathed, stopping dead in her tracks as they entered the next carriage. 

It was the Sightseer Lounge—the observation deck. The entire upper half of the train car was made of curved glass. Massive windows stretched from the floor, curving seamlessly into the ceiling, offering a completely unobstructed, panoramic view of the world outside. 

It felt like floating in a glass bubble directly over the Colorado River. The sheer, red-rock cliffs of Gore Canyon towered above them, practically close enough to touch, while the whitewater rapids raged directly below. 

"We'll come back here after we eat," Stella promised, gently steering Luna forward through the lounge. 

They crossed into the next car, which was filled with the clatter of silverware and the smell of roasting meat. The dining car was packed with passengers sitting at four-person booths with crisp white tablecloths. 

"Reservation for Porter," Stella said, handing a little blue paper slip to the attendant at the door. 

"Right this way," the attendant smiled, leading them to an empty booth right next to a massive window. 

Olivia slid into the booth first, gently depositing Leo onto the padded bench beside her before sliding out to let Luna sit next to him. 

"Okay, this is officially the coolest restaurant I've ever been to," Olivia grinned, grabbing a menu. 

Leo looked out the window. The train was still hugging the edge of the river, carving its way deeper into the American West. He couldn't walk, and he couldn't navigate the heavy doors of the train cars on his own, but sitting here in the dining car, completely surrounded by his pack, he didn't feel trapped at all. He felt like he was flying.
