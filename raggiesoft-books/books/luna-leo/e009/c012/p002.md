## 12:50 PM — Dining Car Service

A sharply dressed waiter in a crisp white shirt and dark vest appeared at their table, placing four heavy, blue-rimmed ARTS menus down on the white tablecloth. 

"Welcome to the *Western Pioneer* dining car," he said with a warm smile, bracing himself against the edge of the booth as the train leaned heavily into another curve along the Colorado River. "Can I start you folks off with something to drink?"

"Four Cokes, please," Stella said, sliding her menu across the table. "And we actually already know what we want."

The waiter pulled out a small notepad. "Excellent. What can I get for you?"

"I'll take the grilled chicken sandwich," Stella said. 

"I'll do the same," Olivia added. 

Stella then pointed to the twins sitting across from her. "And for these two, we'll take two of the Angus Cheeseburgers. But here is the critical part: they need to be made exactly right. Meat, cheese, ketchup, mayo, lettuce, and bacon. Absolutely no tomatoes, no onions, no pickles, no mustard, and no relish anywhere near the plate. Just those safe ingredients and a side of fries."

The waiter didn't miss a beat. He jotted it down smoothly. "Two Angus burgers. Meat, cheese, bacon, lettuce, ketchup, and mayo. No garden surprises. We get specific orders all the time. I'll have those right out."

Leo watched the waiter disappear down the narrow aisle of the swaying car. He reached for his NovaVox, tapping out a quick message. 

<aac>Thank you,</aac> the device spoke, the synthesized voice directed across the table at Stella. He hated having to explain his sensory aversions to strangers, and he loved that Stella always aggressively intercepted those conversations before they even started. 

"Don't thank me yet," Stella chuckled, picking up her water glass. "If they bring out a single onion on that plate, I'm throwing it straight out the window into the river."

Luna grinned, resting her chin on her hands as she looked out the massive dining car window. The sheer, red rock walls of Gore Canyon were still blurring past them. It felt incredibly surreal to be sitting in a nice restaurant, drinking an ice-cold cola, while hurtling through an untamed wilderness at fifty miles an hour. 

Olivia wrapped an arm loosely around Leo's shoulders, pulling him into a sideways hug in the booth. "You holding up okay, Captain?"

Leo leaned into her side, nodding happily. The altitude sickness was completely gone. He was surrounded by his favorite people in the world, eating his favorite safe food, on a train bound for the ocean. 

He didn't think life could get much better than this.

## 6:30 PM — The Utah Border

The transition out of the Rocky Mountains was just as dramatic as the climb into them. 

By early evening, the thick, towering pine forests and sheer granite cliffs of Colorado had completely vanished. The *Western Pioneer* was now carving its way through Ruby Canyon, winding alongside the Colorado River as it approached the Utah border. The landscape had violently shifted into a sprawling, arid desert of towering red sandstone mesas and deep, dry ravines. 

As the sun began to dip below the horizon, it cast long, dramatic shadows across the red rocks, painting the entire canyon in brilliant shades of burnt orange, gold, and deep violet. 

The pack had migrated back to the Family Bedroom on the lower level. Leo was sitting on the floor between the bunks, his back resting comfortably against Olivia’s legs as she sat on the lower couch. She was idly running her hand through his messy brown hair, a quiet, rhythmic gesture that kept him deeply grounded. 

Luna was lying on her stomach on the opposite bunk, her chin propped on her hands as she stared out the massive window at the alien, Martian-like landscape. 

"It's so empty out here," Luna murmured, her voice quiet in the small space. 

"It's the desert, Lu," Stella said, looking up from a paperback book she was reading under the small reading light. "We're about to cross into Utah. Once the sun goes down, it's just going to be hundreds of miles of absolute nothingness until we hit Nevada."

Leo looked out the window. The sky was burning with the intense colors of a desert sunset. It was beautiful, but it was also profoundly isolating. The immense scale of the American West was finally sinking in. They were thousands of miles away from their small, cozy house in Virginia, away from the dense, humid forests of the East Coast, away from the only world they had ever known. 

He reached for his NovaVox, tapping the thick screen. 

*Are there any cities out here?* he asked. 

"Not really," Olivia answered, resting her chin on the top of his head. "We stop in Salt Lake City in a few hours, but it'll be pitch black by then. And then we cross the salt flats in the middle of the night. You'll probably sleep through the whole thing."

Leo shivered slightly, despite the comfortable, climate-controlled air of the sleeper car. The thought of hurtling through a vast, pitch-black desert while everyone slept was unsettling. The ocean felt unimaginably far away. 

But then Olivia's hand resumed its gentle rhythm in his hair, and he looked across the narrow room at Stella and Luna. They weren't out in the desert. They were right here, safely enclosed in their rolling fortress of steel and glass. 

<aac>I'm glad we're together,</aac> his NovaVox spoke, the electronic voice cutting through the ambient hum of the train. 

Stella smiled, reaching across the small aisle to squeeze his shoulder. "Always, kiddo. The fortress moves with us."
