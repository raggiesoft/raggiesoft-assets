The Family Bedroom was located on the lower level of the sleeper car. To get to the dining car, they had to go upstairs and cross through two swaying, rattling train carriages. 

Earlier that morning, when the Lead Service Attendant had walked through the car to take lunch reservations, she had noticed Leo's folded wheelchair and politely offered to have their meals delivered directly to the room on trays. It was standard protocol for passengers with mobility issues. But Leo had reached for his NovaVox and typed out a quick question to Olivia: <aac>Will you carry me?</aac>

Olivia hadn't even hesitated. *Absolutely,* she had smiled. Stella had immediately turned back to the attendant and booked a table for four at 12:45. 

"Alright, wheelchair stays here," Olivia said now, cracking her knuckles as the time arrived. She stepped into the narrow space between the bunks and slid her arms securely under Leo's back and behind his knees. With a smooth, practiced deadlift, she hoisted him effortlessly into her arms. 

As she lifted him, Leo leaned heavily into the carry. He wrapped his arms securely around her neck, pulling himself into a tight, affectionate hug against her chest. He had complete trust in her. She carried him around their small, single-story house in Virginia all the time; a moving train was just a fun new balancing challenge. 

"Lead the way, Captain," Olivia said, shifting Leo's weight against her chest to keep her center of gravity low. 

Stella opened the sliding door and stepped out into the narrow corridor, followed closely by Luna, who was carrying both of their NovaVox tablets. 

They climbed the narrow, twisting staircase to the upper level of the sleeper car. The train was aggressively swaying side-to-side as it carved through the winding canyon, but Olivia’s footing was absolute. She moved with the sway of the train, absorbing the shocks through her legs, carrying Leo as easily as if he weighed nothing at all. 

They crossed through the heavy, sliding metal doors connecting the carriages, stepping over the loud, rattling metal plates that bridged the gap between the cars. 

"Whoa," Luna breathed, stopping dead in her tracks as they entered the next carriage. 

It was the Sightseer Lounge—the observation deck. The entire upper half of the train car was made of curved glass. Massive windows stretched from the floor, curving seamlessly into the ceiling, offering a completely unobstructed, panoramic view of the world outside. 

It felt like floating in a glass bubble directly over the Colorado River. The sheer, red-rock cliffs of Gore Canyon towered above them, practically close enough to touch, while the whitewater rapids raged directly below. 

"We'll come back here after we eat," Stella promised, gently steering Luna forward through the lounge. 

They crossed into the next car, which was filled with the clatter of silverware and the smell of roasting meat. The dining car was packed with passengers sitting at four-person booths with crisp white tablecloths. 

"Reservation for Porter," Stella said, handing a little blue paper slip to the attendant at the door. 

"Right this way," the attendant smiled, leading them to an empty booth right next to a massive window. 

Olivia slid into the booth first, gently depositing Leo onto the padded bench beside her before sliding out to let Luna sit next to him. 

"Okay, this is officially the coolest restaurant I've ever been to," Olivia grinned, grabbing a menu. 

Leo looked out the window. The train was still hugging the edge of the river, carving its way deeper into the American West. He couldn't walk, and he couldn't navigate the heavy doors of the train cars on his own, but sitting here in the dining car, completely surrounded by his pack, he didn't feel trapped at all. He felt like he was flying.
