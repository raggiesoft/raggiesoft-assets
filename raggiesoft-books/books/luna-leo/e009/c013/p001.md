## 12:30 AM — The Salt Lake Stop

The *Western Pioneer* was running almost an hour behind schedule, which meant it didn't pull into Utah's capital until well past midnight. 

Leo was lying on his back in the lower bunk of the Family Bedroom. Olivia was lying next to him on the narrow mattress, one arm thrown heavily across his chest to keep him physically grounded in his sleep. The rhythm of the train usually kept him under, but the sudden, grinding squeal of the air brakes and the lurching deceleration pulled him out of a deep sleep. 

He blinked his eyes open in the dark compartment. Above him, he could hear the soft, rhythmic breathing of Luna and Stella in the upper bunks. 

The train ground to a complete halt, hissing loudly. 

Leo carefully slipped out from under Olivia's arm, making sure not to wake her. He sat up and pulled back the heavy fabric curtain covering the window. 

The platform outside was bathed in the harsh, yellow glare of sodium vapor streetlights. The sign hanging from the station canopy read *Salt Lake City*. Beyond the platform, the distant, glowing grid of the city was completely enveloped by the towering, pitch-black silhouettes of the Wasatch Mountains. 

Salt Lake City sat at roughly 4,200 feet above sea level. It was significantly lower than the towering peak of the Moffat Tunnel they had traversed yesterday, so Stella had removed his pressurized oxygen mask before bed. But as Leo took a breath in the quiet dark of the cabin, he could still feel the lingering strain in his lungs. The air here was still thin, still dry, and his body was deeply, intrinsically exhausted by the lack of oxygen. 

He stared out the window at the glowing streetlights. Part of him desperately wanted to see the city, to understand what a metropolis built in the middle of an arid, mountainous desert looked like. He had read about the Great Salt Lake, and he knew they were about to cross the desolate salt flats in the dark. 

But as his chest heaved with a shallow, slightly labored breath, the other part of him knew the truth: he just wanted to go down. He wanted the thick, heavy, humid air of sea level. 

Olivia stirred beside him, blinking groggily in the dim light spilling through the window. "Are we there?" she whispered, her voice rough with sleep. 

Leo reached for his NovaVox resting on the edge of the mattress. He turned the volume dial down to its lowest setting so he wouldn't wake his sisters above them. 

<aac>Salt Lake City,</aac> the device whispered into the dark room. 

Olivia let out a soft groan and checked her watch. "Past midnight. Go back to sleep, Captain. When we wake up, the mountains will be gone."

She reached out and gently pulled him back down onto the mattress. Leo let the curtain fall shut, plunging the room back into complete darkness. He closed his eyes, listening to the heavy diesel engines rev up again. With a sharp metallic clang, the train pulled out of the station, leaving the mountains behind and pushing out into the vast, empty darkness of the Nevada desert.

***

## 7:30 AM — The Nevada Desert

When Leo woke up the next morning, the world had fundamentally changed. 

The heavy, oppressive weight on his chest was finally gone. He took a long, deep breath of the air circulating through the Family Bedroom, feeling his lungs expand fully for the first time in two days. The train had finally descended from the dizzying heights of the Rockies and the Utah plateau. They were breathing normal, oxygen-rich air again.

He shifted slightly on the bottom bunk. Olivia was already awake, sitting on the end of the mattress with her legs crossed, staring out the window with a cup of coffee she must have grabbed from the end of the hall. 

"Morning, Captain," she smiled softly, noticing him move. "Breathe easier?"

Leo nodded emphatically. He reached for his NovaVox, firing it up. <aac>Where are we?</aac>

"Nevada," Olivia said, pointing out the window. "We crossed the salt flats while you were sleeping."

Leo dragged himself over to the window and looked out. The landscape outside was completely different from the red rocks of yesterday. It was a vast, flat expanse of pale, sun-bleached desert scrub stretching endlessly toward distant, hazy mountain ranges. It was completely barren, yet beautiful in its absolute desolation. 

Above them, there was a loud thud and a groan as Stella swung her legs over the edge of the upper bunk, climbing down the small ladder. Luna followed a moment later, her hair an absolute mess. 

"Alright, fortress, rise and shine," Stella announced, stretching her arms and suppressing a yawn. "We have a very important mission to accomplish this morning."

<aac>What mission?</aac> Leo typed, his NovaVox speaking into the quiet room. 

"Breakfast," Stella grinned. "And the dining car operates on a first-come, first-served basis in the mornings. Which means if we don't get up there right now, we're going to be waiting in the hallway for a booth. Let's move."

Ten minutes later, the pack was making the trek back upstairs. Olivia smoothly hoisted Leo into a carry, navigating the swaying corridors with the same practiced ease as the day before. 

When they arrived in the dining car, the air was filled with the smell of sizzling bacon and fresh coffee. They managed to snag the last open booth by the window just as the train blasted its horn, tearing across the empty Nevada desert. 

The waiter handed them the breakfast menus. "Good morning, folks. What can I start you with?"

"I'll take a coffee with cream and sugar," Stella said smoothly, already knowing exactly what the rest of the pack needed. "An orange juice for her, and two chocolate milks for these two. For food, we'll take two orders of plain pancakes for the twins. No butter, no powdered sugar, no fruit compote on top. Just plain pancakes and a side of bacon."

The waiter jotted it down with a nod. "Two plain stacks, coming right up."

Leo looked across the table at his twin sister. Luna was already staring out the window, mesmerized by the hypnotic, blurring motion of the desert scrub. She caught his eye and smiled, tapping the edge of the table. 

They were back on level ground. They could breathe again. And today, they were finally going to reach California.

## 1:30 PM — The Donner Pass

By early afternoon, the vast, pale scrub brush of the Nevada desert finally began to give way to rising terrain. 

The *Western Pioneer* had left Reno behind and was now beginning its final, grueling climb of the journey: scaling the Sierra Nevada mountain range to cross into California.

Leo was sitting in his manual wheelchair in the Family Bedroom, having finally transferred out of the bunks for the afternoon. He had his NovaVox mounted securely on its metal swing-arm, typing idly as he watched the scenery change. The desert faded into thick, towering forests of incredibly tall pine trees. The train twisted and turned, carving its way up the side of the mountain range. 

"We're crossing the border right now," Stella announced, looking at the route map she had picked up in the Sightseer Lounge earlier. "Welcome to California, everyone."

"Finally," Olivia sighed, stretching her legs out across the lower bunk. "My back is starting to permanently mold to the shape of these ARTS beds."

The train plunged into a long, dark snow shed—a massive, wooden tunnel built over the tracks to protect them from winter avalanches. When they burst back out into the sunlight, they were at the summit of Donner Pass. 

Leo stared out the window. Unlike the Rocky Mountains, which were jagged and terrifyingly steep, the Sierras felt immensely heavy and ancient. He could see Donner Lake sparkling like a sapphire far below them in the valley. 

They were over 7,000 feet high again, but this time, the altitude didn't bother him. His body had already run the grueling gauntlet of the Rockies; this climb felt completely manageable in comparison. 

<aac>How much longer?</aac> Leo typed, his NovaVox echoing in the cabin. 

"We've hit the peak," Stella said, tracing her finger down the map. "From here, we start the long descent into the Sacramento Valley. We'll hit Emeryville by late afternoon. Then we just have to hop on the Thruway bus across the Bay Bridge into San Francisco."

Leo looked over at Luna. She was sitting on the floor, leaning against his wheelchair, completely engrossed in watching the towering pine trees blur past the glass. She caught him looking and reached up, giving his hand a quick, excited squeeze. 

They had crossed the entire North American continent. They had survived the Midwest, the Rockies, the deserts, and the Sierras. 

In just a few short hours, they were going to see the ocean.

***

## 5:00 PM — Arrival in the Bay

The *Western Pioneer* finally hissed to a halt at the concrete platform of the Emeryville station late in the afternoon. 

The air outside the train felt fundamentally different. The dry, thin chill of the mountains and the desert was gone, replaced by a cool, heavy, salt-tinged breeze rolling in off the San Francisco Bay. 

"End of the line for the train, folks," the conductor announced as he lowered the metal bridge to the platform. 

The pack spilled out onto the concrete. After two and a half days confined to the narrow, swaying carriages of the train, the stationary ground beneath their feet felt almost strange. Olivia and Stella were both carrying two massive, heavy duffel bags, burdened with all the supplies they needed for a month-long stay on the West Coast. 

Leo pushed the handrims of his manual wheelchair, rolling easily down the long platform towards the station building. He looked around the bustling transit hub. He knew from his obsessive research before the trip that Emeryville was a major connection point for the East Bay. 

He stopped and tapped quickly on his NovaVox. 

<aac>Why aren't we taking BATS?</aac> the device asked, referencing the heavy-rail Bay Area Transit System that ran directly under the bay into the city. 

"Two reasons," Stella answered, adjusting the strap of a heavy duffel bag on her shoulder. "One: we have way too much luggage to be dragging it up and down the escalators in the BATS stations. And two: our train tickets actually go all the way into the city. ARTS operates a dedicated Thruway bus that connects right here."

She pointed toward the front of the station, where a massive, silver, wheelchair-accessible motorcoach was already idling at the curb. 

"The bus goes straight across the Bay Bridge," Stella explained as they walked toward it. "And it drops us off directly at the San Francisco Ferry Building. Our hotel is literally right across the Embarcadero. It's the flattest part of the entire city."

That sounded perfect to Leo. He expertly maneuvered his wheelchair onto the motorized lift of the coach bus, and a few minutes later, they were merging onto the highway. 

However, they immediately slammed into a wall of cars. It was five o'clock in the evening, and the legendary Bay Area rush hour was in full swing. 

"Well, we're not going anywhere fast," Olivia said, leaning her head against the bus window as they crept inch-by-inch through the massive concrete maze of the toll plaza. 

But Leo didn't mind the gridlock at all. Because they were heading westbound into San Francisco, their bus was routed onto the **upper deck** of the Bay Bridge. Sitting high up in the motorcoach on the top level of the bridge gave them a completely unobstructed, sweeping view of the choppy, dark blue water of the bay. In the distance, the iconic, fog-draped skyline of San Francisco rose up like a city of glass and steel, framed by the rolling green hills of the peninsula. 

It took them almost an hour to crawl across the bridge, but when the bus finally pulled up to the curb outside the historic Ferry Building, the sun was just beginning to dip low over the city. 

Leo rolled down the ramp and onto the wide, paved sidewalk of the Embarcadero. Stella was right. After navigating the uneven bricks, steep curbs, and narrow hallways of the East Coast for his entire life, the Embarcadero was a revelation. It was incredibly wide, perfectly paved, and completely flat. 

He didn't have to fight his wheelchair. He didn't have to scan the ground for obstacles. He could just roll. 

"Hotel is right there," Olivia pointed, hoisting her duffel bags again as she gestured to an elegant, towering building just across the wide boulevard with the glowing words *The Sovereign Regency San Francisco* affixed to the stone facade. 

Leo took a deep breath of the heavy, salty sea air. They were thousands of miles away from the intense humidity and quiet isolation of their small house in Virginia. They were on the edge of the Pacific Ocean. And for the next month, this was going to be their basecamp.

***

## 6:00 PM — The Sovereign Regency San Francisco

The crosswalk over the Embarcadero was incredibly wide, featuring long, steady pedestrian signals that gave Leo plenty of time to roll across the busy boulevard. 

The glowing letters of *The Sovereign Regency San Francisco* dominated the skyline directly in front of them, claiming the iconic waterfront property. 

They passed through the sliding glass doors into an absolutely massive, sprawling atrium. 

"Okay, this is officially the fanciest place we've ever stayed," Luna whispered, tilting her head back to look at the towering, multi-story open space inside the hotel. 

"I managed to get us a fantastic long-term rate," Stella said, looking incredibly relieved to finally drop the heavy duffel bags onto a polished luggage cart. "We're going to be here for the entire month of July. I wanted to make sure it was comfortable."

Ten minutes later, the pack was unlocking the door to a massive corner suite on the twelfth floor. 

Leo rolled into the room and immediately gravitated toward the floor-to-ceiling windows. The view was staggering. He was looking directly out over the Embarcadero. The Ferry Building's clock tower was brightly illuminated right below them, and stretching out into the dark water of the bay was the massive, glittering span of the Bay Bridge they had just crawled across. 

"Wow," Olivia breathed, dropping the last of the luggage on the floor and coming to stand beside Leo. "You definitely picked the right room, Stell."

"I try," Stella said, collapsing onto the edge of one of the massive, plush king-sized beds with a heavy sigh. "Alright. We've officially made it to the West Coast. We don't have anywhere to be tomorrow, no trains to catch, no schedules to follow."

Leo reached for his NovaVox. <aac>What's for dinner?</aac> he typed. 

Stella laughed, rubbing her tired eyes. "I am absolutely not leaving this room again tonight. But there is a massive menu of room service right there on the desk. You guys pick whatever you want, and I'll call it in. The hotel is paying."

Leo grinned, maneuvering his wheelchair over to the desk to grab the leather-bound room service menu. They had spent three days confined to a tiny, swaying train compartment, eating whatever the dining car had available. Tonight, sitting in a sprawling suite overlooking the Pacific Ocean with his pack, he felt completely, entirely free.
