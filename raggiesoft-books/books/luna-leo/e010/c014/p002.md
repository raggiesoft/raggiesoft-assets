## 5:30 PM — East Bay Diner

The EBT bus pulled over to the curb in a relatively quiet, tree-lined commercial district in North Oakland, the pneumatic brakes hissing loudly as it kneeled to the pavement. 

Leo let out a heavy breath as he unclipped his chair from the tie-downs and rolled down the ramp onto the sidewalk. The late afternoon air in the East Bay was noticeably warmer than the freezing wind whipping across the San Francisco waterfront, but Leo was too exhausted to enjoy it. His spine felt like it was made of lead, and every bump on the sidewalk sent a dull ache radiating up his back. 

"Okay, emergency dinner protocol," Stella said, taking one look at Leo's pale, drooping face. She scanned the street rapidly. "We need something quiet, and we need a booth where you can get out of that chair and take the weight off your back."

"There," Olivia pointed down the block. 

It was a classic, completely unremarkable American diner. The neon sign in the window was buzzing faintly, but looking through the glass, the restaurant was mostly empty and quiet. 

Luna pushed Leo's chair the rest of the way down the block. As soon as they got inside, the hostess led them to a wide, vinyl booth in the back corner. 

"Help me up," Leo typed weakly on his NovaVox. 

Luna immediately locked the brakes on his manual chair. She grabbed him securely under the arms, and with a coordinated, practiced heave, she helped him slide out of the wheelchair and into the soft, padded vinyl of the booth. 

Leo instantly slumped over, resting his forehead against the cool, smooth surface of the diner table. The relief on his spine was immediate, but his energy reserves were completely empty. 

"I'll order," Luna said softly, sitting in the booth next to him. She looked up at the waitress. "Two grilled cheese sandwiches, plain, with french fries. And two large chocolate milks."

"I'll take a turkey club," Olivia said. 

"And I need the largest black coffee you have, please," Stella added with a sympathetic smile. 

As the waitress walked away, Stella reached across the table and gently rubbed Leo's shoulder. He didn't lift his head from the table, but he turned his face slightly to look at her through his thick glasses. 

"You orchestrated a brilliant transit ride today, buddy," Stella said softly. "BATS through the Transbay Tube and a flawless EBT bus connection. It was a masterpiece."

Leo managed a small, exhausted smile. 

"But you're paying for it now," Stella continued gently. "So here is the plan. We are eating dinner, I am calling a minivan cab to pick us up at the curb, and they are driving us straight across the Bay Bridge to the hotel. And tomorrow is a zero-impact day. No ferries, no mountains, no complex transfers. We are staying on flat ground."

<aac>Thank you,</aac> Leo tapped slowly on his NovaVox, closing his eyes as he waited for his grilled cheese.

## 6:30 PM — The Bridge at Night

By the time the pack finished their grilled cheese sandwiches and walked out of the diner, the sun was beginning to set over the East Bay. 

Leo sat heavily in his wheelchair on the sidewalk. He reached for his NovaVox, his movements sluggish. <aac>BATS,</aac> he typed weakly. 

"No," Stella said firmly, standing on the curb with her phone in her hand. "No more transit today. You have absolutely zero energy left to navigate a crowded train station, Leo. I just called for a minivan cab."

Leo let his hand drop from the tablet, too exhausted to put up a real fight. 

Ten minutes later, a yellow minivan pulled up to the curb. Luna and Olivia helped Leo transfer into the backseat of the van. With a quick, practiced motion, Stella pulled the release on Leo's manual wheelchair, folding it completely flat and tossing it easily into the trunk. 

As the minivan merged onto the highway and began the long drive across the Bay Bridge, the exhaustion completely overtook Leo. He had absolutely no muscle control left in his core. Sitting in the middle seat next to Olivia, he simply melted sideways, resting his heavy head against her shoulder and letting his body go entirely slack against her side. 

Olivia didn't say a word. She just wrapped her arm tightly around his shoulders, holding him securely against the motion of the car as they drove over the dark water of the bay, the glowing skyline of San Francisco looming ahead of them. 

When they finally arrived back at the Sovereign Regency, it took the combined effort of Luna and Olivia just to get Leo from the minivan into his unfolded wheelchair, and then up the elevators to their corner suite. 

"Alright, straight to the bathroom," Stella ordered as she unlocked the suite doors. "He needs to get the city grime off him before he crashes."

Because Stella had specifically requested an accessible room when she booked the trip, the corner suite was fully accessible. The massive bathroom featured a sprawling roll-in shower with a wide, built-in teak shower bench. 

Luna parked Leo's wheelchair next to the shower. Olivia stepped in, wrapping her arms securely around Leo's waist. With a coordinated heave, she lifted him out of his chair and gently lowered him onto the wet wooden bench. 

Leo slumped forward against the tiled wall, the warm water from the showerhead cascading over his aching back. The heat felt heavenly on his spine, instantly melting away the sharp pain from the bouncing EBT bus. 

"I've got the shampoo," Luna said softly, stepping into the shower fully dressed in her jeans and t-shirt, completely uncaring that she was getting soaked. She gently tilted Leo's head back and began washing his messy brown hair. 

Stella stepped in beside her with a washcloth, gently washing his arms and shoulders while he rested. It was an incredibly vulnerable, completely reliant moment, but Leo didn't feel embarrassed. His family operated like a well-oiled machine, taking over when his body failed him. 

By the time they got him dried off and dressed in sweatpants, Leo was practically falling asleep sitting up on the shower bench. 

The corner suite featured two large queen beds, normally intended to split the pack into pairs. But as Luna and Olivia transferred him onto the mattress of the first bed, Leo weakly grabbed Olivia's wrist. He didn't want to be separated. He was in pain, he was exhausted, and he just wanted all three of them right there. 

Understanding exactly what he needed, Olivia climbed into the bed on his left side. Stella sighed, too tired to argue with the logistics, and climbed into the bed on his right side. 

That left no room on the mattress. But Luna didn't care. 

Without missing a beat, Luna climbed into the bed and smashed herself directly on top of Leo, lying stomach-to-stomach with him. It was the exact deep-pressure strategy she normally used when he was having a severe panic attack, but tonight, it was simply the only physical way she was going to fit into the dogpile. The heavy, warm weight of his twin sister pressing down on him was instantly grounding. 

The second queen bed across the room remained completely empty and perfectly made. 

Surrounded and pinned down by his entire family, Leo was asleep before Stella even turned out the lights.
