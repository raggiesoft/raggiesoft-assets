With their stomachs safely full of plain hamburgers on dry buns, the pack spent the early afternoon rolling inland from the waterfront. They navigated the grid of the city, eventually arriving at the intersection of Market Street and New Montgomery. 

The sprawling, beautifully landscaped public park sat right at 655 Market Street, directly across from the towering, historic facade of the Pacific Commerce Building. It was a beautiful space nestled into the Financial District, but the atmosphere was incredibly solemn. 

Leo rolled his manual chair along the smooth, paved paths toward a large, dark granite reflection pool in the center of the park. Two hundred names were carved into the stone rim of the water. 

Luna gripped the push-handles of his chair, stabilizing herself as she stared at the main bronze plaque. 

"December sixteenth, nineteen seventy-four," Luna read out loud, her voice quiet. "The Crown Point Disaster."

"This is the actual footprint," Stella said, looking up at the sky above the reflection pool. There was a massive, unnatural gap in the San Francisco skyline directly above the park. "It's hard to believe a hundred-and-thirty-five-story skyscraper used to be sitting right on this exact spot."

Leo reached for his NovaVox, his fingers lingering over the screen before typing. 

<aac>They were racing Sterling Plaza,</aac> the device announced softly. 

"They thought they were," Olivia corrected gently, reading a nearby informational placard. "Arthur Sterling was in absolutely no hurry to open his tower up in Meridian City. He only cared about safety. But the Crown Point developers decided they were in a West Coast arms race. If they had actually followed the specifications and installed the right wiring, Crown Point wouldn't have been ready to open until Spring of nineteen seventy-five. But they were so desperate to claim the title of the tallest building first that they squeezed their contractors to shave four months off the schedule."

"And it cost them everything," Luna said darkly. "They opened five days before Sterling Plaza. And that night, an electrical fire sparked on the eighty-first floor and burned the entire thing down."

Leo stared at the 200 names carved into the granite. He had seen the massive, blockbuster docudrama *Crown Point*—everyone had. But seeing the movie was entirely different from sitting in the physical, empty crater where it had actually happened. 

"Arthur Sterling ended up bankrolling the movie," Stella explained, wrapping her jacket tighter against the afternoon chill. "Every Hollywood studio rejected the script because it was too raw and too expensive. But Sterling wrote a fourteen-million-dollar blank check to get it made. He wanted it to be a global public service announcement."

<aac>Vanity over safety,</aac> Leo typed, summarizing the entire tragedy in three words. 

"Exactly," Stella agreed. "Sterling Plaza was built like a fortress. Crown Point was built for a headline. And because they couldn't just implode a burnt-out hundred-and-thirty-five-story husk in the middle of the city grid, they had to deconstruct it piece by piece over two years. Then the city turned the empty lot into this."

Luna ran her hand gently along the cold granite of the reflection pool. "It's so quiet here."

Leo nodded. Surrounded by the roaring traffic and towering glass of San Francisco, the Crown Point Memorial was a permanent, heavy reminder of what happened when people decided that speed was more important than building things the right way.
