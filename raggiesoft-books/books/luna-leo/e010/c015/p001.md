## 9:00 AM — The Atrium

Leo woke up feeling surprisingly refreshed. 

The heavy, warm weight of Luna sleeping directly on top of him all night had completely grounded his nervous system, and the sheer physical exhaustion from the previous day's massive transit ride had forced his body into a deep, uninterrupted sleep. 

True to her word, Stella had promised a zero-impact day. There were no hills, no train transfers, and no rushing. The pack took their time getting dressed before taking the elevator down to the ground floor for a late breakfast. 

The lobby of the Sovereign Regency was physically staggering. It was a massive, seventeen-story brutalist atrium that looked more like the inside of a futuristic spaceship than a hotel. Concrete balconies stacked up endlessly toward a massive skylight, and illuminated glass elevators shot up and down the walls like rockets. 

Stella secured them a large table at the lobby restaurant, right near a glowing water feature. 

As Luna parked Leo's manual chair at the table and sat down, Leo noticed the group of four college students sitting at the booth directly next to them. 

The group was striking. Two of them were clearly identical twin sisters with fiery red hair and heavily freckled faces, though one was noticeably curvy and the other was incredibly skinny. Sitting across from them was a girl with brown hair and standard glasses, and a young man with messy brown hair and thick prescription glasses. 

The young man was sitting in a heavy, motorized wheelchair. Mounted on a swing-arm attached to his chair was a sleek, silver aluminum MagnaByte laptop. 

"I'm telling you, it's the perfect angle for the Ethics in Engineering paper," the skinny redhead was saying, pointing a fork at the young man's laptop. "We focus on Arthur Sterling's architectural choices versus the Crown Point developers."

Leo's ears perked up immediately at the mention of Crown Point. 

The young man in the motorized chair nodded emphatically. He didn't make a single sound. Instead, his fingers flew across the keyboard of his MagnaByte. 

<aac>Isabel is right,</aac> the laptop announced, its deep, synthesized voice cutting clearly through the ambient noise of the restaurant. <aac>The San Francisco developers used under-specced wiring because they were vain and desperate to win a race. Sterling didn't care about the race. He only cared about building a sanctuary.</aac>

"Which is exactly why he bankrolled the movie," the curvy redhead added, leaning over the table. "He wanted to show the world what happens when you prioritize vanity over safety."

"And the ultimate ironic flex?" Isabel grinned, gesturing dramatically up at the towering, seventeen-story brutalist ceiling of the hotel lobby they were currently sitting in. "Sterling rented out this exact atrium. Right here. They filmed all the lavish party scenes and the lobby scenes for the *Crown Point* movie in this room, just to visually capture the sheer, excessive vanity of the doomed tower."

Luna kicked Leo gently under the table, her eyes wide with excitement. They were sitting in the actual filming location of their favorite disaster movie, listening to college students debate the exact engineering ethics they had been discussing yesterday at the memorial crater. 

"Well, put it in the outline, Oliver," the brown-haired girl with glasses said, pulling a notebook out of her bag. "I'll start drafting the introduction. Eleanor, you pull up the real-world casualty reports from seventy-four."

Leo watched the four cousins—Isabel, Eleanor, Oliver, and Sophia—dive enthusiastically into their college project. He tapped his NovaVox happily. 

<aac>I like this hotel,</aac> his device announced.

## 9:15 AM — AAC Solidarity

As the college students wound down their intense debate about the ethics of the Crown Point timeline, the young man in the motorized wheelchair finally turned his head. 

Through his thick prescription glasses, his eyes locked directly onto Leo at the adjacent table. Oliver didn't look at Leo's face first. His eyes tracked down to the manual wheelchair, and then immediately settled on the NovaVox tablet resting in Leo's lap. 

Oliver's face broke into a gentle, knowing smile. He reached down to the joystick on his armrest and expertly pivoted his heavy, motorized chair, bridging the gap between the two tables. 

His fingers danced across the black keyboard of his MagnaByte. 

<aac>I saw you listening to us,</aac> Oliver's laptop announced in its deep, synthesized voice. <aac>Are you Crown Point enthusiasts?</aac>

Leo grinned. He pulled the NovaVox closer to his chest and typed back rapidly. 

<aac>We went to the memorial crater yesterday,</aac> Leo's device replied, its voice slightly higher-pitched than Oliver's. <aac>We were just talking about Arthur Sterling's timeline. Your paper outline is completely historically accurate.</aac>

At the sound of Leo's synthesized voice, Isabel, Eleanor, and Sophia all turned around in their booth, beaming warmly at the interaction. 

<aac>Thank you,</aac> Oliver's laptop said. <aac>It is nice to meet a fellow AAC user in the wild. I am Oliver. These are my cousins, Isabel and Eleanor, and my twin sister, Sophia.</aac>

<aac>I am Leo,</aac> Leo typed back, feeling a sudden, deep sense of camaraderie with this stranger. It wasn't often he met another guy his age who relied entirely on a screen to speak. <aac>This is my twin sister, Luna, my older sister, Stella, and my best friend, Olivia. We are from the East Coast.</aac>

"Rhode Island, originally," Stella clarified with a polite smile, raising her coffee cup toward the college students. "But we live in Virginia Beach now."

"No way," the curvy redhead, Eleanor, gasped, her eyes going wide. "We're from Norfolk! Ocean View!"

"You're kidding," Olivia laughed, leaning forward. "We flew three thousand miles across the country just to sit next to our next-door neighbors?"

Oliver's eyes scanned the lightweight, folding frame of Leo's manual wheelchair, and then looked back up at Leo. 

<aac>You travel very light,</aac> Oliver typed, his laptop projecting the observation across the tables. <aac>My chair weighs three hundred pounds. Flying with it is a logistical nightmare.</aac>

*It folds in half,* Leo replied, gesturing to the cross-braces under his seat. *It is much easier to travel with. My twin sister has CP too, but she is ambulatory. She pushes the chair because she uses it like a walker for balance.*

Oliver nodded understandingly, a quiet, profound respect passing between the two men. They didn't need to explain the complex, invisible logistics of their lives to each other. They just got it. 

<aac>Enjoy the Sovereign Regency,</aac> Oliver's laptop announced as the college students began to gather their bags and notebooks to leave for class. <aac>Take the glass elevators to the top. The view of the bay is completely frictionless.</aac>

<aac>Good luck on your engineering paper,</aac> Leo typed back. 

As Oliver smoothly reversed his motorized chair and rolled out of the restaurant surrounded by his sister and cousins, Leo felt a massive wave of contentment. It was a brief, fleeting interaction, but seeing another guy out in the world thriving with his family and a text-to-speech engine made Leo feel incredibly grounded.

## 10:00 AM — The Ferry Building

As Oliver and the college students disappeared into the sprawling brutalist lobby, Leo looked down at his NovaVox. His fingers tapped a quick, hopeful rhythm against the glass. 

<aac>I want to go to the pool,</aac> the device announced. <aac>Swimming.</aac>

Stella winces, her coffee cup pausing halfway to her mouth. "Oh, buddy," she said gently, her voice incredibly soft. "This hotel doesn't have a pool."

Leo froze. 

The disappointment hit him like a physical blow. Because of his cerebral palsy and the immense toll gravity took on his spine, the weightless, floating sensory relief of a swimming pool was his absolute favorite thing in the world. He had built the idea of swimming into his mental schedule for their rest day. 

His chest tightened. His breathing suddenly hitched, his eyes darting frantically around the massive, cavernous atrium. The sheer scale of the 17-story ceiling suddenly felt suffocating rather than awe-inspiring. He was tipping dangerously close to the edge of a full-blown meltdown. 

Stella immediately pushed her chair back, her hands hovering inches from his shoulders, fully prepared to catch him and initiate their strict lockdown protocol to get him safely back up to the suite. 

But Olivia was faster. 

She stood up from the table, stepped directly behind his wheelchair, and wrapped her arms fiercely around his chest. She locked him into a deep, crushing, full-body hug. She didn't say a word to try to rationalize with him; she just provided the heavy, grounding physical pressure his nervous system was screaming for. Olivia rested her chin firmly on his shoulder, holding him tight. 

Leo squeezed his eyes shut. He gripped Olivia's forearms, leaning his head back against her chest. Slowly, the erratic hitch in his breathing smoothed out. The deep pressure therapy acted like a reset button, pulling him back from the tipping point. 

"I'm sorry, Leo," Stella murmured, her hand resting on his knee. "I know how much you wanted to swim. We'll make sure the next hotel has one. But for today, how about we just go for a quiet roll along the water?"

Leo took one last, shaky breath, patted Olivia's arm in thanks, and gave Stella a small nod. 

Ten minutes later, the pack rolled through the revolving glass doors of the hotel and out into the bright, crisp morning air of the Embarcadero. True to Stella's promise, the waterfront promenade was beautifully flat and paved. There wasn't a single hill in sight. 

Directly across the wide boulevard sat the massive, historic Ferry Building. It had just finished a massive, four-year renovation, transforming the interior into a world-class artisanal food marketplace. 

"Let's see what they've got," Luna said cheerfully, pushing Leo's manual chair toward the heavy glass entrance doors. 

Stella pulled the heavy door open, and Luna pushed Leo inside. 

The noise hit them like a physical wall. 

The massive, cavernous corridor of the Ferry Building was packed shoulder-to-shoulder with thousands of locals and tourists. The deafening roar of hundreds of overlapping conversations, shouting vendors, and clinking coffee cups echoed violently off the hard tile floors and the soaring architectural ceilings, creating an absolute acoustic nightmare. 

Leo flinched violently, his hands flying up to cover his ears as his face contorted in pain. 

Stella didn't hesitate for a single fraction of a second. 

"Nope. Absolutely not," Stella barked. She grabbed the front frame of Leo's wheelchair, violently pivoting the front casters 180 degrees. "Out. Everybody out."

Luna immediately shoved the chair back through the doors, practically sprinting to get Leo out of the echoing building. 

The heavy glass doors swung shut, abruptly cutting off the roar of the marketplace. The pack stood on the quiet concrete of the Embarcadero, the gentle breeze coming off the bay washing over them. Leo slowly lowered his hands from his ears, breathing heavily. 

"Okay," Stella sighed, crossing her arms and looking out at the water. "We are not doing that. Let's just look at the boats."

## 10:15 AM — Engineering Failures

As Leo slowly lowered his hands from his ears, the quiet hum of a heavy motorized wheelchair pulled up alongside them. 

It was Oliver, Sophia, Isabel, and Eleanor. They had clearly just left the hotel to come check out the Ferry Building as well. 

Isabel, the skinny redhead, took one look at Leo's pale face and Luna's deep, unsettled grimace. She glanced at the heavy glass doors of the marketplace, then back at Stella. 

"Acoustic nightmare?" Isabel asked, raising an eyebrow. 

"Like being inside a snare drum," Stella confirmed. "We didn't even make it ten feet."

Isabel nodded decisively. She immediately turned to her sister and her cousins. Oliver, Sophia, and Eleanor were all autistic. As the sole neurotypical member of their pack, Isabel knew exactly how to read the environment. If the sensory feedback was aggressive enough to send Leo and Luna running back out the doors, it would absolutely trigger a sensory overload for her sister and cousins. 

"Change of plans," Isabel announced cheerfully. "We are skipping the Ferry Building."

"Good call," Eleanor agreed, her shoulders already dropping in relief. 

<aac>We can walk the promenade,</aac> Oliver's laptop suggested in its deep, synthesized voice. 

The two groups merged, forming a massive, eight-person pack as they began a slow, leisurely stroll down the wide, paved expanse of the Embarcadero. The cool breeze coming off the bay was incredibly soothing, washing away the lingering anxiety of the near-meltdown from earlier. 

"It's hard to believe this whole area used to be covered in concrete," Sophia noted, pushing her glasses up the bridge of her nose as she looked out at the water. "There used to be a massive, ugly, double-decker freeway right where we are walking."

"The Embarcadero Freeway," Stella nodded. "They tore it down after the eighty-nine earthquake, right?"

"The Loma Prieta earthquake," Eleanor clarified. "It heavily damaged the structure. But honestly, tearing it down was the best thing the city ever did. It opened up the entire waterfront."

Leo tapped on his NovaVox. <aac>Did it collapse like the one in Oakland?</aac>

Oliver's motorized chair slowed to a stop. His fingers flew across his MagnaByte keyboard. 

<aac>No. The Embarcadero Freeway was damaged, but it didn't pancake,</aac> Oliver's laptop announced. <aac>The I-880 Cypress Street Viaduct in Oakland was a catastrophic failure. We actually just wrote another paper for our Ethics in Engineering class about it.</aac>

Leo grinned, deeply fascinated. He loved mechanical and structural disaster analysis. *What happened?*

<aac>Caltrans cut corners,</aac> Oliver explained, his synthesized voice echoing calmly over the sound of the seagulls. <aac>The double-decker structure was built on soft mud, which amplified the seismic waves. But the real ethical failure was the support columns. They lacked adequate steel rebar reinforcement. When the shaking started, the concrete simply sheared apart, and the upper deck pancaked onto the lower deck.</aac>

"They knew the design was flawed," Sophia added quietly. "But it was cheaper to build it that way."

Leo shook his head, typing rapidly. *Just like Crown Point.*

*Exactly like Crown Point,* Oliver agreed. *Vanity and budget over safety.*

The two families strolled together for another twenty minutes, perfectly content to enjoy the quiet, zero-impact waterfront while trading architectural trivia. Eventually, they reached a crosswalk near one of the piers, and Isabel checked her watch. 

"We've got to get to a lecture," Isabel said, turning to Stella. "Hey, since we're basically neighbors back in Hampton Roads, let me give you my number. We should absolutely grab coffee when we all get back to Virginia."

Stella beamed, pulling out her flip phone. "I would love that."

They traded numbers, and the two packs said their goodbyes. Leo and Oliver shared one final, respectful nod before Oliver turned his heavy chair and rolled down the street with his family. 

"They were really nice," Olivia smiled, resting her hand on Leo's shoulder. 

"Yeah, they were," Stella agreed. She looked down at Leo, noting the heavy exhaustion still lingering behind his eyes from the morning's emotional rollercoaster. "Alright. We had breakfast, we met our neighbors, and we got some fresh air. Back to the hotel."

Luna happily spun Leo's manual chair around, and the Fortress Four headed back across the street to the Sovereign Regency, perfectly content to retreat to their room and spend the rest of the day resting.

## 7:00 PM — The Fourth of July Realization

The evening settled over San Francisco, bringing a thick, comforting fog that rolled off the bay and wrapped around The Sovereign Regency. Inside their corner suite, the curtains were drawn tight. The ambient lighting was turned down low, casting a warm, muted glow across the plush carpet. 

After the auditory assault of the Embarcadero, the pack was in full decompression mode. They had ordered a massive spread of room service—baskets of fries, grilled cheese sandwiches for Luna, a custom pureed soup for Leo, and a decadent slice of chocolate cake that Stella and Olivia were splitting. 

Leo was out of his chair, sprawled comfortably on the thick duvet of the master bed. Olivia sat right beside him, cross-legged, leaning against the headboard as they watched a muted nature documentary on the television. 

Down on the floor, Luna was meticulously arranging a set of colorful dominoes in a spiraling pattern, enjoying the quiet, predictable clack of the plastic tiles. 

Stella was at the small writing desk, casually flipping through a local tourism brochure. "You know, despite the sensory landmine down by the Ferry Building, this is a really beautiful city," she remarked softly, taking a sip of her coffee. "We still have two full days left before we have to pack up and head to the ARTS station for the Coast Starlight."

She flipped a page in the brochure. A brightly colored advertisement spanned the centerfold: a massive, bursting firework over the Golden Gate Bridge, with bold text that read: **San Francisco's Annual Fourth of July Extravaganza! Join us on the waterfront for the loudest, brightest show on the West Coast!**

Stella froze. Her coffee cup halted halfway to her mouth. 

She slowly lowered the cup and stared at the date on her watch. It was July 2nd. 

"Oh my god," Stella whispered, the color draining from her face. 

Olivia looked over from the bed, sensing the sudden shift in Stella's posture. "What is it?"

"The Fourth of July," Stella said, her voice tight with rising panic. "It's the day after tomorrow."

Luna paused her domino building, looking up with a slight frown. 

"We're on the waterfront," Stella continued, turning the brochure around so Olivia could see the advertisement. "We are staying in a high-rise hotel right next to the Embarcadero. They shoot the fireworks off barges in the bay. It's going to sound like a warzone."

Leo's eyes went wide. He hated sudden, loud noises more than anything. Thunderstorms were bad enough, but fireworks—especially professional, concussive fireworks meant to echo across a massive body of water—were paralyzing. The pressure changes alone would feel like physical blows to his highly sensitive nervous system.

<aac>Too loud,</aac> Leo typed quickly on his MagnaByte Scribe, his fingers trembling slightly. <aac>Will it shake the glass?</aac>

"I don't know, buddy," Stella admitted, running a hand through her hair. "But it's going to be incredibly loud. And there will be crowds everywhere outside. We can't go anywhere."

Olivia reached out, placing a firm, grounding hand on Leo's shoulder. She could feel the sudden, rigid tension in his muscles. "Hey. We're safe in here. The windows are thick."

"But they're not soundproof," Stella argued, already pacing the short length of the room. "Not against military-grade explosives going off a mile away. What do we do? Do we try to leave early? Change our train tickets?"

"Wait," Olivia said, her eyes lighting up with an idea. She reached into her pocket and pulled out her flip phone. "What about Isabel and her family?"

Stella stopped pacing. "What about them?"

"Isabel gave us her number," Olivia explained, flipping the phone open. "Oliver, Sophia, and Eleanor are all autistic. If Leo and Luna can't handle the fireworks, there is absolutely no way they can handle them either. Especially Oliver, with his acoustic sensitivity. Let's ask them what they're doing. They're college kids, they must have a survival plan."

Stella let out a long breath, a glimmer of hope returning to her eyes. "That's brilliant, Olivia. Text her."

Olivia quickly typed out a message on the T9 keypad. 

*Hey Isabel, it's Olivia (from the Embarcadero). Stella just realized it's almost July 4th and we are right on the water. The fireworks are going to be a sensory nightmare for Leo and Luna. What is your family doing to survive the noise? Are you leaving the city?*

She hit send. 

The suite was quiet for two agonizingly long minutes, save for the soft murmur of the television and the distant hum of the city traffic. Leo watched Olivia's phone intently, his breathing shallow. 

Then, the phone buzzed. 

Olivia flipped it open and read the message aloud. 

*Hi Olivia! We were just talking about that. No, we aren't leaving. Oliver found out that the Sovereign Regency has a sub-basement conference room that is completely interior—no windows, heavy concrete walls. We rented it for the night of the 4th. We are bringing sleeping bags, snacks, and a portable TV to have an indoor 'bunker picnic' while the city explodes outside. Tell Stella you are officially invited.*

Stella slumped against the desk in sheer relief. "Oh, thank God for engineering students."

Leo's shoulders dropped an inch. He looked up at Olivia, a small, hopeful smile breaking through his anxiety. 

<aac>Bunker picnic,</aac> Leo typed, the synthesized voice sounding strangely cheerful in the quiet room. <aac>I like that plan.</aac>
