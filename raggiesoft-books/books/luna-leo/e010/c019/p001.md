## 9:00 AM — Global City

The crushing, 360-degree deep pressure from the dogpile had worked perfectly. When Leo finally woke up on the morning of July 6th, the phantom vibrations of the ARTS Coastliner were completely gone from his bones, replaced by a surge of excitement. 

Today was a theme park day. 

They were heading to **Global Studios Hollywood**, the massive, sprawling movie studio and theme park built into the hills overlooking the San Fernando Valley. 

"Okay, I mapped the transit," Stella said, pacing around the hotel room with her printed LATA map as the rest of the pack tied their shoes and gathered their bags. "And for once, it is incredibly easy. We are taking the LATA Red Line subway from Hollywood/Highland exactly one stop north. We get off at the Global City station, cross the street, and there is a free, accessible open-air shuttle tram that drives us up the massive hill directly to the theme park gates."

"One subway stop and a tram?" Luna asked, raising an eyebrow. "That's it? No changing to three different buses?"

"That's it," Stella confirmed. "The Red Line goes right under the hills."

Half an hour later, they were stepping off the free open-air shuttle tram. The hot, dry Southern California sun beat down on the pavement as they walked toward the massive, iconic gates of Global Studios Hollywood. A giant, spinning metallic globe sat in the center of the entrance plaza, shrouded in mist. 

Olivia pushed Leo's manual wheelchair to the edge of the security checkpoint. "Alright, team," she said, looking down at the heavy park map she had just grabbed from a kiosk. "We have a strategy to figure out."

Global Studios was uniquely built into the side of a steep mountain, naturally dividing the park into two distinct areas: the **Upper Lot** and the **Lower Lot**. 

Connecting the two was the "Starway," a massive, terrifyingly steep, multi-tiered complex of outdoor escalators built into the cliffside. The Starway offered incredible views of the Valley, but it was strictly, legally prohibited for wheelchairs. 

"If we want to go to the Lower Lot," Stella explained, tracing her finger over the map, "we have to find a park attendant and request the ADA shuttle van. It drives you out of the Upper Lot, down the restricted backlot roads, and drops you off at the bottom. It can take a while."

Leo pulled his NovaVox tablet onto his lap, looking at the attraction list. 

<aac>What is even down there?</aac>

"Mostly thrill rides," Luna said, reading over his shoulder. "Cretaceous Island is down there. That's a massive water drop. E.T. Adventure just closed a few months ago for construction. So it's basically just the water ride and a Special Effects stage."

She pointed to the top half of the map. "But the Upper Lot has everything else. The World Famous Studio Tour, the WaterWorld stunt show, Animal Actors, and Terminator 2: 3D. All the big shows and tours."

Olivia rested her hands on Leo's wheelchair grips. "So, what's the call, Leo? Do we deal with the ADA shuttle to get to the bottom, or do we stay up here and dominate the shows?"

***

## 10:30 AM — The Starway

Even if the Lower Lot was mostly just a water thrill ride and construction zones, Stella insisted they had to go down there. 

"We paid a fortune for these tickets," she argued, adjusting her sunglasses as they navigated through the crowds of the Upper Lot. "We are experiencing the entire park. Even if it's just to eat lunch and come right back up."

They followed the signs pointing toward the Lower Lot until the pavement abruptly ended at the edge of the mountain. Leo pushed his wheels forward, resting his hands on the glass barrier overlooking the valley. 

"Whoa," Luna breathed, leaning over the railing next to him. 

The "Starway" was a massive, multi-tiered complex of steep, glass-enclosed escalators built directly into the side of the cliff. It looked less like a theme park attraction and more like a piece of brutalist industrial architecture. Below them, stretching out for miles, were the massive, beige soundstages of the working studio and the sprawling cityscape of the San Fernando Valley.

Leo looked at the terrifyingly steep angle of the moving stairs, then looked down at his wheelchair. He tapped his tablet. 

<aac>I'm not doing that.</aac>

"Nobody is doing that," Olivia said, resting a hand on his shoulder. "I'm strong, Leo, but I am not carrying you down four flights of moving stairs. That's a death wish."

Instead, they followed the blue wheelchair-accessible signs to a nearby elevator. It took them down to a ground-level staging area where a dedicated, air-conditioned ADA shuttle van was waiting. A park attendant quickly locked Leo's chair into the van's tie-downs, and they took a brief, winding drive down a restricted backlot road, bypassing the cliff entirely. 

When the shuttle dropped them off in the Lower Lot, the air was noticeably warmer and significantly louder. The area was dominated by the massive, tropical-themed infrastructure of the *Cretaceous Island* water ride. Massive ferns and piped-in dinosaur roars surrounded the concrete pathways. 

"Okay, so we can't ride the dinosaur ride unless we want to get completely drenched," Stella said, looking around. "And the E.T. ride is boarded up for construction. So what exactly do we do down here?"

Luna pointed to a large, open-air food pavilion nearby. The sign read *The Studio Commissary*. "We eat."

They grabbed a table under a massive canvas umbrella while Olivia and Stella braved the line for food. They returned ten minutes later carrying a tray loaded with aggressively overpriced theme park food: massive, greasy turkey legs, heavily salted fries, and oversized sodas in souvenir cups. 

They ate lunch near the railing of the Cretaceous Island ride's final splashdown zone. Every few minutes, a massive boat full of screaming tourists would plunge down an eighty-foot waterfall, sending a massive tidal wave of water crashing against the retaining wall. 

Leo gnawed on a massive turkey leg, watching the riders stumble out of the exit gates completely soaked to the bone. He watched the massive yellow boats gently float along the track before the final drop. It wasn't a violent, jerking roller coaster. It was just a slow boat ride with one big, straight plunge.

He swallowed a bite of turkey and pulled his NovaVox onto his lap. 

<aac>I want to ride it.</aac>

Luna nearly choked on her soda. "Wait. Seriously? You hate drops."

<aac>It's just a slow boat. No sharp turns. I can do it.</aac>

Olivia grinned, cracking her knuckles. "Alright then. We're doing the dinosaur ride."

***

## 11:30 AM — Cretaceous Island: The Ride

Because the ride was primarily a slow-moving boat, it was surprisingly accessible. An attendant guided them to a specialized ADA loading dock, temporarily halting the massive conveyor belt so the yellow boat was completely still in the water. 

Olivia quickly helped Leo transfer out of his manual wheelchair and over the gunwale of the boat. He slid onto the hard plastic bench in the middle row. To ensure he didn't slide around or lose his balance, Olivia sat tightly against his right side, while Stella immediately took his left, physically wedging him securely in the center. Luna took the row directly behind them.

"You realize," Stella said over the loud, pulsing drumbeat of the ride's thematic music, "that by putting you in the middle, Olivia and I are going to take the absolute brunt of the water."

Leo grinned, giving her a thumbs-up. 

The boat lurched forward, drifting smoothly out of the loading dock and into a massive, heavily forested outdoor river. A dramatic orchestral score swelled from hidden speakers as giant wooden gates slowly groaned open before them.

The first few minutes of the ride were exactly as Leo had hoped. The boat drifted slowly and gently past massive, towering animatronic dinosaurs grazing in the water. There were no sudden jerks, no violent lateral forces, and no spinning. It was a perfectly calm, scenic float. 

But as they drifted deeper into the ride, the music turned ominous. The boat was bumped slightly off course, floating into a massive, dimly lit industrial building. The gentle river ride was over. 

"Okay," Olivia said, tightening her grip on the safety bar and leaning her shoulder heavily against Leo to brace his core. "Here comes the bad part."

The boat engaged with a heavy metal chain, slowly and loudly clicking its way up a massive, terrifyingly steep incline in the dark. Red emergency lights flashed around them as alarms blared. The higher they climbed, the tighter Leo gripped the lap bar, his knuckles turning white. 

They reached the top of the lift hill, plunging into total darkness. 

Suddenly, a massive, terrifying animatronic T-Rex dropped down from the ceiling directly in front of them, letting out a deafening, bass-heavy roar.

Before Leo could even process the sensory assault, the boat plummeted. 

The eighty-four-foot drop was blindingly fast. Leo felt his stomach shoot directly into his throat as gravity vanished. He squeezed his eyes shut and screamed, a raw, breathless sound that was instantly drowned out by the roar of the water.

They smashed into the splashdown pool at the bottom of the drop. A massive, towering wall of water displaced by the heavy boat crashed completely over them. 

Leo gasped, choking on a mouthful of chlorinated water as the boat slowly drifted toward the exit dock. He was completely, entirely drenched. His t-shirt was plastered to his chest, and water dripped heavily off his nose. 

He looked to his right. Olivia's hair was plastered against the side of her face. He looked to his left. Stella was wiping a massive sheet of water off her sunglasses, her jacket completely soaked through. 

"Well," Stella coughed, sputtering slightly. "I think it's safe to say we're sufficiently cooled off."

Leo burst into laughter. It was a rare, breathless, full-body laugh that shook his shoulders. He had survived the drop, and he had loved every terrifying second of it.

***

## 1:00 PM — The Studio Tour

After wringing the excess water out of their shirts, the pack wheeled over to the base of the Starway to wait for the ADA shuttle van back to the Upper Lot. 

Standing at the bottom of the cliff, the Starway looked even more terrifying than it had from the top. The massive, glass-enclosed escalators stretched hundreds of feet up the steep mountainside, disappearing into the concrete overhangs of the Upper Lot. 

Luna craned her neck, shielding her eyes from the sun. "That is absurdly high. If you fell down those stairs, you wouldn't just break a leg. You'd break every bone in your body."

"Which is exactly why the ADA shuttle is a brilliant invention," Stella said, crossing her arms as a white, air-conditioned van pulled into the loading zone. 

The ride back to the Upper Lot was smooth and peaceful. Once Leo was unclipped from the tie-downs, they made a beeline straight for the park's signature attraction: **The World Famous Studio Tour**. 

Because Leo was in a wheelchair, they didn't have to wait in the massive, winding queue. An attendant directed them to a special accessible loading area where the first car of a multi-car tram was waiting. A heavy mechanical lift hoisted Leo's wheelchair directly onto the back row of the tram. Once his brakes were locked in, Olivia sat directly next to him, wrapping a warm, protective arm around his shoulders. Stella and Luna took the row in front of them. 

"Welcome to the Global Studios Backlot!" the enthusiastic tour guide announced over the speakers, pointing to the small LCD screens mounted above the seats. "Keep your hands and arms inside the tram, because things are about to get wild."

Leo leaned his head against Olivia's shoulder, completely relaxed as the tram rumbled out of the loading dock and down into the sprawling, 400-acre movie studio. 

For the next forty-five minutes, Leo was in absolute heaven. It was the perfect ride—a slow, smooth drive through massive, intricate movie sets with thrilling, immersive practical effects. 

He gripped Olivia's arm in awe as the tram drove into a dark soundstage. Suddenly, the entire tram began to violently shake. The ceiling of the simulated BATS Embarcadero Station caved in, sparks flew, and a massive wall of water rushed down the stairs in a terrifyingly realistic 8.3-magnitude earthquake. 

A few minutes later, the tram drove through a quiet, New England fishing village. Leo's eyes widened behind his thick glasses as the dorsal fin of a massive mechanical shark sliced through the water, followed by a sudden, intense wall of real fire erupting from a nearby gas dock. 

But the absolute peak of the tour happened in total darkness. The tram drove into a massive, heavily themed New York City soundstage. The smell of banana breath filled the air before a gigantic, thirty-foot animatronic King Kong roared from the shadows, violently shaking the bridge their tram was parked on. 

Leo laughed into Olivia's shoulder as a simulated helicopter crashed just feet away from them. He loved every explosion, every sudden burst of water, and every massive animatronic monster. It was thrilling, sensory-heavy chaos, but because he was securely anchored against Olivia on the slow-moving tram, he felt entirely, perfectly safe.

***

## 2:15 PM — Timeline Paradox

"I am not letting you skip it," Leo typed aggressively on his NovaVox, holding the screen up for Luna to read. "We have to stop the bully from altering the timestream continuum. This is heavy."

They were standing outside the massive Institute of Future Technology building, home to the Upper Lot's flagship motion simulator: **Timeline Paradox: The Ride**. 

"Leo, it's a motion simulator," Luna argued, looking at the warning signs. "It's violent, it jerks you around, and it's basically a roller coaster in a box. You hate roller coasters."

<aac>It's not a real roller coaster. It's hydraulics. And I love the movies. I'm riding it.</aac>

Olivia cracked her knuckles. "He's the boss. Let's go save the timestream."

Because of the tight loading garages, Leo had to leave his manual wheelchair parked out on the main concourse with an attendant. Olivia effortlessly deadlift carried him through the short queue and into the small, dark garage, depositing him securely in the back seat of the modified, silver sports car. She sat tightly next to him, and Stella pulled the gull-wing door shut.

"Commencing temporal jump," the video screen on the dashboard announced as the car suddenly rumbled to life. 

The garage doors opened, and the entire car lifted eight feet into the air, pushing forward into a massive, multi-story wraparound projection dome. The simulated wind blasted their faces as the car banked sharply, chasing the villain through the prehistoric ice age. Leo gripped the armrests, laughing at the sheer sensory overload. 

And then, everything went black. 

The massive projection screen died. The hydraulic pistons under the car let out a loud, whining hiss. The car dropped abruptly and violently back down into the garage with a heavy *thud*, rattling everyone's teeth.

The bright, harsh fluorescent work lights clicked on overhead. 

"Attention time travelers," a bored voice announced over the intercom. "We have experienced a technical fault. Please remain seated. An attendant will assist you with an evacuation."

"Well," Stella sighed, rubbing her neck. "That was anti-climactic."

A minute later, a park attendant manually popped the gull-wing door open. "Sorry folks, ride's down. We have to evacuate the building immediately through the emergency exits. I can go grab the wheelchair, but it's parked all the way on the upper concourse—"

"Don't worry about it," Olivia said, immediately unbuckling her seatbelt. 

She leaned into the tight back seat, slipped her arms under Leo's back and knees, and hoisted him out of the sports car in one fluid, effortless deadlift. She carried him out of the garage, down a flight of concrete maintenance stairs, and out into the blazing California sun, barely breaking a sweat. They retrieved his wheelchair from the concourse attendant just a minute later. 

Because of the e-stop, a deeply apologetic manager handed them four golden "Front-of-the-Line" re-ride coupons. 

They killed the next thirty minutes taking a bathroom break and wandering through the sci-fi gift shop, letting the adrenaline of the sudden drop fade. Leo bought a replica license plate before proudly leading the pack back to the ride entrance. 

They handed over their golden coupons, loaded back into a fresh sports car, and this time, the timestream continuum was saved without a single hitch.

***

## 4:00 PM — AquaWorld

With the timestream continuum successfully saved, the pack was officially ready to call it a day. But before they left the Upper Lot, Stella checked the park map and noticed one final showtime was about to start. 

"AquaWorld: A Live Sea War Spectacular," she read aloud. "It's a massive outdoor stunt show. Jet skis, explosions, the works."

"I'm in," Olivia said immediately, grabbing the handles of Leo's wheelchair. "Let's go blow some stuff up."

They made their way into the massive, rusted metal arena. The accessible seating was located near the top of the stadium bleachers, which Leo was incredibly grateful for. The first five rows of the stadium were painted bright neon blue, explicitly labeled as the "Splash Zone." Having already spent the early afternoon wringing chlorinated water out of their shirts, nobody in the pack had any desire to get soaked again. 

As the stadium filled up, Leo pulled his heavy noise-canceling headphones over his ears. He loved explosions, but he hated the sharp, piercing crack of pyrotechnics. 

The show was absolute, unadulterated chaos. 

Actors in post-apocalyptic costumes zipped around the massive pool of water on jet skis, performing dizzying spins and jumps. Stuntmen fell from towering rusted metal platforms, plunging sixty feet into the water below. 

Even with his headphones securely on, Leo could feel the massive percussive thump of the fireballs in his chest. Every time a gas line ruptured or a massive tower exploded, he felt a wave of intense, radiating heat wash over the audience. 

But the absolute peak of the show caught everyone off guard. 

In the final, chaotic battle sequence, a massive, full-scale seaplane suddenly launched over the metal wall of the arena. It soared through the air on a hidden track, crashing spectacularly into the water directly in front of the audience. A massive tidal wave of water completely drenched the neon blue bleachers, followed by a final, massive fireball that lit up the late afternoon sky. 

The crowd erupted into a deafening standing ovation. Leo clapped wildly, his eyes wide with adrenaline. 

"Well," Stella laughed, helping Olivia guide Leo's wheelchair toward the exit ramps as the crowd filtered out. "I think that officially concludes our rides and shows."

"But not our wallets," Luna pointed out, gesturing toward the massive neon-lit entrance plaza. "We still need dinner, and we haven't bought any horribly overpriced souvenirs yet."

"Fair point," Stella agreed. "Let's hit the shops before the park closes."

***

## 8:00 PM — The Shipping Box

They spent the final hours of sunlight wandering through the neon-lit promenade outside the park gates, eating dinner at a loud, chaotic burger joint and spending far too much money at the flagship souvenir store. 

By the time they finally rode the LATA Red Line subway back to Hollywood/Highland and collapsed into Room 412 at the Voyage Inn Express, it was well past dark. They were exhausted, their feet ached, and they smelled faintly of theme park pyrotechnics. 

"Alright," Stella sighed, kicking off her shoes and pulling a flattened cardboard box out of her suitcase. She quickly popped it open and taped the bottom shut. "Souvenir drop. Let's get everything packed up."

It was a standard pack travel strategy. Rather than hauling heavy merchandise across the country on trains and planes, Stella always shipped a box of their souvenirs back to their house in Virginia Beach. 

Luna tossed her new Global Studios hooded sweatshirt into the box. Stella carefully wrapped a ceramic coffee mug in hotel towels and placed it in the corner. Leo proudly dropped his replica *Timeline Paradox* license plate and a handful of studio magnets into the center. 

Then, Leo looked up at Olivia. She was still sitting on the edge of the queen bed, her small plastic shopping bag resting on her lap. 

Leo pointed at her bag, then pointed at the cardboard box. 

Olivia looked down at the bag, then offered Leo a small, deeply sad smile. "I'm not shipping mine to Virginia Beach, Leo," she said softly. "I'm staying here on the West Coast."

The words hit Leo like a physical punch to the chest. 

He knew she was leaving. He knew she was moving to Lynnwood, Washington in August. They had talked about it for months. But seeing the physical, tangible separation of their belongings—watching his items go into a box bound for home, while hers stayed completely separate—shattered the carefully constructed wall in his mind. 

It wasn't an abstract future anymore. It was real. The clock was aggressively ticking down to August. The pack was breaking up. 

A sharp, high-pitched whine escaped Leo's throat. His hands flew up to his ears, pressing hard against his head. He dropped his tablet onto the carpet and began to rock back and forth violently, his breathing instantly accelerating into a shallow, ragged hyperventilation. 

"Leo—" Luna started, her eyes widening. 

He shook his head aggressively, frantically gesturing <sgn>NO, NO, NO</sgn> with uncoordinated hands. The sensory overload of the long day, combined with the crushing, overwhelming grief of Olivia's imminent departure, completely short-circuited his brain. He let out a loud, heartbroken sob, unable to stop the sudden, terrifying spiral of a full autistic meltdown. 

Olivia didn't hesitate. She dropped her bag onto the floor, crossed the room in two strides, and dropped to her knees in front of his wheelchair. 

"I'm right here," she said firmly, grabbing his shaking hands and pulling them away from his ears. She wrapped her strong arms around his chest and pulled him forward, pressing his head tightly against her shoulder. "I'm right here, Leo. I'm not gone yet."

Leo buried his face into her neck, his body trembling violently as he sobbed. He clutched the back of her shirt with white-knuckled desperation, terrified that if he let go, she would disappear. 

Stella and Luna quietly sat down on the floor next to them, pressing their hands against his legs, offering silent, grounding pressure. They didn't try to stop him from crying. They just sat together in the quiet hotel room, holding the pack together for as long as they possibly could.
