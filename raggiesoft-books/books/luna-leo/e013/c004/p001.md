## 7:00 AM — Assembling the Fortress

On Monday morning, December 1st, 2003, Stella walked into the downtown Norfolk law office of Sarah Jenkins. She was not alone; Arthur and Eleanor Bennett were with her, their presence a silent, solid wall of support. Stella carried a thick, meticulously organized folder.

Sarah Jenkins greeted them with a warm but professional expression, ushering them into a conference room. "Stella, Mr. and Mrs. Bennett. Please, have a seat. I got your message. Tell me what's happened."

Stella took a deep breath and opened the folder on the polished mahogany table. "It's worse than we thought," she began, her voice steady and devoid of hysterics. She was all business. "Delores Mahone took Leo to Washington State, and we had to execute an emergency rescue."

For the next half hour, Stella laid out the case. She presented the printed, harrowing testimony Leo had written on his MagnaByte Opus , followed by Olivia's detailed witness statement. She then walked the lawyer through two sets of documentation. The first established their financial damages: the receipts for the emergency rescue flights, new clothes, and the invoice for the destroyed **Quantum Communicator XT**. The second established premeditation: records of the two $99 cross-country Pathfinder bus tickets. 

"Her family is trying to claim she forced Leo to pay for the trip because she's on SSI," Stella explained, her voice tightening. "But she didn't. She drained a massive chunk of her own monthly SSI check to buy those tickets herself, just to trap him."

Sarah Jenkins listened intently, her expression growing grimmer with each new piece of evidence. When Stella was finished, the lawyer looked up, her eyes hard with a professional fury. "Stella, this is... this is more than just a civil matter for damages. The financial costs are clear, but what you're describing—transporting a legally incapacitated adult across state lines without the consent of his legal guardians—that's kidnapping. A federal offense."

"I know," Stella said, her voice like ice. "That's why we're here. We are going after her for everything. We'll file the civil suit for the costs, but I also want to press **criminal charges for kidnapping**."

Just as Sarah was about to respond, the intercom on her desk buzzed. Her assistant's voice was apologetic but firm. "Sarah, I'm sorry to interrupt, but there's a Detective Miller from the Kent, Washington Police Department on the line for a Ms. Stella Porter. He says it's urgent and it concerns Delores Mahone."

Stella, Arthur, and Eleanor froze, exchanging stunned, confused glances. *Kent, Washington? How could they possibly know already?*

Sarah, sensing the sudden shift, immediately pressed a button on her phone. "Put him through to the conference line." A moment later, a man's voice, professional and calm, filled the room.

"Ms. Stella Porter? This is Detective Miller with the Kent Police Department. I'm calling to inform you that we had a Ms. Delores Mahone in custody here in Kent."

Stella blinked at the past tense. "Had?"

"She was turned in by her aunt, Carol Mahone, yesterday morning," the detective continued. "Based on her aunt's testimony and Ms. Mahone's own statements, she was arrested in connection with the abandonment of your brother, Leo Porter, and the destruction of his property. Because this incident involved transporting Mr. Porter across state lines on a Pathfinder bus, we notified the **FBI**, who will be taking lead jurisdiction on the **federal kidnapping charge**."

He paused, a note of professional frustration entering his voice. "However, her parents wired a massive sum to a local bondsman late last night. She posted bail and has been released pending trial. We have reason to believe she is already on her way back to Virginia."

A heavy silence descended on the conference room as the detective finished. Stella could only manage a choked, "Thank you, Detective. My lawyer and I will be in touch."

After the call ended, Sarah Jenkins leaned back in her chair, a look of grim astonishment on her face. "Well," she said slowly. "That certainly accelerates things, but also complicates them. Her own family started the legal process for us, so this is now an active, multi-jurisdictional federal investigation that we are a party to."

"But she's out," Stella said, her voice tight. "She's coming back here."

"Yes," Sarah agreed, her eyes narrowing. "And I need you to be prepared, Stella. Federal kidnapping cases, especially when the defense disputes the victim's competency to testify, can be drawn out endlessly. Her attorney will use every dirty trick in the book—continuances, venue disputes, challenging Leo's capacity on the stand—to drag the pre-trial phase out for years. She'll be technically free on bail, walking the streets of Virginia, while the slow wheels of justice turn."

Stella took a shaky breath, the shock giving way to a cold, hard resolve. She looked at her grandparents, who both gave her a supportive nod. "Good," Stella said. "Now," she said to Sarah, her voice firm with a new sense of purpose, "is there anything else?"

"Yes, actually. This whole incident has made it clear that we need to strengthen Luna and Leo's long-term protection. We would like to formally petition the court to add Olivia Walker as a co-guardian and co-conservator for both of them, with the authority to act jointly and severally, just like us."

Sarah Jenkins blinked, then a small, impressed smile touched her lips. "Given her heroic actions, I think that is an exceptionally wise and forward-thinking decision. I can begin drafting that petition right away."

Leaving the office, Stella felt a dizzying mix of grim satisfaction and shock. The wheels of justice weren't just in motion; they were already spinning at a speed she couldn't have imagined.

## 10:00 AM — Setting the Wheels in Motion

The conference room was filled with a quiet, focused energy after Stella finished laying out the harrowing details of Leo's ordeal and rescue. Arthur and Eleanor sat stoically, their faces grim, while Sarah Jenkins reviewed the folder of evidence Stella had meticulously assembled.

Sarah looked up, her expression one of professional fury. "Stella, this is beyond egregious," she said, her voice sharp. "The civil damages for the cost of the flight, the new clothes, and the replacement of that extremely expensive communication device are clear-cut. We can file that suit immediately. But what you're describing... transporting an incapacitated adult across state lines without the consent of his legal guardians... that's kidnapping. A federal offense."

"I know," Stella said, her voice like ice. "That's why we're here. We are going after her for everything. File the civil suit, yes. But I also want to press **criminal charges**. For kidnapping, for abuse and neglect, for destroying his property... for everything."

Sarah nodded, her expression resolute. "Absolutely. Given the severity and the interstate nature, this will likely become a federal matter involving the FBI. Our first step is to file a formal criminal complaint with the Virginia Beach Police Department. You'll need to give them a full statement, provide copies of Leo's and Olivia's testimonies, and the guardianship papers. Once that report is filed, the VBPD will investigate and, given the circumstances, they will almost certainly refer it to the U.S. Attorney's office and the FBI."

She paused, her expression softening with professional sympathy. "Stella," she continued gently, "there's something else we need to consider, especially for the criminal complaint. Given Delores's possessive behavior and the fact she had him alone for days... we need to be prepared for the possibility... Was there any indication of inappropriate physical contact? We need to ensure the police investigate thoroughly to rule out the worst-case scenario."

A wave of nausea washed over Stella. She thought of the forced kissing Leo had described, the hickey. "He mentioned she kissed him in a way that scared him," Stella said, her voice tight. "We need to make sure... we need to be absolutely certain nothing worse happened."

"Exactly," Sarah said, her voice full of empathy. "It's our duty to ensure the authorities ask the necessary questions, as sensitively as possible, to get the full picture. When Leo gives his formal statement to law enforcement, we will control the circumstances completely. We'll insist on a soft interview room, have you all present, ensure a comfort dog is there. But the questions will have to be asked."

Stella looked at her grandparents, then took a deep, steadying breath, her resolve hardening. "Whatever it takes," she said, her voice low and firm. "We do whatever it takes to make sure she can never hurt him, or anyone else, ever again."

"Good," Sarah said with a nod. "So, the immediate plan is twofold. I will file the civil suit tomorrow morning. You will go to the Virginia Beach Police Department this afternoon to file the formal criminal complaint. The wheels of justice move slowly, Stella, but we are setting them in motion today. This is the start of holding her accountable."

The wait was no longer passive. It was the beginning of an active, determined pursuit of justice, initiated by the fortress itself.

## 10:00 AM — Official Cooperation

It was just after nine o'clock on Monday morning, December 1st, 2003. A quiet tension hung in the house on Stella's house. Stella had filed the official police report late yesterday afternoon. Now, as promised by the detective, the next step was underway. The doorbell rang, and Stella took a deep, steadying breath before opening it. Two FBI agents stood on the porch, a man and a woman, dressed in professional suits. The Virginia Beach PD had, as expected, immediately referred the case.

"Ms. Stella Porter?" the male agent asked. "I'm Special Agent Davies, and this is Special Agent Chen. We're following up on the report you filed yesterday regarding the kidnapping of your brother, Leo Porter."

"Please, come in," Stella said, her voice calm and steady. She led them into the living room, where Luna, Olivia, and Leo were waiting. Leo sat in his wheelchair, Olivia on a stool beside him, her hand resting reassuringly on his arm. The shattered remains of his **Quantum Communicator XT** sat on the coffee table in front of him, carefully placed on a clean cloth.

"Thank you for seeing us on such short notice," Agent Chen began, her gaze soft as she looked at Leo. "We understand this is a very difficult time. We've reviewed the preliminary report and the documents you provided. Today, we need to take formal statements from all of you who were involved in Mr. Porter's rescue, and we need to take the damaged communication device into evidence."

At the mention of taking the device, Leo flinched, his hand instinctively moving towards the broken machine.

Stella stepped forward immediately, positioning herself between the agents and her brother. "Agents, before we go any further," she began, her tone respectful but firm, "you need to understand the significance of that device. It was the first real voice my brother ever had, a gift I gave him after I became his guardian. He has a profound, trauma-related need to know it's being handled with care. He could not part with it until I had seen it myself. We can give it to you, but this needs to be done gently."

Agent Davies nodded, his expression one of complete understanding. "Of course, Ms. Porter. We will be as respectful of the process as possible. We can photograph it here first, then we will bag it for evidence."

For the next hour, they worked. While Agent Chen sat with Olivia and Luna, taking their detailed witness statements, Agent Davies focused on Leo and the evidence. He knelt, taking high-resolution photographs of the shattered device from every angle. Then, he opened a large evidence bag.

Stella knelt in front of Leo. "Okay, buddy," she said softly. "It's time. They've taken pictures. Now they need to take it to make sure Delores can't hurt you again. Can I give it to them for you?"

Leo looked at the broken machine, the symbol of both his liberation and his violation. He took a shaky breath and gave a small, solemn nod. Very carefully, Stella lifted the shattered device and placed it into the evidence bag, which the agent then sealed.

With the evidence secured, it was time for Leo's statement. Agent Davies sat opposite him, his voice calm and patient, while Agent Chen took the lead on the more sensitive questions.

"Leo," Agent Chen began gently, "we have a copy of the testimony you wrote for your sister, and it is incredibly helpful. I just need to ask a few clarifying questions now. Some of them might be difficult, but Stella and Olivia are right here with you. Is it okay if we proceed?"

Leo gave a hesitant nod. With Olivia at his side and Stella nearby, he held his new **MagnaByte Scribe**, his fingers hovering over the screen.

**Agent Chen:** "Thank you. In your statement, you mentioned Delores holding you down and kissing you in a way that felt like choking. Can you tell me if there were other times she touched you in a way you did not like?"

Leo typed slowly, describing the possessive touching and the painful hickey from the Sibling Weekend. The process was slow and emotionally draining, but he got through it.

**Agent Chen:** "Okay, Leo. Thank you for being so brave. Now, your sister expressed concern, and we have a duty to ask. I have to ask one more very important and personal question now. Did Delores ever touch you on the parts of your body that your pajamas cover?"

Leo flinched. It was a sharp, violent recoil, as if he'd been struck. His eyes went wide with a look of pure shock and revulsion. The very idea, the mental image the question created, was horrifying to him. He started to tremble, his gaze darting to Olivia.

The agents saw the flinch but waited, allowing him space. Agent Chen's voice became even softer.

**Agent Chen:** "Leo, I can see that question was very upsetting. It's okay. You are safe here. Can you tell us what happened?"

Leo, shaking his head with frantic energy, began to type on his Scribe. The robotic voice that came out was flat, but the words were a torrent of denial.

**Leo's Scribe:** <aac>No. Never. She never touched me there. Never. That is... Disgusting. No.</aac>

The agents exchanged a look, this time one of dawning understanding. His flinch wasn't a memory of trauma; it was a reaction of pure horror to the concept. He wasn't reliving an act; he was repulsed by the suggestion of it.

**Agent Chen:** "Okay, Leo. Thank you for being so clear with us. We understand. We won't ask you about that again."

When they were finished, Agent Davies stood. "Thank you all for your cooperation," he said. "Ms. Porter, we will begin our formal investigation based on this information. The U.S. Attorney's office will likely be in contact with your lawyer soon regarding the next steps, potentially including scheduling a more formal interview for Leo in a specialized setting. You all did a very brave thing."

After the agents left, a profound quiet settled over the house. The shattered device was gone, its journey as a piece of evidence just beginning. For Leo and his family, the long road to justice, initiated on their own terms, had officially begun.

## 10:00 AM — Ordering a New Voice

The day after the FBI visit, a fragile but determined sense of forward momentum settled over the house on Stella's house. The legal machinery was turning, but Stella's focus was on the most immediate, tangible need: restoring Leo's voice.

She sat at the dining room table, her laptop open, a notepad beside her. "Okay, Leo," she said, her voice gentle but full of purpose. "I'm about to call your speech-language pathologist to get the ball rolling on ordering your new Quantum Communicator. Before I do, is there anything specific you want to ask about? Any new features you've heard of?"

Leo, who was at the table with her, Olivia, and Luna, was quiet for a moment. He looked at Olivia’s laptop, where they had been patiently practicing navigating the Quantum OS XN desktop, then back at his sisters. The fear of modern computers was still there, but it was now tempered by the quiet confidence he'd gained from their patient, no-pressure lessons. He took a deep, steadying breath and picked up his MagnaByte Scribe. His fingers moved with a new, resolute purpose.

The robotic voice that spoke was flat, but the words were a bombshell of personal growth.

<aac>Is there a way... To put a real laptop... On my chair? Not a dedicated machine. I want to try... A real computer. With quantum os xn. The talker can be... An app.</aac>

The room went silent. Stella, Luna, and Olivia stared at him, a wave of stunned pride washing over them. This was a monumental step. After years of relying on the safe, closed-off system of his dedicated devices, and after the trauma of having that voice violently taken from him, he wasn’t asking for a replacement. He was asking for an upgrade. He was choosing to face the very technology that had always been a source of anxiety.

Stella was the first to speak, her voice thick with admiration. "Wow, Leo... that's... that's a really big step. I'm so proud of you for wanting to try."

"Are you sure, Leo?" Luna asked softly, her own pride evident in her expression. "The Quantum OS is still... a lot."

Leo looked at Olivia, who gave him a small, encouraging nod. He typed again on his Scribe. <aac>Olivia and stella's lessons help. I want to try. For school. For work. I need a real computer.</aac>

"Then that's what we'll get you," Stella declared, her decision absolute. A brilliant, determined smile broke across her face. "That is an amazing and forward-thinking idea, Leo."

She immediately picked up the phone and dialed the speech-language pathologist. She explained the situation and relayed Leo's specific request. After a lengthy conversation about durable, medical-grade laptops, mounting hardware for his wheelchair's scissor arm, and the latest version of the **Quantum Communicator** software suite, she hung up with a list of model numbers and suppliers.

Without a moment's hesitation, she turned to her own laptop. "Okay," she announced to the room. "The therapist recommends a specific ruggedized Horizon laptop model. It's expensive, but it's built to last."

Acting as his conservator, she navigated to the supplier's website, entered the model numbers, and filled out the order form. She took the debit card for the twins' trust fund from her wallet and decisively typed in the numbers.

"And... ordered," she said, clicking the final confirmation button. "Paid in full. It should be here in about a week. We're not waiting for insurance. We are not waiting for anyone."

Leo watched her, a profound sense of relief and gratitude washing over him. He wasn't just getting his voice back. With the support of his fortress, he was building a new one—stronger, more capable, and more powerful than ever before.
