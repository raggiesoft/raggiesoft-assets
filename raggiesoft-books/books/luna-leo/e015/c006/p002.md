## 10:00 AM — The Shield

The house was quiet. The afternoon sun filtered through the garden windows, but the living room was dim and cool.

Leo was in a state of profound sensory exhaustion after the Aquarium trip. His nervous system was vibrating, a low-level static that made his skin crawl. He needed heavy input to reset.

He lay on the deep sectional sofa, stomach-to-stomach with Luna.

This time, **Leo was on top**. His weight pressed Luna down into the cushions. Her arms were wrapped tightly around his back, locking him in place. His chin rested on her shoulder, his face buried in the fabric of her shirt.

It was a position of absolute vulnerability and biological necessity. The pressure of his own weight against her, combined with her rhythmic breathing against his chest, was slowly dialing down the static in his brain.

**Olivia** sat on the edge of the sofa, right next to them. She wasn't looking away. She was part of the circuit. Her hand rested firmly on the back of Leo’s neck, her thumb rubbing the tension spot at the base of his skull.

To the family, this was therapy. It was love. It was the "Twin Frequency" doing its work.

*DING-DONG.*

The doorbell rang. It was a sharp, intrusive sound.

Stella was in the kitchen. She walked briskly to the foyer.

She opened the front door.

Standing on the porch was a **Census Worker**—a well-meaning, middle-aged woman with a clipboard and a government ID lanyard.

"Good afternoon," the woman chirped, trying to peer past Stella into the house. "I'm doing the supplementary census for Iron Point. Just need to verify the number of occupants."

The woman craned her neck, looking into the foyer.

**What she saw:** A beautiful, 6-foot-high wall of grey fieldstone. A slate floor. A piece of abstract art hanging on the stone.

**What she DIDN'T see (because of the wall):** She did not see the living room. She did not see a 21-year-old disabled man lying on top of his twin sister. She did not see their non-related female co-guardian rubbing his neck.

If the wall hadn't been there, the Census Worker would have had a direct line of sight to the sofa.

**The Hypothetical Disaster:** If she *had* seen them, her mandatory reporter training would have kicked in. She wouldn't have seen "Grounding." She would have seen "Abuse." She would have seen a guardian failing to protect a ward. She would have walked back to her car and called the police.

**The Reality:** "Four occupants," Stella said calmly, standing in the gap. "Myself, my sister, my brother, and another adult female."

"And are there any children?"

"No," Stella said. "All adults."

"Thank you," the woman said. "Have a nice day."

Stella closed the door. She locked it.

She walked around the wall into the living room.

Leo hadn't moved. Luna was humming a low note. Olivia was still rubbing his neck.

Stella looked at the scene. It was innocent. It was necessary. But to the outside world, it was a crime scene.

"Who was it?" Olivia whispered, not stopping her motion.

"Census," Stella said. "She tried to look in."

Olivia looked up. She realized instantly what the woman would have seen. She looked at her hand on Leo’s neck. She realized how it would look to a stranger—like she was condoning something "wrong."

"The wall?" Olivia asked.

"The wall held," Stella confirmed. "She saw stone. She didn't see the Fortress."

Leo let out a long, shuddering breath, his body finally relaxing into Luna’s. The static was gone.

He tapped his hand against Luna’s back—a signal. *I am back.*

Luna loosened her grip. Olivia helped Leo sit up.

"Safe?" Leo typed on his Scribe, sensing the tension in the room.

"Safe," Stella promised, patting the stone partition that separated their world from the one that would never understand them. "Totally safe."

## 10:00 AM — The Supply Run

The house was hushed, settled into the deep, rhythmic quiet that always followed a period of sensory overload.

In the living room, the scene hadn't changed much since the Census worker’s visit, but the tension had dissipated. The "Twin Frequency" was humming at a steady, low vibration.

Leo lay on top of Luna on the sectional, his weight fully surrendered to her support. His eyes were closed, his breathing deep and even, synced perfectly with his sister’s. The buzzing static of the Aquarium crowds had finally faded, replaced by the physical certainty of gravity and touch.

Olivia sat on the floor beside the sofa, her back resting against the cushions. Her hand was still on Leo’s neck, her thumb working a stubborn knot of tension at the base of his skull. She wasn't trying to fix him; she was just being there, a third point of contact in their closed circuit.

Stella walked quietly down the hall from the East Wing, her car keys in hand. Eleanor followed her, looking refreshed after her afternoon rest.

"We're heading out," Stella whispered, pausing at the edge of the living room. She didn't step fully in; she respected the bubble. "We need to stock the fridge properly. **BayState Market** is good for staples, but Grandma wants to go to **Heritage Market** for the produce."

"And the bakery," Eleanor added softly. "They make a cranberry walnut bread that Arthur loved. I think it would be good for breakfast."

Olivia looked up and nodded. "Take your time. We're good here. He's stabilizing."

"Does he need anything specific?" Stella asked.

"Just quiet," Olivia said. "And maybe some of that **Autocrat** milk when he wakes up."

"On the list," Stella promised.

She looked at the pile of siblings on the sofa. It looked like a tangle of limbs to an outsider, but to her, it looked like a masterfully engineered support structure.

"You're in charge, Liv," Stella said. "Fortress is yours."

"Copy that," Olivia smiled.

Stella and Eleanor walked out to the garage. The heavy, insulated door of the **Holt Sentinel** clicked shut, sealing the house back into silence.

As the garage door rumbled open and the car backed out, Olivia turned back to the twins.

Leo shifted slightly, letting out a long, ragged sigh.

"You okay, *mon chum*?" Olivia whispered.

Leo didn't open his eyes. He just tapped his fingers against Luna’s side—a slow, rhythmic pattern. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

"He says he's landing," Luna murmured, her voice vibrating against Leo’s chest. "The spinning stopped."

"Good," Olivia said, resuming the gentle massage on his neck. "Just breathe. We aren't going anywhere."

For the next hour, the only sounds in the house were the hum of the refrigerator and the soft breathing of the Fortress, holding itself together while the world outside rushed by.

## 10:00 AM — The Return of the Fleet

The "Twin Frequency" had done its work. The static in Leo's brain had cleared, replaced by a heavy, comfortable drowsiness.

He was still lying on top of Luna on the sectional, his weight settling into her. The desperate, medical need for deep pressure had faded, but neither of them made a move to separate. Now, it was simply affection—a silent, gravity-based hug between two halves of the same soul.

Olivia sat on the floor beside them, her back against the sofa. Her hand was still moving in slow, rhythmic circles on Leo’s back, working out the last tension knot near his shoulder blade.

"You good?" Olivia whispered.

Leo didn't type. He just hummed—a low, contented sound that vibrated through Luna’s chest.

*I am good.*

The rumble of the garage door opener broke the silence. The heavy, insulated door whirred upward, then clattered shut.

"Reinforcements," Olivia said softly.

The door from the mudroom opened. Stella and Eleanor walked in, carrying brown paper bags from **Heritage Market**.

The smell hit the room instantly—a warm, yeasty, sweet scent that triggered a memory for everyone.

"We got it," Eleanor announced, pulling a loaf of dark, dense bread from the bag. "Cranberry Walnut. Just like Arthur used to buy on Sunday mornings."

"And the fuel," Stella added, holding up two fresh jugs of **Autocrat** syrup.

Stella walked to the edge of the living room. She saw the pile on the sofa. She smiled.

"Okay, cuddle puddle," Stella said gently. "Time to deploy. We have a dinner to cook, and I need my sous-chefs."

Leo let out a sigh, but he nodded. He looked at Olivia.

"Up," he signaled with a small lift of his chin.

Olivia stood up. She leaned over the sofa.

"Alright, *mon chum*, let's get you vertical."

She slid her arms under his shoulders. Leo braced his core. With practiced ease, she lifted his upper body while Luna gently pushed from beneath. It was a seamless transfer. Olivia pivoted him, and he settled back into the plush seat of his new **Titan X-Treme** power chair, which was parked nearby.

Luna sat up, stretching her arms over her head. "You're getting heavy, Leo."

<aac>Gravity is strong today,</aac> Leo typed on his Scribe, his eyes bright again.

*KNOCK-KNOCK-KNOCK.*

A firm, professional rap sounded at the front door.

Stella checked the time. "That should be the delivery."

She walked to the foyer. The **APS Wall** stood its ground, blocking the view of the living room. Stella stepped around it and opened the door.

A driver in a **Metro Mobility** polo shirt stood there. Next to him, unloaded from a van, was Leo’s **ApexLite Aero Z** manual wheelchair.

"Delivery for Porter?" the driver said. "One manual frame, rigid titanium."

"That's us," Stella said. "You can roll it right in."

The driver wheeled the chair into the foyer. He looked at the stone wall, impressed. "Nice entryway. Keeps the drafts out."

"Keeps everything out," Stella agreed. She signed the clipboard. "Thank you. You saved us a headache trying to get that on the bus."

"No problem. Enjoy the new power chair."

The driver left. Stella rolled the manual chair down the hall to the mudroom. She parked it next to the Titan’s charging bay.

"The fleet is assembled," Stella called out. "Manual chair is docked."

Leo drove his Titan into the kitchen. He looked at the manual chair. It was his "Indoor Slipper." The Titan was his "Hiking Boot." He was glad to have both.


"Okay," Eleanor said, tying on an apron. "First night in the new house. That means we eat like locals."

She unpacked the Heritage Market bags.

"Menu," Eleanor declared. "**Stuffies** for the appetizer. **Johnnycakes** on the griddle. And that Cranberry Walnut bread with real butter."

"Stuffies?" Luna asked.

"Stuffed Quahogs," Stella explained, pulling out a package of the massive, breadcrumb-and-clam-stuffed shells from the deli counter. "It’s a Rhode Island religion. Spicy sausage, clams, peppers, breading, baked in the shell."

They set to work.

Stella managed the oven. Eleanor mixed the cornmeal batter for the johnnycakes (the thin, crispy Rhode Island version, not the fluffy pancake kind). Olivia poured the milk and stirred in the Autocrat until it was the color of toffee.

Leo parked his Titan at the head of the table. He hit the **ELEVATE** button, raising himself up to proper dining height.

He looked at the spread. It wasn't fancy. It was brown, fried, and hearty. It was the food of the coast.

He looked at the **Cranberry Walnut Bread** in the center of the table. He thought of Arthur.

He tapped his Scribe.

<aac>Grandpa would like this menu.</aac>

"He's having a slice right now," Eleanor said, placing the warm bread basket next to the urn on the mantle before bringing a second basket to the table. "Now eat. You need your strength for school."

They ate. The stuffies were spicy and briny. The johnnycakes were crisp and earthy. The coffee milk was sweet.

It was their first meal in the Fortress. They weren't visitors anymore. They were Rhode Islanders. And they were home.
