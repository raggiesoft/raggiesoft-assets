## 7:00 AM — The Ferry and the Fortress

The morning was perfect—bright, clear, and smelling of the sea. The Fortress stood on the platform, ready for their first cross-border expedition since moving in.

**The Team:**

- **Stella:** The General (Logistics/Cash).

- **Olivia:** The Muscle (Transfers).

- **Luna:** The Scout.

- **Leo:** The Pilot (Titan X-Treme).

- **Eleanor:** The Historian (Ready to see her old hometown).

Leo rolled up to the digital departure board. He checked the options.

**ARTS COASTAL 94 (To Boston) - 10:45 AM** **BTA SOUTH SHORE (To Braxton) - 08:22 AM**

<aac>Timing mismatch,</aac> Leo typed on his **Quantum Communicator**. <aac>The silver train is later. the orange train is now.</aac>

"That's the trade-off," Stella said. "The **ARTS** train stops here every time it runs, but it only runs every three hours. The **BTA** runs every thirty minutes. We take the commuter."

The **BTA South Shore Line** train glided in—the familiar orange-and-silver BiLevel commuter workhorse.

They boarded their usual accessible car. "Next stop, **Rockwell**," the conductor announced.

The train pulled out. Unlike the ARTS train, which would have blasted straight through, this train settled into the rhythm of the suburbs. It took 35 minutes of stop-and-go travel, but Leo didn't mind; it gave him time to calibrate his chair settings for the cobblestones ahead.

Finally, the ocean came into view.

*"Now arriving... **Narraganset Esplanade**. Transfer for Metro Blue Line and Harbor Ferries."*

They stepped off at the University station. Instead of heading to the campus, they took the glass elevator down to the **Ferry Dock**.


The **BTA Newport Flyer** (Route F3) was docked. It was a sleek, high-speed catamaran.

They rolled onto the aft deck. The salt spray hit them as the boat accelerated, cutting a white wake across Narragansett Bay.

To their left, the massive suspension span of the **Newport Bridge** rose against the sky, its green towers gleaming.

Eleanor looked toward the west passage. She frowned slightly.

"It looks empty," she said.

"What does?" Stella asked.

"The **Jamestown Bridge**," Eleanor said. "The old one. The 'Bridge to Nowhere.' It's gone."

Stella looked. For years, the sleek new concrete bridge had stood side-by-side with the rusting, narrow steel skeleton of the original 1940 bridge. But today, the water was clear. The old bridge had finally been demolished in April.

"They blew it up this spring," Stella remembered. "While we were packing in Virginia."

"Arthur hated that old bridge," Eleanor smiled, her eyes misty. "He said the lanes were so narrow you had to hold your breath to pass a truck. He would be glad it's gone."

Leo parked his Titan at the rail. He hit the **Elevate** button to see over the side. He looked at the clean horizon where the old steel monster used to be.

He tapped his **Quantum Communicator**.

<aac>Obstacle cleared,</aac> he typed. <aac>View unimpeded.</aac>

"Exactly," Stella said. "Out with the old, in with the new."


The ferry docked at **Perrotti Park**. They rolled off the ramp onto the cobblestones of Newport.

"Gateway Center is right there," Stella pointed. "Let's get the tickets."

They entered the **Newport Gateway Center**. It was the hub of OSTA operations—a bustling bus terminal filled with the blue-and-white state buses.

They went to the **OSTA Information Desk**.

"Five DayTickets," Stella requested.

"That'll be thirty dollars," the clerk said. "Cash only. No discounts for DayTickets."

Stella handed over three crisp ten-dollar bills.

"Here you go," the clerk said, handing over five paper magnetic-stripe cards. "Valid on all OSTA buses until midnight."


As they turned away from the counter, Leo stopped. He tapped his chest.

<aac>Bathroom,</aac> he typed. <aac>Urgent.</aac>

"Okay," Stella said. "Scan for facilities."

They looked around the lobby. They saw two doors: **MEN** and **WOMEN**.

They looked for the third door. The **FAMILY / COMPANION** restroom.

It wasn't there.

"Serious?" Olivia muttered. "It's a transit center. Where is the family room?"

Stella walked over to the clerk. "Excuse me. Do you have a companion restroom?"

"No, ma'am," the clerk said. "Just the main ones. There's a handicap stall in both."

Stella walked back to the group. Her face was tight.

"No family room," she reported. "We have to improvise."

Leo looked at the **Men's Room** door. He couldn't go in there alone. And he certainly couldn't bring Stella, Luna, or Olivia in there.

He looked at the **Women's Room** door.

He looked at his team. He trusted them.

He tapped his screen.

<aac>Protocol b,</aac> he typed. <aac><aac>The women's room.</aac></aac>

"Agreed," Stella said instantly. "It's cleaner, and we control the environment."


Stella went in first. "Clear," she signaled.

They moved as a phalanx. Stella held the door. Olivia pushed Leo’s Titan inside. Luna followed as the rear guard.

A woman washing her hands looked up, surprised to see a young man in a power chair rolling into the women's restroom.

Stella met her gaze. She didn't apologize. She just gave a look that said: *Do not say a word.*

The woman dried her hands quickly and left.

They reached the large stall. They went inside and locked the door.

"Okay," Olivia said, taking charge. "I've got the lift."

Eleanor waited outside the stall door, guarding their privacy.

Inside, Olivia wrapped her arms around Leo’s torso. "One, two, three." She lifted him effortlessly out of the Titan.

Leo let out a breath. It wasn't the ideal setup, but he was safe. He wasn't struggling alone.

When they were done, Olivia lifted him back. Stella adjusted his clothes.

They rolled out of the stall. Leo washed his hands. He didn't look embarrassed. He looked relieved.

They exited the restroom back into the lobby.

"Mission accomplished," Stella said. "Next time, we write a letter to the city about installing a family room."

<aac>Infrastructure failure,</aac> Leo typed. <aac>But the team worked.</aac>

"The team always works," Olivia said. "Now, let's go catch the **Route 67** bus. I believe there is a mansion with our name on it."

## 10:00 AM — The Gilded Age Tour

They caught the **Route 67** bus right outside the Gateway Center. It was a "trolley-replica" bus—wood benches, brass rails, but crucially, a modern wheelchair lift in the back.

Leo rolled into the securement bay. The bus was filled with tourists holding guidebooks.

"Next stop: Bellevue Avenue," the driver announced.

The bus wound its way up the hill, past the wall in front of the former Almacs and onto the legendary avenue. The houses changed from colonial cottages to massive stone palaces set back behind iron gates.

"The Gilded Age," Stella narrated. "Where the robber barons spent their summers."

They saw the massive mansions come into view.

Then, the bus slowed in front of the largest, most imposing structure of them all—a massive Italian Renaissance palazzo overlooking the sea.


"Look at that pile of rocks," Eleanor said, shaking her head.

"It's huge," Luna marveled. "Look at those stairs."

"It actually has an elevator," Eleanor corrected, pointing to the massive limestone facade. "Arthur checked. It was an original, installed when they built it in 1895. He was fascinated by the hydraulics. He said it was the only thing in the house that made sense."

"So you could have gotten upstairs?" Olivia asked.

"Technically," Eleanor smiled. "But Arthur looked at the maintenance schedule for a gold-plated, turn-of-the-century lift and said, 'El, I sell cars. I know a money pit when I see one.' We bought the ranch in Middletown instead. No elevator needed."

Leo looked at the mansion. He appreciated the data point—an 1895 electric elevator was cool. But he saw the gravel paths. He saw the sheer, cold scale of the place.

He tapped his **Quantum Communicator**.

<aac>Good engineering,</aac> he typed. <aac>Bad home.</aac>

"Exactly," Stella said. "Our Fortress fits us. That place fits an ego."


The bus reached the end of the line near **Rough Point**. Usually, tourists would get off here to walk the **Cliff Walk**.

Stella looked at the path entrance. It was narrow, unpaved, and riddled with stairs.

"Cliff Walk is a no-go," Stella said. "And Forty Steps is... well, forty steps."

"We've seen the ocean," Luna said. "I'm hungry."

"Me too," Leo typed.

They stayed on the bus as it looped around. They rode it back down Bellevue, past the mansions again, and got off at the stop across from a familiar landmark on West Main Road.

**The Golden Cow.**


They rolled across the street. The sign was exactly as they remembered it from their trip in 2001: **BAYVIEW CREAMERY**.

They entered. It smelled of burgers and sugar.

They found a large booth. Leo rolled up to the end.

"Okay," Stella said. "It's lunch, but we have a tradition to uphold."

The waitress came over. "What can I get you folks?"

"Five Patty Melts," Stella ordered. "And five **Colossal Coolers**."

"Flavors?"

"Chocolate," Leo typed instantly.

"Coffee," Stella and Eleanor said in unison.

"Strawberry," Luna said.

"Vanilla," Olivia chose.

When the drinks arrived, they were massive—thick, frosty milkshakes that required a spoon.

Eleanor raised her glass.

"To Arthur," she said. "He introduced us to these. He said, 'Colossal Big, Colossal Good.'"

"To Arthur," they echoed.

Leo took a spoonful of the thick chocolate shake. It was cold, sweet, and perfect. He looked around the diner. It was noisy, unpretentious, and full of locals.

He preferred this to the mansion. The mansion was cold stone. This was warm food.

<aac>Colossal good,</aac> he typed.

"It sure is," Stella smiled, wiping a bit of foam from her lip. "Now drink up. We have to catch the ferry back to the Fortress."

## 10:00 AM — The Express Return

Lunch was finished. The Colossal Coolers were drained.

They caught the **OSTA Route 67** bus at the stop across the street. It looped back through the city, dropping them at the **Gateway Center**.

They rolled down the block to **Perrotti Park**.

The **BTA Newport Flyer** was waiting at the dock, its engines idling with a deep, powerful rumble.

They boarded. The return trip was smooth, the catamaran slicing through the afternoon chop of the bay. To their right, the Pell Bridge stood like a sentinel. Ahead, the massive white canopy of **Narragansett Esplanade Station** grew larger, rising from the water’s edge on the University peninsula.


They rolled off the boat. They took the glass elevator up to **Level 3 (The Sky Platform)**.

Leo checked the departure board.

**BTA SOUTH SHORE (Local) - 1:25 PM** **ARTS COASTAL 195 (Richmond) - 1:20 PM**

The silver **ARTS** train was already gliding into the station on the express track.

"We take the silver one," Stella said. "We have the passes now."

She tapped her lanyard. The **Bull Pass** was the key.

The ARTS conductor stepped out, deploying the bridge plate for Leo. "Heading south?"

"Iron Point," Stella said, flashing her ID.

"Hop on," the conductor said. "But remember the rule: **Pass holders in Coach Class only.** The Business Class car up front is reserved for ticketed passengers."

"Coach is fine," Stella confirmed. "It’s a twelve-minute ride."


They rolled into the train. It didn't have the curtains or the free coffee of Business Class, but the seats were wide and comfortable.

Leo parked in the designated accessible bay at the rear of the car. There was no conference table here—just a fold-down tray from the wall—but he didn't mind. He plugged his Scribe into the wall outlet (which Coach cars still had).

The conductor came by to scan.

Stella, Luna, Leo, and Olivia held up their **BSU IDs**. *BEEP. "Student Valid - Coach."*

Eleanor held up her **BTA LinkPass (Yearly)**. *BEEP. "Pass Valid - Coach."*

"All set," the conductor smiled. "Next stop, Iron Point."


The train pulled out.

This wasn't the BTA commuter train. It didn't chug. It *launched*.

The electric locomotive accelerated rapidly. Leo watched the window.

**BCI Airport Station** flashed by in a blur of concrete. **Southport** was a smear of green trees. **Rockwell** was gone in a second.

<aac>Skipping stops,</aac> Leo typed on his Scribe. <aac>Velocity high.</aac>

"It’s the Express," Olivia said, relaxing into the seat next to him. "No stops until our driveway."

In **12 minutes**, the train began to slow. The brakes hissed.

*"Now arriving... Iron Point. Suburban Gateway."*

They stepped off onto the platform. It was **01:35 PM**.

"That was fast," Luna said. "We beat the bus by twenty minutes."

They rolled down the ramp to the street. They walked the two blocks down **Ocean View Lane**.

As they turned into the driveway of the Fortress, Leo looked back at the station. He had eaten lunch in Newport and was home before 2:00 PM.

He tapped his Scribe.

<aac>The network is operational.</aac>

"It sure is," Stella said, unlocking the front door. "Now, everyone rest up. Tomorrow is **Orientation Day**. And I have a feeling it's going to be loud."

Leo rolled into the cool, quiet hallway of the house. He was ready. He had his chair, his pass, and his team.

## 10:00 AM — The Calm Before the Herd

The house was quiet. The excitement of the Newport trip had faded into a comfortable, heavy fatigue.

Leo and Luna sat on the deep sectional sofa. They weren't watching TV. They were just holding on.

Leo sat with his back against the armrest. Luna was curled up next to him, her head resting on his chest, her arms wrapped around his waist. He had one arm draped over her, his hand resting on her shoulder, applying a steady, rhythmic pressure.

They weren't talking. They were just syncing. The vibration of the ferry, the noise of the bus, the wind on the deck—it was all draining out of them, replaced by the static warmth of the house.

Olivia sat on the floor in front of them, leaning back against Leo’s legs. She was reading a book, but her presence was a tactical choice—she was the ground wire.

Stella walked in from the kitchen with a clipboard.

"Okay," Stella said, keeping her voice low. "Debrief for tomorrow. **Orientation Day**."

She handed a schedule to Olivia.

"It starts at 8:30 AM at the Stadium," Stella said. "Then it breaks out into sessions."

- **10:00 AM:** Residential Life (Dorm Living).

- **11:00 AM:** Dining Services (Meal Plans).

- **12:00 PM:** Campus Safety (Blue Light Phones).

- **01:00 PM:** ID Card Processing (The Gym).

- **02:00 PM:** Textbook Acquisition (The Bookstore).

Olivia scanned the list. She laughed softly.

"We are going to be so bored," Olivia noted. "We don't live in dorms. We don't have meal plans. And..."

She looked at the lanyard hanging on the hook by the door.

"We already have our IDs," Olivia said.

"And the books," Luna murmured from Leo’s chest. "They're sitting on the table right there."

"Exactly," Stella grinned. "While five thousand freshmen are standing in line at the ID office for three hours, we're going to be eating lunch at The Foundry. We're fully equipped."

Leo looked at the schedule. He saw the chaos it represented. He thought about the crowds fighting for books.

He tapped his **Quantum Communicator**.

<aac>We are pre-loaded,</aac> the robotic voice said softly. <aac>Mission complete before start.</aac>

"That's the Porter way," Stella said. "We just have to show up, sit through the speeches, and sign the attendance sheet so they don't drop our registration."

In the corner of the room, **Eleanor** sat in her favorite rocking chair near the fireplace (which was cold for the summer). Her knitting needles clicked softly—a rhythmic, comforting sound like a clock.

She was working on a large bundle of navy blue wool.

"What are you making, Grandma?" Luna asked, lifting her head slightly.

"Armor," Eleanor said, not missing a stitch. "Winter is coming. This is for Leo. A proper cable-knit sweater. Thick wool. When the wind hits that train platform in November, he's going to need it."

She looked at the rest of them.

"I've got enough yarn for everyone," she promised. "You're all getting one. We might be Rhode Islanders now, but the Atlantic wind doesn't care where you're from."

Leo watched the needles move. *Click-click-click.*

He squeezed Luna tighter. He felt Olivia’s weight against his legs. He heard the rhythm of the knitting.

The Fortress was ready. They had the passes. They had the books. They had the sweaters.

<aac>Ready for the herd,</aac> he typed.

"We sure are," Stella said, turning off the main light. "Rest up. Tomorrow we go to war with the Freshman Class."

## 10:00 AM — The Clean Break

The schedule for tomorrow was set. The bags were packed. Now, it was time for the final prep.

Leo rolled into his bedroom. He looked at Olivia. He tapped his chest.

<aac>Bath,</aac> he typed. <aac>Deep clean. for the first day.</aac>

"Full scrub?" Olivia asked.

Leo nodded. He looked at the door, where Stella and Luna were standing. He waved them in. He didn't want to be alone. He wanted the full team.

They moved into the massive Jack-and-Jill bathroom. It was designed for exactly this—plenty of turning radius, heated floors, and a deep soaking tub.


The routine was efficient. Olivia helped Leo out of his shirt. Stella started the water, checking the temperature with her wrist until it was perfect.

Then, the women began to undress. Not with fanfare, and not with shame. They simply removed their clothes because they were about to get wet helping him, and because they were all queuing up for showers immediately after.

Leo sat in his chair. He looked at them. He saw Stella’s strong shoulders. He saw Luna’s curved spine (the scoliosis from her CP). He saw Olivia’s height.

He looked them in the eye. They looked back, smiling, chatting about tomorrow’s schedule.

Suddenly, a memory spiked in his brain.

*Kent, Washington. The guest room. The heat.*

He remembered Delores taking her clothes off. But she hadn't looked like this—efficient, caring, neutral. She had looked *expectant*. She had stared at him with a hungry, angry intensity, waiting for a reaction he didn't know how to give. He remembered the confusion—*Naked means bath? Where is the water?*—and then the flash of her rage when he didn't respond the way she wanted.

Leo flinched. His breath hitched in his throat.

The room went silent instantly.

"Leo?" Stella asked, her hand hovering over the faucet. "You okay? Water too loud?"

Leo shook his head. He looked at Stella. He looked at Luna. He looked at Olivia.

They weren't looking at him with hunger. They were looking at him with love.

He let out a long, shaky breath. The memory dissolved, washed away by the reality of the steam and the lavender soap.

<aac>Bad memory,</aac> he typed on his Scribe. <aac>Delores. she was wrong.</aac>

"She was wrong about everything," Olivia said fiercely. "We are right."

<aac>Yes,</aac> Leo typed. <aac>Safe.</aac> Stella immediately put the Scribe on the counter, away from the bath water.


Stella turned off the tap. "Ready?"

"Ready," Luna said.

Luna and Olivia stepped to either side of his chair. They performed the two-person lift—smooth, practiced, safe. They lowered him into the warm water.

Leo sighed. The heat loosened the spasticity in his hips. He floated.

For twenty minutes, they washed him. It was a ritual of care. Stella washed his hair. Luna scrubbed his back. Olivia managed the handheld sprayer. They talked about the train schedule, about the ocean, about everything and nothing.

When he was clean, the reverse lift happened.

Olivia had already draped a thick, warm towel over his **Titan X-Treme**. They lifted him out, dripping and warm, and settled him into the chair. They swaddled him in towels instantly to keep the chill off.


"You dry," Stella said. "I'm jumping in."

Stella stepped into the roll-in shower.

Olivia wheeled Leo back into the bedroom. She dried his hair with a towel, rubbing vigorously until it spiked up. She helped him into his softest flannel pajamas.

A few minutes later, Stella emerged from the bathroom, wrapped in a robe, her hair wet. She walked over to Leo. She smelled of the same soap he did.

"You clean?" she asked.

Leo nodded.

"Good," she said, hugging him tight. "Tomorrow is going to be a good day. You're ready."

She kissed his forehead and headed down the hall to the Command Wing to dress.

"My turn," Luna called out from the bathroom.

Luna took her shower. When she was done, she wrapped herself in a towel and padded to her room to change into pajamas. Then she came back to Leo’s room.

She saw Leo sitting by the window, looking at the dark glass. She knew the "Delores Memory" had rattled him.

She didn't say anything. She just walked over and pulled him into a hug, holding him tightly in his chair, her wet hair dampening his pajama shirt. She grounded him.

"Go," Luna told Olivia. "I've got him."

Olivia went to shower.

Ten minutes later, she returned, dressed in her own pajamas. The "Shift Change" was complete. Everyone was clean. Everyone was safe.

"Living room?" Olivia asked.

Leo nodded.

They headed down the hall. In the living room, the lights were dim.

Stella was pacing near the window, her phone to her ear. "Yes, Jim, I know the invoice is due. The check is in the mail. Just make sure the landscaping crew doesn't block the bus stop."

Eleanor sat in her rocker by the fire. The blue wool was growing longer.

"Look at you all," Eleanor smiled, her needles clicking. "Scrubbed and shiny. Ready for school."

Leo rolled up to her. He smelled the wool and the fire.

<aac>Clean slate,</aac> he typed.

"That's right," Eleanor said. "Clean slate. Now come here and tell me if this sleeve looks long enough for your arm."
