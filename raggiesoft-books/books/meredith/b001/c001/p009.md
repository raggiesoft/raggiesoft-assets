# Part 8: The Debrief – 12:45 PM

The interior of Meredith's practical sedan was cool and quiet, the low hum of the tires on the asphalt serving as a comforting white noise. They were cruising down I-264 East, leaving the concrete towers of downtown Norfolk behind them.

David had his compact travel keyboard resting comfortably on his lap, tethered to the OmniTalk 3000 system. He didn't need to hit the audio trigger right away; he typed out his thoughts in a steady rhythm, letting the sentences queue up while Meredith kept her eyes on the road, her right hand resting lightly on the manual gear shift.

"Okay, hit me," Meredith said, glancing at the rearview mirror. "What exactly happened between the lounge and the third floor?"

David pressed the trigger. The heavy, robotic voice filled the cabin.

"She seemed nice in the lounge," the device stated. "The thought of having lunch with someone was nice. But the central court elevators were packed. I knew Elevator M1 at Norwood's would be empty. She complained about the walk."

Meredith let out a sharp chuckle, downshifting smoothly as traffic slowed slightly. "I had the exact same idea. I was on the third floor and didn't want to deal with the M2 crowd, so I went to the Norwood's corner to catch M1 down. Great minds."

David typed his next sentence, his fingers moving a little faster as the frustration of the memory returned.

"When we got inside the glass, she suddenly changed her attitude. She screamed at me that we were eating the value menu. I pulled out the twenty dollars Dad gave me to show I could pay. She got angrier."

Meredith’s jaw tightened, the brief humor vanishing entirely. Her "Ice" protocol was deeply sensitive to people who raised their voices at her cousin. "She screamed at you? In a closed elevator?"

"Yes," the robotic voice answered. "I do not wish to get to know Vesper any further. Can you or Morgan accompany me on Wednesday? So I am not alone."

"Absolutely," Meredith said without a second of hesitation. They were approaching Exit 14A. Meredith pressed the clutch and downshifted, slowing the sedan to take the sweeping ramp for I-64 Eastbound toward Chesapeake. "We'll both come. We can bring Kate, too. Make it a full Vanguard escort."

David's eyes lit up. A sharp, breathy giggle escaped him. He began typing furiously, a massive, unshielded grin spreading across his face.

"If she bothers us, we can deploy the Poop Button."

Meredith burst out laughing as she merged the sedan onto I-64. The "Poop Button" was their ultimate, undefeated psychological operation. Meredith and Morgan were not fighters, and they never threatened physical violence; instead, they engaged in advanced, utterly absurd biological warfare to protect their perimeter.

"Oh, Vesper would absolutely shatter," Meredith laughed, accelerating over the Twin Bridges into Virginia Beach. "Can you imagine? I'd tell her it's an industrial, military-grade model. Then I'd look at Kate's urine bag and tell Vesper she's standing in the splash zone."

David slapped his knee, shaking with silent laughter. The best part of the strategy was always Kate herself. Kate never lied, but she never helped the predator, either. She would just sit in her manual wheelchair, technically honest, while the twins escalated the horror.

"She'd ask Kate if it can actually spray," Meredith continued, wiping a tear of mirth from her eye as she took Exit 286B, looping onto Indian River Road Eastbound. "And Kate would just say, *'It's related to bowel management,'* and Vesper would run away thinking we hold certifications in advanced bowel warfare."

They were both laughing their asses off as the tension of the afternoon completely evaporated. The threat of Vesper Vance felt microscopic compared to the unified front of the cousins.

A few minutes later, Meredith pulled the sedan into the Kemps River shopping center, parking near the glass storefront of Tracy's hair salon.

Meredith killed the engine and turned to David, her tone shifting back to practical caregiving. "Okay, so before we go in, I need to warn you. Morgan is about an hour and a half into a five-hour color correction. You know how she wanted to go back to being blonde so we can run Twin Magic again?"

David offered a sharp, affirmative grunt.

"Right. Well, Tracy had to use a chemical color stripper first. It smelled like straight rotten eggs," Meredith explained, wrinkling her nose. "Right now, Morgan is sitting in the chair with bleach on her head. Her hair is going to look like a rusty penny. It’s going to be bright, brassy orange."

David double-blinked, a fresh wave of amusement hitting him.

"Don't laugh at her," Meredith warned, though she was smiling. "She's suffering for the cause. Tracy still has to tone it back to my exact shade of ash. But by tonight, the Vanguard will be back to full, identical strength. Ready to go secure the perimeter?"

David nodded enthusiastically. He was ready to get Morgan, get home, and count down the hours until he could log onto InfoLink Messenger to tell Hannah about the absolute disaster of his first college lunch.
