# Part 9: The Salon Debrief – 1:15 PM

The moment David and Meredith pushed through the glass doors of Tracy's salon, a wall of putrid, sulfurous air hit them. True to Meredith's warning, the chemical color stripper smelled exactly like rotten eggs. David wrinkled his nose, but he gripped the handrims of his manual wheelchair and pushed forward, determined to power through the stench for Morgan's sake.

They found Morgan sitting in Tracy's chair in the back of the shop. The dark brunette dye was completely gone. In its place, Morgan's heavy, waist-length hair was a shocking, brassy shade of rusty orange.

"Don't say a word," Morgan warned, glaring at Meredith through the reflection in the massive wall mirror. "Tracy is mixing the bleach right now."

David let out a sharp, breathy giggle, wheeling himself directly into the spot next to her styling chair. Meredith pulled up a rolling stool and sat close to David, allowing him to lean his weight heavily against her shoulder. The firm, continuous physical contact provided the grounding Deep Pressure Therapy he needed to offset the sensory nightmare of the salon's chemical odors.

"So, how was the first day?" Morgan asked, wincing slightly as Tracy returned and began painting cold bleach onto her orange strands. "Did the SDV class survive you?"

David flipped his laptop open, resting it on his lap, and quickly typed out his response into WritePad before routing it through the OmniTalk 3000.

"The class was great," the robotic voice echoed over the hum of the salon's blow dryers. "There are only ten students. I am the only guy in a room with nine women."

Morgan laughed. "Oh, you'll be perfectly fine. You were raised in a house completely overrun by women anyway."

David double-blinked in agreement. Living with his mother, Meredith, Morgan, Kate, and having Hannah essentially move in during her father's Diego Garcia deployment meant he was entirely comfortable in female-dominated spaces.

"The mall, however, was a different story," Meredith chimed in, leaning her head against David's. "Tell her about your new friend."

David's fingers flew across the keyboard. He gave Morgan a flawless, pragmatic breakdown of his encounter with Vesper Vance. He explained the polite conversation in the student lounge, the walk across the street, the decision to use Elevator M1 to avoid the crowds, and the sudden, jarring moment Vesper screamed at him about the value menu.

Morgan’s eyes narrowed in the mirror, her "Fire" protocol instantly flaring to life. She didn't hesitate for a single second.

"We are coming with you on Wednesday," Morgan declared, her tone deadly serious despite the foil packets jutting out of her hair. "We’ll bring Kate. If this Vesper girl even looks at you wrong, we are deploying the Poop Button."

Meredith burst into laughter, and David slapped his knee, his shoulders shaking with silent mirth.

"What?" Morgan demanded, looking between them.

"We literally just had the exact same conversation in the car," Meredith wheezed, wiping her eyes. "I told him we'd threaten her with the splash zone."

David offered a bright smile. It was a terrifyingly comforting reminder that the twins operated on the exact same protective wavelength.

Tracy, working the bleach through Morgan's hair with practiced precision, chuckled from behind the styling chair. Having been the Delaney family's cosmetologist for years, she was intimately familiar with their specific brand of psychological warfare. She knew perfectly well that Kate's MACE was a legitimate medical device for bowel management, not an industrial poop launcher, but she completely supported the twins' mythology.

"You know, David," Tracy said, catching his eye in the mirror. "I see a lot of people sit in this chair, and I hear a lot of stories. A girl who acts sweet when it's just the two of you, but snaps the second she thinks she's losing control over a lunch order? That is a massive red flag. You're making the right choice keeping her at arm's length. She sounds like bad news."

David tapped a rhythmic acknowledgment on his laptop case. Hearing it from Tracy was all the validation he needed. If the woman who handled the Vanguard's hair thought someone was a threat, her judgment was as good as gold.

With the debrief complete, David settled deeper into Meredith's side, opening up his class portal to tackle his very first college assignment. Professor Hayes had assigned a "168-Hour Time Management Audit." He needed to map out exactly how he planned to spend every hour of the upcoming week—blocking out time for sleep, SDV 100, transportation, and homework.

As he diligently typed out his schedule, blocking off the evening hours specifically for his daily Link chats with Hannah, he felt a profound sense of peace. He sincerely hoped Vesper would just take the hint and leave him alone. He didn't *want* to subject a stranger to the horrifying, fictional capabilities of Kate's medical equipment.

But as he looked at Morgan's fierce reflection in the mirror, he knew the Vanguard was ready. Vesper seemed exactly like the kind of person who would push the boundary, just to find out if the twins were bluffing.
