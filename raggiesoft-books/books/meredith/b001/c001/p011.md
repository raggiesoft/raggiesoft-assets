# Part 10: The Library Intercept – 6:15 PM

The five-hour marathon finally came to an end. Tracy turned off the blow dryer and spun Morgan’s vinyl chair around to face the mirror.

Morgan stared at her reflection. The dark brunette was entirely gone, but the result wasn't the exact sun-kissed ash she was aiming for. Instead, her long hair was a bright, warm honey-caramel.

"That's as far as the chemistry will let us go today without completely frying your ends," Tracy explained, resting her hands on Morgan's shoulders. "We lifted a massive amount of artificial pigment, and I toned out the harsh orange, but your hair needs to rest. Give it a month, come back for Phase Two, and I can pull the rest of this warmth out so you perfectly match your sister."

Morgan ran her hands through the honey-colored strands, nodding pragmatically. "It's fine. It looks healthy. We'll deploy Twin Magic next month. The 'Fire' protocol still works as a redhead."

David wheeled himself forward, offering a sharp, happy hum as he reached out to hug both of his cousins. The salon ordeal was over, and the Vanguard was back in motion.

When they stepped out into the humid evening air and approached Meredith's sedan, David didn't head for the front passenger seat. He tapped the rear door. Meredith opened it, and David transferred into the back, pulling himself across the upholstery so he could sit directly next to Morgan. As Meredith stowed his chair and got behind the wheel, David leaned his full weight against Morgan, resting his head on her shoulder. Morgan wrapped her arm around him, letting him cuddle into her side, supplying the Deep Pressure Therapy he needed after a long, exhausting day in the public eye.

"Next stop, the city library," Meredith announced, shifting the car into gear. "Kate texted that her doctor's appointment wrapped up an hour ago and her dad dropped her off at the reading room to wait for us."

It was a short drive from the salon to the Virginia Beach public library. Meredith parked near the accessible ramp. She hopped out, and a few moments later, she returned with Kate, pushing her paternal cousin's manual wheelchair to the passenger side of the sedan.

Meredith opened the front door and expertly assisted Kate with the transfer. It was a fluid, highly practiced maneuver, shifting Kate from the chair to the car seat while carefully managing the tubing of her urinary collection bag so nothing snagged. Once Kate was buckled in, Meredith folded the second wheelchair, stowing it in the trunk next to David's.

As Meredith climbed into the driver's seat and started the engine, Morgan leaned forward from the back seat, resting her chin on the center console.

"Hey, Kate," Morgan said, a wicked grin spreading across her face. "Just a heads-up. On Wednesday, Meredith and I are going to escort David to his SDV 100 class. And we might need to deploy the Poop Button."

Kate slowly turned her head in the passenger seat. She locked eyes with Meredith, her face completely deadpan. She offered a ferocious, unblinking glare, staring absolute daggers at her cousin, pretending to be furious that they were once again weaponizing her bowel management system.

The tense silence held for exactly three seconds.

Then, Kate’s shoulders began to shake. She completely broke character, her glare melting into a massive smile as she burst into a series of silent, breathy, uncontrollable giggles.

David squeezed Morgan's arm, laughing silently in the back seat. Kate tapped the dashboard twice—a specific, questioning rhythm they all recognized. *Who is the customer?*

"Her name is Vesper," Meredith explained, navigating the sedan out of the library parking lot. "She's in the class next door to David's. She tried to isolate him in an elevator at the mall today. She was screaming at him about a lunch order because she didn't realize he had his own money."

Morgan's tone darkened. "It was an isolation tactic. Just like what Connor tried to do."

At the mention of Connor's name, the laughter in the car instantly vanished. Kate’s smile faded, replaced by a quiet, lingering sadness. She looked out the passenger window at the passing trees. Connor hadn't just *seemed* like a good guy—he genuinely was one. He had sat with her at lunch, treated her with respect, and they had shared a wonderful, typical high school romance. Because of her literal processing, she had fundamentally believed he cared about her, and he truly did. But then came the day his parents left town. He had thrown away their entire genuine connection for a sudden, impulsive, and terrible choice—a decision that ended with a stolen 1997 Holt sedan wrapped around a utility pole.

Now, her body was permanently altered, and a boy who used to hold her hand was rotting away in a Virginia state penitentiary.

Meredith reached across the center console, resting her hand gently on Kate's knee. "I know, Kate," she said softly. "I know. But Vesper tried to pull the same trick today. She tried to catch David alone because she thought he was an easy target. That's why we're shutting it down."

Kate turned back from the window. She looked at Meredith, then glanced back at David and Morgan, and gave a single, firm nod. The sadness was pushed aside, replaced by the unified, unyielding resolve of the Vanguard. If someone was threatening David, the Poop Button was officially authorized.

"Alright," Meredith said, turning onto Delaney Street, the familiar, comforting sight of their neighborhood coming into view. "Let's get inside. When the adults get home from work tonight, we need to have a full family briefing on Vesper Vance. David, you can relax until the long-distance rates drop at nine. Then, you can finally call Hannah."
