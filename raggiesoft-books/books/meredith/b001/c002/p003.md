# Part 3: The Princess Anne Perimeter – 12:45 PM

The Vanguard navigated the wide, sunny corridors of the Princess Anne Building, a sprawling brick complex at the heart of the Virginia Beach campus. Unlike David's experience in the cramped Martin Building, the hallways here were wide enough that they could easily roll two abreast.

As they approached the correct classroom, Morgan stopped the group in a quiet alcove just outside the door. She unzipped her backpack and pulled out a massive, gallon-sized Ziploc bag filled with a strange, brown-and-orange mixture.

"Behold," Morgan whispered, holding the bag up like a trophy. "Acoustic ammunition."

Kate stared at the bag. She tapped her wheelchair armrest, giving Morgan a highly skeptical look.

"It's a proprietary blend of dried apricots, roasted soy nuts, and sugar-free chocolate chips," Meredith explained, her clinical "Ice" protocol fully analyzing the nutritional data. "The apricots provide a massive hit of soluble fiber. The soy nuts offer heavy complex carbohydrates. And the sugar-free chocolate is loaded with maltitol. If consumed rapidly, the human digestive tract simply cannot process it."

"It creates rapid, highly pressurized gastrointestinal distress within thirty minutes," Morgan added, shaking the bag so the soy nuts rattled. "If some guy tries to corner you, Meredith and I will down two handfuls of this each. We will become weapons of mass flatulence."

David slapped his hands over his mouth, his shoulders shaking violently as he tried to contain a burst of laughter. Kate closed her eyes and let her head fall back against her headrest, letting out a series of breathy, silent giggles. The sheer, calculated insanity of her cousins' biological warfare planning was the perfect antidote to her first-day jitters.

"Keep it in the bag," Kate's AAC device stated flatly. "I am going in alone."

Just like David had done on Monday, Kate was determined to tackle the actual classroom environment by herself. The Vanguard was her shield against the unpredictable hallways and the threat of isolation, but in the structured, heavily supervised environment of the classroom, she wanted to prove her independence.

"You've got this," Meredith said, stepping back to clear the path.

"We'll be right here," Morgan promised, zipping the biological hazard mix safely back into her bag. "If you need us, just tap the SOS rhythm on your desk. We'll hear it."

Kate gave them a firm nod, gripped her push rims, and wheeled herself through the heavy wooden door.

The classroom was modern and spacious. She easily maneuvered her manual chair into a spot at an accessible table near the front. The professor, an older man with a kind smile, was busy writing the syllabus outline on the whiteboard. As the room slowly filled with other freshmen, Kate kept a watchful eye, but no one approached her with any undue interest. A few students offered polite smiles, which she returned, but the terrifying specter of another Connor never materialized.

Out in the hallway, the Vanguard held the perimeter. David backed his wheelchair against the wall, flipped open his laptop, and happily spent the next fifty minutes typing up a history essay draft while Meredith and Morgan stood guard, casually leaning against the brickwork and watching the flow of student traffic.

At exactly 1:50 PM, the classroom door swung open, and the students began to pour out.

Kate wheeled herself into the hallway, a look of profound relief on her face.

David immediately looked up from his laptop. He typed quickly, sending the text through the OmniTalk 3000. "Status report?"

Kate’s fingers moved across her own device. "The class was easy. We just went over the syllabus. And no one bothered me."

Meredith let out a long breath, her posture relaxing completely. "Good. The perimeter holds."

Morgan let out a dramatic sigh, patting the front pocket of her backpack. "Well, I'm glad you're safe, Kate. But I'm not going to lie... part of me is a little disappointed I didn't get to deploy the apricots."

Kate rolled her eyes, but she reached out and grabbed Morgan's hand, giving it a grateful squeeze. The first day of college was officially in the books for both of them, and against all odds, it had been a complete success.
