## 6:00 AM — The Garage Barracks

The weight of the blankets in the converted garage was a heavy, grounding anchor. David woke to the familiar, rhythmic breathing of the three girls surrounding him. He was pinned securely in the center of the massive, custom-built bed. Meredith’s arm was thrown heavily over his chest from the left, acting as a human weighted blanket against his morning spasticity. To his right, Kate was curled on her side, perfectly bracketed by Morgan on the far edge.

It was perfectly safe. It was entirely routine. But David still felt the hollow ache in his chest where Hannah was supposed to be.

He blinked against the early morning light filtering through the blinds, doing the math in his head. *Six hours.* Hannah was still fast asleep in the dark of Pearl Harbor. He let out a low, sustained hum—a sound of quiet longing. Meredith shifted instantly, her eyes cracking open. She didn’t ask what was wrong; she just squeezed his shoulder and gave a single, understanding nod. Hannah had promised to call later that afternoon, and the entire family was counting down the hours until they heard from her.

"Alright, troops," Morgan murmured, her voice raspy as she sat up and stretched her arms toward the ceiling. "Up and at 'em. It’s syllabus day."

The morning routine commenced with clinical, loving efficiency. Modesty was a concept that simply did not exist within the walls of the garage. It had been burned away by years of necessity. Morgan and Meredith threw back the covers and seamlessly transitioned into caregiving mode, stripping off pajamas and moving directly into the adjoining accessible bathroom. It was a rapid, practiced blur of warm water, soap, and mechanical transfers.

"Careful with the shit hose, Mer," Morgan deadpanned, holding up a fresh towel as Meredith expertly flushed Kate's MACE system. "You know she’s got a big day of functioning as a respectable adult today."

Kate, sitting securely in her shower chair, let out a sudden, breathy series of silent giggles, her shoulders shaking at the toilet humor. David caught her eye and offered a double-blink of amusement. But beneath Kate's laughter, David could see the subtle, lingering shadow that hadn't been there a year ago. It was only recently that the protective fog around Kate's literal processing had begun to lift. She was just starting to piece together the terrifying reality that Connor’s "safe ride home" hadn't just been a reckless accident—it had been a trap. She was finally understanding what he would have done to her if the Holt sedan hadn't wrapped around that utility pole.

Meredith caught the shift in Kate's eyes too. She didn't offer empty pity. She just kissed the top of Kate's wet, chestnut-red hair and efficiently attached the fresh urinary bag. "You look beautiful today, Kate. Doctor's appointment at ten to check the hardware, and then we're going to watch movies. You and David are eighteen. You call the shots today."

Morgan leaned over the double vanity, pulling her heavy hair up into a massive, utilitarian claw clip. She paused, catching her reflection in the harsh bathroom light. A distinct, half-inch band of natural sun-kissed ash was creeping in at her scalp, stark against the dark brunette dye she’d meticulously maintained for the last year.

She tilted her head, inspecting the roots, and then looked at Meredith’s reflection right beside her. Meredith’s natural waist-length ash hair was exactly the same shade as Morgan’s roots.

"I think I'm done with the dye," Morgan announced suddenly, letting the heavy brunette mass fall back down her back.

Meredith paused, holding a clean shirt for David. "You're ditching the brunette? Why?"

A slow, wicked grin spread across Morgan's face, her "Fire" protocol already calculating the possibilities. "Because if we go back to being completely identical, we can play Twin Magic again. Think about the tactical advantage. Someone gives Kate a hard time, you step in and quietly warn them off. They walk away, turn a corner, and boom—I'm standing right there, looking exactly like you, ready to ruin their day. It’ll make us look like we can teleport."

David let out a sharp, amused huff from his wheelchair, his lips twitching into a smile at the sheer chaos Morgan was planning to inflict on the world.

"It's going to take a lot of chemical stripper to get that dark box dye out," Meredith noted pragmatically.

"I know. I'll call Tracy," Morgan said, pulling a ribbed tank top over her head. "I'll make an appointment to ask about color removal for this afternoon, right after we get her hardware checked."

## 8:15 AM — The Commute

The drive from the quiet perimeter of Delaney Street to the concrete sprawl of downtown Norfolk took about thirty minutes. David watched the city wake up through the passenger window of the modified family van, the heavy, resistive-touch OmniTalk 3000 already clamped securely to his manual wheelchair's frame. He felt a tightening in his chest—a spike of sensory anticipation. Today was his very first day of college at the Community College of Hampton Roads. There were no high school IEP meetings here, no familiar aides, just thousands of strangers and his freshman SDV 100 orientation class.

"We've got some extra time," his dad said, navigating the morning traffic with practiced ease since his own office was located right in the heart of downtown Norfolk. "Let's hit the Rocket Burger drive-thru. Get you some real food before you're stuck in that building all morning."

David offered a sharp, agreeable grunt. Even after his bowl of cereal at home, a warm breakfast sandwich and a hashbrown sounded perfect to anchor his stomach before the sensory overload of a new campus set in.

As they idled in the drive-thru lane, the scent of hot grease and coffee filling the van, David’s gaze drifted toward his lap. His hands tightened slightly against his legs. The excitement of his first day was warring with the heavy, persistent ache of missing Hannah. During high school, she had always been his safe harbor in chaotic school environments. Facing a massive college building without her sitting next to him felt incredibly isolating.

His dad handed the cashier a few bills, grabbed the paper bag, and passed it over to the center console before glancing at David. The older man possessed the same hyper-observant radar as the twins.

"I know it's hard, Player Two," his dad said softly, putting the van back into gear. "But she's going to be so proud of you today. And remember, you'll talk to her tonight. Hawaii is six hours behind us. By the time you get home and decompress, she'll be waking up for her day, and you can tell her all about yours."

David tapped a quick, rhythmic acknowledgment against his thigh, his posture relaxing just a fraction. His dad was right. Their emotional bond remained entirely intact, easily surviving the 4,800 miles between Virginia and Pearl Harbor.

The van navigated the final few blocks, eventually pulling to a stop at the curb right outside the front entrance of the CCHR Martin Building.

"Alright, we're here," his dad said, putting the van in park and moving to unhook the wheelchair restraints in the back. He pulled his wallet from his pocket, extracted a crisp twenty-dollar bill, and tucked it securely into the side pocket of David's bag. "For lunch later. Treat yourself at the mall across the street."

David gave his dad a firm nod, gripping the friction tape on the handrims of his chair.

"Head straight up to the third-floor student lounge," his dad instructed, stepping back so the van's mechanical ramp could deploy. "You've got plenty of time before your eleven o'clock class. Find a quiet table, pull out your laptop, and turn it on. The second that screen boots up, you're going to see that picture of you and Hannah from her visit this summer. Let her ground you before the day starts, okay?"

David smiled, a genuine, unshielded expression. He wheeled himself down the ramp and onto the downtown pavement, ready to face the overwhelming environment of the college campus, knowing exactly whose face was waiting for him behind the glass of his screen.

## 8:30 AM — The Walker Building Lounge

The automatic doors of the Walker Building slid open, welcoming David into the blissfully air-conditioned lobby. He gripped the handrims of his manual chair and navigated toward the elevator bank, the friction tape steadying his hold. He pressed the up button, waited for the familiar mechanical *ding*, and rolled inside, hitting the button for the third floor.

When the doors parted again, David wheeled out and took an immediate right, heading down the quiet corridor toward the restrooms to handle his morning business. Once finished, he pivoted the chair and rolled back down the full length of the hallway. He passed the elevator bank he had just come from and continued to the very end of the corridor, turning right into the student lounge.

It was completely empty. The quiet hum of the fluorescent lights was the only sound in the room, creating a perfect, low-sensory environment.

David found a table near an electrical outlet and parked his chair. He pulled the warm, grease-spotted paper bag from his backpack and took his time eating his Rocket Burger breakfast sandwich and hashbrown. The food settled his stomach, grounding him in the morning routine. Once he finished and wiped his hands, he pulled his laptop from his bag, set it squarely on the table, and hit the power button.

The screen flickered to life, loading his desktop. David felt an immediate, involuntary smile pull at the corners of his mouth. The wallpaper was a high-resolution scan of the photo Meredith had taken over the summer. Hannah was standing behind his wheelchair, her arms wrapped fiercely over his shoulders, her chin resting near his. They were both beaming. Staring at it, the overwhelming reality of the massive college campus outside the windows felt a little more manageable.

He clicked the mouse and launched Quantum Suite WritePad. The stark white document window snapped open, maximizing across the entire screen and completely covering Hannah’s face.

David stretched his fingers and began jotting down a few preparatory notes. He typed out the name of his first class: <aac>SDV 100 - Student Development</aac>. He knew it was quite literally an introduction to college—a course designed to teach freshmen how to study, how to use the library, and how to survive higher education. He wanted to be ready.

A few minutes later, the sound of footsteps echoed in the hallway.

A girl walked into the lounge, a heavy backpack slung casually over one shoulder. She paused, glancing around the empty tables before her eyes landed on David. She offered a bright, easy smile and walked directly over to his table.

"Hey," she said, her voice friendly and conversational. "Do you mind if I join you? The tables downstairs are freezing, and it's practically a ghost town up here."

David offered a sharp, rhythmic tap on the table—his affirmative cue—and gestured toward the empty chair across from him. It was nice to have some company. Sitting alone in a silent room was peaceful, but having someone to pass the time with before his first-ever college class felt like a quintessential freshman experience.

"Thanks," she said, dropping her bag and pulling out the chair. "I'm Vesper."

David rested his hands on his laptop keyboard. He opened a new, blank WritePad document, quickly typed a response, and swiveled the laptop a few inches toward her so she could read the screen.

*Hi Vesper. I'm David. It's my first day at CCHR.*

Vesper read the screen, her brow furrowing slightly in polite confusion. She looked back up at him. "Nice to meet you, David. How come you're typing? Did you lose your voice?"

David didn't mind the question; people usually asked when they realized he wasn't going to speak. He pulled the laptop back slightly and typed again.

*I have cerebral palsy. I can't speak verbally.*

He turned the screen back to her. Vesper read it, her expression smoothing out into an easy, understanding nod. "Oh, gotcha," she said, leaning forward and resting her elbows on the table, completely unfazed. "Well, you type really fast, so that works perfectly. First day, huh? What class are you waiting for?"

<aac>SDV 100,</aac> David typed. <aac>It's in the Martin Building at 11:00.</aac>

Vesper’s eyes lit up with genuine surprise. "No way. I'm in the Martin Building at 11:00 too. I'm taking Applied Calculus right next door to the SDV rooms."

David raised an eyebrow, his fingers flying across the keys. *Calculus doesn't sound like an easy morning class.*

Vesper laughed, a light, airy sound. "It is if you like numbers. I'm just taking it for an easy A, honestly. Math has always kind of been my thing." She checked the clock on the wall. "Since we're heading to the exact same place at the exact same time, I can walk over with you later, if you want?"

David nodded, offering a small smile. His literal, pragmatic brain processed the interaction exactly as it was presented: a fellow student waiting for class, making polite conversation, and offering to walk to the same building. It was a perfectly normal, pleasant start to his college career.

## 10:15 AM — The InfoLink Update

For the next hour, the conversation flowed easily. Vesper proved to be incredibly talkative, which suited David perfectly. He didn't have to strain to keep the interaction going; he simply listened, nodded, and occasionally typed brief responses into his open Quantum Suite WritePad document to answer her questions.

She asked about his schedule, and he typed out his plan for the afternoon. <aac>I'm going to The Promenade at Granby after class. My dad gave me twenty dollars for a burger combo at the food court.</aac>

Vesper’s face lit up. "Oh, I love the mall food court. I actually have a huge gap between my calculus class and my afternoon lab. Do you mind if I tag along? I'd love to grab lunch with you."

David offered a sharp, agreeable grunt and typed, <aac>Sure. That sounds great.</aac> His literal processing cataloged the interaction cleanly: he was going to eat lunch, and she also needed to eat lunch. It made logical sense to go together.

A familiar, high-pitched chime suddenly echoed from his laptop speakers. David minimized the WritePad window just enough to reveal the buddy list of InfoLink Messenger, keeping the main document stretched over the background photo of Hannah so he wouldn't accidentally close it. He saw Morgan’s screenname highlighted in bright green, signaling she was online.

He opened a chat box and began typing rapidly.

*Dave: Hey. Made it to campus. Sitting in the lounge.*

*Morg: Awesome! How is it? Overwhelming?*

*Dave: It's quiet. I met a girl named Vesper. She has a math class next to my SDV class. We're going to get lunch at the mall after.*

Vesper leaned over slightly, looking at the chat window. "Who are you messaging?"

David pointed to the screen and typed a quick explanation in WritePad. <aac>My cousin, Morgan. We live together.</aac>

"Oh, that's sweet," Vesper smiled, leaning back in her chair.

On the screen, Morgan’s reply popped up.

*Morg: That’s great! Make sure you get a good burger. Just remember Mer is picking you up at the mall entrance at 2:00 PM. I gotta run, leaving for the salon now. Wish me luck getting this dark box dye stripped out! Going back to blonde so Mer and I can play Twin Magic.*

David smiled at the screen, double-blinking in amusement at his cousin's tactical plans before typing a quick goodbye. He closed the InfoLink window just as the digital clock in the corner of his screen rolled over to 10:45 AM.

He tapped the table to get Vesper’s attention, pointing to the time.

"Oh, wow, you're right. We should head over," Vesper said, standing up and slinging her backpack over her shoulder. As David reached for the friction tape on his wheelchair's handrims, Vesper stepped behind him. "Here, let me. The brick sidewalk outside can be a pain. I don't mind."

David paused, but then gave a short hum of appreciation, relaxing his grip. He was perfectly capable of pushing himself, but it was a warm morning, and saving his energy before his first class was a welcome luxury. He let her take the heavy rubber grips of the push handles.

Vesper navigated the chair with surprising ease, pushing him out of the lounge, into the Walker Building elevator, and down to the ground floor. They rolled out into the humid Virginia air, crossing the single city block until the imposing brick facade of the Martin Building loomed ahead of them.

Vesper pushed him through the automated doors and directly into the main elevator bank, hitting the button for the fourth floor. When the doors opened, the hallway was a chaotic, buzzing hive of freshmen trying to find their rooms.

Vesper pushed him down the corridor until they reached the door for SDV 100. Right next door, the plaque for the 200-level Applied Calculus room gleamed under the fluorescent lights.

"Alright, here we are," Vesper said, stepping around to the front of his chair with a bright smile. "Have a great first class, David. I'll meet you right out here when it's over, and we'll head across the street for those burgers."

David nodded, offering a warm smile and a polite wave as she turned and walked into the math classroom. He gripped his wheels and rolled himself into SDV 100, feeling incredibly accomplished. His first college interaction was a complete success, and he hadn't even opened a textbook yet.

## 11:15 AM — The Color Correction

The chemical smell of the strip mall salon hit the twins the second they walked through the glass doors. It was a potent, sterile mix of aerosol hairspray, burning flat irons, and sharp ammonia. Morgan checked in at the reception desk, with Meredith standing shoulder-to-shoulder right beside her.

Ten minutes later, Morgan was sitting in a black vinyl styling chair with a heavy nylon cape draped over her shoulders, while Meredith claimed the station right next to hers.

Their stylist, an older woman named Tracy with heavily highlighted hair and a pragmatist's gaze, stood behind Morgan. Tracy ran her fingers through Morgan's waist-length, dark brunette hair, lifting the heavy sections and inspecting the half-inch band of natural, sun-kissed ash roots at the scalp.

"So," Tracy said, catching Morgan’s eye in the massive wall mirror. "You want to go back to your natural blonde. How long have you been dyeing it dark?"

"A little over a year," Morgan replied, leaning back into the chair. "I just touch up the roots every month. But I'm done. I want it perfectly matched to my sister's."

Tracy looked over at Meredith. Meredith calmly reached back, pulled her signature plastic claw clip loose, and let her own waist-length, natural sun-kissed ash hair cascade down her back.

"That's the target," Meredith said cleanly, her "Ice" protocol operating even in a salon setting. "Since we're identical, her underlying pigment is exactly the same as mine. I'm just here to serve as the physical color swatch."

Tracy let out a heavy sigh, dropping Morgan's hair and resting her hands on her hips. "Honey, I'm going to be straight with you. You both have gorgeous, healthy hair, but permanent dark dye is stubborn. It stains the cuticle."

"I know," Morgan nodded, having done her homework the night before. "I read that color doesn't lift color. You can't just slap blonde dye over the brunette and call it a day, right?"

Tracy raised a genuinely impressed eyebrow. "Exactly. We have to strip it, and it is going to be a massive project. You're going to be in my chair for the next five hours. First, I have to apply a chemical color reducer to pull the artificial brunette molecules out. Fair warning: it smells exactly like rotten eggs. After we wash that out, your hair is going to look like a rusty penny. Then, I have to go in with bleach to lift you past that orange stage. Finally, we tone it back to match your sister's."

"What about the damage?" Meredith asked, her eyes narrowing slightly in the mirror.

"We'll take it slow," Tracy assured her. "But it's going to cost a pretty penny, and your scalp is going to tingle."

Morgan stared at her reflection, imagining Vesper Vance. She pictured the inevitable moment someone tried to corner David or Kate, thinking they had outsmarted Meredith, only to realize the "Fire" protocol was standing right behind them, wearing the exact same face. The psychological warfare of Twin Magic was unparalleled.

"Do it," Morgan said, her voice entirely devoid of hesitation. "Strip it. Whatever it takes to get it perfectly matched."

Tracy chuckled, shaking her head as she turned toward the chemical mixing room in the back. "Alright, boss. Get comfortable. I'll go mix up the sulfur."

While Tracy was mixing the color stripper, another stylist gestured for Meredith to move into her chair. Since she was already there, Meredith requested her standard maintenance—a thorough wash and a clean trim to dust the dead ends off her waist-length hair.

Morgan watched her sister in the mirror. "You don't have to stay for the rotten egg part, Mer. It's going to reek."

"I'm not going anywhere until I have to leave to pick David up at two," Meredith replied calmly, leaning her head back into the ceramic washbowl. "We're in this together. Plus, if Tracy gets the toner wrong, I need to be here to hold up a lock of my hair and object."

Morgan smiled, settling deeper into the vinyl chair. The perimeter was secure, David was safe in his SDV 100 class, and the twins were preparing to rebuild their ultimate visual weapon.
