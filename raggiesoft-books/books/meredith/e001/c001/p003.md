## 12:45 PM — The Debrief

The interior of Meredith's practical sedan was cool and quiet, the low hum of the tires on the asphalt serving as a comforting white noise. They were cruising down I-264 East, leaving the concrete towers of downtown Norfolk behind them.

David had his compact travel keyboard resting comfortably on his lap, tethered to the OmniTalk 3000 system. He didn't need to hit the audio trigger right away; he typed out his thoughts in a steady rhythm, letting the sentences queue up while Meredith kept her eyes on the road, her right hand resting lightly on the manual gear shift.

"Okay, hit me," Meredith said, glancing at the rearview mirror. "What exactly happened between the lounge and the third floor?"

David pressed the trigger. The heavy, robotic voice filled the cabin.

"She seemed nice in the lounge," the device stated. "The thought of having lunch with someone was nice. But the central court elevators were packed. I knew Elevator M1 at Norwood's would be empty. She complained about the walk."

Meredith let out a sharp chuckle, downshifting smoothly as traffic slowed slightly. "I had the exact same idea. I was on the third floor and didn't want to deal with the M2 crowd, so I went to the Norwood's corner to catch M1 down. Great minds."

David typed his next sentence, his fingers moving a little faster as the frustration of the memory returned.

"When we got inside the glass, she suddenly changed her attitude. She screamed at me that we were eating the value menu. I pulled out the twenty dollars Dad gave me to show I could pay. She got angrier."

Meredith’s jaw tightened, the brief humor vanishing entirely. Her "Ice" protocol was deeply sensitive to people who raised their voices at her cousin. "She screamed at you? In a closed elevator?"

"Yes," the robotic voice answered. "I do not wish to get to know Vesper any further. Can you or Morgan accompany me on Wednesday? So I am not alone."

"Absolutely," Meredith said without a second of hesitation. They were approaching Exit 14A. Meredith pressed the clutch and downshifted, slowing the sedan to take the sweeping ramp for I-64 Eastbound toward Chesapeake. "We'll both come. We can bring Kate, too. Make it a full Vanguard escort."

David's eyes lit up. A sharp, breathy giggle escaped him. He began typing furiously, a massive, unshielded grin spreading across his face.

"If she bothers us, we can deploy the Poop Button."

Meredith burst out laughing as she merged the sedan onto I-64. The "Poop Button" was their ultimate, undefeated psychological operation. Meredith and Morgan were not fighters, and they never threatened physical violence; instead, they engaged in advanced, utterly absurd biological warfare to protect their perimeter.

"Oh, Vesper would absolutely shatter," Meredith laughed, accelerating over the Twin Bridges into Virginia Beach. "Can you imagine? I'd tell her it's an industrial, military-grade model. Then I'd look at Kate's urine bag and tell Vesper she's standing in the splash zone."

David slapped his knee, shaking with silent laughter. The best part of the strategy was always Kate herself. Kate never lied, but she never helped the predator, either. She would just sit in her manual wheelchair, technically honest, while the twins escalated the horror.

"She'd ask Kate if it can actually spray," Meredith continued, wiping a tear of mirth from her eye as she took Exit 286B, looping onto Indian River Road Eastbound. "And Kate would just say, *'It's related to bowel management,'* and Vesper would run away thinking we hold certifications in advanced bowel warfare."

They were both laughing their asses off as the tension of the afternoon completely evaporated. The threat of Vesper Vance felt microscopic compared to the unified front of the cousins.

A few minutes later, Meredith pulled the sedan into the Kemps River shopping center, parking near the glass storefront of Tracy's hair salon.

Meredith killed the engine and turned to David, her tone shifting back to practical caregiving. "Okay, so before we go in, I need to warn you. Morgan is about an hour and a half into a five-hour color correction. You know how she wanted to go back to being blonde so we can run Twin Magic again?"

David offered a sharp, affirmative grunt.

"Right. Well, Tracy had to use a chemical color stripper first. It smelled like straight rotten eggs," Meredith explained, wrinkling her nose. "Right now, Morgan is sitting in the chair with bleach on her head. Her hair is going to look like a rusty penny. It’s going to be bright, brassy orange."

David double-blinked, a fresh wave of amusement hitting him.

"Don't laugh at her," Meredith warned, though she was smiling. "She's suffering for the cause. Tracy still has to tone it back to my exact shade of ash. But by tonight, the Vanguard will be back to full, identical strength. Ready to go secure the perimeter?"

David nodded enthusiastically. He was ready to get Morgan, get home, and count down the hours until he could log onto InfoLink Messenger to tell Hannah about the absolute disaster of his first college lunch.

## 1:15 PM — The Salon Debrief

The moment David and Meredith pushed through the glass doors of Tracy's salon, a wall of putrid, sulfurous air hit them. True to Meredith's warning, the chemical color stripper smelled exactly like rotten eggs. David wrinkled his nose, but he gripped the handrims of his manual wheelchair and pushed forward, determined to power through the stench for Morgan's sake.

They found Morgan sitting in Tracy's chair in the back of the shop. The dark brunette dye was completely gone. In its place, Morgan's heavy, waist-length hair was a shocking, brassy shade of rusty orange.

"Don't say a word," Morgan warned, glaring at Meredith through the reflection in the massive wall mirror. "Tracy is mixing the bleach right now."

David let out a sharp, breathy giggle, wheeling himself directly into the spot next to her styling chair. Meredith pulled up a rolling stool and sat close to David, allowing him to lean his weight heavily against her shoulder. The firm, continuous physical contact provided the grounding Deep Pressure Therapy he needed to offset the sensory nightmare of the salon's chemical odors.

"So, how was the first day?" Morgan asked, wincing slightly as Tracy returned and began painting cold bleach onto her orange strands. "Did the SDV class survive you?"

David flipped his laptop open, resting it on his lap, and quickly typed out his response into WritePad before routing it through the OmniTalk 3000.

"The class was great," the robotic voice echoed over the hum of the salon's blow dryers. "There are only ten students. I am the only guy in a room with nine women."

Morgan laughed. "Oh, you'll be perfectly fine. You were raised in a house completely overrun by women anyway."

David double-blinked in agreement. Living with his mother, Meredith, Morgan, Kate, and having Hannah essentially move in during her father's Diego Garcia deployment meant he was entirely comfortable in female-dominated spaces.

"The mall, however, was a different story," Meredith chimed in, leaning her head against David's. "Tell her about your new friend."

David's fingers flew across the keyboard. He gave Morgan a flawless, pragmatic breakdown of his encounter with Vesper Vance. He explained the polite conversation in the student lounge, the walk across the street, the decision to use Elevator M1 to avoid the crowds, and the sudden, jarring moment Vesper screamed at him about the value menu.

Morgan’s eyes narrowed in the mirror, her "Fire" protocol instantly flaring to life. She didn't hesitate for a single second.

"We are coming with you on Wednesday," Morgan declared, her tone deadly serious despite the foil packets jutting out of her hair. "We’ll bring Kate. If this Vesper girl even looks at you wrong, we are deploying the Poop Button."

Meredith burst into laughter, and David slapped his knee, his shoulders shaking with silent mirth.

"What?" Morgan demanded, looking between them.

"We literally just had the exact same conversation in the car," Meredith wheezed, wiping her eyes. "I told him we'd threaten her with the splash zone."

David offered a bright smile. It was a terrifyingly comforting reminder that the twins operated on the exact same protective wavelength.

Tracy, working the bleach through Morgan's hair with practiced precision, chuckled from behind the styling chair. Having been the Delaney family's cosmetologist for years, she was intimately familiar with their specific brand of psychological warfare. She knew perfectly well that Kate's MACE was a legitimate medical device for bowel management, not an industrial poop launcher, but she completely supported the twins' mythology.

"You know, David," Tracy said, catching his eye in the mirror. "I see a lot of people sit in this chair, and I hear a lot of stories. A girl who acts sweet when it's just the two of you, but snaps the second she thinks she's losing control over a lunch order? That is a massive red flag. You're making the right choice keeping her at arm's length. She sounds like bad news."

David tapped a rhythmic acknowledgment on his laptop case. Hearing it from Tracy was all the validation he needed. If the woman who handled the Vanguard's hair thought someone was a threat, her judgment was as good as gold.

With the debrief complete, David settled deeper into Meredith's side, opening up his class portal to tackle his very first college assignment. Professor Hayes had assigned a "168-Hour Time Management Audit." He needed to map out exactly how he planned to spend every hour of the upcoming week—blocking out time for sleep, SDV 100, transportation, and homework.

As he diligently typed out his schedule, blocking off the evening hours specifically for his daily Link chats with Hannah, he felt a profound sense of peace. He sincerely hoped Vesper would just take the hint and leave him alone. He didn't <aac>want</aac> to subject a stranger to the horrifying, fictional capabilities of Kate's medical equipment.

But as he looked at Morgan's fierce reflection in the mirror, he knew the Vanguard was ready. Vesper seemed exactly like the kind of person who would push the boundary, just to find out if the twins were bluffing.

## 6:15 PM — The Library Intercept

The five-hour marathon finally came to an end. Tracy turned off the blow dryer and spun Morgan’s vinyl chair around to face the mirror.

Morgan stared at her reflection. The dark brunette was entirely gone, but the result wasn't the exact sun-kissed ash she was aiming for. Instead, her long hair was a bright, warm honey-caramel.

"That's as far as the chemistry will let us go today without completely frying your ends," Tracy explained, resting her hands on Morgan's shoulders. "We lifted a massive amount of artificial pigment, and I toned out the harsh orange, but your hair needs to rest. Give it a month, come back for Phase Two, and I can pull the rest of this warmth out so you perfectly match your sister."

Morgan ran her hands through the honey-colored strands, nodding pragmatically. "It's fine. It looks healthy. We'll deploy Twin Magic next month. The 'Fire' protocol still works as a redhead."

David wheeled himself forward, offering a sharp, happy hum as he reached out to hug both of his cousins. The salon ordeal was over, and the Vanguard was back in motion.

When they stepped out into the humid evening air and approached Meredith's sedan, David didn't head for the front passenger seat. He tapped the rear door. Meredith opened it, and David transferred into the back, pulling himself across the upholstery so he could sit directly next to Morgan. As Meredith stowed his chair and got behind the wheel, David leaned his full weight against Morgan, resting his head on her shoulder. Morgan wrapped her arm around him, letting him cuddle into her side, supplying the Deep Pressure Therapy he needed after a long, exhausting day in the public eye.

"Next stop, the city library," Meredith announced, shifting the car into gear. "Kate texted that her doctor's appointment wrapped up an hour ago and her dad dropped her off at the reading room to wait for us."

It was a short drive from the salon to the Virginia Beach public library. Meredith parked near the accessible ramp. She hopped out, and a few moments later, she returned with Kate, pushing her paternal cousin's manual wheelchair to the passenger side of the sedan.

Meredith opened the front door and expertly assisted Kate with the transfer. It was a fluid, highly practiced maneuver, shifting Kate from the chair to the car seat while carefully managing the tubing of her urinary collection bag so nothing snagged. Once Kate was buckled in, Meredith folded the second wheelchair, stowing it in the trunk next to David's.

As Meredith climbed into the driver's seat and started the engine, Morgan leaned forward from the back seat, resting her chin on the center console.

"Hey, Kate," Morgan said, a wicked grin spreading across her face. "Just a heads-up. On Wednesday, Meredith and I are going to escort David to his SDV 100 class. And we might need to deploy the Poop Button."

Kate slowly turned her head in the passenger seat. She locked eyes with Meredith, her face completely deadpan. She offered a ferocious, unblinking glare, staring absolute daggers at her cousin, pretending to be furious that they were once again weaponizing her bowel management system.

The tense silence held for exactly three seconds.

Then, Kate’s shoulders began to shake. She completely broke character, her glare melting into a massive smile as she burst into a series of silent, breathy, uncontrollable giggles.

David squeezed Morgan's arm, laughing silently in the back seat. Kate tapped the dashboard twice—a specific, questioning rhythm they all recognized. *Who is the customer?*

"Her name is Vesper," Meredith explained, navigating the sedan out of the library parking lot. "She's in the class next door to David's. She tried to isolate him in an elevator at the mall today. She was screaming at him about a lunch order because she didn't realize he had his own money."

Morgan's tone darkened. "It was an isolation tactic. Just like what Connor tried to do."

At the mention of Connor's name, the laughter in the car instantly vanished. Kate’s smile faded, replaced by a quiet, lingering sadness. She looked out the passenger window at the passing trees. Connor hadn't just *seemed* like a good guy—he genuinely was one. He had sat with her at lunch, treated her with respect, and they had shared a wonderful, typical high school romance. Because of her literal processing, she had fundamentally believed he cared about her, and he truly did. But then came the day his parents left town. He had thrown away their entire genuine connection for a sudden, impulsive, and terrible choice—a decision that ended with a stolen 1997 Holt sedan wrapped around a utility pole.

Now, her body was permanently altered, and a boy who used to hold her hand was rotting away in a Virginia state penitentiary.

Meredith reached across the center console, resting her hand gently on Kate's knee. "I know, Kate," she said softly. "I know. But Vesper tried to pull the same trick today. She tried to catch David alone because she thought he was an easy target. That's why we're shutting it down."

Kate turned back from the window. She looked at Meredith, then glanced back at David and Morgan, and gave a single, firm nod. The sadness was pushed aside, replaced by the unified, unyielding resolve of the Vanguard. If someone was threatening David, the Poop Button was officially authorized.

"Alright," Meredith said, turning onto Delaney Street, the familiar, comforting sight of their neighborhood coming into view. "Let's get inside. When the adults get home from work tonight, we need to have a full family briefing on Vesper Vance. David, you can relax until the long-distance rates drop at nine. Then, you can finally call Hannah."

## 6:45 PM — The Family Briefing

The kitchen at 1854 Delaney Street was filled with the chaotic, comforting hum of the evening routine. The microwave beeped repeatedly as various containers of leftovers were reheated and passed around the large dining table. Morgan was eating with absolute, ravenous focus. Because her marathon color-correction appointment at the salon had taken five grueling hours, she had completely missed lunch, and she was making up for it now with a massive plate of baked ziti.

David sat between his mother and Meredith, a plate of warm food in front of him. The entire family—his parents, Kate's father, and the four cousins—were finally gathered together. It was time for the debrief.

Meredith set her fork down and looked across the table at the adults. With the same icy, clinical precision she used to dismantle threats in public, she laid out the exact sequence of events regarding Vesper Vance. She detailed the seemingly polite introduction in the student lounge, the walk to the mall, the deliberate isolation tactic in the glass elevator, and the sudden screaming over the lunch menu.

David’s father stopped eating, his posture instantly rigid. Kate’s father leaned forward, his eyes narrowing as he listened to the familiar, terrifying pattern of an outsider trying to corner a vulnerable member of their pack.

"The moment the elevator doors opened on the third floor, David threw himself at me," Meredith explained, her voice steady. "And the very first thing Vesper did wasn't to ask if he was okay. She stared directly at my chest, and then she glared at me like she didn't believe for a single second that we were actually cousins."

David let out a low, exasperated groan, tapping his hand against the table in annoyance. He knew exactly what Meredith meant. Meredith, Morgan, and Kate all possessed chests that were disproportionately large for their frames—a physical trait that made them constant targets for the neurotypical world's superficial, judgmental stares.

David couldn't possibly care less about any of that. To him, the women in his family were simply his safe harbor. Their frames meant that when the sensory overload of the world became too much, they gave the most amazing, secure, all-encompassing hugs that instantly grounded him. Society saw an aesthetic; David just saw the people who were always there to offer comfort.

The adults at the table exchanged a dark, knowing look. They didn't need to hear about the twins' secret plans for the Poop Button or the Pee Bag; the reality of the situation was serious enough.

David and Kate were eighteen years old, which meant they were officially and permanently under strict legal Guardianships and Conservatorships administered through the Circuit Court. Because of their lifelong mental incapacity, neither David nor Kate possessed the cognitive framework to ever legally consent to a sexual encounter. To the family, Vesper wasn't just an annoying college girl; if she was actively trying to isolate David for her own desires, she was a predator walking blindly toward a felony boundary violation.

"You handled it perfectly, Mer," David's father said, his voice thick with protective authority. He looked at both twins, silently validating the proxy guardianship powers he and his brother had officially delegated to them. "If she approaches him again on Wednesday, you shut it down immediately. You have full clearance."

Morgan swallowed a massive bite of ziti and pointed her fork across the table. "Don't worry, Uncle Leo. We've got it covered. She won't get within ten feet of him."

Kate, sitting across from David, offered a sharp, breathy giggle, fully aware of exactly *how* Morgan planned to keep Vesper away.

With the perimeter officially secured and the adults briefed, the tension at the table finally dissipated. David finished his leftovers, his eyes constantly darting toward the digital clock on the microwave.

It was 6:45 PM. He felt a nervous, excited flutter in his stomach. He just had to wait a little over two hours. At 9:00 PM, the exorbitant long-distance phone rates would finally drop. He could retreat to the safety of the converted garage, pick up the landline, and finally place the call across the Pacific Ocean to hear Hannah's voice in Honolulu.
