## 8:00 PM — The Evening Routine

Morgan stood in the center of the expansive, accessible bathroom, wrestling her waist-length, honey-caramel hair into a massive plastic claw clip. With a dramatic sigh, she stretched a crinkly, oversized plastic shower cap over her head, snapping the elastic band securely against her forehead.

"Tracy was very clear," Morgan announced, pointing a stern finger at Meredith as the twins wheeled David and Kate into the tiled room. "Seventy-two hours. The bleach blew my hair cuticles wide open. If I get this wet, or if I even stand in too much steam, this expensive toner washes right down the drain and I go back to looking like a rusty penny."

"Understood," Meredith said, rolling up her sleeves. "You are officially banished from the splash zone."

Within the walls of the converted garage, privacy and shyness simply did not exist. The evening caregiving routine was handled with the same casual, deeply practical love as making dinner. Modesty had long ago been replaced by absolute trust and clinical efficiency.

Morgan and Meredith seamlessly coordinated to assist David with his toileting routine, moving with practiced, gentle mechanics that preserved his dignity while handling his physical needs. Once David was settled, the twins turned their attention to Kate.

Meredith executed a flawless pivot-transfer, moving Kate from her manual wheelchair to the toilet. Morgan knelt beside her, retrieving the specialized fluid bags to connect to Kate's MACE.

"Alright, initiate launch sequence," Morgan deadpanned, prepping the hose to introduce the fluid into the surgical channel on Kate's abdomen. "Preparing the Poop Chute for deployment. We need this hardware fully calibrated if we're going to threaten Vesper with it on Wednesday."

Kate’s shoulders began to shake immediately. She stared at the bathroom wall, trying her hardest to maintain a serious expression, but the sheer absurdity of the twins' toilet humor broke her down into a series of silent, breathy giggles. David watched from his chair, double-blinking in warm amusement as Morgan efficiently unhooked Kate's used urinary collection bag, properly disposing of it and swapping it out for a fresh, sterile plastic pouch.

When the medical maintenance was complete, it was time for the showers. Morgan stepped in to help Meredith carefully undress David and Kate, managing the heavy lifting and untangling of clothes. As soon as her cousins were ready for the water, Morgan backed toward the door, shielding her plastic-wrapped head.

"I'm out," Morgan declared, pulling the door open. "Good luck in the humidity."

Meredith groaned playfully as the door clicked shut. Stripping down to join her cousins in the shower was a daily necessity; it was the only way to safely support their spastic muscles and thoroughly wash their hair without soaking her own clothes. Following the strict rules of their household caregiving, she focused entirely on the practical mechanics of the routine, ensuring the warm water eased the tension in David and Kate's limbs while maintaining the utmost respect for their physical boundaries.

"I literally cannot wait for this seventy-two-hour rule to be over," Meredith complained over the hiss of the showerhead, carefully lathering David's hair. "Doing the heavy lifting in here by myself is exhausting."

Out in the cool, dry air of the garage barracks, Morgan changed into her comfortable cotton pajamas. She moved around the room, pulling out clean sleepwear for Meredith, Kate, and David, laying them out in neat piles on the edge of the massive, shared mattress.

She stared at the empty space on the bed where Hannah used to sleep.

"Man," Morgan muttered quietly to herself, smoothing out a wrinkle in David's favorite tagless shirt. "I really wish Hannah was here. Four sets of hands made this so much easier."

But as the digital clock on the bedside table glowed 8:30 PM, Morgan smiled. The physical heavy lifting for the day was almost over. In just thirty minutes, the long-distance rates would drop, and the missing piece of their Vanguard would finally be on the other end of the line.

## 8:55 PM — The Summons

The bathroom door cracked open just an inch, releasing a thick, humid cloud of warm air. Morgan stood in the dry hallway, her plastic shower cap firmly in place, extending her arms blindly through the gap to hand off the neat stacks of cotton pajamas.

"I'm keeping my distance," Morgan called out through the crack. "Hurry up and get dressed. It's almost nine."

Inside, the shower was off, and Meredith worked with her usual clinical efficiency. She quickly dried and dressed herself, then moved seamlessly to assist David and Kate. Once they were comfortably in their pajamas and Kate’s fresh urinary collection bag was securely tucked out of the way, Meredith opened the door and wheeled her cousins out into the hallway.

They bypassed the converted garage entirely and headed straight for the living room, where David's parents and Kate's father were already gathered around the couch.

David parked his manual chair near the coffee table, pulling his travel keyboard onto his lap and ensuring it was securely tethered to the OmniTalk 3000. He watched the digital clock on the cable box flip from 8:59 to 9:00 PM.

The exorbitant long-distance rates had officially dropped.

David didn't hesitate. He picked up the cordless landline receiver from the end table, dialed the familiar 808 area code, and pressed the phone between his ear and shoulder.

It rang exactly once before the line clicked open.

"David!" Hannah's voice burst through the receiver. She sounded breathless, practically vibrating with excitement. But there was a slight, tinny echo to her voice. She was on speakerphone. "Don't type anything yet. Put me on speaker on your end. Are the twins and your parents there? I need everyone to hear this!"

David blinked, completely caught off guard. He had fully prepared his AAC device for a standard, quiet recap of their respective first days of school. He fumbled slightly, pressing the speaker button on the handset and setting the phone down on the coffee table.

Meredith, Morgan, and the adults immediately leaned in, the low murmur of the living room dropping to absolute silence.

"We're here, Hannah," David's dad said. "You're on speaker. What's going on?"

The deep, commanding voice of Lieutenant Commander Thomas Everett came through the line next. "Good evening, everyone. I hope you're all sitting down, because I have an official announcement."

David gripped the armrests of his wheelchair, his heart hammering against his ribs.

"After twenty years of serving Uncle Sam," Tom continued, the immense pride evident in his tone, "my retirement paperwork has officially cleared. The ceremony will be held this summer at Naval Station Pearl Harbor. The date is Friday, June 25th, 2004. And consider this a direct, non-negotiable summons. I want the entire Delaney family in the front row."

David let out a loud, sustained hum of absolute joy, his spastic muscles tightening with excitement. He looked at Morgan and Meredith, whose jaws had dropped in shock.

"But that's not all," Hannah's voice cut back in, cutting through the cheers erupting in the Virginia Beach living room. "Dad gets a Home of Selection move from the Navy. They're paying to ship all our stuff wherever we want." She paused, and David could practically hear the smile on her face. "We chose Virginia Beach. We're coming back for good."

David slapped his hands onto his lap, overwhelmed by a rush of pure adrenaline. The two-year countdown was almost over.

"David," Hannah said, her tone softening, speaking directly to him through the speakerphone. "I don't care about the legal paperwork, or the guardianship, or any of that. None of it matters to me. I just want to be with you. We're coming home."

The sheer magnitude of the words hit David’s neurological system like a freight train. His autistic brain, which relied so heavily on routine and predictable data, simply could not process the overwhelming, infinite tidal wave of pure joy. It short-circuited entirely.

A severe, involuntary meltdown overtook him in an instant. A ragged, unshielded sob tore out of his throat, and tears began streaming down his face in hot, heavy tracks. The emotional overload immediately triggered the spasticity in his cerebral palsy; his limbs went rigid, his hands pulling awkwardly toward his chest as he gasped for air, crying his eyes out in the middle of the living room.

Meredith and Morgan did not hesitate for a single microsecond. The "Ice" and "Fire" protocols were abandoned completely. They lunged forward, sandwiching him in the center of his wheelchair. Morgan wrapped her arms fiercely around his chest, and Meredith pressed heavily against his back and shoulders. It was a bone-crushing, desperate hug, delivering immediate and maximum Deep Pressure Therapy to manually override his sensory overload.

On the coffee table, the speakerphone remained entirely free of judgment. Hannah and Tom didn't hang up, and they didn't try to talk over his sobs. They stayed right there on the line in Oahu, projecting absolute, unwavering patience. They knew exactly how David's autism worked, and they refused to abandon him in his moment of need.

Tears soaked into Meredith’s pajama shirt as David desperately tried to reach for his travel keyboard. His fine motor control was completely shattered by the spasms and the crying. He jabbed blindly at the keys, his trembling fingers slipping off the board. He let out a frustrated, heartbroken keen.

"I've got you," Meredith whispered, reaching down. She didn't type it for him; she simply placed her steady hand over his shaking one, guiding his stiff fingers to the correct keys, one by one.

He slammed the trigger.

"I love you," the robotic voice echoed, entirely failing to capture the massive, overwhelming emotion behind the words, but Hannah understood perfectly.

"I love you too," she promised, her own voice thick with unshed tears. "And you don't even have to wait until June to see me. Dad can't get leave from the command to travel, but he bought me a ticket. I'm flying into ORF to spend Christmas with you guys this year."

The absolute certainty in her voice, combined with the crushing, continuous weight of his cousins' embrace, finally broke through the static in David's mind. His breathing hitched, then slowly began to steady. Meredith and Morgan maintained their fierce grip until the rigid spasticity finally left his muscles, successfully anchoring him and pulling him out of the meltdown.

The living room exploded into celebration. Kate clapped her hands together, letting out a series of breathy giggles, while Morgan yanked her plastic shower cap off and threw it across the room in triumph.

For the next half hour, the phone call transitioned into a chaotic, joyous logistical planning session. They had ten months to prepare for the massive deployment across the Pacific Ocean. Meredith immediately grabbed a legal pad, taking notes on flight routes through Atlanta and gate-checking protocols for the manual wheelchairs. David's parents began calculating the time-off requests they would need to submit.

David just sat back in his chair, listening to the cross-talk between the people he loved most in the world. He didn't need to type anything else. Between Hannah coming for Christmas and the entire Vanguard flying to Oahu next summer, the agonizing era of long-distance survival was effectively dead.

He looked over at Meredith and Morgan, who were already enthusiastically debating the best way to pack Kate's medical supplies for a ten-hour flight. Vesper Vance and her petty, manipulative elevator traps felt a million miles away.

The perimeter was secure, and the anomaly was finally coming home.

## 10:15 PM — The Vanguard Rests

The adrenaline of the phone call slowly gave way to the deep, heavy exhaustion of a long, emotionally massive day. As the adults dispersed to their own rooms and the house began to quiet down, Meredith and Morgan took the push handles of the manual wheelchairs and guided David and Kate back into the cool, familiar safety of the converted garage barracks.

With the evening caregiving routine already completed, all that was left was the final transition. Working in perfect, unspoken tandem, the twins assisted their cousins with the transfers. They moved Kate first, settling her comfortably into the center of the massive shared mattress and ensuring the tubing for her urinary collection bag was completely secure and unkinked for the night. Next, they helped David out of his chair, supporting his weight and gently easing him down onto the soft blankets right beside her.

As soon as his body hit the mattress, David reached out, his muscles loose and thoroughly relaxed after the earlier meltdown. He pulled Kate into a warm, secure cuddle. She nestled happily against his chest, letting out a soft, contented hum as she accepted the grounding weight of his embrace.

Meredith climbed onto the far left edge of the bed, pulling the thick comforter up over her shoulders. Morgan navigated around the manual wheelchairs parked at the foot of the bed and climbed onto the far right edge. By taking the outermost flanks, the identical twins effectively formed a physical, impenetrable perimeter. They acted as living, breathing bed rails, ensuring that if David or Kate’s spastic muscles twitched or rolled during the night, they would be met with the safe, immovable presence of their fiercely protective cousins.

"Alright," Morgan said softly, reaching over to click off the bedside lamp. The garage plunged into a comfortable, quiet darkness, illuminated only by the faint amber glow of the digital clock. "Lights out, Vanguard. We have a war to plan for Wednesday."

Meredith let out a quiet, tired laugh from the other side of the bed. "Vesper doesn't stand a chance. Go to sleep."

Kate’s breathing had already begun to slow and deepen, lulled by the safety of the sleep pile. But David lay perfectly awake in the dark, his arm draped protectively over Kate.

His body was exhausted, completely drained by the sensory input of the college campus, the mall, the hair salon, and the overwhelming emotional overload of the evening. Yet, his mind was absolutely racing. He stared up at the dark ceiling of the garage, a massive, unshielded smile spreading across his face.

The agonizing wait was finally breaking. The long-distance phone calls, the painful time zones, and the empty space in the bed were coming to an end. He didn't just have a plan to deal with his college class, and he didn't just have the absolute security of his family.

He had Christmas. He had June.

Hannah was coming home.
