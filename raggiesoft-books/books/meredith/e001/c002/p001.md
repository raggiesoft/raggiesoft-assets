## 8:30 AM — Morning in the Barracks

The Tuesday morning sun filtered through the high, frosted windows of the converted garage, casting a soft, warm glow across the massive shared mattress.

There were no blaring alarms today. With Kate's Student Development class not scheduled until 1:00 PM at the CCHR Virginia Beach campus, the Vanguard was allowed the rare luxury of a slow, deeply peaceful morning.

In the center of the bed, David and Kate were still perfectly secure in their sleep pile. David’s arm was draped loosely over Kate's waist, his breathing slow and even. On the outermost edges of the mattress, Meredith and Morgan remained firmly stationed as the living bed rails, their bodies creating an impenetrable physical perimeter.

Kate was the first to wake. She blinked her eyes open, feeling the comforting, heavy weight of David's arm anchoring her. She let out a quiet, contented hum, staring up at the familiar ceiling of the garage. But as the reality of the day began to boot up in her mind, a small knot of anxiety tightened in her chest.

Today was her turn.

She turned her head slightly, looking over at Morgan. Morgan was already awake, silently staring at the ceiling while wearing a massive, ridiculous silk sleep bonnet to protect her highly vulnerable, honey-caramel hair from the friction of the pillows.

Kate reached out, tapping a specific, rapid rhythm on the mattress near Morgan’s elbow. *Are you all coming?*

Morgan turned her head, the silk bonnet shifting slightly. She offered Kate a soft, reassuring smile, immediately reading the anxiety behind the literal processing. Kate had listened to the entire debrief about Vesper Vance last night. After the trauma of Connor—who had used the guise of a polite, helpful student to isolate her—the thought of navigating a college campus alone, or even with just one cousin, was terrifying. She didn't want to risk running into a male version of Vesper. She wanted maximum security.

"Every single one of us is coming," Morgan whispered fiercely, reaching across the blankets to squeeze Kate's hand. "Meredith is driving, I'm riding shotgun, and David is rolling right next to you. It's a full Vanguard deployment."

On the other side of the bed, Meredith shifted, her pragmatic internal clock finally waking her up. She stretched her arms over her head and sat up, looking down at her cousins.

"Good morning," Meredith said, her voice rough with sleep. She looked at the digital clock on the nightstand, which read 8:45 AM. "We have hours before we need to leave for the Virginia Beach campus. We can take our time today."

David stirred at the sound of Meredith's voice, his muscles tightening slightly before relaxing as he realized where he was. He offered a sharp, happy grunt, pulling Kate into a brief, tight morning cuddle before letting go so they could start the day.

The morning routine was a masterclass in relaxed, coordinated efficiency. Meredith and Morgan climbed out of bed, pulling the manual wheelchairs over to the edge of the mattress. They assisted David and Kate with their morning transfers, moving them safely from the bed to their chairs.

Once they wheeled into the bathroom, the dynamic shifted back to the strict rules established by Tracy the cosmetologist.

"I'm on dry duty again," Morgan announced, adjusting her silk bonnet and keeping a safe distance from the sinks. "Meredith, you're handling the splash zone."

Meredith rolled her eyes playfully but immediately took point. She assisted Kate with her morning hygiene and efficiently checked her MACE site, ensuring the area was clean and secure. Then, she unhooked Kate's overnight urinary collection bag, properly disposing of the fluid and swapping it for a fresh, low-profile day bag that could be discreetly strapped to Kate's calf under her jeans.

By 9:30 AM, the entire Vanguard was dressed, transferred back to their chairs, and rolling into the Delaney Street kitchen for breakfast.

David parked his manual chair at the table and flipped open his laptop, tethering his travel keyboard to the OmniTalk 3000. He was still riding the incredible emotional high of last night's phone call with Hannah.

"Are you ready for your class?" the robotic voice asked, directed at Kate as she sat across from him.

Kate looked down at her own AAC device mounted to her wheelchair. She typed slowly, her expression serious.

"Yes," her device spoke, its tone perfectly flat. "As long as you are all there to stare menacingly at anyone who approaches me."

Morgan laughed, sliding a plate of toaster waffles onto the table in front of her. "Oh, we're going to do way more than stare, Kate. If any guy even thinks about pulling a Connor or a Vesper today, we're skipping Stage One entirely and going straight to threatening them with Acoustic Bathroom Warfare."

Kate buried her face in her hands, her shoulders shaking with silent, breathy giggles. It was absurd, but it worked. With the Vanguard surrounding her, the Virginia Beach campus didn't seem so scary after all.

## 12:15 PM — The Acoustic Clarification

By noon, the slow, peaceful morning had transitioned into a highly coordinated military operation. The Vanguard assembled in the driveway around Meredith’s practical sedan.

Meredith and Morgan operated like a well-oiled pit crew. Meredith took the driver’s side, expertly transferring Kate into the backseat while managing the tubing of her day bag. Morgan handled the passenger side, assisting David into the seat right next to his cousin. In a matter of seconds, both manual wheelchairs were collapsed, stacked, and secured in the spacious trunk.

Meredith climbed behind the wheel, engaged the clutch, and shifted into first gear. As they pulled out of the Delaney Street driveway and headed toward the CCHR Virginia Beach campus, Kate tapped a specific, questioning rhythm against the back of Meredith's seat.

"Don't worry," Meredith said, glancing at Kate in the rearview mirror. "I know what you're asking. We've refined the protocol."

Morgan turned around in the passenger seat, carefully keeping her silk bonnet from snagging on the headrest. "Just to be absolutely clear on the rules of engagement today, Kate: we are not dragging anyone into a bathroom. That’s kidnapping."

Kate nodded slowly, offering a deadpan blink. *Glad we established that.*

"Exactly," Morgan continued, a wicked grin spreading across her face. "Acoustic Warfare is a strictly mobile operation. If some guy tries to isolate you in the hallway or the student lounge, Meredith and I will simply execute a flanking maneuver. We will stand on either side of him and deploy maximum-volume, wet fog-horn noises in stereo."

David slapped his knee in the back seat, letting out a sharp, breathy giggle.

"We bring the Battle Shits directly to the customer," Meredith agreed, downshifting as they approached a red light. "Right out in the open. By the time we hit the second volley, he'll be questioning reality and running for the exits."

Kate buried her face in her hands, shaking her head. Through the gaps in her fingers, she was smiling. The sheer absurdity of her cousins threatening to publicly fart on an unsuspecting college student was ridiculous, but it completely eradicated her anxiety. A predator looking for a quiet, vulnerable target would never survive an encounter with the twins' chaotic energy.

Ten minutes later, Meredith pulled into the sprawling, suburban CCHR Virginia Beach campus. Unlike the vertical concrete towers of downtown Norfolk, the VB campus was a wide collection of brick buildings connected by open courtyards and massive parking lots.

Meredith parked in an accessible space near the main academic building. The twins jumped out, quickly retrieving the manual wheelchairs from the trunk. They transferred David and Kate with their usual clinical efficiency, ensuring Kate's leg bag was completely hidden beneath the hem of her jeans.

"Alright, Vanguard," Meredith said, slamming the trunk shut. "Formation."

They moved toward the glass entrance doors as a unified front. Kate rolled in the center, her hands gripping her push rims. David wheeled himself right beside her, his travel keyboard resting securely on his lap. Meredith and Morgan took the outer flanks, walking with their shoulders squared and their eyes scanning the crowds of college freshmen.

As they entered the air-conditioned hallway, Kate felt a profound wave of security wash over her. Students automatically stepped aside, giving the four of them a wide berth. No one was going to try and catch her alone today.

They navigated to the B-Wing, finding Kate's Student Development classroom with twenty minutes to spare. Unlike David's room with its tiny, restrictive bolted desks, this room had been recently updated with wide, modular tables.

Kate wheeled into a spot near the front, and David parked right next to her. Meredith and Morgan immediately claimed the two chairs directly behind them, establishing a physical barrier between their cousins and the rest of the room.

David typed quickly on his keyboard, hitting the trigger to send the text through his OmniTalk 3000.

"Perimeter secured," the robotic voice announced quietly to the group.

Morgan leaned forward, resting her elbows on the table behind Kate. "Let's see what the VB campus has to offer. My vocal cords are warmed up and ready for stereo."

## 12:45 PM — The Princess Anne Perimeter

The Vanguard navigated the wide, sunny corridors of the Princess Anne Building, a sprawling brick complex at the heart of the Virginia Beach campus. Unlike David's experience in the cramped Martin Building, the hallways here were wide enough that they could easily roll two abreast.

As they approached the correct classroom, Morgan stopped the group in a quiet alcove just outside the door. She unzipped her backpack and pulled out a massive, gallon-sized zip-top plastic bag filled with a strange, brown-and-orange mixture.

"Behold," Morgan whispered, holding the bag up like a trophy. "Acoustic ammunition."

Kate stared at the bag. She tapped her wheelchair armrest, giving Morgan a highly skeptical look.

"It's a proprietary blend of dried apricots, roasted soy nuts, and sugar-free chocolate chips," Meredith explained, her clinical "Ice" protocol fully analyzing the nutritional data. "The apricots provide a massive hit of soluble fiber. The soy nuts offer heavy complex carbohydrates. And the sugar-free chocolate is loaded with maltitol. If consumed rapidly, the human digestive tract simply cannot process it."

"It creates rapid, highly pressurized gastrointestinal distress within thirty minutes," Morgan added, shaking the bag so the soy nuts rattled. "If some guy tries to corner you, Meredith and I will down two handfuls of this each. We will become weapons of mass flatulence."

David slapped his hands over his mouth, his shoulders shaking violently as he tried to contain a burst of laughter. Kate closed her eyes and let her head fall back against her headrest, letting out a series of breathy, silent giggles. The sheer, calculated insanity of her cousins' biological warfare planning was the perfect antidote to her first-day jitters.

"Keep it in the bag," Kate's AAC device stated flatly. "I am going in alone."

Just like David had done on Monday, Kate was determined to tackle the actual classroom environment by herself. The Vanguard was her shield against the unpredictable hallways and the threat of isolation, but in the structured, heavily supervised environment of the classroom, she wanted to prove her independence.

"You've got this," Meredith said, stepping back to clear the path.

"We'll be right here," Morgan promised, zipping the biological hazard mix safely back into her bag. "If you need us, just tap the SOS rhythm on your desk. We'll hear it."

Kate gave them a firm nod, gripped her push rims, and wheeled herself through the heavy wooden door.

The classroom was modern and spacious. She easily maneuvered her manual chair into a spot at an accessible table near the front. The professor, an older man with a kind smile, was busy writing the syllabus outline on the whiteboard. As the room slowly filled with other freshmen, Kate kept a watchful eye, but no one approached her with any undue interest. A few students offered polite smiles, which she returned, but the terrifying specter of another Connor never materialized.

Out in the hallway, the Vanguard held the perimeter. David backed his wheelchair against the wall, flipped open his laptop, and happily spent the next fifty minutes typing up a history essay draft while Meredith and Morgan stood guard, casually leaning against the brickwork and watching the flow of student traffic.

At exactly 1:50 PM, the classroom door swung open, and the students began to pour out.

Kate wheeled herself into the hallway, a look of profound relief on her face.

David immediately looked up from his laptop. He typed quickly, sending the text through the OmniTalk 3000. "Status report?"

Kate’s fingers moved across her own device. "The class was easy. We just went over the syllabus. And no one bothered me."

Meredith let out a long breath, her posture relaxing completely. "Good. The perimeter holds."

Morgan let out a dramatic sigh, patting the front pocket of her backpack. "Well, I'm glad you're safe, Kate. But I'm not going to lie... part of me is a little disappointed I didn't get to deploy the apricots."

Kate rolled her eyes, but she reached out and grabbed Morgan's hand, giving it a grateful squeeze. The first day of college was officially in the books for both of them, and against all odds, it had been a complete success.
