## Ground Rules

The fluorescent lights of the classroom hummed, a stark contrast to the humid warmth of a Blacksburg summer afternoon outside. This was HLTH 201: Dimensions of Human Sexuality.

For Freddie, this class was a strategic maneuver. The university’s Health and Physical Education requirement was mandatory for graduation, usually filled by sweating through tennis, jogging, or swimming. With his cerebral palsy, those options ranged from "difficult" to "logistical nightmare." But HLTH 201 counted for the same credit, and the only muscle he had to exercise here was his brain.

He sat in the second row, the sweet spot for serious students who didn’t want to appear eager. Flanking him were Paige and Jessica, the three of them claiming a block of desks like a defensive line.

"I give it ten minutes before the frat boys in the back row crack," Jessica whispered, leaning in from Freddie's left. She had her notebook open but her pen was capped, her eyes scanning the room with amused cynicism.

"Five," Paige countered from his right. She had already written the date and the course title at the top of her page in neat, architectural block letters. "Look at them. They’re already vibrating."

She wasn’t wrong. The room was set for thirty, and while half the class looked like Freddie—students checking a box for a degree requirement—the other half were the types who had seen the word "Sexuality" in the course catalog and assumed this would be an easy A or a comedy hour.

The door shut with a heavy click, and Dr. Aris Thorne walked to the podium. She was a woman who radiated a clinical, no-nonsense authority. She didn’t smile. She didn’t try to be cool. She set a stack of syllabi on the desk and looked over her glasses at the room.

"Welcome to Dimensions of Human Sexuality," Dr. Thorne said. Her voice was calm but projected to the back corners of the room without a microphone. "Let us establish the ground rules immediately."

The shuffling of backpacks ceased.

"In this room, there are no secrets," she began, pacing slowly in front of the whiteboard. "We are studying human biology, psychology, and sociology. We will treat these subjects with the scientific dignity they deserve. If you are here to giggle, drop the class now. There is a waiting list of students who actually need this credit."

Freddie adjusted his posture, grateful for the sturdy desk chair. He saw Paige nod in approval.

"I will use proper terminology," Dr. Thorne continued. "You will hear words like 'penis,' 'vulva,' 'intercourse,' and 'masturbation' frequently. However..." She paused, picking up a dry-erase marker. "You are entering a world where you may be educators, healthcare providers, or counselors. The people you interact with will not always use textbooks terms."

She turned and wrote a single word on the board: *Terminology*.

"We will learn the clinical definitions," she said, facing them again. "But we will also introduce and discuss the slang. The street terms. The words you hear in movies and locker rooms. Not because I want you to use them in your papers, but because when a patient or a student comes to you confused and uses those words, you need to know exactly what they are talking about without flinching."

From the back row, a stifled snort broke the silence. It was the distinct sound of someone trying to hold back a laugh and failing.

Dr. Thorne didn't even look up from her notes. "I assume that was a involuntary reflex," she said dryly. "Get it out of your system now. By Chapter 3, the novelty will have worn off."

Jessica leaned toward Freddie, her voice barely a breath. "She's good."

"She has to be," Freddie whispered back.

"This is a safe space," Dr. Thorne said, her eyes locking on the back row. "Questions are encouraged. Ignorance is expected—that is why you are here. But disrespect will result in an immediate ejection. We are discussing the mechanics of human life. You will respect the subject, and you will respect each other."

She tapped the stack of papers on her desk.

"Syllabuses are coming around. We start with anatomy on Wednesday. I suggest you do the reading. If you are uncomfortable discussing the human body, the registrar closes at 5:00 PM."

Freddie took a syllabus as the pile passed him, handing the rest to Paige. He glanced at the course schedule. It was dense, clinical, and thorough. It was exactly what he needed to get his degree without worrying about his legs giving out on a track. He uncapped his pen. He was ready to work.

## Zero Tolerance

Dr. Thorne moved through the syllabus with the efficiency of a surgeon. She didn’t pace; she planted herself behind the podium and let the weight of the topics settle over the room.

"Week four covers reproductive anatomy," she said, tapping the document. "Week five, the physiology of sexual response. Week six, we begin our unit on contraception and STI prevention. Do not assume you know this material because you took a health class in ninth grade. The statistics suggest otherwise."

She flipped a page. "Regarding visual aids: We will be viewing diagrams and clinical photography. These are medical images. They are not pornography. If you cannot distinguish between the two, you will struggle to pass the midterm."

The room was silent, save for the scratching of pens. Freddie appreciated the bluntness. There was no ambiguity here.

Then, Dr. Thorne reached the section on sexual dysfunction. "We will discuss common issues ranging from erectile dysfunction to vaginismus..."

A sharp, high-pitched giggle erupted from the third row.

It wasn’t a cough disguised as a laugh. It was a full-blown, open-mouthed cackle. A girl with blonde highlights and a popped collar—who had spent the last ten minutes whispering to her neighbor—had apparently found the medical terminology hilarious. She was exactly the type Freddie had clocked earlier: someone who signed up hoping for an "easy A" and perhaps some titillating live demonstrations, treating a university course like a bachelorette party.

The silence that followed was absolute.

Dr. Thorne stopped mid-sentence. She didn’t sigh. She didn’t scold. She simply pointed a manicured finger at the door.

"Out," she said. Her voice was level, devoid of anger but terrifyingly final.

The girl blinked, her smile faltering. "What? I was just—"

"You were disrupting my lecture and disrespecting the material," Dr. Thorne said. "Pack your bag. You are dropped from the course effective immediately. I will email the registrar before this class ends."

"But—"

"Now."

The girl’s face flushed a deep crimson. The reality of losing three credits in the first twenty minutes of the semester seemed to crash down on her. She shoved her notebook into her bag, the sound of the zipper loud in the quiet room. She stood up, smoothing her skirt, trying to salvage some scrap of dignity by pretending she didn't care.

She walked down the aisle, chin held high, passing the second row.

As she came abreast of Freddie, she paused. She had seen him walk in earlier, noting the uneven rhythm of his gait, the way his foot dragged slightly, and how heavily he leaned on the blonde girl next to him for stability. In her mind, she did the quick, cruel calculus of the immature: *Disability means desperation.*

She slowed her pace and locked eyes with him. Her expression shifted from embarrassment to a bizarre, heavy-lidded sultry gaze—an exaggerated, "I want you" look. It was performative and predatory, a smirk that said, *I bet you’d take whatever you could get.*

She winked, then pushed through the double doors, letting them slam behind her.

Freddie stared at the closed door, his stomach turning. He wasn't flattered; he was insulted. She had looked at him and seen an easy target, a lonely guy who would be grateful for the attention of a girl who just got kicked out of sex ed.

She couldn't have been more wrong.

He felt a hand slide firmly onto his forearm, squeezing with a familiar, grounding pressure. It was Paige. She didn't look at the door; she was looking at him, her blue eyes calm and knowing. On his other side, Jessica shifted in her seat, her shoulder pressing solidly against his, creating a physical barrier of support.

Freddie let out a breath, the tension in his spastic muscles easing under Paige's touch. He didn't need the validation of a stranger looking for a rebound. He had Paige, his twin and safe person. He had Jessica, his rock. He had five other sisters back home in Virginia Beach who made up his entire world. He had more love and support in his life than that girl would likely ever understand.

He glanced at Paige, offering a small, dismissive shrug. She smiled—a tiny, private expression that said, *I know. She’s gone. You’re safe.*

"As I was saying," Dr. Thorne continued, her voice slicing through the lingering awkwardness. "Vaginismus. Now, let’s turn to page four regarding the attendance policy..."

## Safe Harbor

The rest of the lecture blurred into a stream of biological terms and syllabus dates. Dr. Thorne didn’t let the tension linger; she buried it under a mountain of academic expectations. By the time she capped her marker and announced, "Class dismissed," the incident with the girl felt like a distant memory to everyone—except the three people in the second row.

As the room erupted into the sound of zippers and shuffling feet, Freddie didn't move immediately. This was part of the routine. The adrenaline spike from the confrontation, combined with sitting still for fifty minutes in a rigid desk chair, meant his legs would be seized up. The spasticity always hit harder after stress.

"Take your time," Jessica said, her voice low and steady. She stood up first, smoothing the front of her dark green top. She turned to face the aisle, effectively becoming a human shield against the rush of students trying to exit. Her posture was relaxed but firm—the Matriarch holding the line. No one was going to push past her to rush Freddie.

On his right, Paige shifted her backpack to her outside shoulder, leaving her left side clear. She stood up and turned toward him, her expression softening into that familiar, easy smile—the same one she’d worn in family photos since they were kids.

"Ready?" she asked.

Freddie nodded, gripping the edge of the desk to lever himself up. His right leg dragged, the muscle tight and resisting the command to straighten. He wobbled for a fraction of a second, a moment of vulnerability that usually made him grit his teeth in frustration.

But the moment he swayed, Paige was there.

She didn't make a big deal out of it. She simply stepped into his space, offering her shoulder as a solid, immovable anchor. Freddie wrapped his arm around her, pulling her close. To an outsider, it might have looked like a simple side-hug between siblings. To Freddie, it was a structural necessity.

He leaned his weight onto her, exhaling sharply as the tension in his lower back released. This was their default state. The grey cotton of her shirt against his arm, the scent of her shampoo, the way she instinctively braced her stance to support him—it was deep pressure therapy in motion.

"You okay?" Paige murmured, her voice vibrating against his side.

"Yeah," Freddie said, the tightness in his chest finally unspooling. "Just stiff."

"I got you."

Jessica glanced back at them, doing a quick visual check. Seeing Freddie secure against Paige, she nodded—a quick, professional confirmation that the "server" was stable.

"Let's get out of here," Jessica said, finally stepping aside to open the path. "I need coffee, and if we hurry, we can beat the rush at the student center."

They walked out together, a tight formation against the chaos of the hallway. Freddie moved with his distinct, uneven gait, heavily leaning on Paige with every other step. He wasn't hiding the limp, and he wasn't hiding the affection. If anyone else in the hallway looked at him with pity or mockery, he didn't notice.

He was too busy holding onto the sister who made the world stop spinning.

## The Predator

The journey from the classroom door to the building lobby was only fifty yards, but to Freddie, it felt like a forced march across a gravity-heavy planet.

His arms were draped heavily over his sisters’ shoulders. To his left, Jessica bore his weight, her shoulder pressing into his armpit. She was wearing a maroon CPI t-shirt emblazoned with the school mascot—**The Forger**—a muscular, bearded blacksmith raising a hammer. The graphic seemed to mock the current situation; while the painted man on her chest was made of iron, Freddie felt like he was made of wet sand.

To his right was Paige. She was wearing a plain navy blue t-shirt that was, objectively, a size too small. The fabric pulled tight across her chest and shoulders, outlining her bust in a way that usually made Freddie avert his gaze out of respect. But right now, he wasn't looking at her geometry; he was relying on her structure. She gripped his waist firmly, her hip locking against his thigh with every step to prevent his knee from buckling.

Freddie himself wore a simple tan t-shirt that fit perfectly, though it was currently damp with the sweat of exertion.

"Almost there," Paige whispered, adjusting her grip on his belt loop. "The bench is at twelve o'clock. Visual confirmation."

They rounded the corner into the lobby. The air was cooler here, the sunlight streaming in through the glass walls.

"Target acquired," Jessica murmured. "Preparing for docking procedure."

But the path to the bench was blocked.

Leaning against a pillar, right in the center of the lobby, was the girl.

She hadn't left the building. She was waiting. Her arms were crossed over her chest, pushing up her cleavage in a way that seemed calculated. She was popping gum with a rhythmic *snap-crack* that cut through the lobby noise.

When she saw them emerge—Freddie sagging between his sisters, his face pale—her eyes lit up. She didn't see three college students moving as a unit. She didn't see the Forger on Jessica’s shirt or the family resemblance in their faces.

She saw a target. She saw a guy who, in her estimation, probably spent his Saturday nights alone and would be grateful for a scrap of attention from someone like her.

She pushed off the pillar and stepped directly into their path.

"Hey," she purred. Her voice had dropped an octave from the shriek she’d used in class. It was now smoky, suggestive.

The Avery unit came to a halt. Freddie blinked, trying to clear the sweat from his eyes. He looked at her, then past her to the bench.

"Obstruction," Freddie whispered to Paige. "Path blocked."

The girl ignored the women holding him up. To her, they were background noise—staff, aides, maybe volunteers from the disability office. She scanned over Paige’s tight navy shirt with a dismissive sneer, writing her off completely.

"Rough class, huh?" the girl said, stepping closer, invading Freddie's personal space. She smelled of cheap vanilla perfume and ambition. "That teacher is such a prude. I bet you were bored out of your mind."

Freddie frowned. "The material was... standard," he rasped. "She enforced the syllabus."

The girl laughed, tossing her hair. "You're funny. I like that." She looked him up and down, her gaze lingering on his denim-clad legs, then moving up to his eyes with a predatory smirk.

"You look like you’ve had a hard day," she said, lowering her voice to a conspiratorial whisper. "You look tense. Maybe you need someone to help you... relax?"

She reached out, her hand aiming for the tan fabric of his chest. "I was thinking, since I got kicked out, maybe I need a private tutor? Someone to show me the... practical application?"

Jessica stiffened. Her grip on Freddie’s waist tightened. The Forger on her shirt seemed to glare.

Freddie stared at the girl. His brain attempted to parse the data. *Input:* Girl from class. *Status:* Expelled. *Action:* Blocking the bench. *Dialogue:* Illogical. Suggestive.

"I am not a tutor," Freddie said flatly. "I am a student. And I require a seat. My legs are failing."

The girl pouted, a theatrical expression. "Aww, poor baby. Your legs hurt?" She took another step, virtually chest-to-chest with him now. "I bet I can make you forget all about your legs. I bet I can make you feel *real* good."

She shot a dismissive glance at Paige and Jessica. "Why don't you ditch the help? We can go to my dorm. It's close."

The silence that followed was heavy.

"The help?" Jessica repeated. Her voice was ice cold.

The girl rolled her eyes. "Yeah. The nurses. The aides. Whatever. You guys can go. I’ve got him from here."

She reached for Freddie’s arm, trying to pull him away from Paige’s navy-clad shoulder.

That was a tactical error.

Paige didn't let go. She clamped down on Freddie’s bicep like a vice. She didn't look at the girl with anger; she looked at her with the pity one reserves for a mosquito about to hit a bug zapper.

"He isn't going anywhere," Paige said pleasantly.

"Excuse me?" the girl snapped, her mask slipping. "I'm talking to him. Not you."

"And he is talking to you," Jessica interjected, stepping forward slightly, effectively using her shoulder to box the girl out. "He said he needs to sit down. You are in the way."

"He wants to go with me," the girl insisted, looking at Freddie with that same sultry, desperate look. "Don't you, tiger? You want to have some fun? You want to know what a real woman feels like?"

Freddie looked at her. He looked at the heavy makeup and the popped collar. Then he looked at the two women holding him up.

He looked at Jessica in her maroon school colors, the architect of his safety. He looked at Paige in her tight navy tee, the anchor of his sanity. These were the women who bathed him when he couldn't move, who slept next to him to keep the nightmares away, who learned an entire medical textbook just to make him feel safe.

He knew exactly what a real woman felt like. He was holding two of them.

"No," Freddie said. His voice wasn't loud, but it was clear. "I do not want 'fun.' I want stability. And you represent chaos."

The girl’s mouth fell open.

"Chaos?" she sputtered.

"Variable rejected," Freddie confirmed. "Please move. I need the bench."

He didn't wait for her to move. He gave the signal—a slight lean forward.

"Forward," Jessica commanded.

The phalanx moved. They didn't walk around her; they walked *through* the space she was occupying. The girl had to scramble backward, her sneakers squeaking on the tile, to avoid being run over by the sheer momentum of the Avery unit.

She stood there in the middle of the lobby, mouth agape, as the three siblings bypassed her completely.

They reached the bench. Paige and Jessica coordinated the descent, lowering Freddie gently until he was seated. He leaned back against the wall, closing his eyes, letting out a long, ragged exhale.

"Safe," Freddie whispered.

"Safe," Paige confirmed, sitting next to him and wrapping her arm around his shoulders.

The girl watched them for a moment, her face twisting into a sneer of rejection. She huffed, spun on her heel, and stormed out the front door, muttering something about "freaks."

Jessica watched her go, brushing a piece of lint off her Forger t-shirt. Then she sat on Freddie’s other side.

"She thought we were staff," Jessica noted dryly. "Nurses."

"She thought I was desperate," Freddie corrected, opening one eye. "She calculated that disability equals a zero-sum game for romantic selection. She assumed I would take any input provided."

"She sucks at math," Paige said, resting her head on his shoulder.

"Agreed," Freddie said, closing his eye again. He felt the warmth of his sisters on both sides—maroon on the left, navy on the right. The bench was hard, the lobby was bright, but the perimeter was secure.

"Five minutes," Freddie requested. "Then the bus."

"Take ten," Jessica said, crossing her legs in her blue jeans. "We aren't going anywhere."
