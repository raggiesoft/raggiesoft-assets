## The Cool Down

The **Blue Ridge Transit** bus hissed to a halt in the sawtooth bay of **Pepper Plaza**. The doors folded open, and the Avery unit stepped out onto the concrete.

The heat in Christiansburg was just as aggressive as it had been in Blacksburg. The air was heavy, thick with moisture and the smell of asphalt baking in the sun.

"Home," Jessica announced, pointing diagonally across the street.

They waited for a break in the traffic on First Street, then crossed quickly to the sanctuary of **105 Pepper Street**.

Jessica unlocked the front door. They stumbled inside, the air conditioning hitting them like a blessing. The hum of the window unit was the best sound Freddie had heard all day.

"Locking," Jessica said, throwing the deadbolt and engaging the security chain.

"Decontamination," Paige gasped, pulling her sticky t-shirt away from her skin. "I feel gross. The humidity is... excessive."

"Agreed," Freddie said, leaning against the wall to kick off his sneakers. "Thermal regulation is required immediately."

They moved as a unit down the hallway to the Master Bedroom—the **Silo**.

There was no hesitation. The "Modesty Protocol" had been deleted days ago. In this heat, and after the stress of the encounter at Kincaid Hall, clothes were just another layer of friction.

Freddie peeled off his orange hoodie, which felt heavy and suffocating. He dropped it into the hamper. His jeans followed.

Paige and Jessica did the same, shedding their sweat-dampened clothes with sighs of relief.

"Tub," Jessica commanded. "Cool water. Not cold. Just cool."

They walked into the bathroom. The clear curtain was pulled back. Jessica reached in and turned the tap. She adjusted the mix until the water running over her wrist was a refreshing, tepid temperature—perfect for bringing down a core body temperature without shocking the system.

"Ready, Freddie?" Paige asked.

"Ready," Freddie said.

His legs were trembling from the exertion of the day. The spasticity was flaring up due to the heat. He couldn't lift his leg high enough to clear the porcelain rim on his own.

He didn't have to ask. Jessica took his left arm; Paige took his right. They braced themselves, their skin slick against his.

"Up," Jessica counted. "And over."

They managed the transfer with the efficiency of a pit crew. Freddie slid down into the water. He let out a long, groaning exhale as the cool liquid surrounded him, sapping the heat from his tired muscles.

"Better?" Paige asked, kneeling on the bathmat.

"System cooling," Freddie murmured, closing his eyes. "Operating temperature normalizing."

Jessica stepped into the tub behind him, sitting on the edge to let her feet soak, grabbing the sponge. Paige knelt beside the tub, taking the soap.

They began to wash him. It wasn't a frantic scrub; it was a rhythmic, calming maintenance routine. Paige soaped his arms, her hands firm and sure. Jessica squeezed the sponge over his shoulders, letting the water cascade down his back.

Freddie opened his eyes. He looked at his sisters. He saw them clearly—no clothes, no barriers, just skin and care.

And suddenly, he started to laugh.

It wasn't a nervous laugh. It was a genuine, bubbling chuckle that shook his shoulders and made the water ripple.

Paige paused, the soap hovering over his elbow. "What? What's funny?"

"Krystal," Freddie wheezed, a smile breaking across his face.

"What about her?" Jessica asked, wringing out the sponge.

"I am running a simulation," Freddie said, looking from Jessica to Paige. "I am imagining Krystal standing in this doorway right now."

He gestured to the scene: him, naked in the tub; his sisters, naked and washing him.

"She believed you were aides," Freddie explained. "Hired staff. Employees."

"Right," Jessica scoffed. "The 'Help'."

"If you were 'The Help'," Freddie continued, his logic engine finding the humor in the absurdity, "this scenario would be a violation of Occupational Safety and Health Administration protocols. Aides remain clothed. Aides wear gloves. Aides wash with clinical detachment."

He looked at Paige’s hand resting gently on his chest.

"Aides do not provide... this. This is not clinical. This is structural."

Paige giggled. She imagined the look on Krystal's face—the absolute, brain-melting confusion she would experience if she saw them right now.

"She would explode," Paige laughed. "Her brain wouldn't be able to process it. She’d probably call the police."

"Or she would think it was an orgy," Jessica added dryly, pouring water over Freddie’s hair. "Because her database only has two folders: 'Professional' and 'Sexual.' She doesn't have a folder for 'Family.'"

"She lacks the source code," Freddie agreed. "She cannot compile the file."

The three of them laughed together, the sound bouncing off the tiled walls. It was a victory laugh. They had taken the thing Krystal tried to make weird—their closeness, their intimacy—and they had reclaimed it.

"Well," Jessica said, wiping a bubble of soap from Freddie’s nose. "She is never going to find out. Because she is never setting foot in this house."

"Banned," Freddie stated firmly. "Blacklisted."

"Added to the firewall," Paige agreed. "List of Banned Entities: 1. Robert. 2. Krystal."

"Security level: Maximum," Jessica confirmed. "No unauthorized packets allowed."

She rinsed Freddie’s back one last time. "Okay, Navigator. You're clean. And you're cool. Let's get dried off and get in the bed. I think we earned a nap."

"Affirmative," Freddie said, reaching for the towel.

He felt clean. Not just from the soap, but from the laughter. The static was gone. The heat was gone.

He was in the Base, and the perimeter was secure.

## The Signal

The decontamination process was a staged operation. Freddie was clean, cool, and dressed in a fresh pair of soft grey lounge pants and a white undershirt.

They had established a temporary base camp in the living room while the bathroom was occupied. The **MagnaView** TV was the focal point.

"Rotation protocol," Jessica announced, grabbing her towel. "I’m taking the first shift in the shower to wash my hair. Paige, you hold the perimeter."

"Copy," Paige said. She was wearing a loose t-shirt and panties—her "at-home" uniform—sitting next to Freddie on the beige sofa. "We aren't going anywhere."

Jessica disappeared into the hallway. A moment later, the sound of the shower started again—a distant, white-noise hiss behind the closed door.

Freddie held the remote control. He aimed it at the TV. He wasn't looking for news (too much friction) or cartoons (too loud). He needed something stable.

He cycled through the channels. *Static. News. Soap Opera. Static.*

He landed on **Channel 11**, a local independent station known as **WNRV**. In the afternoons, WNRV didn't play trashy talk shows. They played "The Classics."

The screen flickered. A logo appeared: a radio tower transmitting lightning bolts over a city skyline.

*Title Card:* **"W-JAM in Philly"**

Freddie had never seen this show before. The date on the screen said **1978**.

Then, the music started.

It wasn't a jarring orchestral blast like the graduation song. It was a soft, rhythmic piano melody, joined quickly by a smooth, melodic bass line.

A singer with a warm, raspy voice began to croon. It sounded like a friend talking to you across a table in a diner.

*"Baby... if you're listenin' to the wind..."*

Freddie stopped breathing for a second. The sound wasn't just audio; it was a texture. It was like velvet. It was warm, analog, and perfectly balanced.

*"...and you're lookin' for a place to fit on in..."*

He watched the opening montage. He saw a man with a mustache running a soundboard. He saw a blonde woman smiling at a receptionist's desk. He saw a guy in sunglasses pointing coolly at a microphone.

*"...just turn that dial to the right, and let the music light the night..."*

Freddie lowered the remote. He didn't change the channel. He stared at the screen, mesmerized.

"Status?" Paige asked, noticing his stillness.

"The audio profile," Freddie whispered, not looking away. "It is... welcoming. It lacks aggression."

*"W-JAM... we're livin' on the air in Philly..."*

The song ended on a gentle fade-out that felt like a hug.

"What is this?" Freddie asked.

"I think it's **W-JAM**," Paige said, reading the screen. "It's an old sitcom about a radio station."

"A radio station," Freddie repeated. "An audio transmission hub."

He liked the concept immediately. A radio station was a system. It had transmitters. It had frequencies. It had a control board with buttons and sliders.

The show started. The scene opened in the broadcast booth. The DJ—the guy in sunglasses—was queuing up a vinyl record. He moved with a practiced, rhythmic efficiency that Freddie recognized.

*He is operating the machine,* Freddie thought. *He is the Admin of the airwaves.*

The door to the hallway opened. Jessica walked in, drying her hair with a towel. She was wearing a fresh t-shirt and shorts, looking scrubbed and pink from the heat of the shower.

"All clear," Jessica said. "Water pressure is nominal. Paige, you're up."

"Roger," Paige said, standing up. "Freddie found a new show. It's about radio."

Jessica walked over and sat in Paige’s spot, instantly re-establishing the contact pressure against Freddie’s side. She looked at the TV.

"Oh, **W-JAM**," Jessica smiled. "Dad used to hate this show. He said the DJs had too much hair."

"Dad was incorrect," Freddie stated, watching the DJ slide a fader up. "The hair density is irrelevant. The signal quality is the primary variable."

"Fair point," Jessica agreed, wrapping the towel around her wet hair. "Is it funny?"

"I do not know yet," Freddie admitted. "But the theme song... it felt like the King Bed."

Jessica looked at him. That was the highest praise in the Avery dictionary.

"Warm?" she asked.

"Warm," Freddie confirmed. "And safe."

Paige grabbed her towel. "Okay, I'm going in. Don't let him change the channel. We need to see if the blonde receptionist runs the place."

"Hypothesis: She is the true Matriarch," Freddie predicted.

"Likely," Jessica nodded.

As Paige went to shower, Freddie settled back into the cushion, pressing his shoulder against Jessica. On the screen, the chaotic, funny, and warm world of the fictional radio station unfolded.

For the first time all day, Freddie wasn't analyzing threats or dodging smoke. He was just watching a system that worked.

"I will add this to the scheduled recordings," Freddie decided. "Daily at 16:00."

"Adding to the queue," Jessica said, reaching for the popcorn bowl. "Welcome to the playlist, W-JAM."


The credits for *W-JAM in Philly* rolled, the funky bass line fading into a commercial for a local car dealership (Holt Automotive).

Freddie remained rooted to the spot. The warm feeling from the first show lingered, a sense of having witnessed a functional system.

"Channel hold," Freddie instructed. "Do not switch inputs."

"Holding," Jessica confirmed, crunching a piece of popcorn. She was still in her post-shower towel turban, relaxed and pink-cheeked.

The screen flickered. The station ID for **WNRV** played. Then, a new show began.

It didn't start with a high-energy montage. It started with a slow, sepia-toned slide show of old city streets—cobblestones, gas lamps, and people in bowler hats.

Then, a piano began to play.

It wasn't funky. It was... melancholic. But hopeful.

A gentle, male voice began to sing.

*"Making your way in the world today takes everything you've got..."*

Freddie felt a tightness in his chest loosen. The lyrics resonated instantly. He knew what it took to make his way in the world—it took structure, it took admins, it took energy he didn't always have.

*"Taking a break from all your worries, sure would help a lot..."*

"True," Freddie whispered.

*"Wouldn't you like to get away?"*

The chorus hit, and the energy lifted. It wasn't loud; it was an invitation.

*"Sometimes you want to go..."*

*"Where everybody knows your name..."*

Freddie’s eyes widened. That was the core desire. Not to be famous, but to be *known*. To walk into a room and not be a glitch. To be a recognized user with full permissions.

*"And they're always glad you came..."*

The title card appeared over a shot of a cozy, basement-level pub with stained glass windows:

**THE LOCAL**

"What is this?" Freddie asked, entranced.

"It's **The Local**," Jessica said, smiling fondly. "It's about a bar in Boston. The owner is an ex-baseball player for the **Beacons**."

The show started. A tall man with great hair stood behind the bar, polishing a glass. The door opened. A heavy-set man walked in.

"Norm!" the entire bar shouted in unison.

Freddie sat up straight. "They... acknowledged him."

"It's the ritual," Jessica explained. "Every time he enters, the system recognizes him. Immediate validation."

"Norm," Freddie whispered, testing the sound. "It is a login script."

The door to the hallway opened. Paige walked in. She was clean, dressed in soft shorts and a tank top, her hair damp and smelling of apple shampoo.

"Decontamination complete," Paige announced. She saw Freddie staring at the screen. "Another one?"

"It is called **The Local**," Freddie said, not looking away. "The primary variable is community. They know the names."

Paige walked over and sat on Freddie’s other side, completing the formation on the beige sofa. She leaned her head on his shoulder.

"Do you like it?" she asked.

"The theme song," Freddie said, his voice quiet. "It describes a sanctuary. A place where the social mask is not required."

*"You want to go where people know, people are all the same..."* the song finished on the TV. *"You want to go where everybody knows your name."*

"That's what this is," Paige whispered, squeezing his arm. "Here. 105 Pepper Street. We know your name, Freddie."

"And we're always glad you came," Jessica added, poking his ribs gently.

Freddie looked at the TV, where the characters were bickering affectionately. He looked at his sisters, clean and safe in their fortress.

He realized that **The Local** wasn't just a bar on TV. It was a blueprint for what they were building right here.

"I will add this to the recording schedule," Freddie decided firmly. "Daily at 16:30. It is... essential viewing."

"Double feature," Jessica agreed. "W-JAM and The Local. The Avery Afternoon Block."

As the show continued, Freddie didn't just stay seated. He shifted, sliding down until he was horizontal again, swinging his legs up onto the sofa. He laid his head back down in Paige's lap, exactly as he had done at Kincaid Hall.

But this time, there was no smoke. There was no Krystal. There was no sweat or gritty humidity.

There was only the scent of apple shampoo, the cool air of the silent house, and the rhythmic, grounding motion of Paige’s hand resuming its place in his hair.

The heat outside, the confusion of the day, the friction of the world—it all felt very far away. Here, in the cool light of the TV, listening to the laughter of the bar where everyone belonged, Freddie closed his eyes for a moment.

He was in the Safe Zone. He was with the people who knew his name. And that was all he needed.

## The Final Frontier

The afternoon sun was still blazing outside, but inside **105 Pepper Street**, the blackout curtains held the line. The living room was a cool, dim sanctuary illuminated only by the glow of the MagnaView TV.

"News block initiated," Jessica announced. "Prepare for local intake."

The screen shifted to the familiar blue-and-gold graphics of **WNRV-TV Channel 11**.


The broadcast began with **"Valley News Tonight"**. Freddie tolerated the 5:00 PM and 5:30 PM segments—mostly traffic reports on I-81 and weather updates confirming the oppressive humidity—but he perked up for the flagship **6:00 PM Broadcast**.

The anchor, a man with silver hair and a voice like gravel, read the headlines with comforting predictability. There were no surprises. Just facts.

"National news at 6:30," Paige noted, handing Freddie a bowl of grapes. "Do you want to watch the national feed?"

"Negative," Freddie decided. "Global variables are too volatile. Stick to the local signal."


At 7:00 PM, the tone of the evening shifted. The news desk was replaced by a brightly colored set filled with flashing lights and a giant, glittering wheel.


Freddie watched as three contestants spun the heavy wheel, the clicking sound—*tik-tik-tik-tik*—providing a satisfying auditory stim.

"R," a contestant shouted. "Is there an R?"

*Ding!*

"Variable confirmed," Freddie whispered, popping a grape into his mouth. He liked this show. It was just pattern matching. He was faster than the contestants. He saw the fragments of the letters and the logic engine in his head filled in the blanks instantly.

"Walking on Sunshine," Freddie blurted out before the lady on screen even spun the wheel.

"Show-off," Jessica grinned.

At 7:30 PM, the smart show started.


At 7:30 PM, the tone shifted from luck to logic. **The Daily Quiz** began.

This was serious business. The three of them sat forward, slices of pizza forgotten, competing for the title of "Living Room Champion."

"History for 400," the host announced. "The treaty that ended World War I." "What is the Treaty of Versailles?" Jessica shouted. "Correct," the host said.

"Science for 600." "The process by which plants use sunlight to synthesize foods." "What is... Photosynthesis?" Freddie guessed. "Correct."

Then came the visual clue. An image of a brass instrument with mirrors and an arc appeared on the blue screen. "Navigation for 800," the host read. "This doubly reflecting navigation instrument measures the angular distance between two visible objects."

"Sextant," Freddie corrected instantly. "Navigation tool."

"WRONG!" Jessica shouted, pointing a pizza crust at him like a gavel. "Error! Syntax error!"

Freddie blinked, looking at her. "The identification is accurate. It is a sextant."

"Phrasing, Freddie!" Paige laughed, nudging his ribs. "You have to format the data!"

"Oh," Freddie realized. The arbitrary rule of the game show. The social contract of the quiz. He sighed, adjusting his glasses. "What is a sextant?"

"That is correct," the host on the TV said.

"Point restored," Jessica declared, taking a bite of her crust. "But you're on thin ice, mister. Respect the format."

Freddie allowed a tiny smile. He liked the format. Even if the rule was silly, it was a rule. And in this house, rules were followed.


Then, the game shows ended. The screen went black for a moment.

"Okay," Jessica said, checking the TV Guide. "Channel 11 is running a double feature of that sci-fi show. **'Stellar Voyage: The New Era'**. You want to try it?"

"Genre?" Freddie asked.

"Space," Paige said. "Exploration. Dad said it was boring, but..."

"Dad is an unreliable narrator," Freddie corrected. "Proceed."

The screen lit up.

It began with a field of stars. Just blackness and pinpricks of light, moving slowly, majestically.

Then, the voice spoke. It was a deep, resonant baritone—the voice of a Captain.

***"Space... The infinite expanse."***

Freddie stopped chewing his grape.

***"These are the voyages of the Starship Avalon. Its continuing mission: to explore strange new worlds. To seek out new life and new civilizations."***

The music swelled—an orchestral fanfare of French horns and strings that felt like a sunrise. It wasn't cheesy; it was noble. It was soaring.

***"To boldly go where no one has gone before."***

A massive, sleek silver ship swept across the screen, its warp engines glowing a soft, pulsating blue. It didn't look like a machine of war. It looked like a machine of *science*.

Freddie sat up, pulling himself out of Paige's lap for the first time in hours. He leaned toward the screen, his eyes wide behind his glasses.

"The music," Freddie whispered. "It sounds like... hope."

The episode started. It wasn't about shooting lasers. It was about diplomacy. It was about a crew of hyper-competent officers solving a complex astrophysical puzzle involving a binary star system.

Freddie watched **Captain Miller** sit in his chair and ask for "Input." He watched **Commander Stone** execute a maneuver. He watched **Unit 7** analyze the situation without emotion, purely on logic.

"He is like me," Freddie pointed at the synthetic officer. "He processes data. He does not understand the joke."

"Yeah," Paige smiled softy. "He is like you."

But it was the **Main Engineering** set that captivated him. The Master Systems Display—a giant, backlit wall of schematics showing the energy flow of the entire ship. The interface was clean, organized, and perfectly logical.

"Look at the interface," Freddie breathed. "It is a perfect structure. Everything is labeled."

By the time the second episode started at 8:30 PM, Freddie was gone. He wasn't in the living room anymore. He was on the bridge of the *Avalon*.

"Astronomy," Freddie announced during the commercial break.

"What?" Jessica asked.

"The field of study," Freddie clarified. "It is the study of the ultimate system. Stars. Orbits. Gravity. It is all math. It is all predictable."

He looked at his sisters.

"I require books on astronomy. And I require a website update."

"A website update?" Paige asked.

"The portfolio," Freddie said, his mind racing faster than the warp drive on screen. "We are building a digital archive. It should be modeled after the *Avalon* computer. Dark background. Glowing text. Structured data."

"An astronomical theme," Jessica mused. "RaggieSoft... in space."

"Affirmative," Freddie said, turning back to the screen as the theme song swelled again. "Space. The final frontier."

He watched the ship streak into warp speed, a streak of light against the darkness. For a boy who spent his whole life feeling gravity hold him down, looking up at the stars felt like the first time he could truly fly.
