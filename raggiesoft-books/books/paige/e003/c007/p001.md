## The Debugger

The waiting room of **Dr. Arrington’s** office was located in a quiet brick building off North Main Street in Blacksburg. It felt less like a clinic and more like a professor's study, which helped lower the ambient threat level.

The floor was carpeted in a muted blue that absorbed the sound of footsteps. A small fountain in the corner provided a constant, rhythmic *trickle-splash-trickle*—white noise for the soul.

Freddie sat in the corner chair. He was wearing his "Standard Uniform": loose-fitting jeans and a soft grey t-shirt with no tags.

On his right sat Paige. She was his mirror. She wore an almost identical grey t-shirt, her leg pressed firmly against his to provide a constant stream of tactile data.

On his left sat Jessica. She wore a long-sleeved forest green top—the color of the mountains, the color of stability. She held the clipboard with the intake forms.

"Question 4," Jessica read softly. "Does the patient experience distress during changes in routine?"

Freddie looked at the fountain. He thought about the time his father tried to trade the family's Holt Club Wagon for a sedan without a family meeting.

"Distress is... inaccurate," Freddie corrected. "It is a fatal error. The system hangs."

"Checking 'Severe'," Jessica murmured, marking the box. "Question 5: Does the patient have difficulty interpreting non-verbal cues?"

Paige squeezed Freddie’s knee. "Remember when Heidi told you to 'break a leg' before the concert, and you asked her which bone?"

"It was a valid query," Freddie defended. "The tibia is the most structurally sound choice."

"Checking 'Yes'," Jessica said.

She flipped to the back page. "Okay, last part. **HIPAA Authorization**. Who is allowed to access your medical records and discuss your care?"

"The Admin Team," Freddie stated immediately.

"I need names, Freddie," Jessica smiled.

"Paige Avery," Freddie listed. "Jessica Avery. And Ellen Avery."

"Mom?" Paige asked softly.

"She is the primary source of genetic code," Freddie said. "She requires read-only access."

Jessica wrote the names down and signed the witness line. Just as she finished, the heavy oak door to the inner office opened.

A man stood there wearing a tweed jacket with patches on the elbows. He held a mug of tea and looked kind, but tired.

"Freddie Avery?" he called out. His voice was gravelly but soft.

Freddie stood up. Paige and Jessica rose with him, forming a wall.

"I'm Dr. Arrington," the man said. He looked at the three of them. "I usually only take one support person back with the patient. The room is a bit small."

Freddie froze. Separation anxiety spiked. He looked at Jessica.

"Go," Jessica said firmly, handing the clipboard to Paige. "I’ll hold down the fort out here. Paige is the Primary Anchor today."

"Protocol accepted," Freddie whispered.

Paige took Freddie’s hand. "Come on, Architect. Let's go see the wizard."

## Operating Systems

Dr. Arrington’s office was lined with bookshelves stuffed with medical texts and psychology journals. There was a leather couch and two armchairs. On his desk sat a beige Quantum workstation with a bulky CRT monitor that hummed with a high-pitched frequency only Freddie seemed to hear.

Freddie sat on the leather couch. Paige sat right next to him, her shoulder pressed into his.

"So," Dr. Arrington started, sitting in his armchair and crossing his legs. "The referral says you suspect Autism. Tell me why."

Freddie took a breath. He squeezed Paige’s hand.

"I process sensory input at a higher resolution than standard hardware," he began. "Light. Sound. Texture. It does not get filtered. It accumulates until the buffer overflows."

"And when it overflows?" Dr. Arrington asked, taking notes.

"System crash," Freddie said. "Meltdown."

Dr. Arrington nodded. He didn't write that down; he just watched Freddie’s face. He reached for a small wooden box on the table, took out a set of colored blocks, and placed them on the coffee table.

"Organize these," Dr. Arrington said. "Any way you like."

Freddie looked at the blocks. He didn't build a tower. He didn't build a house. He sorted them by color spectrum—Red, Orange, Yellow, Blue—and aligned the edges perfectly, ensuring the spacing between each block was equidistant. Exactly 5 millimeters.

"Done," Freddie said. "The geometry is consistent."

Dr. Arrington watched him. "Pattern recognition. Excellent. Now, tell me about the social aspect. Eye contact?"

"Inefficient," Freddie stated, looking at Dr. Arrington’s tie knot. "Eyes provide too much variable data. Micro-expressions. Pupil dilation. It introduces noise."

Dr. Arrington leaned back. "You speak in systems metaphors. Computers. Architecture."

"It is the only language that follows the rules," Freddie said. "Code executes exactly what is written. If I write a script in Quantum Basic, and it fails, it is a syntax error. I can find it. If a conversation fails, it is a mystery."

Dr. Arrington smiled. "It's not a mystery, Freddie. It's just a different operating system. You are running MagnaDesk or Open Systems in a Quantum OS world."

Freddie froze. That was exactly it.

"Am I... broken?" Freddie whispered.

"No," Dr. Arrington said firmly. "You are not broken. You are specialized hardware."

## The King Bed Defense

Dr. Arrington picked up his notepad again. "Let's talk about regulation. How do you reboot? How do you handle the 'static'?"

Freddie hesitated. The voice of his father—the "Robert Virus"—flared up in his mind. *Don't tell him. He'll think you're a freak. He'll take you away.*

He felt Paige’s hand tighten around his. She rubbed her thumb over his knuckles.

"I require... deep pressure," Freddie said, his voice trembling slightly. "Tactile grounding."

"Like weighted blankets?"

"Better," Freddie said. "I have a twin."

Dr. Arrington looked at Paige. "You provide the pressure?"

"I do," Paige said, her voice steady and protective. "We hug. Hard. And... we sleep together."

The silence in the room was heavy. The air conditioner hummed.

"In the same bed?" Dr. Arrington clarified.

"Yes," Paige said, lifting her chin defiantly. "A King Size. Us and our older sister, Jessica. We sandwich him. It stops the night terrors."

She waited for the judgment. She waited for the lecture about boundaries or age-appropriateness.

Dr. Arrington didn't frown. He didn't look disgusted. He nodded slowly, seemingly impressed.

"Co-regulation," he said.

Freddie blinked. "Variable definition?"

"Co-regulation," Dr. Arrington repeated. "Your nervous system is dysregulated. Hers is calm. By maintaining physical contact, she acts as a grounding wire. You've built a biological life-support system."

He wrote something down on his pad. "Given your history... it is brilliant."

Freddie let out a breath he felt like he’d been holding since childhood. *Brilliant.* Not sick. Not weird. *Brilliant.*

"So... we can keep the bed?" Freddie asked.

"Freddie," Dr. Arrington said seriously. "If it works, you keep it. You do whatever you need to do to survive the static."

## The Source Code

Dr. Arrington typed on his computer for twenty minutes. The keys clacked rhythmically. Finally, the dot-matrix printer whirred to life, spitting out a sheet of crisp white paper.

He signed it and handed it to Freddie.

**To Whom It May Concern:** **Patient:** Freddie P. Avery **Diagnosis:** Autism Spectrum Disorder **Recommendations:** Sensory accommodations. Deep Pressure Therapy as needed. Allow support persons (Paige Avery, Jessica Avery) full access to educational and medical environments.

Freddie held the paper. It was the Source Code. It was the answer key to the test he had been failing for eighteen years.

"This is the manual," Freddie whispered. "This explains the errors."

"It’s a map," Dr. Arrington corrected. "Now you can navigate."

They walked back out to the waiting room. Jessica looked up from a magazine, her eyes scanning their faces for distress.

"Status?" Jessica asked, standing up.

Freddie held up the paper. "Validated."

Jessica let out a long sigh of relief. "Okay. Let's get out of here."

They walked out of the office building into the bright July sunshine. The heat was oppressive, but Freddie felt light. He folded the paper carefully and placed it in his pocket, right next to his Poly Pass.

He turned to Paige. She was smiling at him, wearing the same grey shirt, the same glasses.

"We did it," she said.

Freddie stepped forward and wrapped his arms around her right there on the sidewalk. He squeezed her, feeling her arms lock around his back. Jessica placed a hand on his shoulder, completing the circuit.

"Thank you," Freddie whispered. "System stable."

"Always," Paige whispered back.

Freddie pulled back and adjusted his glasses. He checked his watch.

"The diagnostics are complete," he announced. "Now... we have a class to attend."

"Human Sexuality?" Jessica asked with a smirk.

"Affirmative," Freddie said seriously. "I have questions about the diagrams in the textbook. The labeling is inconsistent."

Paige laughed, hooking her arm through his. "Let's go, Architect."

## The Uplink

The walk from **105 Pepper Street** to the **Pepper Plaza Transit Center** took exactly three minutes. It was a diagonal line across the street, a vector Freddie had already memorized.

The summer heat was oppressive, shimmering off the asphalt, but Freddie didn't mind. He had the letter. He had the "Source Code" folded neatly in his back pocket, right next to his wallet.

"Bus inbound," Jessica announced, shielding her eyes against the sun.

Turning the corner from North Franklin Street was a white bus with maroon and orange stripes. It was a heavy-duty, 40-foot **Stalwart** city bus, the workhorse of the **Blue Ridge Transit** fleet.

The LED destination sign above the windshield glowed amber in the bright light: **1 CPI**

"On time," Freddie noted, checking his watch. "The grid is active."

They stood in the shelter of the sawtooth bay. As the bus hissed to a halt, the air brakes purged with a sharp *psshhh*—a sound Freddie associated with the start of a journey. The doors folded open.

"Badges out," Jessica said, pulling her **Poly Pass** from her purse.

She stepped up first. *Beep.* The card reader chirped a valid read. Paige followed. *Beep.* Freddie stepped up last. He pulled his card from his pocket—the warm plastic rectangle that identified him as **FREDDIE AVERY**. He tapped it against the target.

*Beep.*

The driver nodded. "Welcome aboard."

Freddie walked down the aisle. It was the summer session, so the bus wasn't at "crush load." There were plenty of seats. He chose a row near the back window. He slid in next to the glass. Paige sat next to him. Jessica took the seat in front, turning sideways to face them.

"Next stop," the automated voice announced as the bus rumbled out of the plaza. *"Business 460."*

The bus merged onto the highway, the diesel engine shifting gears smoothly. Freddie pressed his forehead against the cool glass. For the next thirty minutes, he didn't have to make decisions. He didn't have to interpret facial expressions. He just had to sit.

He felt the vibration of the road through the seat. He felt Paige’s shoulder against his. He felt the paper in his pocket.

"We are delivering the patch," Freddie whispered, watching the trees blur by.

"We are," Paige agreed. "Straight to the SSD office. Once they have that letter, the accommodations are locked in. Extended time. Quiet rooms. No more surprises."

"No more surprises," Freddie repeated.

The bus rolled past the strip malls of Christiansburg, climbing the hill toward Blacksburg. The landscape shifted from commercial sprawl to the green, rolling hills of the New River Valley.

Usually, this transition made Freddie anxious—leaving the known for the unknown. But today, he wasn't just a passenger. He was an Admin. He had his credentials.

"Automated Voice," the speaker crackled overhead. *"Approaching CPI. End of the line."*

The stone towers of **Commonwealth Polytechnic** rose in the distance, looking less like a fortress to be conquered and more like a server farm waiting to be programmed.

"Ready to upload?" Jessica asked, standing up as the bus slowed.

Freddie stood up. He adjusted his glasses. He checked his pocket one last time to make sure the letter was still there.

"Upload sequence initiated," Freddie said. "Let's go."

## The Thunderhead

The sky over the **New River Valley** had turned a bruised, angry purple. The summer heat had broken, but it had broken violently.

Inside **Archer Hall**, the world was shaking.

A massive thunderstorm was parking itself directly over the campus. Rain lashed against the plate-glass windows of the student lounge area with such force that it sounded like gravel. Outside, visibility was zero; the building across the quad had vanished behind a curtain of grey water.

*BOOM.*

Thunder cracked directly overhead, vibrating through the terrazzo floor.

Freddie flinched violently. He was lying on a vinyl bench in the corner of the lounge, his legs curled up, his head pressed hard into Paige’s lap. He had his hands clamped over his ears, eyes squeezed shut.

"Sensory overload," Freddie gasped. "Audio input exceeds safety limits."

"I know," Paige whispered, leaning over him to create a human shelter. She pressed her hands over his hands, adding another layer of pressure. "It’s just noise, Freddie. It’s just electricity discharging. It can’t get in here."

*FLASH.*

Lightning lit up the lounge like a strobe light, followed instantly by another earth-shaking *CRACK*.

Freddie whimpered, burying his face in Paige’s stomach. To him, the thunder wasn't just sound; it was a physical assault. It was unpredictable. It was chaotic. It was the opposite of structure.

Jessica walked quickly across the lounge, balancing a cardboard tray from the vending machines. She looked wet; she had sprinted from the snack bar down the hall.

"Fuel secured," Jessica announced, sliding onto the bench at Freddie’s feet. "I got pretzels and water. The main cafeteria is a no-go. We are trapped until this cell passes."

"Status of the storm?" Paige asked, looking up.

"Radar is red," Jessica reported grimly. "It's a heavy cell. Probably another twenty minutes of the heavy stuff."

Freddie groaned into Paige’s shirt. "Twenty minutes is 1,200 seconds. That is too many seconds."

"Focus on the count," Paige instructed gently. "Breathe in... one, two, three..."

They were in their formation. The Avery Unit. Shield up. Anchor down. Waiting out the chaos.

But the chaos had an avatar.

Krystal emerged from the hallway. She was bored. She was stuck in the building, her flip-phone had no signal in the storm, and she was agitated.

She spotted them in the corner. She saw Freddie curled up in a fetal position, hiding in his sister's lap.

To anyone else, it looked like a medical situation. To Krystal, it looked like an opportunity.

*He’s scared,* she thought. *He’s vulnerable.*

She walked over, her heels clicking on the hard floor. She stopped right in front of the bench, looming over them.

"Wow," Krystal said, her voice cutting through the hum of the rain. "He really doesn't like the thunder, huh?"

Jessica looked up. Her eyes were hard. "Go away, Krystal. Now is not the time."

Krystal ignored her. She stepped closer to Freddie’s head. She leaned down, putting her face uncomfortably close to his ear.

"Hey, Freddie," she purred. "It's pretty loud out here, isn't it? All that banging? It’s scary."

Freddie stiffened. He could smell her perfume—too sweet, too chemical. It mixed with the ozone smell of the storm to create a nauseating cocktail.

"I have an idea," Krystal whispered, glancing at the sisters with a smirk. "I know a place where you can't hear the thunder. There's a janitor's closet down the hall. In the interior corridor. It’s thick concrete. Totally soundproof."

She paused, letting the implication hang in the air like humidity.

"It's dark in there," she added. "And it's private. Just you and me. I could... distract you. Make you forget all about the storm. We could have some real fun while we wait."

Paige froze. Her hands stopped rubbing Freddie’s back.

Freddie peeled one eye open. He looked at Krystal.

"Janitor's closet," Freddie repeated, his voice shaking. "Storage for chemical solvents. Mops. Buckets. High risk of contamination."

"No, silly," Krystal giggled, reaching out to touch his arm. "It's cozy. Come on. Leave your sisters here. Let me take care of you. I promise, I can make you feel *so* much better than a hug."

That was the line.

Jessica didn't stand up. She *launched* herself.

She slammed the tray of pretzels onto the table and rounded the bench in a single, fluid motion. She stepped directly into Krystal’s personal space, forcing the girl to stumble back in her high-heeled sandals.

"THAT IS ENOUGH!" Jessica screamed.

The sound was louder than the thunder. The few other students in the lounge turned to look. Jessica didn't care.

"Are you deaf?" Jessica shouted, pointing a finger inches from Krystal’s nose. "Or are you just stupid? He doesn't want you! He is in distress! He is overwhelmed! And you are trying to *seduce* him into a broom closet?"

"I was just trying to help!" Krystal stammered, shrinking back. "He needs—"

"He needs safety!" Jessica roared. "He needs stability! He does not need a quickie in a room that smells like bleach with a girl who thinks 'no' is just a suggestion!"

Krystal flushed red. "You're just jealous because he—"

"Jealous?" Jessica laughed, a harsh, terrifying sound. "I am his sister! I am his protector! You are a predator, Krystal. You see a boy who struggles to walk, a boy who is sensitive to noise, and you think, 'Easy target.' You think you can just wear him down."

Jessica took another step forward. Krystal hit the wall.

"You are done," Jessica hissed, her voice dropping to a lethal, low register. "If you come near him again—if you offer him sex, cigarettes, or a 'good time'—I will file a harassment complaint with the Dean so fast your head will spin. I will get you expelled. Do you understand me?"

Krystal looked at Jessica. She looked at the fury in her eyes. Then she looked past her at the bench.

Freddie had sat up. He wasn't looking at Krystal. He was looking at Paige.

Paige had her arms wrapped tight around his shoulders. She was rocking him gently.

"She is a virus," Freddie stated clearly, his voice cutting through the silence of the room. "Quarantine her."

"You heard him," Jessica snarled. "Get. Out."

Krystal scrambled. She didn't have a comeback. The fantasy of being the savior, the seductress, crumbled under the weight of Jessica’s rage. She turned and practically ran down the hallway, disappearing toward the restrooms.

Jessica stood there for a moment, her chest heaving. She smoothed her shirt. She took a deep breath.

She turned back to the bench.

"Threat neutralized," Jessica announced, her voice returning to normal.

She sat back down. She picked up a pretzel and handed it to Freddie.

"Eat," she commanded. "Salt helps the shock."

Freddie took the pretzel. He took a bite. The crunch was grounding.

"That was... loud," Freddie said, looking at Jessica.

"Sometimes you have to be louder than the storm," Jessica shrugged.

*BOOM.*

Another clap of thunder shook the room. Freddie flinched, dropping the pretzel and diving back into Paige’s lap.

"It is still here," he whimpered.

"I know," Paige whispered, resuming her stroking of his hair. "But Krystal isn't. It's just us, Freddie. Just the Unit."

"Hug required," Freddie mumbled. "Duration: Infinite."

"Accepted," Paige promised, leaning her cheek on top of his head as the rain battered the glass. "I will hug you for the rest of your life if you want, Freddie. I'm not going anywhere."

"Good," Freddie sighed, closing his eyes. "The closet... was an illogical proposal."

"Very illogical," Paige agreed.

Outside, the storm raged on. But inside the circle of the Avery Unit, the perimeter was finally, completely secure.
