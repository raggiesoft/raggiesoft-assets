## The Detour

The silver **Avalon** sedan tore down the left lane of Interstate 81, the engine whining as the RPMs climbed.

Jessica Avery was driving. Her hand rested on the stick shift, gripping the knob so hard her knuckles were white.

*Clutch. Shift. Fourth to Fifth. Gas.*

She drove with a terrifying, mechanical precision. She wasn't driving to class. She wasn't driving to the grocery store. She was driving to war.

In the passenger seat, Paige was curled into a ball. She clutched Freddie’s orange pill bottle in one hand and the printout of the flight itinerary in the other. She stared out the window at the blurred trees, her eyes red-rimmed and unblinking.

"We're making good time," Jessica announced, her voice tight. "ETA to Roanoke Long Term Parking is 09:40. Flight departs at 11:00."

"He's been gone twelve hours," Paige whispered, the math playing on a loop in her head. "Twelve hours without meds. Twelve hours of vibration. Jess, what if he gets off the bus before Seattle? What if he panics and gets off in... I don't know, Missouri?"

"He won't," Jessica said, downshifting smoothly as a truck cut them off. "He follows the plan. The ticket says Seattle. He will go to Seattle. And we will be there."

They pulled into the **Roanoke Regional Airport**. Jessica bypassed the expensive garage and headed for the Long Term lot. She parked the Avalon under a light pole—Position G-14.

"Move," Jessica commanded.

They grabbed their single duffel bags. They ran to the shuttle stop.

Inside the terminal, **Gateway Airlines** was a chaotic sea of people. It was a budget carrier, famous for its "cattle call" boarding process. No assigned seats. First come, first serve.

"We need Boarding Group A," Jessica said, eyeing the monitors. "If we get stuck in middle seats, we can't plan."

They checked in at the kiosk. **Group A, Position 14 & 15.**

"Perfect," Jessica breathed.

They moved through security. Paige carried Freddie’s medication in a clear plastic bag, ready to fight any TSA agent who questioned it. No one did.

At **Gate 4**, the plane was waiting. A 737 painted in gaudy orange and blue.

"Flight 492 to Nashville," the agent announced. "Group A, line up."

Jessica and Paige stood at the front of the line.

"We're coming, Captain," Paige whispered, clutching the boarding pass. "We're flying over the void."


The landscape had changed. The endless flat cornfields of Kansas had given way to the rolling, scrub-brush foothills of the Rockies.

Freddie sat by the window. He was exhausted. The vibration of the bus had settled into his bones, a constant, buzzing frequency that made his teeth ache.

He looked for the mountains. He wanted to see the snow-capped peaks Vanessa had promised.

But as they passed **Fort Collins**, the bus didn't stay on the massive, comforting concrete ribbon of **Interstate 25**.

The blinker clicked. The bus took an exit.

"Query?" Freddie asked, his voice raspy. "Why are we exiting the primary artery?"

"Detour," Vanessa said, checking the map she had bought at the last gas station. "The main highway goes through the Eisenhower Tunnel. It's high altitude. Sometimes they route the buses north to avoid the grade and the traffic."

The bus turned onto **US-287**.

It wasn't an interstate. It was a two-lane blacktop cutting straight through the high desert.

The civilization of the highway—the gas stations, the billboards, the other cars—vanished.

Suddenly, they were alone.

To the left, jagged rock formations rose up like teeth. To the right, endless sagebrush. The sun was dipping below the horizon, casting long, menacing shadows across the road.

Freddie gripped the armrest.

"This is incorrect," Freddie whispered. "This is a secondary route. It is dark. There are no lights."

"It's a shortcut to Laramie," Vanessa soothed, though she looked a little nervous herself. "It's fine, Freddie. Look at the rocks. It's scenic."

"It is desolate," Freddie corrected.

The bus picked up speed, roaring down the empty road. Without the streetlights of the interstate, the cabin was plunged into darkness.

Freddie felt the isolation wrap around him. On I-81 or I-70, help was always an exit away. Here, on this lonely stretch of 287, if the bus broke down, they would be swallowed by the night.

He squeezed his eyes shut and started to rock.

*Paige would not take this road,* he thought. *Paige takes the well-lit path.*


The bus hissed into the **Salt Lake City** intermodal hub. It had been a grueling run through Wyoming, bouncing over expansion joints in the dark.

"Bus change," the driver announced. "Layover is two hours. Next bus departs at 04:00."

"Okay," Vanessa yawned, standing up. "Let's get off. We need food."

"Stand by," Freddie murmured.

He tried to stand up.

A bolt of white-hot lightning shot up his spine.

"Ah!" Freddie gasped, collapsing back into the seat.

"Freddie?" Vanessa asked, looking back. "Leg cramp?"

"Back," Freddie gritted out, tears springing to his eyes. "Lower quadrant. Burning."

He tried again. He used his arms to push himself up, keeping his weight off his tailbone. He shuffled into the aisle, his legs stiff and uncooperative.

They made it off the bus and into the terminal. It was bright, sterile, and cold.

"Restroom," Freddie said urgently.

Vanessa pointed to the men's room. "I'll wait here."

Freddie shuffled into the restroom. It was empty. He moved into the large accessible stall and locked the door.

He leaned against the sink, breathing hard. The burning sensation on his lower back wasn't fading. It was throbbing.

He turned around and lifted the back of his shirt in the mirror.

He twisted his neck to see.

There, right at the base of his spine, the skin wasn't just red. It was angry. A dark, purple bruise the size of a half-dollar had formed, and in the center, the skin was broken and weeping. **Pressure Sore. Stage 2.**

Freddie stared at it in horror.

At home, Jessica checked his skin every night. Paige made sure he shifted his weight every hour. They had special cushions. They had protocols.

Vanessa had told him to "walk it off" in Colby. She hadn't reminded him to shift. She didn't know the schematic.

He let his shirt fall. The fabric scraped against the open wound, sending a fresh wave of nausea through him.

He unlocked the stall. He walked out to the waiting area.

Vanessa was buying a bag of pretzels.

"You okay?" she asked cheerfully. "Ready for the last leg?"

Freddie looked at her. He wanted to scream. He wanted to tell her he was bleeding. He wanted to demand a doctor.

But he heard her voice from the lobby in his head: *He's a burden. He can't function.*

If he told her he had a sore, she would be right. She would see how broken he was.

"Affirmative," Freddie lied, his voice trembling. "Systems nominal."

"Great," Vanessa smiled, handing him a pretzel. "Next stop, Idaho. Then... Seattle."

Freddie took the pretzel. He sat down gingerly on the hard plastic bench, forcing himself not to wince as the sore pressed against the seat.

He was 2,000 miles from home. He was injured. And he was alone.

## The Burning Mile

The PAC 737 banked sharply, the engines whining as they began their descent into Nashville.

"Ladies and gentlemen, we're starting our initial approach into BNA," the captain’s voice crackled over the intercom. "Weather in Nashville is sunny and seventy-five."

In Seat 14A, Paige Avery didn't look out the window. She was gripping the armrests so hard her knuckles were white.

"We land in twenty minutes," Jessica said from Seat 14B. She had her **MagnaByte** laptop open on the tray table, running a spreadsheet of arrival times. "Layover is forty-five minutes. Tight, but manageable. We stay in the A-terminal."

"He's still moving," Paige whispered. She wasn't looking at the flight tracker. She was staring at the back of the seat in front of her, visualizing a silver bus moving across a map in her head. "I can feel it. He's bouncing."

"He's in Utah," Jessica calculated, checking her watch. "Or crossing into Idaho. We are travelling at 500 miles per hour. He is travelling at 65. We will pass him over the Rockies."

"Did you pack the cushion?" Paige asked suddenly, panic flaring in her eyes. "The gel cushion for his chair? If he's sitting on a bus seat for this long..."

"I couldn't bring the chair cushion, Paige," Jessica said gently. "It wouldn't fit in the overhead. But I grabbed the memory foam travel pillow. It's better than nothing."

Paige squeezed her eyes shut. She knew Freddie’s skin. She knew how fragile it was.

"He's hurting," Paige murmured. "I know he is. And he's not telling her."

"We'll fix it," Jessica promised, closing her laptop with a snap. "Tonight. In Seattle. We fix everything."


The landscape had flattened out into a relentless, monotonous expanse of brown scrub brush and gray asphalt. This was **The Idaho Line**.

Inside the bus, the air was stale and hot. The air conditioning was struggling against the high desert sun beating down on the metal roof.

Freddie sat in the window seat. He wasn't looking at the scenery anymore. He was focused entirely on the screaming nerve endings in his lower back.

The pressure sore—the "secret fire" —was no longer a dull ache. It was a sharp, biting agony that throbbed with every vibration of the engine.

*Shift right,* his brain commanded. He leaned to the right, pressing his shoulder against the hot window.

*Pain spike.* The movement pulled the skin.

*Shift left.* He leaned toward Vanessa.

Vanessa looked up from her magazine. She lowered her sunglasses.

"You're fidgeting, Captain," she said. Her voice wasn't mean, but it was tired. She was exhausted too. "Try to sit still. We'll be in Boise soon."

"It hurts," Freddie whispered. He didn't say *skin*. He didn't say *sore*. He just said the generic word.

"I know," Vanessa sighed. "It's a long ride. My back hurts too. Just try to sleep."

She didn't offer to help him move. She didn't check his positioning. She just told him to endure it.

Freddie looked at her.

In the beginning, back in Roanoke, she had been the Pilot. The partner in the adventure. Now, as he looked at her profile against the endless gray plains, the lyrics of his own future song began to form in the static of his mind.

*Are you my lover, or are you my guard?* *Is this a journey, or a prison yard?*

She held his phone in her bag. She held the tickets. She held the water bottle. She held the key to every single part of him .

"Boise," Freddie croaked, his throat dry.

"Just a quick stop," Vanessa said. "Driver swap. Then we push to Washington."

The bus rumbled on. Mile after mile. The vibration traveled up the seat, through his spine, and into his teeth.

He closed his eyes and tried to summon the image of the King Bed on Pepper Street. He tried to feel the weight of the blanket. But the image was fuzzy, distorted by the pain and the distance.

*I am drifting,* Freddie thought. *The anchor is gone.*

## The Pasco Lock

The door to the Family Restroom clicked shut. Vanessa threw the deadbolt with a sharp, metallic clack.

The room was small, smelling of industrial bleach and stale water. The fluorescent light overhead buzzed with a dying, flickering hum that grated against Freddie's nerves like sandpaper.

Freddie leaned against the sink, his legs trembling uncontrollably. The pressure sore on his lower back was a "fire burning on his skin," throbbing in time with his racing heartbeat. He just wanted to sit down. He just wanted water.

But Vanessa didn't move to the sink. She moved to him.

She stepped into his personal space, pressing him back against the cold tile wall. Her eyes were bright, almost feverish. She wasn't looking at him with kindness; she was looking at him with expectation.

"We're alone," Vanessa whispered, her voice tight. "Finally. No sisters. No bus driver. Just us."

She placed her hands on his shoulders, her grip tight, digging in.

"Stop shaking, Freddie," she commanded. "Stop being the victim. I want to see the *real* you. I want to see the Wolf."

Freddie blinked, the buzz of the light seeming to get louder. "Wolf?"

"The part of you that takes what he wants," Vanessa hissed, pressing her body against his. "The primal part. The part that isn't afraid to break the rules."

She reached for the waistband of his jeans.

"Let's make this real. Sunday morning is coming, Freddie. Do you know what the scripture says about Sunday morning?"

Freddie shook his head, his breath hitching. "No data."

"It says a man shall leave his father and mother," Vanessa recited, her voice taking on a strange, fanatical edge, "and he shall **cleave** to his wife."

Freddie’s eyes widened behind his glasses. The word hit him like a physical threat.

"Cleave?" Freddie stammered. "Like... to cut? Like a butcher?"

"Like glue!" Vanessa laughed, but it wasn't a happy sound. It was impatient. "No, Freddie. You leave them behind. You cut them off. You sever the limb to save the body. That’s what we’re doing. You have to cleave to me."

"I do not want to be cut," Freddie whispered, terrified. "I do not want to amputate."

"Stop talking!" Vanessa snapped. She was losing patience with his literalism. She grabbed his hands and forced them onto her hips. "Stop thinking! Feel! Be the Wolf, Freddie! Take this!"

She kissed him. It wasn't soft. It was aggressive. She ground her hips against him, trying to force a reaction, trying to drag a response out of his exhausted, pain-racked body.

Freddie flinched violently. The sensory input was overwhelming—her perfume, the hum of the light, the pain in his back, the terrifying image of a meat cleaver severing him from Paige.

"Negative," Freddie rasped, pulling his head back. "Stop. Variable rejected."

"Don't you dare reject me," Vanessa growled, pulling him back in. "I kidnapped you for this! I dragged you across the country to set the animal free! Now show me!"

"Refusal!" Freddie shouted, panic rising.

He didn't just pull away. He shoved her. It was a frantic, clumsy shove of survival, fueled by the terror of being "cleaved."

Vanessa stumbled back, her hip hitting the hard plastic of the baby changing table.

The rejection hit her like a physical blow. To Vanessa, who used sex as control, this wasn't just a "no." It was an insult. It was a declaration that she—even here, alone, offering herself—wasn't enough to wake the "man" inside him.

The mask shattered.

"You ungrateful little freak!" Vanessa screamed.

The sound was deafening in the tiled room. It bounced off the walls, a sonic weapon.

"I am trying to save you!" she yelled, her face twisting into ugliness. "I am trying to give you a life! And you just stand there shaking like a child! You ruin everything, Freddie!"

"Please," Freddie whimpered, sliding down the wall to the floor, covering his ears. "Too loud. Too loud."

"Oh, now it's too loud?" Vanessa shrieked, looming over him. "You didn't mind the noise when Paige was babying you! But when I want something? When I want one moment?"

She didn't just scream. She lashed out.

She swung her hand and slapped him. Hard.

**CRACK.**

Her palm connected with the side of Freddie's face, knocking his glasses askew and snapping his head to the side. The sound was sharp and shocking in the tiled room.

Freddie gasped, a sharp intake of air. The pain was immediate, but the shock was worse. Tears sprang to his eyes instantly. He let out a sob—a raw, broken sound of absolute betrayal.

He dropped to the floor, curling into a tight ball against the dirty tiles. He covered his head with his arms, burying his face in his knees.

"Please," Freddie wept, his shoulders shaking. "Please don't. No cleaving. No more."

"Pathetic," Vanessa spat, looking down at him with pure disgust.

She checked her watch. The bus for Seattle departed in four minutes.

She looked at the door. She could leave him. She could walk away right now.

But if she arrived in Seattle alone, she was just a girl who ran away to live in a trailer. She was a failure. If she arrived with *him*, she was still the leader. She was still the one in charge. She needed him as proof that she had done something.

"Get up," she commanded coldly.

Freddie didn't move. He was in full system lockout, rocking slightly, humming a low note of distress.

"I said, get up!" Vanessa yelled.

She didn't wait for him to comply. She bent down and grabbed a handful of his orange hoodie near the shoulder. She grabbed his upper arm with her other hand, her nails digging in.

She yanked.

It wasn't a helping hand. It was a dead-lift.

"No!" Freddie shrieked as he was hauled upward.

His spastic legs, stiff from days of travel, didn't want to unbend. His feet scrambled uselessly on the slick tile. The sudden movement stretched the skin on his lower back, tearing the pressure sore wide open.

Vanessa didn't care. She dragged him up, hauling his dead weight until he was forced to stand or fall. She shoved him toward the sink.

"Wash your face," she hissed, breathing hard from the exertion. "Put your glasses on. We are getting on that bus, and you are going to smile."

## The Silence

The bus was quiet, the engine humming a monotonous drone as it climbed the arid hills toward Yakima. The afternoon sun was hidden behind a thick layer of gray cloud cover, making the cabin feel dim and claustrophobic.

Freddie sat in the window seat, pressed as far against the glass as he could get, trying to put millimeters of distance between himself and the aisle seat.

He wasn't rocking. He wasn't humming. He was frozen.

The pain in his lower back was a constant, white-hot knife. The stage 2 sore had ruptured during the scuffle in the Pasco bathroom, and now the rough denim of his jeans chafed the raw wound with every vibration of the road.

But the physical pain was secondary to the cold, hard knot of terror in his stomach.

*Kidnapped.*

Vanessa had said it. *I kidnapped you.*

She hadn't stuttered. She hadn't joked. She had screamed it in the tiled echo of the restroom.

He looked at her out of the corner of his eye. She was sitting next to him, arms crossed, staring straight ahead at the seatback in front of her. She had her headphones on, listening to a CD player, blocking him out.

She wasn't his girlfriend. She wasn't his friend. She was his captor.

He thought about the bathroom. The way she had tried to force him. The way she had touched him when he said no. The way her hand had cracked across his face.

*Violation,* the logic center of his brain cataloged. *Assault.*

He gripped the armrest. His hands were shaking—not from spasticity, but from a cold, lucid rage he had never felt before.

He had let her do this. He had walked onto the bus. He had turned off his phone and left it in Christiansburg.

*I broke the protocol,* Freddie thought bitterly. *I left the Unit.*

He made a vow then, staring out at the sagebrush and the dark, looming mountains of the Cascades ahead.

*The moment this bus stops. The moment the doors open.*

*I am not going with her. I will find a phone. I will call the Operator. I will say: "Collect call from Freddie." And Paige and Jessica will accept the charges.*

He didn't know they were already in the city. He didn't know they were watching the bus terminal from a window across the street.

He just knew he had to survive the next two hours without breaking.
