## The Bypass

Jessica dropped the room key on the front desk counter. The *clack* of plastic on marble was the final punctuation mark of their time in the city.

"Checking out," Jessica told Dave the clerk. "We’re heading for the trees."

"Safe travels," Dave said.

They took the elevator down to the garage. The white **Vanguard Senator** rental car waited in the concrete gloom.

"Keys," Paige demanded, holding out her hand. "I need to drive. I need to do something with my hands besides worry."

Jessica tossed her the keys. "All yours, Lieutenant. Get us out of the grid."

Paige climbed into the driver's seat. She adjusted the mirrors and reached for the gear stick. She gripped it, her foot instinctively stomping on the floorboard for a clutch pedal that wasn't there.

Her foot hit empty carpet.

"Ugh," Paige groaned, wiggling the shifter in its gate. "It’s a slushbox. An automatic. It feels like driving a golf cart."

"It’s a rental, Paige," Jessica laughed, opening the back door. "They don't trust tourists with clutches."

"It’s boring," Paige muttered, putting the car in Drive with a look of disdain. "No torque control. Just... mush."

Freddie stood by the open back door. He looked at the front passenger seat where he usually sat as the Navigator. Then he looked at the back seat where Jessica was sliding in.

He thought about the last few days. Paige had held him in the hotel bed. Paige had grounded him in the lobby.

He looked at Jessica. She was the Shield. She had organized the rescue, booked the flights, and scrubbed the bus dirt off his back. He missed her gravity.

"Change of formation," Freddie announced.

He climbed into the back seat next to Jessica.

"Backseat driver?" Jessica teased, scooting over to give him room.

"I require... equal time," Freddie whispered. "The Shield needs to be deployed."

He buckled his seatbelt, pulling it snug across his hips. Then, he slid across the bench seat until he was pressed against Jessica’s side. He wrapped his arm through hers, leaning his head on her shoulder.

Jessica smiled, a warm, bright expression that lit up the dim garage. She wrapped her arm around him, pulling him in tight.

"I've got you," Jessica said, resting her cheek on top of his head. "I'm right here."

"Secure," Freddie breathed, closing his eyes.

Paige navigated out of the garage, grumbling about the car's turning radius. They turned onto Stewart Street one last time, driving past the **Pathfinder Terminal**.

Freddie didn't flinch. He felt the solid warmth of Jessica’s arm. He felt the steady rhythm of her breathing. The terminal was just a building.

"Ghost town," Freddie whispered into Jessica’s sweater.

"Ghost town," Jessica agreed, squeezing his hand.


They reached the massive junction south of the city. The traffic on **I-5 South** was heavy—a river of red taillights and grey steel.

"Navigation update," Jessica called out from the back, playing the role of Navigator now. "We aren't taking I-5 all the way. Take Exit 154B. Merge onto **I-405 North**."

"North?" Paige asked, checking her blind spot. "We're going south."

"Just for a minute," Jessica explained. "To catch the drop to the valley."

Paige executed the merge, the automatic transmission whining as it downshifted aggressively. "Come on, shift," she muttered. "Find a gear."

A mile later, she took **Exit 2**, sweeping onto the ramp for **SR-167 South**.


Freddie stiffened slightly. "Kent," he read aloud.

"That’s where she said she was going," Jessica whispered, rubbing his arm. "The aunt's trailer park."

"If there even *is* an aunt," Paige muttered from the driver's seat, her eyes narrowing as she glared at the exit sign. "Knowing Vanessa, she probably made the whole thing up. She’s probably sitting on a curb somewhere wondering why her life isn't a movie."

"It is a valid hypothesis," Freddie murmured, looking out at the sprawling, bleak expanse of the suburb. It wasn't the romantic, sleepless city Vanessa had promised. It wasn't the mountains. It was just... parking lots. Fast food. Concrete.

He thought about Vanessa standing at the payphone in the terminal, desperate and alone. He thought about her lies—about New York, about Italy, about the "freedom" she claimed to offer.

And he thought about where he was right now—warm, safe, held by his sister in a car driven by his twin.

A sound bubbled up in his throat. A chuckle.

"She missed the exit," Freddie said.

"What?" Jessica asked, looking down at him.

"She wanted a life," Freddie said, a real smile breaking across his face for the first time since the bus station. "She wanted to be free. She wanted to be the hero. And she ended up... in Kent."

He laughed. It was a dry, dark, satisfied laugh.

"Or she ended up nowhere," Freddie added. "Maybe the aunt is a fiction. Maybe she is just... deleted."

"Fate," Jessica grinned, pressing a kiss to the top of his head. "The variable resolved itself. She is static. We are the signal."

"We are just passing through," Paige agreed, pressing the accelerator.

The Vanguard roared past the exit. They left Kent—and the ghost of Vanessa—in the dust, just a blur of grey in the side mirror.

## The Iron Creek Lodge

The drive south on SR-167 had turned into SR-161, winding through the Puyallup Valley.

Then, they hit the turn for **State Route 7**.

The four-lane highway narrowed. The concrete median disappeared. The road became a simple, two-lane blacktop winding through the trees.

The warehouses vanished. The strip malls disappeared. They were replaced by Douglas firs that towered two hundred feet into the air.

Freddie sat up straight in the back seat. He looked out the window.

"Two lanes," Freddie whispered. "Yellow line in the middle. Trees on the shoulder."

"Just like home," Jessica said, squeezing his hand.

"It looks like the New River Valley," Freddie said, his voice full of wonder. "It looks like Route 8 to Riner. Or the road to Wind Rock."

He relaxed completely. The knot of "city tension" that had been in his chest since Seattle finally unraveled. This was a scale he understood. One lane forward. One lane back. No merging. No chaos.

"Approaching target," Paige announced from the front. "Ashford. Population: Small."

They turned off the main road onto a gravel drive marked by a heavy wooden sign: **THE IRON CREEK LODGE**.

It wasn't a hotel. It was a collection of heavy timber cabins nestled deep in the woods, spaced far enough apart that you couldn't see your neighbors.

"Cabin 4," Jessica read the key packet she had picked up. "The Bear's Den."

Paige pulled the car up to a sturdy log cabin with a wide front porch and a stone chimney. She put the car in Park.

"Engine off," Paige said.

The silence was instant. No sirens. No bus engines. No traffic drone. Just the sound of the wind in the trees and the rushing water of the Nisqually River nearby.

"Audio check," Freddie whispered, stepping out of the car.

"Clear," Jessica said, standing beside him. "Zero static."

They walked into the cabin. It smelled of cedar and woodsmoke. The main room was cozy, with a leather sofa and a fireplace. But the main attraction was through the bedroom door.

There it was. **The California King.**

It was massive. Bigger than the one at the Cascade Suites. A vast expanse of flannel sheets and heavy quilts.

"72 by 84 inches," Freddie recited the specs, a smile touching his lips. "Maximum capacity."

"We fit," Paige said, dropping her bag.

Freddie walked to the window. He looked out at the forest. He couldn't see the road. He couldn't see a single bus stop.

"We stay here," Freddie decided. "Until the battery is 100%."

"As long as it takes," Jessica agreed, locking the heavy deadbolt on the front door.

They were off the grid. The rescue was over. The vacation had begun.

## The Bear's Den

The silence was the first thing Freddie noticed.

Usually, when he woke up, there was a background track: the hum of a refrigerator, the distant whine of traffic, or the frantic pounding of his own heart.

But here, waking up in the center of the Alaska King bed, there was only the sound of breathing.

He lay still. To his left, Jessica was breathing in a slow, deep rhythm. To his right, Paige was snoring softly—a tiny, vibrating snuffle that she always denied doing.

The weight of the heavy down duvet was perfect. It was a "Class 4" weighted blanket, pressing him into the mattress.

He wiggled his toes. He was wearing his thick grey wool socks. The flannel sheets felt soft against the denim of his jeans.

"Status: Restored," Freddie whispered to the ceiling.

Beside him, Paige shifted. She groaned, stretching her arms over her head, her knuckles cracking. She blinked her eyes open, staring at the massive log beams crossing the ceiling.

"Ugh," Paige muttered, rubbing her face. "I had a dream I was driving a go-kart in a swamp."

"Interpretation?" Jessica mumbled, not opening her eyes yet.

"That stupid rental car," Paige complained, sitting up and shaking her hair out. "The transmission feels like pudding. You step on the gas and it just... thinks about it for three seconds before doing anything. I need a clutch. I need to tell the car what to do, not ask it for permission."

"It got us here," Freddie pointed out, pushing himself up on his elbows. "The slushbox was adequate."

"Don't encourage the machines to be lazy, Freddie," Paige teased, nudging him with her shoulder. She looked at him, really looked at him.

His eyes were clear behind his glasses. His hands weren't shaking. The "vibration" that usually hummed under his skin—the anxiety of existing in a loud world—was gone.

"You look good," Paige said softly. "You look... settled."

"I am regulated," Freddie agreed. He looked around the massive bed. "The perimeter held."

"Okay," Jessica said, throwing the duvet back and swinging her legs over the side of the high mattress. Her feet, clad in white cotton socks, hit the wooden floor with a soft *thud*. "Nap protocol complete. Let's inspect the Base."

They slid off the bed. The floorboards were wide-plank pine, smooth and polished. They were cool through their socks, but not cold.

They walked out of the bedroom into the main living area.

"Architecture check," Freddie announced, looking up.

The ceiling vaulted twenty feet high, supported by peeled cedar logs. Two skylights let in the afternoon sun, filtering it through the canopy of the fir trees outside so the light on the rug was dappled and green.

"Leather," Freddie noted, walking to the sofa. He ran his hand over the back. "Cool to the touch. No microfiber static."

"And look at this," Paige said, kneeling in front of the hearth.

It wasn't just a fireplace; it was a black cast-iron woodstove with a glass door. A stack of dry, split birch logs was piled neatly in a copper bin next to it.

"Thermal unit," Freddie said, joining her. "Controlled combustion. We can seal the door and watch the fire without the smoke smell getting in the fabric."

"I'll build one tonight," Paige promised. "Get it roaring."

They moved to the kitchenette. It was small but efficient. Slate countertops (smooth, cold). A small fridge (quiet). A basket on the counter contained packets of local coffee and hot cocoa mix.

"Cocoa detected," Jessica noted, holding up a packet.

"Inventory approved," Freddie smiled.

He walked to the sliding glass door that led to the back porch. He didn't open it yet; he just pressed his hand against the glass.

Outside, the forest was a wall. Ferns grew waist-high right up to the porch railing. Moss hung from the maple trees like green beards. It was dense. It was wild.

"Visual isolation," Freddie observed. "I cannot see the road. I cannot see the office. I cannot see the other cabins."

"Just the trees," Jessica said, coming up to stand on his left.

"And the river," Paige added, standing on his right.

They could hear it clearly now—the constant, rushing white noise of the Nisqually River churning over glacial rocks. It sounded like a massive ventilation fan, scrubbing the air clean.

Freddie looked down at his socks on the wood floor. He wiggled his toes again.

He felt the floor beneath him. Solid. Real.

"This is better than the hotel," Freddie decided. "The hotel was a transition point. This... this is a bunker."

"A Bear's Den," Paige corrected, wrapping an arm around his waist. "And we're hibernating."

"Hungry?" Jessica asked, opening the mini-fridge to check the supplies they had bought at a gas station. "I think we have cheese, crackers, and very expensive beef jerky."

"Rations," Freddie nodded. "Acceptable."

He turned away from the window. He didn't need to check the perimeter anymore. The woods were doing it for him. The static of the city—the buses, the neon, the disappointment of Vanessa—felt a thousand miles away.

Here, there were only logs, fire, river, and the Unit.

"Let's eat," Freddie said. "Then... fire protocol."

## The Supply Run

"Logistics run," Jessica announced, grabbing the car keys. "The fridge is empty, and the Vanguard is thirsty. We need fuel and food."

They piled back into the car. The drive from the lodge to the "town" of Ashford took less than three minutes.

Ashford wasn't a city. It was a strip of civilization carved out of the forest: a few inns, a pottery shop, and the main hub of local life—**The Ashford Outpost**.

It was a gas station, but it looked like a log cabin that had grown gas pumps.

"Pulling in," Paige said, steering the white sedan up to the pump.

"I'll handle the fuel," Jessica said. "You two handle the rations. We need dinner. Something hot. Something simple."

"Copy that," Freddie said.

He stepped out of the car. The air smelled of gasoline, yes, but also of damp earth and the massive cedar trees that loomed right behind the store.

He walked into the store with Paige.

It was perfect.

It wasn't a sprawling supermarket with fluorescent glare and forty aisles of choices. It was a village store. Tight, cluttered, and cozy. The aisles were narrow wooden shelves packed with camping gear, fishing bait, and cans of soup.

"Scale: Manageable," Freddie whispered, adjusting his glasses.

He walked down the aisle. He didn't have to dodge carts. He didn't have to filter out a thousand faces. There was just a guy in a fishing hat buying worms and a woman buying milk.

"What's the menu, Captain?" Paige asked, grabbing a red plastic hand basket.

Freddie scanned the shelves. He didn't want fancy. He wanted *grounding*.

"Macaroni and Cheese," Freddie decided, pointing to the shelf. "The deluxe kind. The one with the liquid cheese pouch. Not the powder."

"Shells and Cheddar," Paige identified, tossing two blue boxes into the basket. "Solid choice."

"And meat tubes," Freddie added.

"Hot dogs?"

"Affirmative. Beef franks. We can roast them on the woodstove if the power fails. Or just boil them."

They moved through the small store, gathering the components of a comfort meal.

- A bag of **Ridged Potato Chips** (essential for the crunch texture).

- A gallon of 2% milk.

- A pack of **Milk Chocolate Bars**, **Honey Grahams**, and **Marshmallows**.

"S'mores protocol?" Paige grinned, holding up the bag of marshmallows.

"Essential thermal experiment," Freddie agreed.

They brought their haul to the counter. The cashier was a teenage girl reading a paperback book. She didn't look at Freddie like he was weird. She just scanned the items.

"Heading up the mountain?" she asked.

"Just to the lodge," Paige said.

"Nice night for a fire," the girl said. "Total was \$24.50."

Freddie watched the transaction. Simple. Exchange of goods. Friendly data. No threats.

They walked back out to the pumps. Jessica was just clicking the nozzle off.

"Tank full," Jessica reported. "Rations?"

"Secured," Freddie said, holding up the grocery bag. "We have the components for a feast."


Back at the cabin, the sun was beginning to dip behind the tree line, casting long, indigo shadows through the forest.

Inside, the cabin was warm. Paige had made good on her promise; a small fire was crackling in the cast-iron woodstove, orange light dancing behind the glass door.

Jessica took charge of the kitchenette. The smell of boiling pasta and rich, melting cheese soon filled the small space, masking the scent of the rental car's upholstery that still clung to their clothes.

They didn't eat at the small dining table. They ate on the floor, sitting on the thick rug in front of the fire.

Freddie held his bowl of shells and cheese. It was hot, salty, and creamy—pure comfort data. He took a bite of a hot dog.

"Status?" Jessica asked, sitting cross-legged next to him.

Freddie looked at the fire. He listened to the river outside. He looked at his sisters, eating macaroni in their socks on a cabin floor in the shadow of a volcano.

He thought about the "fancy" dinner Vanessa had promised him at the Century Spire—a rotating view, expensive wine, strangers watching him eat.

This was better. This was real.

"Optimal," Freddie whispered, scraping his spoon against the bowl. "Battery is charging."

"Good," Paige smiled, reaching over to steal a ridged chip from his plate. "Because after this, we are testing the S'mores Protocol. And I don't roast marshmallows lightly. I burn them."

"Carbonized sugar is an acceptable variant," Freddie allowed.

He took another bite. He was full. He was warm. He was home—even if home was temporarily a log cabin in Pierce County.
