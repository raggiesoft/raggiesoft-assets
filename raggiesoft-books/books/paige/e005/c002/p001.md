## The Descent

The morning light filtered through the blackout curtains of the Master Bedroom, signaling that the "Silo" had successfully protected its occupants for another rotation.

Freddie woke up in the center of the mass. The heat was immense.

To his left, Paige was still asleep, her arm thrown over his chest. To his right, Jessica was awake, staring at the ceiling. At the foot of the bed, Jenna was sprawled diagonally across everyone’s ankles, and Sarah was curled into a tight, efficient ball on the far edge, holding Freddie’s wrist.

"Status?" Freddie whispered, his voice raspy.

"Crowded," Jessica whispered back, smiling. "But secure."

Jenna stirred, kicking out a leg. "I need coffee. And I need to calibrate the servers."

"Coffee first," Sarah mumbled into the pillow.

They disentangled themselves from the "Heap." One by one, they climbed out of the King bed.

No one reached for clothes. The "No Armor" protocol was in full effect. The air conditioning was set to a brisk 68 degrees, making the cool air feel refreshing against their warm skin.

They moved into the living room. Jenna grabbed the digital camera from the coffee table.

"I can film it again," Jenna offered, seeing Freddie look toward the kitchen door—the gateway to the basement. "I can get better lighting this time. I’ll do a commentary track."

Freddie sat in The Rover, spinning the wheels slightly. He looked at the closed door.

He appreciated the data. He appreciated the video. But staring at a screen was not the same as verifying the architecture. He needed to know the distance. He needed to gauge the acoustic properties.

He needed to know exactly where his sisters were going to sleep so he could map them in his mind when he was upstairs.

"Negative," Freddie stated. "I require physical verification. The video lacks depth perception."

Sarah paused in the middle of stretching. "The stairs are non-compliant, Freddie. Steep gradient. No landing."

"I have legs," Freddie said, though they felt stiff. "And I have a team."

He looked at Jessica and Paige.

"Spotters?" Freddie asked.

"Always," Paige said immediately, moving to his side.

"If you want to go down there, we get you down there," Jessica confirmed. "Formation Alpha."

They moved to the kitchen. Jessica opened the narrow pantry door. The smell of dry concrete and old lumber wafted up—a sharp contrast to the lemon polish of the main floor.

The stairs were indeed steep. They were unfinished wood, narrow and open-backed.

"Okay," Jessica strategized, looking down into the gloom. "I take point. I’ll be two steps below you. I’m the catch net. If you slip, you fall into me."

"I take the rear," Paige said, stepping behind Freddie. "I control the descent speed. Hand on the waist."

"Jenna, Sarah—go down first," Jessica ordered. "Clear the landing zone."

The twins scampered down the stairs, their bare feet thumping on the wood. They waited at the bottom, looking up like ground crew waiting for a shuttle landing.

"Ready?" Jessica asked, descending two steps and turning around to face him. Her eyes were level with his knees.

Freddie stood up from The Rover. He gripped the handrail with his right hand. It was rough wood—he made a mental note to sand it later.

"Ready," Freddie said.

He took the first step.

His right leg—the tighter one—trembled as it took the weight. He wobbled.

Instantly, Paige’s hands were on his hips, stabilizing him. Jessica reached up and placed her hands on his thighs, guiding his foot placement.

"Easy," Jessica murmured. "One step. Good. Next."

It was a slow, intimate procession. Without clothes, there was no fabric to slip, no loose shirts to get caught.

Paige’s skin pressed against his back, her chest warm against his spine. Jessica’s hands on his legs were firm and grounding.

They moved as a single organism, flowing down the narrow chute.

Step. Stabilize. Step. Stabilize.

"Halfway," Freddie breathed, focusing on the top of Jessica’s head.

"You got it," Paige whispered in his ear. "Gravity is under control."

They reached the concrete floor. Jessica stepped back, allowing Freddie to take the final step.

He landed on the slab. It was cold under his feet—a sharp, industrial shock compared to the hardwood upstairs.

"Touchdown," Freddie announced.

He looked around.

The basement was vast. It ran the full length of the house. The concrete floor was bare, save for the temporary setups the twins had thrown together last night.

To his left, Sarah’s domain was currently just a sleeping bag neatly rolled out on the floor near the washer and dryer. Her carry-on case was open, her clothes already stacked in precise piles on top of the dryer.

To his right, Jenna’s kingdom was a chaotic nest of blankets and pillows thrown into the corner, surrounded by boxes of computer parts that hadn't been unpacked yet.

"It is... potential," Freddie noted. "Currently unfurnished."

"We haven't ordered the beds yet," Jessica explained, walking over to inspect the water heater. "We were going to do a run to the mattress store tomorrow. Two twins? Or maybe bunk beds to save floor space?"

Jenna looked at her pile of blankets. Then she looked at Freddie, who was rubbing his arms in the cool basement air.

"Do we want beds down here?" Jenna asked suddenly.

Sarah looked up from her neat stack of shirts. "Clarification?"

"Last night," Jenna said, gesturing upstairs. "The Heap. The Silo. It was... crowded. But it was good crowded."

She looked at Freddie.

"You slept better, didn't you?" Jenna asked. "With all of us?"

"I did," Freddie admitted softly. "The pressure was... complete. The perimeter was sealed on all sides."

"If we move down here," Jenna continued, pacing the concrete floor, "then at night, the team splits. You guys are upstairs. We're downstairs. If Freddie wakes up scared... we aren't there."

Sarah frowned, processing the logic. "Separation of forces creates vulnerability."

"Exactly," Jenna said. "And honestly? I liked it too. I liked the weight. It stopped my brain from spinning."

"So what's the proposal?" Jessica asked, leaning against a support pole. "We can't fit five people in a King bed forever. Someone is going to get kicked in the face."

"We get a bigger bed," Sarah stated.

"They don't make bigger beds," Paige said. "King is the max."

"Then we build one," Freddie said.

The idea hit him like a lightning bolt. He looked at the vast, open floor plan of the basement.

"We possess the technology," Freddie murmured, his eyes scanning the space. "We can combine mattresses. Two Queens? Or a King and a Twin? We can build a frame that locks them together. A 'Mega-Bed'."

"A Mega-Bed," Jenna whispered, eyes shining. "Down here?"

"The space is sufficient," Freddie confirmed. "But..." He looked back at the stairs.

The steep, dangerous, narrow stairs.

"Access is the bottleneck," Freddie said, his shoulders slumping. "I cannot commute to the basement. The energy cost of the stairs is too high for daily ingress and egress."

"Then we fix the stairs," Jenna said. "We build a ramp!"

"Too steep," Freddie corrected. "The angle would require fifty feet of run."

Sarah walked over to the corner of the room, near where the old coal chute used to be. She looked up at the ceiling.

"What about... an elevator?" Sarah asked.

"An elevator?" Jessica laughed. "Sarah, this is a ranch house, not a hotel. You can't just put in an elevator. It costs fifty thousand dollars and you need a shaft."

"Not a people elevator," Sarah clarified, her hands moving as she visualized it. "Like... a platform. Just for one person. Like a forklift, but for a house."

Freddie perked up. "A vertical lift," he whispered. "Hydraulic or screw-drive. Low footprint."

"Yes!" Jenna shouted. "We cut a hole in the floor! Right there!" She pointed to a spot between the joists. "We put in a little platform. Freddie rolls on upstairs, pushes a button, zzzzzip, he's downstairs! No stairs! No spotters!"

Freddie looked at the ceiling. He imagined it. A dedicated lift. A direct line from the living room to the basement.

"If we had the lift," Freddie said, his voice trembling with excitement, "the basement becomes accessible. The Mega-Bed becomes viable."

"And we all sleep together," Paige finished the thought, squeezing his hand. "No one gets left out."

"We need to research," Freddie said. "The terminology. The cost. The structural requirements."

"I'll get the laptop," Jessica said, starting for the stairs. "Let's look it up. Right now."

"Wait," Freddie said. "I want to... verify the dimensions first."

He walked—slowly, carefully—across the concrete to the center of the room. He lay down on the floor.

"Freddie?" Paige asked.

"Mapping," Freddie said. "Lie down. Let's see if five fit."

Jenna didn't hesitate. She flopped down next to him. Sarah lay down on his other side. Paige and Jessica filled in the gaps.

They lay on the cold concrete floor of the unfinished basement, five naked adults staring up at the exposed rafters, visualizing a future where walls didn't matter, stairs didn't matter, and they never had to sleep apart again.

"It fits," Freddie whispered. "The architecture holds."

"Then we build it," Jessica declared. "We build the hive."

## The Search Query

The Ops Room (formerly the second bedroom) was operating at peak capacity.

Jessica sat at her desk, her **MagnaByte** laptop open. Jenna and Sarah stood behind her, leaning in like co-pilots in a cockpit.

Freddie sat in **The Rover** near the door, watching the screen. Paige sat on the floor next to his wheel, her arm resting on his knee.

"Okay," Jessica said, cracking her knuckles. "Operation: Vertical Access. Let's find us an elevator."

She opened the search engine. She typed: elevator for house

She hit Enter.

The first result loaded. It was a website for **Royal Ascendancy Elevators**.

The landing page featured a photo of a woman in a ballgown stepping out of a glass cylinder in the middle of a marble foyer. The elevator had gold trim. It had a chandelier inside it.

"Whoa," Jenna said. "Is that a spaceship?"

"It is... ornate," Freddie noted. "Gold plating is unnecessary weight."

Jessica clicked the **Pricing** tab.

**The Monarch Series:** Starting at \$55,000 (Installation not included). **Requirements:** 4-foot deep concrete pit. Dedicated machine room.

"Fifty-five thousand dollars," Jessica read flatly. "And we have to dig a hole under the house."

"Negative," Sarah said immediately. "That exceeds the budget. And it violates the structural integrity of the foundation."

"Okay," Jessica said, hitting the back button. "Too fancy. Let's look at the stairs."

She typed: wheelchair lift for stairs

Two types of results appeared.

**Option A: The Chair Lift** The first image showed an elderly woman smiling as she sat on a beige seat sliding up a rail.

"No," Freddie said instantly. He recoiled slightly in his chair. "Absolutely not."

"It gets you down there," Jenna pointed out.

"It requires a transfer," Freddie explained, his voice tight. "I would have to get out of The Rover. I would have to sit in that... seat. I would have to ride down slowly, exposed. And then? I would need a *second* wheelchair waiting at the bottom. I do not leave the vehicle."

**Option B: The Inclined Platform Lift** Jessica clicked the next link. "Okay, what about this?" Jessica asked. "It's called an Inclined Platform Lift. It’s a platform that rides the rails. You roll The Rover right onto it, and it carries you down the stairs."

Freddie leaned forward. "Better," he admitted. "No transfer. I stay in the chassis."

"But look at the dimensions," Sarah interrupted, pointing to the specs. "Folded width: 14 inches. Unfolded width: 32 inches."

She turned to look at the hallway door.

"Our basement stairs are exactly 30 inches wide," Sarah calculated. "They are steep and narrow. If we bolt that rail to the wall, and the platform folds down... it takes up the entire stairwell."

"Which means," Jessica realized, "if Freddie is using it, no one else can use the stairs. And even when it's folded up, we lose half the walking space. We’d be squeezing past a metal wall every time we did laundry."

"It blocks the artery," Freddie said, shaking his head. "We cannot sacrifice the primary egress for the rest of the Unit. The stairs must remain clear."

"Okay," Jessica said. "So the stairs are out. We can't go *down* the stairs. We have to bypass them."

She typed: Thu-Floor Lift home

The results populated with images of industrial equipment.

**Option C: The Shaft Lift** The image showed a simple platform inside a Plexiglas box.

**Indoor Installation:** Requires a hoistway (shaft) constructed of drywall or plexiglass to prevent shear hazards.

"A shaft," Sarah noted. "If we put that in the living room, we have to build a closet around it. It blocks the sightlines. It eats up square footage."

"I do not want to ride in a closet," Freddie murmured. "I want to see the Bridge."

"Wait," Jenna said, pointing to a small link on the sidebar. "What's that? 'Through-Floor Series'?"

Jessica clicked the link.

**The Access-Pro TFL-3000 (Through-Floor Lift).** The image changed.

It wasn't a box in a shaft. It was a sleek, open capsule that ran on two vertical rails bolted to the wall. But the genius was in the floor.

The diagram showed the lift in the "Up" position. The car was in the living room. Then it showed the lift in the "Down" position. The car was in the basement. And the hole in the ceiling was sealed by a panel attached to the top of the lift. "Look at the mechanics," Jessica breathed, reading the specs. "**Self-Sealing Aperture.** When the lift goes down, the 'roof' of the car stays in the floor to plug the hole. When the lift goes up, a 'false floor' on the bottom of the car fills the hole so you can walk over it."

"It plugs the breach," Freddie whispered, mesmerized. "It is a vanishing airlock."

"No shaft required," Jessica read. "No pit. It bolts to the floor and the ceiling joists. It travels through the aperture."

"Through-Floor Lift," Freddie tested the name. <aac>Tfl.</aac>

He looked at the image of the tracks—two skeletal steel rails that looked like the launch towers for a rocket. It was industrial. It was raw. It didn't try to hide what it was.

"It looks like the turbo-lift on the *Avalon*," Freddie decided. "It transports between decks."

"Price?" Sarah asked, ever the pragmatic one.

Jessica clicked **Get Quote**.

**Standard Model:** \$9,500. **Installation Kit:** Included.

"It's at the top of the budget," Jessica said, biting her lip. "But we don't have to build a shaft. That saves us lumber and drywall costs. And it saves the stairs."

Freddie looked at the screen. He looked at the term **Through-Floor Lift**.

It wasn't an "elevator" for a rich person. It wasn't a "stair lift" that blocked the hallway. It was a piece of specialized hardware designed to punch a hole in the universe and let him pass through.

"This is the solution," Freddie stated firmly. "We have found the terminology."

"Then let's order it," Jenna cheered. "I'll go get the tape measure. We need to find the spot!"

As Jenna ran out of the room, Freddie sat back in The Rover. He looked at the diagram on the screen—the tracks, the floating floor, the safety sensors.

It looked like something that belonged on the *Sanctuary*.

<aac>Tfl,</aac> Freddie repeated, testing the acronym. "It sounds... operational."

"It sounds like freedom," Paige whispered, resting her head on his shoulder.

## The Mega-Bed

The "Thru-Floor Lift" was found. The TFL-3000 was in the virtual shopping cart. The method of descent was secured.

Now, they had to secure the destination.

"Phase Two," Jessica announced, opening a new browser tab. "The Landing Pad. We need a bed for the basement that actually fits five adults without someone waking up with a foot in their mouth."

"The King is 76 inches wide," Freddie recited, staring at the ceiling as he ran the numbers. "Divided by five occupants, that creates a lateral allotment of 15.2 inches per person."

"That is... coffin width," Sarah noted with a shudder. "It restricts circulation. I woke up with a numb arm."

"I woke up on the floor," Jenna added, though she didn't look upset about it.

"We need width," Paige said, sitting on the floor and leaning against *The Rover*. "But not *too* much width. Freddie likes the squeeze."

Freddie nodded. It was a delicate variable.

He thought about the night before—the weight of Jenna on his legs, the pressure of Sarah’s grip on his arm, the warmth of Paige and Jessica flanking him. The sensation of being *buried* in his sisters was the most secure he had ever felt.

"I require... contact," Freddie clarified. "I do not want isolation. I want the tangle. But I require the option to rotate my spine without elbowing a ribcage."

"So, bigger than a King," Jessica summarized. "But we have to get it down those stairs."

She typed: biggest mattress size

The search results populated.

**Alaskan King:** 108" x 108". "Whoa," Jenna gasped. "Nine feet? That’s an arena!"

Jessica clicked the price tag. **\$7,500 (Mattress Only). Custom Freight Shipping Required.**

"Negative," Sarah said instantly. "That is the price of a used car. And we cannot get a nine-foot mattress down those narrow stairs. It physically won't fit through the door. It is a logistical impossibility."

"Agreed," Freddie said. "We need a modular solution."

"Modular," Jessica mused. "Like... building blocks."

She opened a new tab. combine mattresses to make bigger bed

The results were full of "family bed" forums and DIY hacks.

- **Option A:** Two Full Beds side-by-side. (54" + 54" = 108"). Same width as an Alaskan King.

- **Option B:** King + Twin XL. (76" + 38" = 114").

- **Option C:** Two Queen Beds. (60" + 60" = 120").

"Two Queens," Freddie calculated instantly. "Total width: 10 feet. Depth: 80 inches. That provides 24 inches of lateral space per person."

"That’s double what we have now," Paige noted. "That’s luxury. We could all lay flat on our backs and barely touch."

"But," Sarah interrupted, her brow furrowing as she visualized the schematic. "The seam. The crack between the mattresses. If you sleep in the middle—which is Freddie's position—you fall into the void. It collects debris. It pinches. It is unsanitary."

"There is a patch for that," Jessica said, scrolling down. She pointed to a product image: a T-shaped piece of foam called a **Bed Bridge**.

"You put this wedge in the crack," Jessica explained. "Then you use this..." She pointed to a heavy-duty nylon strap system. "...a connector belt. It wraps around the perimeter of both mattresses and ratchets tight so they don't slide apart."

"Structural integrity," Freddie noted. "But the texture... the bridge creates a bump."

"Layering," Jessica said. "We put the bridge in. We strap the beds together. And then..."

She clicked on a listing for a **Memory Foam Topper**.

"We buy two massive Queen-sized memory foam toppers. We lay them over the mattresses *and* the bridge. It unifies the surface. You won't even feel the seam."

Freddie closed his eyes. He pictured it.

Two Queen beds locked together in the center of the basement. A ten-foot-wide island of soft foam. No edges to fall off of. No fighting for covers.

He could lie in the center. Paige could be on his left, Jessica on his right. Sarah and Jenna could take the flanks, or they could all pile in the middle. The extra space meant they *could* spread out if it was hot, but the Bed Bridge meant they could still roll to the center and form the "Heap" without a gap stopping them.

"Two Queens," Freddie decided. "The geometry is sound. The modularity allows for transport down the narrow stairs."

"And the frame?" Jenna asked. "We can't just put them on the concrete. Spiders. Moisture."

Freddie looked at the specs for the *Access-Pro TFL* they had just found. He looked at his sisters.

"We are Forgers," Freddie stated, his voice firm. "We do not buy a frame. We build a platform."

"Wood?" Jessica asked.

"Lumber," Freddie confirmed. "2x4s and plywood. We build a riser in the basement. We carpet it. We drop the mattresses in. A custom install."

"I can build that," Jenna said, flexing her fingers. "I built stage sets in drama club. Give me a drill and a saw."

"Then it is settled," Jessica announced, closing the laptop. "Project: Mega-Bed is a go."

She looked at Freddie.

"We get the lift. We get the bed. And then... we never have to leave the pile."

"Never," Freddie whispered. "Systems... optimal."
