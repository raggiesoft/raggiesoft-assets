## The Last Supper

The silver Horizon Avalon sat in the gravel parking lot, its trunk heavy with Harper’s luggage. Inside the restaurant, the Avery Unit occupied a large corner booth.

This was the fueling stop. The "Last Supper" before deployment.

The table was covered in heavy ceramic plates piled high with fried chicken, mashed potatoes, and green beans. It was comfort food—heavy, warm, and designed to induce a food coma that would dull the anxiety of separation.

"Eat," Jessica commanded, pushing a basket of rolls toward Harper. "Dorm food is an unknown variable. You need a caloric buffer."

Harper picked at a roll. She was wearing her favorite purple hoodie, the hood down, her headphones resting around her neck like a safety collar. She looked small in the booth, sandwiched between Freddie and Paige.

"I'm nervous," Harper admitted, dropping the roll. "What if I get a bad roommate? What if she listens to country music?"

"Then you use your noise-canceling headphones," Freddie said, dipping a fry in gravy. "You have the technology."

"And remember the specs," Jessica added, consulting the intake packet she had memorized. "You're in the PERT block. That means High School age only. You won't be rooming with a 40-year-old welder retraining for a new career. It’s peers only. You are separated from the adult population."

"Peers," Harper muttered. "Great. Teenagers. Even worse."

"You are a teenager," Paige smiled, squeezing her shoulder. "You'll be fine. Just find the other geeks. Look for the people with soldering irons or sketchpads."

"Status check," Aunt Linda said, checking her watch. "Check-in at Barnett Hall starts at 15:00. We have one hour to finish this pie."

"Pie is mandatory," Freddie stated. "Peanut butter cream."

They ate the pie. They drank the sweet tea. They delayed the inevitable for as long as they could, sitting in the warm, noisy restaurant, a family unit holding onto its satellite before the launch.

***

## Barnett Hall

The drive from the restaurant to the campus was short. They took Exit 222, winding past the hospital and up the hill to the rehabilitation center.

They pulled up to Barnett Hall. It was a large, brick dormitory building, functional and institutional, but softened by the autumn leaves blowing across the lawn.

"Target acquired," Freddie whispered from the back seat. "Dormitory Office."

They unloaded the car. Harper transferred into her purple chair. She gripped her bag in her lap.

"Let's go," Jessica said.

They walked into the lobby. The air smelled of floor wax and industrial cleaner. A sign on the wall pointed to the Dormitory Office.

Inside, the office was buzzing with activity. Staff members in polo shirts were checking names off lists.

"Avery, Harper," Jessica announced at the desk.

The housing coordinator looked up. "Ah, yes. PERT Program. You're in the West Wing. All-female suite."

She handed Harper a key card and a packet of rules.

"You are in Suite 200," the coordinator explained. "It’s a three-room suite. Rooms A, B, and C. You are in Room A. There are two students per room, so you'll have one direct roommate, and four suitemates. All high school age."

"Security check," Freddie asked. "Are the adult clients isolated?"

"Strictly," the coordinator nodded to Freddie. "The adult vocational students are in a different wing. The PERT students have their own staff and their own curfew. It's a controlled environment."

"Acceptable," Freddie noted.

"We have an escort ready to take you up," the coordinator said, signaling a staff member—a young woman named Sarah (not Avery) with a lanyard.

"Right this way," the escort said. "Elevators are down the hall."

***

## Suite 200

They rode the elevator to the second floor. The escort led them down a wide hallway to a door marked 200.

She swiped a master key. The door clicked open.

They stepped into a small common area. It had standard dorm furniture—a vinyl couch, a sturdy table. Three doors branched off it.

"Room A," the escort pointed to the left.

Harper rolled in.

The room was simple. Two twin beds. Two desks. Two wardrobes. The floor was tiled linoleum—easy to roll on.

"Bed 1 is yours," the escort said. "Your roommate, Kayla, hasn't arrived yet."

"Okay," Jessica said, dropping the duffel bags. "Setup protocol. We have thirty minutes before we have to clear out."

The Avery Unit sprang into action.

Jessica unpacked the clothes, hanging them in the wardrobe with military precision. Paige made the bed. She put on the sheets Harper had brought from home—flannel, purple, smelling of the laundry detergent from Pepper Street. She arranged the pillows to create a "nest." Freddie rolled around the room, checking the outlets and the clearance.

"Desk height is adjustable," Freddie noted. "Good ergonomics. The turning radius between the beds is tight, but sufficient."

He looked at the bathroom attached to the suite.

"Shared facility," Freddie observed. "You must coordinate shower times with five other females. I suggest a schedule."

"I'll make a chart," Harper muttered, putting her laptop on the desk.

They set up her station. Laptop. Headphones. A picture frame with the photo Aunt Linda had taken on the sofa—the "Heap."

Within twenty minutes, the sterile dorm room looked like a forward operating base.
