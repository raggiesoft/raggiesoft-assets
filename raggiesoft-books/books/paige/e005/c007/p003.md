## The Third-Floor Gallery & Line 18

On Thursday, February 4, 2016, the Avery Unit deployed to Richmond for Virginia Transit Legislative Day.

Because the family relied on Blue Ridge Transit Route 1 and Route 2—along with the Radford Connector across the New River—to link their base on Pepper Street, Aunt Linda's apartment in Radford, the Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center, and the Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute campus in Blacksburg, Jessica had registered the entire unit with the New River Valley transit advocacy coalition. Pinned to the front of Freddie's jacket, Harper's hoodie, and the coats of Paige, Jessica, Sarah, and Jenna were bright blue *RIDE TRANSIT VIRGINIA* stickers.

At twelve-ten in the afternoon, Freddie parked *The Rover* in the accessible third-floor public gallery of the Virginia House of Delegates, with Harper's neon-purple wheelchair positioned flush against his right wheel so her left hand could stay anchored to his forearm. Directly behind them stood Paige and Jessica in Formation Alpha, while Sarah and Jenna flanked the outer rail—right beside another CPI and Christiansburg family group they knew from the Huckleberry Trail and the aquatic center: Jordan in his power wheelchair *S.S. Freedom* with his sisters and partners Aubrie Jean, Megan, Zoë, and Isabel, and two Virginia Dominion University students down the rail with a blue manual wheelchair.

Down on the House Floor, the Speaker called up House Bill 663—the "anatomical sex" bathroom bill patroned by Delegate Martin Cobb of Spotsylvania, which proposed a fifty-dollar civil fine for the Literary Fund against anyone whose "physical anatomy" did not match the sign on a public school, university, or government restroom door.

Beside Freddie's right wheel, Harper's fingers tightened around his sleeve.

"I hate that bill," Harper whispered, her eyes fixed on the floor microphones below. "What if I'm at a rest stop or a state building and I freeze up and need you, or what if you need Paige and Jessica to guard the door? They want to put police officers outside the bathroom doors?"

"The statute is logically defective," Freddie said quietly, his eyes narrowing behind his glasses as he reviewed the text of House Bill 663 on his tablet. "It mandates verification of physical anatomy without a constitutional mechanism of proof."

Seconds later, down on the green carpet of the House Chamber, thirty-year-old [Delegate Katie Prairie of Virginia Beach](https://books.raggiesoft.com/katie/the-gentlewoman-from-virginia-beach-the-house-of-delegates/house-bill-663-live-on-meet-the-nation/part-1-line-18-seated-by-delegate-ronald-villa-on-the-floor-a-question-across-the-chamber)—sitting upright in her compact black SwiftChair power wheelchair with her twin brother Nate parked in his matching powerchair in the adjacent bay—held up the two printed pages of House Bill 663 and delivered the exact mathematical strike Freddie had just calculated:

"Why on earth should I—or any woman or girl in the Commonwealth of Virginia—have to pull my pants down for a government official or a police officer to prove I have female parts just to use the ladies' room?!"

Jenna let out a triumphant whoop before Sarah clapped a hand over her sister's mouth, and even Jessica and Paige broke into sharp applause as the gallery erupted and the Speaker hammered his gavel. Down at his desk microphone, Delegate Cobb turned beet-red and froze in complete silence for four full seconds until the Majority Leader jumped up to pass the bill by for the day.

"Target neutralized," Sarah murmured approvingly.

## Room 518: Six Wheelchairs in the Bus Delegate's Office

At two-forty that afternoon, after delivering their Blue Ridge Transit and Radford Transit ridership packets to the New River Valley legislative offices on the fourth floor, the Avery Unit rode the elevator up to the fifth floor alongside Jordan, Aubrie Jean, Megan, Zoë, and Isabel to pay a visit to Room 518.

Even though Delegate Katie Prairie represented the Eighty-Fifth House District down in Virginia Beach rather than Montgomery County or Radford, to Freddie, Harper, and Paige, a computer programmer in a wheelchair who rode public buses every day, read statutory code literally, and refused to let politicians station bathroom police outside public restrooms was the gold standard of constitutional governance.

When they rolled into Room 518—passing two VDU students with a blue manual wheelchair who were just heading toward the elevators—Delegate Katie Prairie and her twin brother Nate wheeled their two black SwiftChairs out from behind her desk with wide, welcoming smiles.

"Welcome to Room 518!" Katie said warmly as Freddie positioned *The Rover* and Harper's purple chair alongside Jordan's powerchair in the center of the office. "Look at all those blue *RIDE TRANSIT VIRGINIA* stickers!"

"Delegate Prairie," Freddie said, his voice steady as Paige rested her hands on his shoulders and Harper kept her hand linked through his elbow, "from a systems-engineering perspective, your ninety-five-second cross-examination of House Bill 663 on the floor today exposed a fatal runtime error in the patron's statute. Because law enforcement officers do not carry hospital birth records, proving anatomical sex under Line 18 requires an unconstitutional physical search."

"That is the exact literal truth of the bill, Freddie," Katie nodded earnestly. "Nate and I write software for a living too, and when a legislature writes bad code into the Code of Virginia, everyday citizens and disabled families are the ones who pay the price. That bill is not passing."

"And Nate already put open captions on the ninety-five-second video!" Jenna grinned, holding up her phone as she, Sarah, Paige, and Zoë blasted the link from Nate's server out to every computer science mailing list and student group chat at Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute. "By the time our bus gets back to Christiansburg tonight, the whole campus in Blacksburg is going to be watching Delegate Cobb turn the color of a tomato!"

For the first time all day inside a crowded government building, Harper's shoulders dropped two inches against Freddie's arm, and she smiled across the room at the Delegate in the wheelchair who had spoken up for all of them.
