## A Picnic with Cousins

Instead of a formal restaurant, the MagnaByte van dropped them off at a large, sun-drenched courtyard nestled in the center of the Infinite Loop campus. Long picnic tables were arranged on a vast lawn, and the air was filled with the smell of fresh coffee, bacon, and grilled pastries. Hundreds of MagnaByte employees were already there, chatting in groups and filling their plates from a lavish breakfast buffet. It was a company-wide, picnic-style breakfast, a regular tradition to mark the end of the quarter.

David greeted them with a wide smile, handing them each a plate. "Welcome to our version of a team meeting," he said. "Less MagnaPodium, more pancakes."

They found a spot at a table with the QA team from the bug bash, and were immediately welcomed into the conversation. For Kelly and Ryan, it was another surreal experience—casually eating breakfast alongside the engineers who designed the operating system they had spent the last year learning about.

Halfway through breakfast, a hush fell over the crowd as a figure walked onto the small, permanent stage at the front of the courtyard. It was Stephen Marchand.

This was the first time the twins had seen him in person. He wasn't the larger-than-life icon from the keynote video; he was just a man in a black mock turtleneck and jeans, but his presence commanded the attention of the entire campus. He spoke for a few minutes, outlining his vision for the next quarter, praising the work of various teams, and reinforcing the importance of the new partnership with Quantum.

From their table, Kelly and Ryan watched, captivated. They weren't close enough to shake his hand, but they were there, part of the company he was leading. He was no longer just a name in a tech magazine; he was a real person, and they were working on a project that was a key part of his vision.

As Marchand finished his speech to a round of applause, David leaned over to the twins. "Pretty cool, huh?"

"Yeah," Ryan said, his eyes still on the stage. "It is."

The breakfast wound down, and soon it was time to leave for the airport. They said their goodbyes to David and the other engineers, who all wished them a safe flight and told them to "keep shipping great code." As the accessible van pulled away from Infinite Loop, Kelly looked back at the campus, the last, extraordinary morning of their trip cementing a feeling of profound belonging.

***

## Homeward Bound

The morning sun was higher in the sky when the accessible van returned to take them back to the airport. David and a few of the other MagnaByte engineers walked them out, the farewells feeling less like a corporate send-off and more like seeing family off after a holiday visit.

"You've got my email," David said, clapping Ryan on the shoulder. "If you find any more kernel panics, you know who to call."

"And you have a standing invitation to our next bug bash," another engineer added with a grin to Kelly. "We could use the help."

The ride back to SJC was quieter than the trip in. The initial, overwhelming awe had been replaced by a deep, buzzing sense of accomplishment. Kelly and Ryan sat together, talking in low, excited voices, not about the glamour of the campus, but about the code. They discussed how they could have optimized their bug fix, what they had learned from the code review, and what they hoped to work on next.

Jessica listened from her seat, a quiet smile on her face. The transformation was remarkable. In just two days, they had gone from timid, first-time flyers to confident young engineers discussing race conditions and memory leaks.

Checking in at the airport was a completely different experience this time. There was no hesitation, no nervous fumbling. Ryan handled their bags at the counter while Kelly confidently held onto their boarding passes. They navigated the security checkpoint like seasoned travelers. Jessica still guided them, but they no longer needed the constant reassurance.

As they settled in at the gate, waiting for their flight back to Seattle, Kelly looked out at the planes on the tarmac. "It feels different now," she said thoughtfully. "The airport, the planes... it all feels a little less scary."

"That's because you know where you're going," Jessica replied.

And she was right. They weren't just going back to Seattle. They were going back to their new desks at Quantum, to a project they were a part of, and to a future that, for the first time, felt bright, clear, and full of possibility.

***

## The Flight Home

The SJC departure gate was bright and airy, and this time, the twins navigated it with the quiet confidence of seasoned travelers. When the gate agent called for pre-boarding, they knew the drill. Kelly and Ryan simply fell into place on either side of Jessica, ready to board as her traveling companions.

At the aircraft door, the now-familiar routine played out smoothly. Jessica transferred to the aisle chair, her power wheelchair was tagged for gate-check, and she led the way onto the plane. They settled into the same configuration as before: Jessica on the aisle, and the twins in the middle and window seats.

As the plane climbed above the golden California landscape, the adrenaline of the past two days finally began to fade, replaced by a deep and happy exhaustion. Kelly leaned her head against the cool plastic of the window, watching the world shrink below. Ryan, feeling the familiar pull of sleep, simply snuggled into his sister's side, his head resting on her shoulder as he drifted off. Kelly wrapped a protective arm around him, the two of them a small, peaceful island in the humming cabin.

Jessica watched them for a moment, a profound sense of pride swelling in her chest. The transformation was astonishing. The nervous, wide-eyed kids she had met at SEA yesterday morning were gone. In their place were two young, confident engineers who had debugged code on a foreign OS, impressed a team of senior developers at a rival company, and found a system-crashing bug on their first day. They weren't just interns anymore; they were a part of the team, a part of the bridge she had spent her career building.

She looked away, giving them their privacy, and turned her gaze toward the clouds. The conversation with Mark replayed in her mind. A long-term plan. It wasn't enough. These two were special, and MagnaByte's interest, however joking, was a clear signal of their value.

Jessica made a quiet promise to herself. As soon as they were back in Redmond, she wasn't just going to talk to her higher-ups; she was going to build a case. She would outline a multi-year development path, propose mentorship programs, and secure a commitment from Quantum to see the Madsen twins not just through their internship, but through their college degrees and into full-time roles. They had taken care of Kelly when she was in trouble. Now, it was time to make sure her future, and her brother's, was secure. They had found a home at Quantum, and Jessica would make sure they never had to leave.
