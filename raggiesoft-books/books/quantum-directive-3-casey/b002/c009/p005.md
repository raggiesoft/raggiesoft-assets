After we hung up with Liz and Erin, the chaotic energy in my childhood bedroom finally began to settle. The Delaneys stayed for another hour, standing guard like a physical wall, until my mother quietly brought in a tray of leftover Thanksgiving pie. 

By the time 9:30 rolled around, Casey was completely exhausted. The emotional whiplash of the day had drained her processor entirely. She was curled up on my bed, her head resting heavily on my chest, fast asleep. 

Cassandra, however, was wide awake. 

She wheeled herself over to my desk, opened her silver Quantum laptop, and booted it up. The glowing screen illuminated her face in the dark room as her fingers flew across the keyboard. 

"You should get some sleep, Cass," I whispered, not wanting to wake Casey. "You just engineered a cross-country relocation in under twenty minutes. You have to be tired."

Cassandra didn't stop typing. She didn't even look up. "I don't sleep when there's an open vulnerability in the perimeter. But the December extraction is locked. Now, I'm looking at April."

"April?" I frowned, carefully shifting my weight under Casey. "What happens in April?"

"April 8th," Cassandra stated, finally looking over the rim of her glasses at me. "You are turning twenty."

"Cass, we don't need to do anything big," I said instantly, the ingrained instinct to minimize myself kicking in. "Just being in Washington with you, Casey, and Liz is enough."

"It's not enough," Cassandra said, her voice dropping into a fierce, absolute register. "For the last six months, you have been isolated, manipulated, and trapped by a sociopath who used your own loyalty against you. I am not throwing a standard birthday party. I am giving you a fortress."

She turned the silver laptop around so I could see the screen. 

Displayed on the glowing display was a sprawling, majestic stone lodge perched on the edge of a massive, jagged mountain gorge. The architecture looked like something out of a medieval fantasy—huge timber framing, three-foot-thick stone walls, and a cobblestone portico. 

"The Prospector's Keep," Cassandra read aloud. "It's operated by a private conservation trust deep in the North Cascades. They only take a highly limited number of commercial bookings during the summer season before the Department of Transportation drops the barricades and the highway closes for the winter."

She clicked through to the logistics page. 

"Look at this architecture, David," Cass pointed to the screen. "The property is completely isolated. Guests have to leave their personal vehicles at a subterranean Lower Motorpool at the base of the mountain. The venue provides a fleet of heavy-duty transit vans to haul everyone up a five-mile private road."

"A moat," I noted, my threat-detection system instantly admiring the sheer security of it. "A literal, five-mile physical moat. Nobody gets in unless they are authorized and driven up by the staff."

"Exactly," Cassandra nodded. "Total perimeter control. And because it's the off-season, they allow total buyouts. I can rent the entire mountain for the weekend of your birthday. Once our pack is on that summit, the rest of the world simply ceases to exist."

She scrolled down to the pricing matrix, and my stomach physically dropped. The number on the screen was astronomical. It was the kind of money that could buy a small house outright. 

"Cass, absolutely not," I said immediately, shaking my head. "That is insane. You are already paying Liz's out-of-state tuition. You bought the house in Redmond. I am not letting you drop that kind of cash on a birthday weekend."

Cassandra didn't even blink. She pulled the laptop back toward her. 

She looked at me, her expression radiating the absolute, unyielding financial gravity of an executive who operated on the same floor as William Keswick. 

"David, it is a single line item in my operational budget," Cassandra stated, her voice calm and absolute. "It is already funded. The only question is, do you love the venue?"

I looked at the screen again. The towering scale of the timber. The roaring walk-in fireplaces. The promise of absolute, impenetrable safety for my family. 

"It's perfect," I admitted quietly. 

"Then the decision is made," Cass said, turning her attention back to the keyboard. 

Without a single microsecond of hesitation, Cassandra Vance began filling out the digital booking contract. She agreed to pay the astronomical public price without requesting a single discount. She navigated the strict liability waivers, her legal mind appreciating the draconian, airtight language the Trust used to protect their property. 

When she reached the final payment gateway, she inputted her name as the primary billing contact: *Cassandra Vance*. 

"Booking confirmed," Cass announced, hitting the submit key. The screen flashed green, sealing our fate. "The Prospector's Keep is ours."
