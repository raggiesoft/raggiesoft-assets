The storm wasn't letting up. If anything, the coastal system was intensifying, dragging heavy sheets of rain across the roof of the house. 

I sat huddled on the floor of my bedroom with Casey, our backs pressed firmly against the frame of my bed. I had my jacket pulled up over my head to block out the sudden, blinding flashes of light, and Casey had her hands clamped over her ears, her plush lion cub, Leona, squeezed tightly under her arm. Mom had managed to dig the toy out of the trunk and toss it into my room before rushing back out to help Dad with the luggage. 

Another violent flash strobed across the room, casting long, sharp shadows across my walls. A split second later, the thunder hit—a deep, concussive boom that literally rattled the floorboards under us. 

Casey let out a sharp cry, burying her face into Leona's synthetic fur. 

I couldn't take it anymore. The sensory isolation of being trapped in the dark room with the flashing lights was too much. 

"Come on," I said, grabbing Casey's arm and hauling her to her feet. "We can't stay in here."

She didn't argue. She just gripped Leona and followed me as I carefully opened my bedroom door. 

The hallway was dark, save for the flickering lightning bleeding through the windows. We crept down the corridor, passing our parents' room, and stopped dead in front of Cassandra's doorway. 

The door was cracked open. A warm, yellow light spilled out into the hall from her bedside lamp. 

We hovered nervously in the doorway, peering inside. Cassandra was already in her pajamas, lying propped up in her bed with a massive, heavy-bound textbook resting on her chest. She looked completely, utterly unfazed by the raging thunderstorm outside. The loud crashes of thunder didn't even cause her to flinch. 

She looked up from her book and saw us standing there in the hallway, trembling, wide-eyed, and terrified. 

Cassandra didn't say a word. She didn't ask us what was wrong or tell us to go back to bed. She just let out a quiet sigh, closed her textbook, and reached down. With one smooth motion, she pulled the heavy comforter back, exposing a wide swath of the mattress beside her. 

Casey and I didn't hesitate. We bolted into the room and scrambled up onto the mattress, diving completely under the heavy blankets to escape the flashing lights. 

I wrapped my arms around Casey, pulling her against me to provide the tight, deep pressure squeeze she desperately needed to regulate her nervous system. Cassandra reached over and threw the heavy comforter over both of us, sealing out the visual flashes of the storm and anchoring us firmly to the bed. 

The storm raged outside for another two hours, rattling the windowpanes and hammering the roof with torrential rain. But buried under the blankets, pressed tightly against my siblings, the chaotic, terrifying sensory overload of the world finally faded away.
