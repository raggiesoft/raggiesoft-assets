## 3:15 PM — The Terminal

The drive down Interstate 64 to Norfolk International Airport was buzzing with anticipation. I had both hands gripping the steering wheel of my mother’s standard sedan, my leg bouncing lightly against the floorboard. It had been months since I had seen Cassandra and Casey, and my processor was eagerly counting down the minutes until their flight from Seattle landed.

Sitting in the passenger seat, Kristine Mercer watched the highway markers pass by.

Since we started dating in early September, Kristine and I hadn't spent more than a few waking hours apart. We shared every meal, ran every errand together, and she even waited for me in the parking lot while I was in my classes. I just figured that was how serious adult relationships worked. We were a constant, uninterrupted unit, so naturally, she was in the passenger seat for the airport run.

"Your mom was really nice to let us borrow the car for this," Kristine noted, checking her reflection in the passenger-side sun visor mirror and smoothing her hair.

"Yeah, it was her idea," I smiled, navigating the exit toward Norview Avenue. "Cass hasn't actually seen Mom since the summer of 2000, and Casey hasn't seen her since 2001. So the girls were really hoping to steal me for a few days so the four of us could just hole up at Mom's place and do the whole family time thing."

Kristine offered a polite nod, snapping the visor shut. "I know, but my apartment in Portsmouth is just so much more central for everything we need to do this weekend. Plus, I want to host them. I just hope they aren't too exhausted from the flight. We have a busy schedule."

I banked the car off State Route 247, merging into the heavy holiday traffic funneling toward the main public terminal. I parked the car in the short-term lot, and we walked inside, navigating the crowded concourse to wait at the bottom of the escalators just outside the TSA security checkpoint.

I knew my sisters were flying Domestic First Class into the main terminal, and I knew the exact minute their flight touched down. Cassandra had briefed me over the phone the night before with explicit, non-negotiable instructions: under no circumstances was I to mention her private corporate jet, her standard VIP pickup at the executive aviation terminal, or the massive scale of her position at Quantum. I didn't fully understand *why* she was being so intensely covert or why she had downgraded her normal travel profile for this trip, but Cassandra always had an operational reason for everything she did. So, I kept my mouth shut and stuck strictly to the script.

The moment I spotted my sisters coming down the escalator, the ambient noise of the airport completely vanished.

They looked perfectly average. Seventeen-year-old Casey Vance was wearing a standard winter jacket, gripping a scuffed canvas duffel bag. Twenty-six-year-old Cassandra Vance wheeled herself forward in her manual chair, dressed in a simple, pragmatic sweater, a heavy nylon messenger bag resting on her lap. There was absolutely nothing in their posture or their luggage that gave away the truth.

"Casey!" I called out.

Casey dropped her carry-on bag instantly. She broke into a run, her face lighting up with absolute joy. I met her halfway, wrapping my arms around her and lifting her slightly off the floor in a massive, deep-pressure bear hug. She buried her face in my neck, letting out a soft, happy hum that immediately regulated my own nervous system.

I set her down, keeping one arm wrapped securely around her shoulders, and turned to catch Cassandra in a tight, one-armed embrace as she wheeled up to us.

"It is so good to see you, David," Cassandra smiled, squeezing my shoulder before adjusting her glasses. She looked past me, offering a polite, practiced smile. "Hi, Kristine. Thank you guys for picking us up."

"Of course! Welcome to Virginia," Kristine beamed. Her voice was bright, sugary, and perfectly pitched for a welcoming host. Kristine looked at their modest bags and simple clothes, her internal processor instantly categorizing them as completely unthreatening, average siblings.

But as I pulled my arm back from Casey, I felt Kristine step immediately into my space. She slid her hand smoothly down my arm, lacing her fingers tightly through mine, and pressed her side flush against my ribs. Her grip was just a fraction too tight, but I assumed she was just nervous meeting my family, so I squeezed her hand back to reassure her.

## 6:15 PM — The Return to the Coast

We gathered their luggage and loaded up the trunk. The drive to Virginia Beach was filled with easy, rapid-fire catching up between me, Cassandra, and Casey. Kristine chimed in politely from the passenger seat, asking standard, friendly questions about the flight and the Seattle weather.

When we arrived at my mother’s house, the environment grew a little more crowded. Wendy Vance and Tom welcomed everyone warmly. We sat in their living room, eating snacks and talking about the upcoming Thanksgiving plans.

Throughout the visit, I sat on the sofa with Casey tucked right against my side, our shoulders resting comfortably against each other as we listened to Mom talk. It was our standard, lifelong baseline for physical proximity.

"I still can't believe how fast you kids are growing up," Wendy smiled, handing Cass a glass of water. She looked over at me. "Have you talked to Liz lately, David? I know she's neck-deep in her exams up in Dayton right now."

"We talked on Tuesday," I answered happily, the mention of Liz Carter immediately bringing a smile to my face. "She's doing great at Shenandoah Valley University. I really miss her, though. She said she's going to try to drive down for a weekend before Christmas."

Out of the corner of my eye, I caught Kristine shifting in her armchair across the room. Her smile was still perfectly fixed in place, but her fingers tightened around her plastic cup. She had always been a little quietly insecure whenever I brought up my honorary sister. I had explained to Kristine a dozen times that Liz was just a lifelong best friend, but Kristine's posture always went a little rigid at the mention of her name.

Kristine took a slow sip of her drink. She didn't say a word to interrupt the sibling cuddles or complain about the conversation, but a vague, unplaceable need to manage her mood began to quietly hum in the back of my mind. I quickly convinced myself it was just standard adult relationship maintenance, and that she just wanted to make sure she was the most important girl in the room.

***

## 8:30 PM — The Perimeter Shift

Since we needed to return Wendy's car to her, my mother grabbed her keys once the visit wrapped up. She drove the four of us out of Virginia Beach, crossing the water and dropping us off directly at the curb of Kristine’s apartment building in Portsmouth before waving goodbye and driving off into the night.

We hauled the luggage into the three-bedroom apartment. Kristine's mother, Robyn, and her boyfriend, Tom, were already gone for the holiday visiting his family, leaving the space entirely to us. Because the apartment was located on the ground floor, we didn't have to navigate any stairs with the heavy bags. The living room was modest, anchored by two sofas positioned perpendicular to each other with a small wooden end table wedged in the corner between them.

"We can easily grab a hotel room down the road for the three of us," Cassandra offered casually, setting her heavy messenger bag on the floor. "I know Mom really wanted us to stay in Virginia Beach with her so we could get some family time with David, but since we're out here in Portsmouth, we don't want to crowd your space, Kristine. David can just ride back here with us in the morning."

"Oh, no, that’s absolutely not a problem at all!" Kristine answered.

The response was instant. I didn't think much of it—I just assumed she was being a highly accommodating host who wanted everyone under one roof.

"If you're sure," Cassandra replied smoothly. She reached into her canvas duffel bag and pulled out a tightly folded, heavy-duty air mattress, along with a stack of pillows and blankets. Because she was constantly deployed by Quantum to different corporate sites, she never left her physical sleeping arrangements to chance.

"Casey and I can take the air mattress and the sofas," Cassandra continued. "David can just grab a blanket and camp out in the living room with us. It'll be just like when we were kids."

My chest instantly lightened. The idea of spending the night camped out in the living room, surrounded by my pack, sounded perfect. Casey didn't care that a sofa was meant for one person; she was fully prepared to figure it out, needing the deep pressure and proximity to regulate her own system after the long flight.

"Actually," Kristine chimed in, her voice dropping into a soft, pouty register as she stepped into my space and wrapped her arms around my waist. "I was really hoping David would sleep in my room with me tonight. We've been running around all day, and I just want to spend some time with my boyfriend."

She looked up at me with a sweet, expectant smile. It was a difficult spot. We had literally spent every single waking hour of the last twelve weeks together, but I worried that if I said no and chose my sisters, I would look like a neglectful, childish boyfriend rejecting his partner's affection after she had just volunteered to host my family.

I hesitated, my eyes darting to Cassandra. I desperately wanted to stay in the living room.

Casey opened her mouth, her protective instincts flaring as she prepared to argue for our sibling time. But before Casey could utter a single syllable, Cassandra caught her eye. Cassandra gave a sharp, almost imperceptible downward motion with her hand—a silent, absolute command for Casey to hold her tongue.

"Have a good night, David," Cassandra said politely, unpacking her blankets.

"Night, guys," I muttered, feeling a heavy, confusing knot form in my stomach as I grabbed my bag.

I followed Kristine down the hall to her bedroom. The door clicked shut behind us, separating me from the rest of my family in the living room. I was exhausted from the driving and the travel logistics, and my internal clock was begging for sleep.

But the second we were alone, Kristine wrapped her arms around my neck, pulling me into a soft, eager kiss. She was radiating affection, whispering about how happy she was to finally have me all to herself after a long day of hosting. Sleep wasn't on her immediate agenda. Even though my eyes were heavy, I kissed her back, easily convincing myself that staying awake to be intimate with my loving girlfriend was exactly how a normal adult relationship worked.
