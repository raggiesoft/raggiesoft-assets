## 7:00 AM — The Morning Anchor

I woke up early on Thursday morning, long before the gray daylight fully filtered through the bedroom blinds. Beside me, Kristine was still fast asleep, her breathing slow and heavy.

My internal processor was already humming with anticipation. I quietly slipped out from under the covers, gathered my clothes, and dressed as silently as possible in the dim light. I didn't want to wake her, but my body was craving the familiar, grounding presence of my pack.

I eased the bedroom door open, slipping down the short hallway into the living room.

Cassandra and Casey were just beginning to stir. Because Kristine had swiftly shot down the air mattress idea the night before, my sisters had simply claimed the two perpendicular sofas. Cassandra was sitting up on the other couch, adjusting her glasses, while Casey was buried under a pile of blankets on the adjacent sofa.

I didn't say a word. I just got down on my knees next to Casey's sofa, reached under the heavy blankets, and pulled a groggy Casey into a tight, deep-pressure hug.

Casey blinked her dark eyes open. The disorientation of waking up in a strange apartment vanished the absolute second she realized she was looking at my face. A massive, sleep-heavy smile broke across her lips, and she wrapped her arms fiercely around my neck, humming a quiet, happy note.

Once I was satisfied that my favorite girl was regulated and smiling, I stood up and moved over to the other sofa. I leaned down and wrapped my arms around Cassandra, pulling my older sister into a solid embrace.

"Happy Thanksgiving, David," Cassandra smiled warmly, squeezing my shoulders. "It's so good to actually have you here."

"I missed my two favorite women," I whispered.

Casey beamed from her pile of blankets, and Cassandra’s smile widened affectionately. But almost immediately, a subtle, calculating crease appeared on Cassandra’s forehead. Her dark eyes darted briefly toward the dark hallway leading to Kristine's bedroom.

"We missed you too, David," Cassandra said, her voice dropping into a very quiet, careful whisper. "But do me a favor. Maybe don't use the phrase 'favorite women' when Kristine is in earshot."

I blinked, genuinely confused. "Why? You ladies are my sisters, and you *are* my favorite women."

"I know that, and you know that," Cassandra replied gently, giving my arm a reassuring pat. "But new girlfriends don't always understand our pack dynamic. Some people can be a little sensitive about where they rank. Just keep it in mind to keep the peace, okay?"

I nodded, accepting her logistical advice.

Casey abandoned her sofa, dragging her blanket with her, and climbed up onto Cassandra's couch. She patted the space right between them. I happily obliged, slotting myself dead center on the cushions. The seating geometry was a tight, logistically perfect squeeze. Casey pressed her side flush against my ribs, throwing her arm over my chest, while Cassandra leaned against my other shoulder, wrapping her arm around my back.

Cassandra's lightweight, manual travel wheelchair was folded neatly out of the way against the far wall, leaving us completely enclosed in our own perimeter. I was perfectly sandwiched between them, fully enveloped in the deep, restrictive pressure I had been starved of for months. We just sat there for a while, holding each other tightly, desperate to feel like a complete family again.

Cassandra reached for the television remote on the wooden end table, navigating the channels until she found the live broadcast.

"Perfect," Cassandra announced as the brightly colored marching bands and massive balloons filled the screen. "We are right on time for the Thanksgiving Day Parade."

We settled in to watch the broadcast, the comfortable, easy banter of my childhood rushing back into my system. We were pointing out the massive character balloons and critiquing the marching bands when the hallway floorboards creaked.

I looked up. Kristine was standing at the edge of the living room, wearing her robe.

Her eyes immediately locked onto the sofa. I saw her gaze track the way Casey’s arm was draped across my chest, and the way my head was resting near Cassandra’s shoulder. For a fraction of a second, a sharp, undeniable frown pulled at the corners of Kristine's mouth. Her posture went rigid.

But just as quickly as the micro-expression appeared, it vanished. She smoothed her features into a polite, accommodating smile, effortlessly masking the sudden shift in her baseline.

"Good morning, everyone," Kristine said, walking over to the small kitchenette to grab bowls from the cabinet. "Happy Thanksgiving."

"Morning," I smiled back, completely miscategorizing her brief frown as morning grogginess. I gently untangled myself from my sisters just enough to sit up straight, giving Kristine her space as the host.

Kristine brought a large box of generic cereal into the living room, setting it on the end table along with four bowls and spoons.

Casey immediately started to unspool herself from my side. "I can grab the milk from the fridge," she offered eagerly, ready to help with the breakfast logistics.

"I can reach it if you want to stay seated, Casey," Cassandra chimed in, leaning forward. "My travel chair is right there against the wall."

"Oh, we don't have any," Kristine replied casually, pouring the dry cereal into her bowl. "I'm lactose intolerant. We just eat it dry."

"Ah. Okay, cool. Dry cereal it is," Cassandra nodded politely, accepting her bowl without a second thought.

I took my own bowl, crunching on the dry squares. My analytical brain logged the dietary parameter instantly. It made perfect, logical sense; if the primary resident couldn't process dairy, there was no reason to waste money stocking it.

Kristine sat down in the armchair across from the sofas, eating her dry cereal while we turned our attention back to the television.

"Look at the scale of that float," Casey rasped happily, pointing at a massive, glittering pirate ship rolling down the streets. "The structural engineering required to balance that on a single chassis is incredible."

"I just want to know how they navigate the streetlights with the giant balloons," I added, laughing. "You know somebody has to be doing the wind-shear math."

"Kristine, have you ever been up to the city to see the parade in person?" Cassandra asked, making a deliberate, polite effort to pull my girlfriend into the pack's conversation.

Kristine took a slow bite of her cereal, keeping her eyes fixed on the television screen. "No. Too crowded."

"Yeah, the crowds would probably be a nightmare," I agreed, trying to bridge the gap. "But the spectacle must be amazing."

Kristine just offered a terse, non-committal shrug, refusing to engage with the topic any further.

I leaned back against the sofa cushions, feeling a vague, confusing friction settling over the room. We tried a few more times to pull her into our commentary about the parade performances, but her answers remained clipped and distinctly bothered. I couldn't figure out the social math. My sisters were being perfectly friendly, and I was doing my best to be an attentive partner, but Kristine was radiating a quiet, simmering annoyance.

## 2:00 PM — The Kitchen Ambush

The comfortable sanctuary of the living room held steady into the early afternoon. By two o'clock, the parade broadcast had ended, but the three of us remained anchored to the sofa, happily chatting and enjoying the rare, uninterrupted proximity of our pack.

The layout of the Portsmouth apartment was compact and highly compartmentalized. If you were walking down the main hallway from the bedrooms, the small kitchen sat immediately to the right, sectioned off by a mid-height divider wall. The back of our long sofa ran directly parallel to that walkway, acting as a physical barricade that defined the living room space to the left.

The floorboards in the hallway creaked.

I assumed Kristine was finally coming out to start prepping the massive Thanksgiving meal. I didn't turn my head immediately, but I felt Cassandra suddenly go completely rigid beside me.

Cassandra looked up, her dark eyes tracking Kristine’s movement from the hallway into the kitchen space. I turned my head to see what had triggered my sister’s reaction.

Kristine was standing in the kitchen. Because of the height of the divider wall and the back of our sofa, her lower half was obscured, but from the waist up, she was completely, undeniably naked.

"It gets suffocatingly hot in this tiny kitchen when the oven is running all afternoon," Kristine announced casually, pulling a heavy roasting pan onto the counter. "This way I don't overheat while I cook."

I sat frozen on the cushions, my processor violently stalling.

Normally, this is where Cassandra’s corporate diplomacy would engage. But as Casey shrank back into the sofa, trembling and burying her face into my bicep, Cassandra’s patience instantly evaporated. The polite, accommodating guest vanished, entirely replaced by the furious, protective guardian.

"Put your clothes on," Cassandra ordered. She didn't yell. Her voice was terrifyingly calm, dropping to a glacial, absolute zero that cut straight through the ambient noise of the apartment.

Kristine paused, her hands resting on the counter. She blinked, taken aback by the sheer, unvarnished authority. "Excuse me? It's my house, Cassandra. I should be able to—"

"I do not care whose name is on the lease," Cassandra interrupted, her tone sharpening into a blade. "My sister is seventeen years old. You are parading around without clothes on in front of a minor. It is not just wildly inappropriate; it is entirely unacceptable." 

Cassandra shook her head in sheer disbelief. "I can only imagine the absolute tactical meltdown Liz and the Delaney women would have if they were here to see this."

"Put your clothes on right now," she finished, leaving absolutely zero room for negotiation. 
The kitchen descended into a radioactive silence. Kristine's face flushed a deep, furious red. She had expected shock or embarrassment; she hadn't calculated being dressed down like a subordinate who had just committed a massive compliance violation.

Kristine slammed the roasting pan onto the counter, marching back down the hallway to her bedroom and returning a moment later with her heavy robe tied aggressively around her waist.

"You have no right to come into my home and tell me—" Kristine started, her voice raising into a hostile shout.

"David," Cassandra said, completely ignoring Kristine and turning her focus entirely to me. Her voice softened slightly, but the executive command remained. "Go to the bedroom. Pack your bag. Pack all of it."

My stomach dropped into a freefall. "Cass, wait—"

"You are not in trouble, David," Cassandra promised fiercely, looking me dead in the eye. "I am not mad at you. But I am Casey's guardian on this trip, and I am officially pulling the plug on this environment. We are leaving. Right now. Go pack your bag."

My threat-detection system was screaming, but Cassandra’s unyielding momentum bypassed my paralysis. I scrambled off the sofa and hurried down the hall, shoving my clothes into my heavy nylon backpack with shaking hands.

When I emerged, Cassandra was already sitting in her manual wheelchair, her messenger bag on her lap. Casey was standing right behind her, clutching her duffel bag, looking deeply relieved to be standing behind her older sister's shield.

Cassandra had her silver cell phone pressed to her ear. She hadn't dialed a taxi dispatcher. She had dialed the only local number that mattered. 

"Meredith," Cassandra said, her voice dropping into a crisp, authoritative register as the call connected. "We have a critical perimeter breach at the Gateway Drive apartment. The environment is actively toxic. I am pulling the plug on the operation. We need an immediate exfiltration."

I couldn't hear Meredith's exact words through the tiny earpiece, but I could clearly hear the sharp, instantly protective tone of her voice. A second later, Morgan’s voice joined the line—Meredith had clearly put the phone on speaker. 

"Understood," Cassandra replied, her shoulders relaxing slightly. "Gateway Drive. We will be waiting on the curb. Thank you."

She snapped the phone shut. 

Kristine realized with sudden, catastrophic clarity that her power play had just cost her everything. The anger vanished, instantly replaced by sheer, weeping desperation. She lunged forward, trying to grab my arm.

"David, please!" Kristine sobbed, the crocodile tears welling up instantly. "I was just hot! Don't leave me, please, I'm sorry!"

"Do not touch him," Cassandra warned, wheeling her manual chair directly into Kristine's path, cutting off her physical access to me.

"You can't take him!" Kristine cried, glaring down at Cassandra. "He lives here! He's my boyfriend!"

"He is my brother," Cassandra fired back, her dark eyes flashing with a terrifying, protective fury. "And you have spent the last twenty-four hours trying to isolate him from his family and completely disregard our basic boundaries. But here is the truth you are terrified of: his pack loves him. Unconditionally. And he is going to be with his pack."

Cassandra wheeled herself backward toward the front door, keeping herself between Kristine and us. She looked at Kristine, delivering the final, fatal logistical blow.

"And while we wait for the rescue transport," Cassandra added coldly, "I am calling the airline. Because when Casey and I fly back to Seattle on Sunday, I am buying David a ticket, and he is getting on that plane with us."

Kristine let out a dramatic, heartbroken wail, burying her face in her hands. My chest was tight with panic, the guilt threatening to drag me back, but Cassandra didn't give me a single microsecond to hesitate.

"Out the door. Now," Cassandra commanded gently but firmly.

Casey grabbed my hand, her grip incredibly strong, and physically pulled me over the threshold. The three of us exited the apartment, the heavy front door clicking shut on Kristine's sobs.

We stood on the concrete curb in the crisp November air. My heart was hammering against my ribs, terrified of the massive shift in reality. I had just lost the milestone. I was going to Washington State.

Cassandra engaged the brakes on her chair. She reached out and wrapped her hand warmly around my wrist, grounding me.

"Take a breath, David," Cassandra said, her tone finally softening back into the sister I knew. "I know that was scary. But I will *never* let anyone treat you, or our family, like that. Meredith and Morgan are bringing the minivan. You are safe now."

## 2:30 PM — The Hard Sever

The cold November wind whipped across the concrete curb, biting through my thin jacket.

I was crying, the tears hot and fast against my freezing cheeks. My chest heaved with a devastating, confusing grief. My processor was in a total tailspin. I wasn't just losing a girlfriend; in my trauma-wired nineteen-year-old brain, I was losing the ultimate adult milestone. I felt like a catastrophic failure, being dragged away from my own life because I couldn't keep the peace.

Casey was pressed tight against my side, her fingers gripping the fabric of my sleeve like a vice. She was crying, too, her breath hitching in her throat. She wasn't mourning a relationship; she was terrified. The sheer, unpredictable hostility of Kristine's naked territorial display in the kitchen had completely shattered Casey's sense of safety, and she was clinging to me for deep-pressure grounding.

I wrapped my arm around her shoulder, pulling her close, crying into the top of her head.

The heavy front door of the apartment building suddenly swung open.

Kristine rushed out into the cold. She was fully dressed now, having thrown on a pair of jeans and a heavy sweater. Her face was streaked with massive, welling tears. She looked like the ultimate picture of a heartbroken, misunderstood victim.

"David!" Kristine sobbed, rushing down the concrete walkway toward us. "David, please, just listen to me! I'm so sorry, I didn't mean to scare anyone! You can't just leave me on Thanksgiving!"

My threat-detection system faltered. The tears looked so real, and my deeply ingrained instinct to comfort her immediately flared up. I took a half-step forward.

Cassandra intercepted her instantly.

She wheeled her manual chair directly into the space between the walkway and the curb, creating an impenetrable physical barrier.

"Back away," Cassandra ordered, her voice cracking like a whip in the cold air.

"I just want to talk to him!" Kristine cried, trying to step around the chair, the crocodile tears flowing freely. "He's my boyfriend! You are ruining our relationship over a misunderstanding!"

Before Cassandra could fire back, a massive, dark-blue passenger minivan came roaring around the corner of the apartment complex. It slammed to a halt directly against the curb, completely blocking the street.

The heavy sliding doors and the front passenger doors flew open simultaneously. 

Meredith and Morgan bounded out of the vehicle like a highly synchronized strike team. The first thing Kristine’s eyes darted to, with immediate, calculating jealousy, was their physical build—the absurdly massive chests sitting on their otherwise slender, athletic frames. But as I looked at the identical twins, their waist-length, natural sun-kissed ash blonde hair whipping in the wind, I didn't see an intimidation tactic. 

I just saw my pack. 

They completely ignored Kristine. Meredith zeroed in on Cassandra, immediately stepping to the rear of the chair to assist with the transfer, while Morgan marched straight up to me on the curb. 

Morgan didn't hesitate. She threw her arms around my neck, pulled me into a fierce, protective hug, and kissed me squarely on the lips right in front of Kristine. 

"Get your bags in the back," Morgan ordered softly, pulling back just enough to look me in the eye. "We're going home."

Kristine stood completely frozen on the walkway, her jaw practically on the concrete. Her brain short-circuited trying to process the sheer audacity of two absolutely gorgeous women showing up in a minivan, entirely disregarding her authority, and kissing her boyfriend. 

"You can't just take him!" Kristine finally shrieked, taking a step toward the minivan. "He lives here!"

Meredith turned away from loading Cassandra’s messenger bag and looked Kristine dead in the eye.

"His name isn't on your lease, and his driver's license still says Virginia Beach," Meredith said, her voice dropping into a tone of absolute, terrifying finality. "He belongs with us."

"Delete his number," Cassandra told Kristine, securing herself in the passenger seat of the minivan. "And do not ever call him again."

Meredith smoothly loaded Cassandra's manual chair into the back while Morgan guided Casey and me into the middle row of seats. The heavy sliding door slammed shut, instantly severing the cold wind and the chaotic noise of the street. 

I sat sandwiched safely next to Casey, who immediately rested her head on my shoulder. I looked out the tinted window as the minivan accelerated away from the curb. Kristine was left standing completely alone in the cold November afternoon, growing smaller and smaller in the rearview mirror until Meredith turned the corner, leaving her entirely in the dust.
