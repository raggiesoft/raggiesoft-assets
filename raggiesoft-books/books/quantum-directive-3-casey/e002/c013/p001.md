## 5:30 PM — The Social Boundary

By the time the Quantum van finally escaped the gridlock of Pacific Highway and pulled up to the Federal Way apartment complex, the winter sun had completely set. We hauled our luggage out of the vehicle, thanked the driver, and took the elevator up to Cassandra's floor.

The moment the front door unlocked and our heavy travel bags were dropped onto the living room floor, Casey’s excitement reached a boiling point. She didn't even take her winter coat off. She simply grabbed my hand, her grip incredibly tight, and physically dragged me down the short hallway.

"Come here, you have to see my room!" Casey insisted, pulling me through the second doorway.

I stumbled in behind her, laughing as she dramatically gestured to her space. It was a perfectly organized, cozy room that clearly belonged to a seventeen-year-old, but the centerpiece was a massive, incredibly comfortable-looking queen-sized bed.

"Look at how much space there is!" Casey beamed, dropping my hand and bouncing backward onto the mattress. She looked up at me, her dark eyes shining with the pure, unadulterated hope of having her safe person back in her daily routine. "David, you should just sleep in here with me tonight. It'll be exactly like when we were kids. We can just pile up all the blankets and you can hold me."

Before I could even process the logistics of the apartment's sleeping arrangements, the quiet hum of rubber tires on the carpet signaled Cassandra's arrival.

Cass wheeled herself into the doorway, with Liz stepping in right behind her. Cass’s expression was incredibly gentle, but her executive baseline was fully engaged.

"He can't do that, Case," Cassandra said softly, parking her manual chair in the doorframe.

Casey’s face immediately fell, her brow furrowing in confusion. "Why not? The bed is huge. He fits."

"It has nothing to do with the square footage," Cassandra explained, navigating the emotional landmine with absolute precision. "It has to do with how the outside world interprets things, Casey. I personally have absolutely no issue with you two sharing a bed. Even Liz is entirely okay with it. We all know there is absolutely nothing inappropriate about you needing deep-pressure comfort from your brother."

Cassandra wheeled forward just an inch, making sure she had Casey's complete understanding.

"But you are seventeen years old, and David is a nineteen-year-old legal adult. If someone outside this family were to find out, they might assume inappropriate things are happening. We cannot risk anyone misunderstanding the situation and causing trouble for David or for you. It's just a social boundary we have to respect for now."

Casey stared at Cassandra, the heavy reality of the outside world's perception sinking in. She understood the math. The perimeter had to remain absolutely flawless to protect David.

Liz stepped around Cassandra's chair, walking over to the edge of the queen bed. She sat down next to Casey, wrapping an arm warmly around my little sister's shoulders.

"But here is the best part about the calendar," Liz smiled, bumping her shoulder gently against Casey's. "You were born on May 25th, 1987. Which means that on May 25th of this coming year, you are officially turning eighteen. The absolute second you are a legal adult, no one can say a word about it. Those rules will no longer matter. Until then, we just have to play it safe."

Casey took a deep breath, processing the data, and finally gave a small, resolute nod. "Okay. May 25th."

## 6:15 PM — Comfort Food and The Ledger

Casey eagerly grabbed one end of the heavy sofa cushions while Liz took the other. Together, we dragged the mattress frame out from the depths of the couch. The metal springs groaned, unfolding to take up nearly the entire footprint of the small living room.

"I think we just leave this out permanently," I suggested, tossing a heavy blanket over the mattress. "It’s going to be a tight squeeze in this apartment for the next few weeks, but it saves us having to fold it up every single morning."

"Agreed," Liz smiled, smoothing the sheets down. "We'll see how apartment living goes. I honestly don't care if it takes up the whole floor, as long as we're all together."

Right on cue, Cassandra wheeled herself out of the kitchen, carefully balancing a tray on her lap.

"Dinner is served," she announced, a warm, genuine smile breaking across her face.

She handed out massive, steaming bowls of the ultimate childhood comfort food: thick macaroni and cheese mixed with sliced, cheesy hot dogs. But it was the drinks she passed out next that made my processor stall for a fraction of a second. She handed me a tall, ice-cold glass of whole milk.

For the last three months, Kristine’s lactose intolerance had dictated every item in the pantry, forcing me to eat dry cereal and completely eliminating milk from my diet. Sitting on the edge of the pulled-out sofa bed, holding a glass of milk and a bowl of mac and cheese, the stark contrast between Kristine’s restrictive control and my sister's unconditional hospitality hit me like a wave. I took a long drink, feeling a profound sense of relief wash over my system.

We all sat cross-legged on the edge of the mattress and the floor, eating in comfortable, easy silence for a few minutes before Cassandra shifted into her logistical baseline.

"Now that we are all physically consolidated, we need to update the financial architecture," Cassandra explained, setting her bowl on the end table. She looked directly at me. "David, I am officially stopping the ACH transfers."

I blinked, momentarily surprised. While I had been isolated in Virginia, she had been using Quantum's internal payroll portal to automatically route a portion of her pay directly into my checking account via ACH transfer.

"I am not cutting off your support," Cassandra clarified instantly, sensing my momentary confusion. "This is strictly a maneuver to bypass the IRS. Because the annual total I was sending you exceeded the IRS gift tax exclusion limit for 2005, my CPA firm had to silently file Form 709 in the background. Every dollar over the limit deducts the excess from my lifetime exemption. I refuse to needlessly burn through that cap."

"So how does it work now?" Liz asked, genuinely fascinated by how Cassandra treated the federal tax code like a solvable software bug.

"Now that David is living under my roof, the legal classification changes," Cass explained smoothly. "I will simply absorb your daily living expenses directly at the point of sale. However, I am not always going to be at your side. You and Liz will be on the NPU campus taking classes, and you will need to buy lunch, supplies, and your monthly TAPS Passes."

Cassandra pulled two sleek, metallic cards from her pocket and set them on the table.

"I have already ordered authorized user cards in both of your names," Cass stated. "They are linked directly to my primary accounts. If you need something, you swipe the card. It bypasses the gift tax entirely and ensures you maintain total operational autonomy when I am at work."

"Thank you, Cass," I said softly, completely overwhelmed by the sheer, friction-less safety net she had engineered for us.

"Speaking of school," Casey chimed in proudly, practically vibrating with excitement as she scooped up a bite of hot dogs. "I got my progress reports back yesterday. Straight A's in every single class. My calculus teacher said I have the highest average in the district."

"That is incredible, Case!" Liz beamed, reaching over to give Casey a high-five. "You are going to completely destroy college next year."

"I expect nothing less," Cass smiled, radiating absolute pride for our younger sister.

"What about you, Cass?" I asked, leaning back against the sofa cushions. "What's going on at Quantum? Any big deployments?"

Cassandra, who works in Building 33 alongside Quantum's founder William Keswick, let out a rare, exasperated sigh and rolled her eyes.

"The deployments are fine," she dismissed, waving her hand. "It is the internal rumor mill that is currently malfunctioning. Half the executive suite is whispering that Keswick is secretly grooming me to be the next CEO of Quantum."

Liz choked slightly on her milk, her eyes going wide. "Are they serious?"

"They are, but I refuse to believe it," Cass stated flatly, shaking her head at the sheer mathematical improbability of the gossip. "I am twenty-six years old. I have only been with the company for four years. The idea that the board of directors would hand the keys to the largest tech empire on the planet to someone in her twenties is completely absurd. They are just rumors."

I watched my older sister brush off the idea with pure, pragmatic logic. She might not have believed it, but as I looked at the flawlessly executed perimeter she had built around our pack—navigating family law, federal tax codes, and cross-country logistics without breaking a sweat—I knew exactly why William Keswick was watching her. If anyone was capable of running the empire, it was Cassandra Vance.

## 8:30 PM — The Evening Routine

By the time the dinner dishes were cleared from the kitchen island, a heavy, comfortable exhaustion had settled over the apartment. The adrenaline of the cross-country flight and the sheer relief of our consolidation had finally run its course.

"Alright," Cassandra announced, wheeling herself away from the kitchen counter. "Let's initiate the evening routine."

The Federal Way apartment was efficiently laid out, but space was absolutely at a premium. There were exactly two bedrooms and two bathrooms. Cassandra’s master suite sat at the end of the hall, boasting a massive king-sized bed and a heavily modified ensuite bathroom. Casey’s room, with the large queen-sized bed she was so eager to share, sat adjacent to the standard hallway bathroom, which featured a standard sink, toilet, and a bath-and-shower combo unit.

Casey lingered in the hallway, looking longingly from her bedroom door to the living room where my duffel bag sat on the pull-out sofa.

"I know it feels arbitrary, Case," I said gently, stepping over to give her a quick hug. "But the rules are absolute. Just until you turn eighteen."

"May 25th," Casey recited, giving a small, resilient nod. "I know. No loopholes for Tom."

"Exactly," Cassandra agreed, smoothly establishing the logistical boundaries for the night. "To ensure the perimeter remains legally and visually flawless, Casey will use her bedroom and the hallway bathroom to change. David and Liz, grab your bags and come into the master suite with me. We keep the sightlines entirely separated."

Casey grabbed her pajamas and disappeared into her room, clicking the door shut.

Liz and I hoisted our duffel bags and followed Cassandra down the hall into the master bedroom. The space was dominated by the sprawling king-sized mattress.

"You two are more than welcome to share this bed with me if the living room sofa gets too uncomfortable," Cass offered, parking her chair near the doorway of her ensuite bathroom. "There is plenty of square footage."

"We'll be fine in the living room for tonight," Liz smiled warmly, setting our bags on the edge of the mattress. "But we might take you up on that tomorrow. We're just happy to be here."

Cassandra offered a grateful nod before looking over at me. Her executive mask dropped completely, leaving only the practical, unfiltered reality of our sibling dynamic.

"David, I could use your help with the bathing and toileting tonight, if you don't mind," Cass asked quietly.

"Of course," I nodded immediately, stepping into the ensuite bathroom behind her.

Liz remained in the bedroom to unpack our sleepwear, entirely unfazed. She was a fully integrated member of the pack, and she understood that my physical presence was a functional asset to my sisters, not just an emotional one.

Cassandra’s bathroom was a masterpiece of accessibility, retrofitted entirely to accommodate the permanent reality of her 1999 axial load injury. The space featured a custom roll-under sink, heavy-duty grab bars bolted directly into the wall studs next to the toilet, and an oversized shower stall equipped with a sturdy transfer bench.

We had executed this routine dozens of times over the years. Because the hollow, pinned-together remnants of her heels and ankles simply could not support the weight of her body without catastrophic failure, transferring from the chair to the shower bench required immense upper-body strength and careful leverage. I acted as her physical anchor. I braced her chair, offered my arms for stability, and provided the secure, unyielding support she needed to safely navigate the transfers without exhausting her own system.

It was a quiet, highly coordinated process built on absolute trust. I handed her the towels, secured her chair, and made sure she was completely safely situated before stepping out to give her privacy.

Twenty minutes later, the entire apartment had successfully transitioned.

The four of us emerged back into the living room, all dressed in comfortable, oversized pajamas. The heavy winter rain was still drumming a steady rhythm against the apartment windows, but inside, the thermal envelope was perfect.

Liz and I climbed onto the sprawling pull-out sofa bed, pulling the heavy blankets over our laps. Casey immediately abandoned her bedroom, practically diving onto the mattress next to me. She curled her legs up, pressing her side firmly against my ribs, instantly locking into her deep-pressure baseline. Cassandra wheeled her chair right up to the edge of the mattress, resting her hand lightly on my knee to complete the circuit.

I grabbed the television remote and flipped the screen on, the ambient glow washing over the dark living room. We didn't really care what was playing. For the first time in months, my processor was completely silent. The perimeter was secure, the legal boundaries were flawless, and the entire pack was finally anchored exactly where we belonged.

## 9:30 PM — The Blackout

By 9:30 PM, the quiet hum of the television was the only light source in the living room.

Outside, the rain had been drumming a steady, predictable rhythm against the apartment windows. At first, it was just the standard, comforting drizzle of the Pacific Northwest winter. But within minutes, the atmospheric pressure violently shifted. The gentle tapping rapidly escalated into a deafening, torrential downpour that violently battered the glass.

The sudden, intense spike in auditory sensory input caused my processor to instantly tense. Beside me on the sofa bed, Casey flinched, her grip tightening on my arm as the roar of the wind rattling the window panes grew significantly louder.

Then, a massive, concussive crash echoed from the street outside, followed immediately by the sharp, electric crack of a transformer exploding.

The television died instantly. The apartment plunged into absolute, pitch-black darkness.

The sudden, violent sensory overload and the total loss of visual data pushed Casey over the edge. She let out a sharp, terrified gasp, burying her face into my chest and immediately starting to cry. Her system simply couldn't process the sudden chaos, and she shook violently against my ribs.

"I've got you, Case, it's okay," I whispered rapidly, wrapping both of my arms around her trembling shoulders, trying to provide as much deep-pressure grounding as I could in the dark.

"Nobody panic," Cassandra’s voice rang out. It was perfectly steady, cutting through the ambient roar of the storm with absolute executive authority. "It's just a blown transformer."

"It's so loud," Casey sobbed into my shirt, terrified by the sheer volume of the storm outside.

Cassandra didn't hesitate. In a crisis, standard operational protocols were immediately overridden by the prime directive: the absolute physical and emotional security of the pack.

"Safety comes first, and we are staying together," Cassandra commanded in the darkness. "Everyone into my bedroom. Right now."

Liz used the faint backlight of her cell phone to illuminate the floor. We carefully untangled ourselves from the sofa bed blankets. I kept one arm securely wrapped around Casey, guiding her down the dark hallway while Liz illuminated the path for Cassandra’s chair.

We filed into the master suite, the sprawling king-sized mattress waiting in the center of the room.

I sat down near the center of the bed, pulling Casey up right beside me. Liz climbed in on my other side, while Cassandra smoothly locked the brakes on her chair and transferred herself onto the edge of the mattress. Within seconds, all four of us were piled onto the massive bed, enveloped in the heavy wool blankets.

Casey was still crying softly, clinging to my arm. Cassandra reached out in the dim light, resting her hand firmly on Casey’s shoulder.

"Casey, listen to me," Cassandra said gently, her voice an anchor in the dark room. "This is an emergency protocol. We are doing this tonight because the power grid failed and safety comes first. But I need you to understand that this is an exception."

Casey sniffled, looking up at our older sister.

"This will not become the norm," Cassandra reminded her firmly, maintaining the absolute legal boundary required to keep our father away from us. "The rules still apply. We are only doing this tonight because of the storm."

Casey wiped her wet cheeks with the back of her hand. She was sad, her system still craving the routine guarantee of always having this proximity, but her logical brain processed the math. She knew Cassandra wasn't rejecting her; she was protecting the perimeter from Tom's vindictive legal threats.

"I understand," Casey cried softly, giving a small, resilient nod. "Just for tonight."

"Just for tonight," Cassandra agreed, her voice softening into pure affection. She leaned back against the headboard, settling into the mattress. "Now come here."

The pack completely consolidated. Casey pressed her back flush against my chest, her breathing hitching as the tears slowly subsided. I wrapped my arms securely around her, while Liz curled up tightly against my back. Cassandra rested against my shoulder, completing the circuit.

The weight and proximity of all four of us acting as a single, grounded unit provided the ultimate deep-pressure regulation. The chaos of the outside world simply ceased to exist.

Outside the bedroom walls, the wind howled and the Pacific Northwest rain battered the glass harder and heavier than before. But inside the dark apartment, entirely insulated by the unconditional love of the pack, the ambient noise faded into a steady lullaby. Surrounded by my sisters and my best friend, my processor finally went quiet, and we slowly drifted off to sleep.
