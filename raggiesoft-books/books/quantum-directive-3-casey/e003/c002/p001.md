## 2:15 PM — The Sea-Tac Crush-Load

The arrivals terminal at Sea-Tac International Airport was a chaotic blur of rolling suitcases, tired business travelers, and families reuniting. But standing near Baggage Carousel 4, our local perimeter was completely impenetrable. 

I was leaning casually against the metal railing, a massive cup of coffee in my hand. Liz was tucked securely under my arm, leaning against my side. Casey was practically vibrating with nervous energy, pacing back and forth in front of us, checking her flip phone every thirty seconds. 

Cassandra sat parked slightly to my right, her manual wheelchair positioned to give her a clear, unobstructed tactical view of the escalators descending from the secure concourse. She was wearing a perfectly tailored dark trench coat, radiating her usual executive authority, completely unfazed by the dense airport crowds flowing around her. 

"The flight landed twenty minutes ago," Casey groaned, aggressively flipping her phone open and shut. "How long does it take to walk down a concourse?"

"They are traveling with Kate and David Delaney, Casey," Cassandra reminded her patiently, her dark eyes scanning the escalators. "It takes time to deplane two individuals who require wheelchair transfers, especially when dealing with commercial airline protocols. Have patience."

"I don't have patience, I want my pack!" Casey complained, crossing her arms.

"They're coming, Case," Liz laughed, squeezing my waist. 

A few moments later, a massive surge of noise began filtering down from the top of the escalators. It wasn't the usual murmur of tired travelers; it was a loud, overlapping, undeniably East Coast wave of chaos. 

"I see them," Cassandra announced smoothly, leaning forward in her chair. 

I looked up. Descending the escalators was a veritable army. 

Meredith and Morgan hit the ground floor first, wearing matching dark winter coats, their identical waist-length blonde hair turning heads across the terminal. Behind them, Kate and David Delaney rolled off the adjacent elevators, flanked by Wendy and Tom. 

And directly behind the Virginia Beach crew was the Rhode Island household. Megan, Erin, Chloe, and Claire cascaded down the escalator steps, loudly arguing about the lack of legroom on the PAC 757, hauling an absolute mountain of carry-on bags. 

"DAVID!" Erin screamed, her voice completely overriding the ambient noise of the entire baggage claim area. 

The entire pack zeroed in on our position. 

Before I could even set my coffee cup down, the perimeter collapsed. The twins hit me first, hitting my chest with the force of a tactical strike and wrapping their arms around my neck. Casey shrieked with joy, immediately diving into a massive hug with Chloe and Claire. 

"You survived!" Megan yelled, practically throwing her duffel bag onto the floor and joining the huddle. 

"I survived!" I laughed, completely buried under a mountain of blonde and brunette hair, inhaling the familiar scents of the East Coast pack. The sheer volume of deep-pressure contact instantly reset my baseline. For the first time since Thanksgiving, the phantom ache of geographic separation vanished. The architecture was whole again. 

<aac>Coast to coast,</aac> David Delaney’s AAC device announced cheerfully as he rolled up to the edge of the pile. 

<aac>Seattle is wet,</aac> Kate’s AAC added, looking toward the massive glass windows that displayed the standard Pacific Northwest drizzle. 

"Welcome to Washington," Cassandra smiled, reaching out to grasp Kate's hand. 

"Cassandra, it is so good to see you," Wendy said, stepping over the chaotic pile of hugging twenty-somethings to greet my sister. 

"How are we moving all these people?" Morgan asked, finally stepping back and assessing the sheer volume of bodies and luggage. "There are fourteen of us. Do we need to rent a bus?"

Cassandra offered a small, calculating smirk and reached into her trench coat pocket, pulling out three heavy, paper-wrapped rolls of quarters. 

"We do not need to rent a bus, Morgan, because Washington State already has an excellent public transit infrastructure," Cassandra stated smoothly, unlocking the brakes on her manual chair. "We are taking TAPS King County Route 194 directly into Downtown Seattle."

"Are we going to your new house?" Erin asked, her eyes going wide. "Wait, you actually bought it?"

"I closed on the Redmond property on Tuesday," Cassandra confirmed, her tone completely matter-of-fact. "It's an average-sized single-family home, but it is exactly three blocks from my office at Quantum Headquarters. However, it is currently completely gutted. My contractors are retrofitting the entire first floor to be ADA compliant, installing ramps, and lowering the kitchen counters. It will not be habitable for another month."

"And my tiny apartment in Federal Way only has one bathroom," Casey chimed in. "We can barely fit four people in it, let alone fourteen."

"Exactly," Cassandra said. "Which is why I rented out an entire block of suites at the Cascade Crest Hotel in downtown Seattle for the week. There is a bed for every single person standing in this terminal. Grab your bags. The bus leaves from the lower drive in fifteen minutes."

As the pack mobilized, grabbing suitcases and duffel bags from the carousel, I walked alongside Cassandra as she wheeled toward the elevators. 

"Cass, wait," I said, doing the math in my head. "The buses on the TAPS routes only have two wheelchair tie-down bays. We have three chairs."

Cassandra looked up at me, entirely unbothered. "I am aware, David. Kate and David Delaney are our guests, and they just flew across the country. They get priority docking."

"So what are you going to do?" Liz asked, walking up on her other side. 

"I am going to execute a completely standard physical transfer into a regular bus seat," Cassandra replied effortlessly. "David will pop the quick-release wheels off my chair, fold the frame, and stow it in the luggage rack or hold it in the aisle. And when we board, I will pay the exact fare for all fourteen of us in quarters, and I will request fourteen paper transfer slips from the operator, because TAPS does not issue day passes."

She smiled, radiating total operational control. "I have it entirely handled. Let's go."

## 2:45 PM — The Route 194 Deployment

The lower transit drive at Sea-Tac Airport was bustling with travelers, but our pack completely dominated the sidewalk. Moving fourteen people and a small mountain of luggage required military precision, but the East Coast household was used to operating as a single, highly coordinated unit. 

The teal, gold, and white TAPS King County Route 194 bus pulled up to the curb with a heavy hiss of air brakes. The front doors folded open, but the operator didn't immediately lower the hydraulic ramp. Instead, his eyes widened as he looked out at our massive group and the three wheelchairs waiting on the sidewalk. 

He leaned out of his seat, looking down at us apologetically. "I'm sorry, folks. I can take your group, but I only have two wheelchair tie-down bays on this bus. It's a federal safety regulation. I can't have a third chair blocking the aisle. You'll have to split up and wait for the next bus."

Cassandra rolled forward, positioning herself right at the edge of the curb near the open doors. 

"I am entirely aware of the TAPS operational guidelines," Cassandra stated smoothly, projecting her voice so the operator could hear her clearly over the idling engine. "I will be executing a physical transfer into a standard priority seat, and my brother will fold and stow my manual chair frame in the aisle. The two tie-down bays will be exclusively utilized by the other two chairs. We are not splitting up."

The operator blinked, processing the logistical solution, then offered a relieved nod. "Alright, if you can transfer and fold it, come on aboard."

He hit the pneumatic switch, and the heavy hydraulic ramp finally folded out onto the concrete. 

Cassandra rolled up the ramp first, immediately maneuvering her manual chair next to the priority seating at the front of the bus. She locked her brakes, planted her feet, and executed a flawless, perfectly controlled physical transfer into the closest aisle seat. 

I stepped onto the bus right behind her, dropping my backpack. Within five seconds, I popped the quick-release wheels off Cassandra's manual chair, folded the ultra-light titanium frame in half, and tucked the entire assembly securely between my legs as I sat down next to her. 

The operator blinked, clearly impressed by the sheer speed of the maneuver. "Well, that works. Fares?"

Cassandra reached into her coat and dropped the heavy rolls of quarters directly into the farebox hopper. The machine loudly chugged as it processed the exact change for fourteen adults. 

"I will also need fourteen paper transfer slips, please," Cassandra requested formally. 

The operator nodded, tearing off a thick stack of paper transfers and handing them to her as Kate and David Delaney successfully rolled up the ramp and secured themselves into the two designated tie-down bays. 

The rest of the pack swarmed the bus, dragging suitcases down the aisle and taking up nearly every available seat in the front half of the vehicle. Casey sat across from me, bouncing her leg with excitement, while Meredith and Morgan took the seats directly behind us, loudly arguing about the Seattle weather. 

"So, where exactly are we getting off?" Liz asked, holding onto the grab rail as the bus merged onto Interstate 5, speeding north toward the city skyline. 

"Westlake Station," Cassandra answered, handing the stack of paper transfers to Megan to distribute. "Route 194 utilizes the Downtown Seattle Transit Tunnel. It is a fully grade-separated subterranean route, which bypasses all surface traffic."

"Why Westlake?" Erin asked from a few rows back. "I looked at a map on the plane. Pioneer Square is closer to the waterfront."

"Because Pioneer Square is built on a massive, unforgiving hill," I explained, leaning back in my seat. "You try pushing a mountain of luggage up a forty-five-degree incline while navigating historic cobblestones. Westlake is in the retail core. It’s significantly flatter."

"David is correct," Cassandra nodded. "The Cascade Crest Hotel is entirely accessible from the Westlake Station surface elevators. It requires exactly two blocks of flat, paved travel. We do not do hills unless operationally necessary."

The bus plunged into the Downtown Seattle Transit Tunnel, the roar of the diesel engine shifting as the dual-mode systems adjusted. Since it was March 2005, the tunnel was still fully open to bus traffic, completely insulated from the heavy rain that had just started to fall on the streets above. 

When the automated voice announced Westlake Station, our massive pack mobilized. I quickly reassembled Cassandra's chair in the aisle, allowing her to transfer seamlessly back into her seat. As we filed off the bus and onto the subterranean platform, I noticed a series of bright yellow transit placards plastered across the tiled walls, warning riders that the entire tunnel would be shutting down in September for a massive, two-year light rail track retrofit.

We deployed across the platform in a highly organized wave. We commandeered the station elevator, taking two trips to get all fourteen people and three wheelchairs up to the surface. 

When the elevator doors opened onto Pine Street, the cool, damp Pacific Northwest air hit us instantly. But looking at the massive, tight-knit group of people surrounding me on the sidewalk, the Seattle gloom didn't matter at all. The perimeter was locked, the pack was home, and Spring Break had officially begun.

## 3:20 PM — Checking In

The walk from Westlake Station to the Cascade Crest Hotel was a short, flat, perfectly paved two-block commute. After enduring the chaos of the airport and the subterranean rumble of the transit tunnel, the massive glass doors of the hotel lobby offered a luxurious, quiet sanctuary. 

We swarmed the lobby. Moving fourteen people into any space instantly changed its dynamic, and the East Coast pack immediately claimed a large section of the plush leather couches near the front windows. Suitcases and duffel bags were piled into a defensive perimeter. 

Cassandra rolled directly up to the front desk, the heavy marble counter sitting at eye level for her. I stood right behind her right shoulder, offering a silent physical escort. 

"Good afternoon, I am checking in under Vance," Cassandra announced to the front desk agent. "It is a large family block reservation. We are expected."

The agent tapped on her keyboard, her eyes widening slightly as she pulled up the massive reservation. "Yes, Ms. Vance. Seven suites on the twelfth floor. The reservations were made months ago, and since it's just past three o'clock, all of your rooms are fully serviced and ready for entry."

"Excellent," Cassandra said, reaching into her suit jacket. 

She absolutely despised using credit cards. Cassandra was a strict, meticulous accountant who vastly preferred the immediate transaction settlement of a debit card. Debt of any kind, even temporary rolling debt, bothered her. But she also understood hotel logistics. Hotels despised debit cards for incidentals and holds. So, playing the game, she pulled out a sleek, heavy personal credit card and handed it over. 

"I am placing the entire block on this card," Cassandra instructed firmly. "That includes all room charges, taxes, and a hundred percent of all incidentals. Any room service, any meals charged to the suites by anyone in my party—it all routes to this master account."

"Cassandra, absolutely not," Wendy interjected, having walked up to the counter just in time to hear the mandate. She reached into her purse. "Tom and I are paying for our own suite, and the Rhode Island girls can pay for theirs. You are not covering a fourteen-person hotel bill."

Cassandra didn't even turn her head. She just held her hand up in a gentle, but utterly immovable stop gesture. 

"Wendy, you flew across the continent to support my brother," Cassandra said, her tone carrying the absolute, uncompromising weight of a Quantum executive. "You provided him with a sanctuary in Virginia Beach when he needed it most. You are my guests. Your money is no good in the Pacific Northwest."

"It's easily thousands of dollars, Cassandra," Tom warned, stepping up beside his wife. 

"It is a rounding error," Cassandra corrected him without a trace of arrogance. It was just a logistical fact. "The hotel is paid for. The meals are paid for. You are here to enjoy Spring Break with my family."

The front desk agent, clearly recognizing an executive power play when she saw one, quickly swiped the credit card and began encoding a massive stack of keycards. 

Wendy looked at me, completely stunned by the sheer financial force of my sister. 

"Just let her do it," I smiled, leaning against the marble counter. "It's how she takes care of the pack."

"She is terrifyingly generous," Megan laughed from the couches, having overheard the entire exchange. 

"Alright, room assignments," Cassandra announced, taking the stack of keycards from the agent and spinning her wheelchair around to face the lobby. "Everyone grab your bags. We are taking over the twelfth floor."

## 3:30 PM — The Twelfth Floor

Cassandra wheeled away from the front desk holding a massive stack of plastic keycards. She motioned for the pack to follow her toward the main elevator bank. 

"Alright, listen up," Cassandra commanded as we crowded into two separate elevator cabs and hit the button for the twelfth floor. "When you book a single hotel room, you are placed wherever the hotel's algorithm finds an opening. When you book a corporate-tier block of suites, you control the geography."

The elevator doors pinged open. 

"I have bought out the entire west wing of the twelfth floor," Cassandra explained, rolling out onto the thick, patterned carpet of the hallway. "That means every door past the ice machine belongs to us. No neighbors to disturb, and nobody to disturb us. We control the perimeter."

"That is so intensely hardcore," Erin whispered, looking down the long, private corridor. 

"Room assignments," Cassandra announced, stopping in the middle of the hallway and dealing the keycards out like a Vegas blackjack dealer. "Wendy and Tom, you are in Suite 1220. Meredith and Morgan, you are directly across from them in 1221. Megan and Erin, 1222. Chloe and Claire, 1223."

She handed over the plastic cards as the girls happily grabbed their luggage and started swiping themselves into their respective suites. 

"Now, the ADA logistics," Cassandra continued, pivoting her manual chair to face Kate and David Delaney. "Standard hotel rooms are a nightmare to navigate when multiple wheelchairs are involved, because the furniture footprint does not account for the turning radius of two power chairs simultaneously."

<aac>Tight spaces,</aac> David Delaney agreed through his AAC device, having experienced that exact logistical nightmare on family vacations before. 

"Exactly," Cassandra nodded. "Therefore, you are not sharing. Kate, you have Suite 1224 all to yourself. It is a fully compliant ADA suite with a roll-in shower. David, you have Suite 1225 right next door, also a dedicated ADA suite."

Kate’s face lit up. <aac>My own room?</aac> 

"You are an adult," Cassandra smiled. "You deserve your own space."

"Wait, so that's six suites," Casey said, doing the mental math as she leaned against the wall. "That leaves fourteen people minus... wait, that leaves four of us for the final suite?"

"Correct," Cassandra said, holding up the final set of keycards. "Suite 1226 is the Presidential ADA Suite at the very end of the hall. It has a massive living room, a master bedroom, and two connected adjoining rooms. I will be in the master bedroom so I have access to the primary roll-in shower. Casey, Liz, and David, you three will take the adjoining rooms."

"You purposely put us all in one suite?" I asked, raising an eyebrow. 

"Yes," Cassandra said, her voice softening just a fraction. "We spent the last four months separated by three thousand miles. I am not splitting our core perimeter up tonight."

"And let me guess," Casey pouted, crossing her arms and glaring at Liz. "You two get the king bed in one adjoining room, and I'm stuck sleeping alone in the other one."

"You're seventeen, Casey," Liz laughed, grabbing my hand and squeezing it tightly. "You can survive a hotel bed by yourself."

"I hate sleeping alone!" Casey complained, though a small smile was cracking through her pout. "I want pack cuddles!"

"Drop your bags and get settled," Cassandra called out down the hallway as the various Delaney girls popped their heads out of their doorways. "The hotel is setting up a private catered dinner for us in the Cascade Banquet Room on the mezzanine level. I expect everyone downstairs in exactly one hour!"
