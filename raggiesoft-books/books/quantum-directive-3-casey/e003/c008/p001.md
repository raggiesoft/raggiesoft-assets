## March 23, 2005 • 9:00 AM — The BRAT Diet

I woke up the next morning with my face buried in the soft fabric of the sofa bed, completely sandwiched between the two most important women in my life. Casey was still curled tightly against my chest, while Liz’s arms were wrapped securely around my waist from behind. 

For a few minutes, everything felt perfectly peaceful. Then, my stomach let out a massive, echoing growl. Casey shifted against me, her own stomach immediately mirroring the sound. 

"I'm starving," Casey murmured, her voice thick with sleep. 

"Me too," I groaned, trying to sit up. 

As soon as my abdominal muscles contracted, a wave of deep, physical soreness radiated across my stomach. The nausea was completely gone, but the violent, repeated muscular contractions of yesterday's purge had battered the organ wall and the surrounding abdominal muscles. It felt like I had spent three hours taking direct punches to the gut. 

Liz stirred behind me, sitting up and rubbing her eyes. "Morning, guys. How are the stomachs feeling?"

"Empty," I said, wincing as I carefully pulled myself into a sitting position. "And incredibly bruised."

"That's normal," Cassandra's voice drifted in from the kitchen. 

I looked over and saw my older sister standing by the stove, still wearing the sweatpants she had thrown on last night. She had a pan on the burner and was carefully stirring a small pot. 

"Cass?" I asked, looking at the clock on the microwave. It was past nine in the morning. "Aren't you supposed to be at the Quantum campus today?"

"I called in," Cassandra said smoothly, not taking her eyes off the stove. "I told the executive board that I'm taking a PTO day. My little brother and sister were poisoned by a suburban mall, so corporate strategy can wait."

"You took a day off?" Casey asked, her eyes widening slightly. Cassandra Vance did not take days off. She was a relentless corporate machine who regularly worked eighty-hour weeks to secure the family's financial empire. 

"I did," Cassandra smiled, walking over to the edge of the sofa bed. "You two are my priority. Always."

She handed me a steaming mug of peppermint tea, and then handed one to Casey. 

"Drink that slowly," Cassandra instructed. "It will help relax the bruised muscles in your digestive tract. The soreness usually lasts for two or three days after a severe purge. Your stomach lining is incredibly sensitive right now, which means you cannot jump straight back into normal food."

"We're initiating the BRAT protocol," Liz agreed, walking into the kitchen to help Cassandra. 

<aac>Brat?</aac> I asked, taking a tiny, cautious sip of the peppermint tea. The warmth instantly felt soothing against my battered throat. 

"Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, and Toast," Cassandra listed off. "Bland, easily digestible carbohydrates that will give you energy without triggering your raw stomachs to reject it."

A few minutes later, Liz and Cassandra returned to the living room with two plates. They had prepared a carefully measured breakfast: half of a mashed banana, a small scoop of plain white rice, and a single slice of dry toast. 

"Take it slow," Liz warned, sitting back down on the edge of the sofa bed next to me. 

Casey and I ate with excruciating care. The muscles in my abdomen ached with every swallow, a dull, bruised throb that served as a constant reminder of the toxin. But the food tasted like heaven. 

"So," Cassandra said, sitting down in the armchair and crossing her legs. "You two are officially confined to the apartment for the next forty-eight hours. What movie are we watching first?"

I looked at Casey, who was happily taking a tiny bite of her mashed banana, and then at Liz, who was resting her head against my shoulder. 

Getting food poisoning was a miserable, violent experience. But spending the day locked down in the apartment, surrounded by a pack that was willing to drop everything to keep us safe? 

I wouldn't have traded it for anything.

## March 23, 2005 • 11:00 AM — The Bidding Game

I knew I was going to marry Liz. That realization had locked itself into my brain with absolute certainty the night before. But right now, plotting a proposal wasn't even on my radar. I had much more immediate priorities: my seventeen-year-old sister was still pale, exhausted, and desperately needed to cuddle with her safe person. 

"Move over," Casey mumbled, nudging my ribs with her elbow. 

I shifted slightly on the sofa bed, wincing as the bruised muscles in my abdomen protested the movement. Casey immediately crawled into the space I had made, pulling the heavy fleece blanket up to her chin and resting her head squarely on my chest. 

Liz smiled from the armchair, picking up the television remote. "Alright. We have forty-eight hours of strict quarantine to burn. What's the daytime television strategy?"

"Channel four," Casey whispered, not lifting her head. "It's almost eleven."

Liz punched the buttons on the remote, and the massive cathode-ray tube television in the corner of the living room flared to life. The screen filled with bright, garish studio lights and a wildly cheering audience. 

It was *The Bidding Game*, the legendary daytime game show that had been a staple of American sick days for decades. The iconic host, Bill Parker, strode out onto the stage with his trademark skinny microphone, flashing a brilliant smile at the screaming contestants. 

For the next hour, Casey and I lay perfectly still on the sofa bed, letting the mindless, chaotic energy of the game show wash over us. Every time a contestant wildly overbid on a new jet ski or a dinette set, Casey would let out a soft, breathy laugh against my chest. 

"They always forget about the destination charges," Cassandra commented dryly from the kitchen counter, not even looking up from her laptop. "It's a rookie mistake. You have to factor in freight logistics when bidding on heavy recreational vehicles."

Liz laughed, shaking her head. "Only you would analyze the supply chain logistics of a daytime game show."

"It's a flawed bidding structure," Cassandra replied simply, taking a sip of her coffee. 

On the television, Bill Parker wrapped up the Showcase Showdown. He looked directly into the camera, delivering the same sign-off he had used for thirty years. 

*"And don't forget, help control the domestic pet population. Have your pets spayed or neutered! Goodbye, everybody!"*

As the closing credits rolled over the cheering audience, my stomach let out another hollow, echoing growl. Casey's stomach immediately answered with a quiet rumble of its own. 

"Perfect timing," Liz announced, standing up from the armchair. "Lunchtime."

Cassandra closed her laptop and moved into the kitchen alongside Liz. Ten minutes later, they returned to the living room carrying two steaming bowls and a familiar white cardboard box. 

They set the bowls down on the coffee table in front of the sofa bed. It was classic, simple chicken noodle soup—mostly clear broth with a few soft noodles and tiny fragments of chicken. Next to the bowls, Liz placed a freshly opened sleeve of saltine crackers. 

"Saltines and broth," Cassandra instructed, handing us both a spoon. "The salt will help replenish the electrolytes you lost during the purge, and the crackers will absorb any lingering excess acid in your stomachs."

I carefully pulled myself into a sitting position, helping Casey sit up next to me. The simple act of bending forward made my bruised abdominal wall ache, but the smell of the warm chicken broth was incredible. 

I took a cracker, broke it in half, and let it soak in the hot broth for a few seconds before eating it. The bland, salty starch was exactly what my battered system needed. Beside me, Casey was doing the exact same thing, eating in slow, cautious silence. 

I looked over at Liz, who was watching us eat with a gentle, satisfied smile. 

The proposal could wait. Right now, I was exactly where I needed to be.

## March 24, 2005 • 9:30 AM — The Assignment

By the second morning of our strict quarantine, the divergent paths of our recovery became painfully obvious. 

When I woke up, the deep, bruised aching in my abdominal wall had noticeably faded. It still hurt to engage my core, but I actually had the strength to sit up on the edge of the sofa bed without needing Liz to haul me upright. I looked over at the armchair, where Liz was quietly reviewing her notes for a missed lecture. 

The urge to walk over, pull her into the kitchen out of earshot, and tell her that I was going to marry her was almost overwhelming. The clarity I had found two nights ago hadn't faded; it had hardened into absolute, unyielding fact. 

I took a breath, preparing to stand up and do exactly that. 

Then, Casey let out a miserable, pathetic groan from the mattress beside me. 

I looked down. While my twenty-year-old system was finally starting to clear the residual inflammation, Casey was not bouncing back. Her seventeen-year-old body had been hit significantly harder by the mall pizza toxin. She was incredibly pale, her forehead damp with a cold sweat, and she was trembling slightly under the heavy wool blankets. 

"Davy," she whispered, her voice cracking. 

"I'm right here, Case," I said, immediately aborting my plan to stand up. 

"My stomach still hurts so much," she whimpered, curling her knees tightly up to her chest in a desperate attempt to relieve the cramping pressure. "It feels worse than yesterday."

I understood the assignment. 

The ring, the logistics, the grand romantic gesture—none of it mattered right now. I was a Vance, which meant I was the protective anchor for my pack. And right now, my little sister was in acute physical distress and needed her safe person. 

I slid back under the covers, wrapping my arms securely around Casey and pulling her tight against my chest. She immediately buried her face into my t-shirt, letting out a long, shaky sigh as the deep-pressure contact of the cuddle provided the only real comfort available to her. 

Liz looked up from her textbook, her eyes softening as she took in the scene. She didn't look annoyed that our quarantine was dragging on, and she certainly wasn't jealous that I was devoting all my physical attention to another woman. She just smiled warmly, setting her book aside. 

"I'll get the peppermint tea and the heating pad," Liz whispered, quietly slipping into the kitchen. 

Cassandra wheeled out of the master bedroom a moment later, instantly assessing Casey's pale complexion. 

"She's regressing?" Cassandra asked, her voice dropping into a low, tactical murmur as she approached the sofa bed. 

"Not a full relapse," I reported quietly, keeping my arms wrapped tightly around Casey. "Just prolonged muscle bruising. The contractions hit her harder."

Cassandra nodded, pressing the back of her cool hand against Casey's forehead. "No fever. Just sheer physical exhaustion. She stays on the BRAT protocol for another twenty-four hours, minimum."

Liz returned from the kitchen, plugging an electric heating pad into the wall and carefully sliding it under the blankets, resting it gently against Casey's cramped stomach. 

"Thanks, Liz," Casey mumbled into my chest. 

"You're welcome, sweetie," Liz said softly, brushing a strand of hair out of Casey's face. 

I rested my chin on top of my sister's head, holding her steady. I wanted to marry Liz more than anything in the world, but as I sat there flanked by the two women I loved most, I knew the proposal would happen exactly when it was supposed to. 

Until then, I wasn't moving an inch.
