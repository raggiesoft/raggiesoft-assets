## 10:05 AM — The MAR Protocol

The hearing room was still humming with the quiet, lingering tension of Senator Thomas’s public humiliation. The press gallery was practically vibrating, reporters furiously typing out drafts of what was already shaping up to be greatest congressional takedown of the decade.

Chairman Hayes cleared his throat, effectively resetting the room. "Moving on to matters of actual substance, Ms. Vance. I'd like to direct your attention to the cybersecurity frameworks proposed in Section 4 of the infrastructure bill—"

As the Chairman began his line of questioning, a silent, haptic timer vibrated against David’s wrist.

It was 10:05 AM—precisely aligned with Cassandra's mid-morning calibration protocol.

David didn’t hesitate, nor did he ask for a recess. He simply shifted into his professional LPN baseline.

Cassandra, listening intently to the Chairman, casually extended her left arm toward her brother without ever breaking eye contact with the dais. It was a fluid, deeply rehearsed movement.

With absolute, practiced efficiency, David unzipped the secondary compartment of his leather medical kit. He withdrew a compact, near-silent digital wrist sphygmomanometer and slipped it over Cassandra’s cuff, tapping the activation button. As the cuff gently inflated with a faint, imperceptible hum, he checked the reading on the pulse oximeter already clipped to her finger.

Up on the dais, Chairman Hayes paused for a fraction of a second, his eyes tracking the sudden movement at the witness table. He watched as David expertly recorded the systolic and diastolic numbers, unclasped the cuff, and tapped the vitals into the secure digital Medication Administration Record (MAR) on his tablet.

"Section 4 relies heavily on closed-loop encryption, Chairman," Cassandra responded seamlessly, not missing a single beat of the complex cybersecurity discussion as David worked. "But closed loops are only as secure as their physical access points."

As she spoke, David reached into a specialized, insulated thermal pouch tucked within his kit—maintaining the strict cold-chain integrity required for her scheduled biological peptide injection. With clinical precision, he withdrew a pre-filled syringe and a sterile alcohol prep pad.

Sensing the shift in routine, Cassandra subtly adjusted her posture, sliding the left sleeve of her dark blazer up just enough to expose her upper arm. She didn't stop speaking; her tone remained commanding and perfectly level.

"If the physical servers are vulnerable to localized intrusion, the encryption standard becomes irrelevant," Cassandra continued, explaining the architecture to the Senate panel.

Beside her, David tore open the alcohol swab. He cleaned a small patch of skin on her arm, his touch clinical and reassuring. He uncapped the syringe, pinched the muscle slightly to ensure a clean subcutaneous delivery, and administered the scheduled medication.

It took less than five seconds.

He withdrew the needle, applied a small sterile gauze pad with a piece of medical tape, and instantly deposited the used syringe into a compact, travel-safe sharps container inside his bag. Cassandra smoothly rolled her sleeve back down, completely unbothered, her focus never leaving the Senators.

David picked up his tablet, navigating to jurisdictional MAR interface. He quickly and quietly typed out the entry: <aac>Medication administered. Site: Left upper arm. Vitals nominal (BP 112/72, HR 68, SpO2 99%). Patient baseline stable. No adverse reactions.</aac>

He closed the kit, zipped it shut, and rested his hands comfortably back in his lap, resuming his quiet overwatch.

The entire medical intervention had taken less than sixty seconds. It had been executed with zero disruption to the congressional record, zero hesitation, and absolute dignity.

On the dais, Chairman Hayes lowered his reading glasses, his gaze lingering on the insulated thermal pouch as David zipped it back away. Having chaired healthcare oversight committees for years, Hayes instantly recognized the meticulous, temperature-controlled logistics of managing a strict biological regimen on a rigid schedule.

He looked at Cassandra, who was currently dismantling a flaw in the federal firewall topology. Then he looked at David, who was quietly monitoring his sister's breathing pattern from the periphery. Finally, Chairman Hayes glanced over at Senator Thomas, who was staring at the witness table in stunned, uncomfortable silence.

Chairman Hayes didn't say a word about the injection, but the look he gave Senator Thomas spoke volumes.

*This,* the look said, tracking the cold-pack and the precise timing, *is why the young man is sitting at the table.*

It wasn't a stunt. It wasn't an entourage. It was an immutable, temperature-sensitive medical reality, handled with a level of grace and discipline that put half the United States Senate to shame.

"I completely agree with your assessment of the server vulnerabilities, Ms. Vance," Chairman Hayes said warmly, leaning into his microphone. "Your clarity on this matter is deeply appreciated. Please, continue with your analysis."

## 11:15 AM — The Finch Expedition

The gavel struck the wooden block, officially adjourning the Senate Judiciary Committee hearing.

As the gallery cleared and congressional aides scrambled to gather briefing binders, Cassandra wheeled herself out of the witness box to rejoin her family. But before they could clear the committee room exit, a familiar, warm voice called out across the aisle.

"Cassandra! Hold up a second!"

Walking down the center carpet was Senator Patricia "Patty" Morrison of Washington State. Born and raised in Bothell, the veteran lawmaker was a proud product of the Puget Sound area, currently in the thick of her 2014 reelection campaign. As a relentless champion for Pacific Northwest infrastructure, Morrison had watched the morning's testimony with sheer delight.

"Senator Morrison," Cassandra greeted warmly, stopping her chair and extending a hand. "It's good to see you."

"Fabulous opening statement," Morrison smiled, shaking Cassandra's hand before greeting David, Liz, and Casey with an easy, practiced warmth. "You completely neutralized the opposition. But listen—while I have you in the building, walk with me. I want to show you something, and we need to talk about home."

Rather than navigating the crowded public corridors, Senator Morrison led the Vance pack through a private, secured side door into the subterranean network of tunnels connecting the Senate office buildings directly to the United States Capitol.

She guided them onto the platform of the Capitol Subway System—the small, open-top underground train that shuttles lawmakers beneath Capitol Hill.

David's eyes lit up instantly. An open-top electric monorail running through private, vaulted government tunnels was an unexpected transit treat.

"I wanted to talk about the regional expansion," Senator Morrison said as they boarded the open subway car, the wind blowing through the quiet tunnel as the train sped along its track. "TAPS is pushing hard on the proposed light rail extension across Lake Washington out to Redmond. I know local opposition in a few Eastside neighborhoods is getting noisy, but if we want this regional grid to function, we need the TAPS East Corridor anchored at the Quantum campus."

Cassandra offered a confident, knowing smile.

"Senator, you don't have to sell me on TAPS East," Cassandra assured her over the hum of the electric motor. "Quantum is already fully on board. In fact, we are finalizing the paperwork with the City of Redmond to establish a Community Facilities District. Quantum is agreeing to act as the single ratepayer to drop over thirty-three million dollars into the Overlake Transit Center, building the weather-shielded bus canopies and the pedestrian bridge over SR 520."

Senator Morrison raised an eyebrow, genuinely impressed. "Thirty-three million out of your own corporate pocket? That's going to make my job in the Senate Appropriations Committee a hell of a lot easier."

"Infrastructure isn't an expense, Senator; it's a foundation," Cassandra replied smoothly as the subway car hissed to a stop beneath the Capitol. "And next time you're back home in Bothell, let me know. I'll personally buy your fare and we can take a TAPS bus ride together down the I-405 corridor."

Morrison let out a rich, genuine laugh. "You've got a deal, Cassie. I'll hold you to that."

Exiting the Capitol through the plaza doors, the pack stepped out into the brilliant Mid-Atlantic sunshine. The air was warm, a soft breeze rustling the trees along the Mall. It was a beautiful day.

"Where to next, Cass?" David asked, looking out over the wide green lawns.

"We go to the Finch," Cassandra announced, pointing down the paved pedestrian path.

She wasn't referring to a high-priced lobbying steakhouse or a corporate boardroom. She meant the world-famous **Finch Air and Space Museum**—the crown jewel of **The Finch Endowment**, founded by the 18th-century British scientist Lord Alistair Finch, who had famously bequeathed his massive fortune to the United States to establish an institution dedicated to the "increase and diffusion of knowledge."

They set off down the wide, flat, wheel-friendly concrete pathways of the Mall, enjoying the open air. Casey immediately pulled out her camera to photograph the towering monuments framing the horizon, while Liz and David walked side-by-side, soaking in the quiet afternoon.

Halfway down the path, Cassandra’s phone began to vibrate violently in her messenger bag.

It was Greg, the K Street lobbyist. Having finally abandoned his trapped motorcade of Apex Mountaineers on the 14th Street Bridge and sprinted up Capitol Hill, he had arrived at the committee room only to find it completely empty.

Cassandra tapped the speakerphone button on her mounted dashboard. "Go ahead, Greg."

"Ms. Vance!" Greg’s voice crackled through the speaker, breathless and bordering on sheer panic. "I am at the Dirksen Building! Where did you go?! Did you leave the Capitol grounds?! Is there an emergency executive briefing at the White House?! Did you schedule an unscheduled press conference?!"

Behind Greg, the entire K Street handling team was in a tailspin, frantically trying to figure out why the CEO of Quantum was moving on foot toward the Mall instead of heading to a high-priced corporate luncheon.

Cassandra glanced back at David, who was smiling as Casey pointed out a vintage aircraft hanging in the glass atrium of the museum ahead.

"There is no emergency, Greg," Cassandra answered calmly, her tone dropping into a warm, unyielding register. "I finished my testimony, I had a lovely subway ride with Senator Morrison, and now I am fulfilling a promise."

"A promise?!" Greg stammered. "To who?! What conference?!"

"To my family," Cassandra said smoothly. "We are going to The Finch Endowment's Air and Space Museum. Do not call dispatch, do not send an Apex Mountaineer, and do not follow us. We will see you tomorrow evening for the gala at the Zoo."

She hung up the phone, flipped the hardware mute switch, and rolled effortlessly through the wide, level-boarding entrance of the Finch Museum, completely content to leave the world of corporate handlers behind for the rest of the afternoon.

## 12:00 PM — The Civilian Perimeter

The transition from the marble halls of Capitol Hill to the bustling atrium of the Finch Air and Space Museum required a hard reset of the pack's operational profile.

As soon as they entered the massive, glass-walled building, Cassandra bypassed the awe-inspiring Apollo capsules and steered her family directly toward the spacious family restrooms tucked near the food court.

"Corporate armor off," Cassandra instructed once the heavy door clicked shut and the deadbolt slid into place, granting them complete privacy.

She unshouldered her Quantum laptop bag and shed her razor-sharp tailored blazer. Within minutes, the intimidating, trillion-dollar CEO who had just lectured the United States Senate completely vanished. They swapped their formal Capitol attire for comfortable blue jeans, soft cotton t-shirts, and well-worn sneakers.

When Cassandra wheeled back out into the concourse, she looked absolutely indistinguishable from the thousands of other tourists navigating the National Mall. She blended in perfectly, which was exactly the tactical advantage she wanted. There were no bodyguards, no PR handlers, and no flashing cameras. She was just an older sister on vacation with her family.

They grabbed a corner booth at **Rocket Burger**, the bustling fast-food joint that anchored the museum's food court.

The table was soon crowded with red plastic trays piled high with standard-issue cheeseburgers, heavily salted french fries, and large fountain sodas. It was about as far from a high-end K Street lobbying luncheon as physically possible, and the pack couldn't have been happier.

"Alright, here is the itinerary," Cassandra said, stealing a french fry from David's tray and pointing to a digital map on her phone. "We have the entire afternoon to explore the exhibits. Whenever we're tired, we'll head back outside, roll up the street to L'Enfant Plaza, and catch either the Orange or Blue Line."

"Where's the hotel?" Liz asked, dipping a fry into a pool of ketchup.

"Farragut Square," Cassandra smiled. "We take the train to Farragut West, take the elevator up to street level, and our lobby is literally a block away. We can just crash."

David took a bite of his burger, his eyes drifting out of the food court toward the massive, suspended aircraft hanging in the adjacent exhibition hall. His earlier anxiety from the flashbulbs at the light rail station was completely gone, replaced by the deep, focused hum of his astronomy and aviation hyperfixations.

"Thank you for this, Cass," David mumbled happily around a mouthful of fries. "You promised we'd come here."

"I always keep my promises, Davy," Cassandra replied softly, her dark eyes entirely focused on her brother and sister.

To the rest of the world, Cassandra Vance was a titan of industry. But as she sat in a plastic booth in a crowded Rocket Burger, watching David happily explain orbital mechanics to Liz while Casey reviewed the photos on her digital camera, her true hierarchy of needs was glaringly obvious. Her autistic younger brother and sister were the absolute center of her universe. Managing Quantum was just her day job; protecting and providing for her pack was her actual life's work.

"Alright, eat up," Cassandra instructed, wiping her hands with a napkin and unlocking her wheelchair brakes. "The Finch museums are totally free to wander around, but I checked the schedule and there's a documentary playing in the large-format theater in thirty minutes. We need to swing by the box office and buy tickets before they sell out."

## 1:00 PM — The Telescope Protocol

The large-format theater inside the Finch Air and Space Museum required a strict adherence to civilian etiquette. Before joining the queue for the documentary, the pack stopped at the front visitor counter, checking their heavy weekend duffel bags with the attendant so they wouldn't have to haul them through the crowded theater aisles.

As they found their seats in the massive, steeply tiered auditorium, the lights began to dim.

"Phones off," Cassandra instructed, pulling her device from her pocket and holding the power button until the screen went completely black.

David, Liz, and Casey followed suit. To Cassandra, shutting down her digital connection in a movie theater wasn't just common courtesy; it was a sacred boundary. Quantum’s executive suite in Redmond knew her itinerary down to the minute. Marcus, her Chief of Staff, had explicitly noted the ninety-minute communications blackout on the master schedule.

The K Street lobbyists, however, did not have the master schedule.

An hour and a half later, the credits rolled, the house lights came up, and the pack made their way out into the sunlit exhibition halls. Cassandra reached into her pocket and held down the power button to boot up her phone.

The moment the device reconnected to the cellular network, it practically convulsed in her hand.

*Bzz. Bzz. BZZ-BZZ-BZZ-BZZ.*

The haptic motor went into overdrive as the phone frantically downloaded an avalanche of missed calls, panicked voicemails, and dozens of text messages. Cassandra stopped her wheelchair near the entrance of the Apollo exhibit, letting out a sharp, annoyed sigh as she opened the <aac>Vance deployment - d.c.</aac> group thread.

\[1:15 PM\] Greg (D.C. Lobbyist): Ms. Vance, your GPS signal just dropped off the map. Please confirm your location.

\[1:30 PM\] Greg (D.C. Lobbyist): Did you go underground? Are you in a secure bunker?

\[2:05 PM\] Pierce (Manhattan PR): Greg says you've been offline for an hour. Are you still on the Mall? Do we need to dispatch Capitol Police to your last known coordinates?!

\[2:12 PM\] Marcus (Quantum Chief of Staff): Stand down, both of you. She is in a movie theater.

\[2:15 PM\] Greg (D.C. Lobbyist): A MOVIE THEATER?! In the middle of the afternoon?! On a deployment day?!

\[2:17 PM\] Marcus (Quantum Chief of Staff): She promised her brother a trip to the Finch. Do not call the police, Greg. Just wait for the documentary to end.

Cassandra shook her head. Her fixers were operating on a frequency of pure, unchecked paranoia. She wasn't going to validate their hysteria with an explanation, a phone call, or an apology for being unreachable.

She tapped a single emoji into the group thread and hit send.

\[2:31 PM\] Cassandra Vance: 🔭

She locked the screen, dropping the phone back into her pocket before Greg could even begin to type a response.

"Let me guess," David smiled, walking up beside her chair. "Greg?"

"Greg," Cassandra confirmed, rolling her eyes. "He was about five minutes away from initiating a federal manhunt. Now, where to?"

David didn't hesitate. He was completely in his element, his hyperfixation fully engaged as he looked up at the towering rockets and orbital command modules suspended from the ceiling. For the next two hours, the family happily deferred to his expertise. David guided them through the space exploration wing, passionately explaining the intricacies of orbital mechanics, gravitational slingshots, and lunar descent trajectories to Liz and Casey. Cassandra just wheeled quietly beside him, profoundly content to watch her brother light up with absolute, unburdened joy.

By late afternoon, the sensory input of the crowded museum finally began to wear on the pack.

"Alright, let's head to the hotel," Cassandra called out, recognizing the subtle shift in David and Casey's posture.

They returned to the front counter, retrieved their duffel bags, and exited the museum through the southern doors onto Independence Avenue. Rather than walking back across the National Mall to the crowded Finch station, Cassandra mapped the optimal, frictionless route. They simply walked two blocks south down 7th Street, descending into the L'Enfant Plaza Metro Station.

The sprawling subterranean transit hub was perfectly climate-controlled. Cassandra pulled her CapitalPass card from her pocket, tapped the glass faregate, and rolled through without breaking her momentum. David, Liz, and Casey tapped their own cards right behind her, acting as a flawless, synchronized perimeter.

They descended in the elevator to the lower platform, bypassing the Red and Yellow lines entirely. Within minutes, a silver Blue Line train hissed to a halt. The doors opened flush with the platform, and the pack boarded effortlessly.

The ride through the heart of the District was smooth and cool. After just a few stops, the train arrived at Farragut West.

Cassandra rolled off the train, guiding her family to the street-level elevator. When the doors opened, they stepped out into the bustling evening air of Farragut Square. The heavy, humid D.C. heat had begun to break, and the lush green park in the center of the square was filled with commuters heading home.

Cassandra led them exactly one block over to their luxury hotel. There was no motorcade, no frantic lobbying team waiting in the lobby, and no press pool. Thanks to the absolute autonomy of the Capital Transit grid, the CEO of Quantum checked in at the front desk looking like nothing more than a standard tourist, her family safe and insulated by her side.
