The walls of the Westbreeze Apartments in Portsmouth were incredibly thin. I was sitting on the edge of the mattress in the small bedroom I shared with Delores, trying to block out the ambient noise of her mother, Robyn, moving around in the kitchen. 

Robyn had welcomed me into their apartment with open arms, and I took that welcome entirely at face value. Because they told me I was welcome, I believed I was welcome. As far as I knew, I was a permanent, equal member of the household. I had absolutely no idea that I wasn't on the lease, or that my complete lack of legal rights made me incredibly vulnerable. To my literal, system-oriented brain, we were simply a family sharing a space. 

The bedroom door clicked open. Delores walked in, closing it firmly behind her. She had a strange, almost manic energy radiating from her posture. 

"I have a surprise," she announced, leaning against the doorframe. "We're going on a trip."

I looked up, my internal processor immediately spinning up to parse the sudden deviation from our routine. "A trip? Where?"

"Washington State," she smiled, her eyes wide with what looked like genuine excitement. "To see your sisters."

The words hit me like a physical shock. Seeing Jessica and Rachel was something I desperately craved, but the logistics of getting to the Pacific Northwest were massive. 

"We're going to see America," Delores continued, stepping into the room. "We're going to take a Pathfinder bus all the way across the country. It'll be a romantic road trip. Just the two of us, seeing the states. And the best part? I'm paying for it. It's my treat."

My analytical brain immediately started running calculations. A cross-country bus ride in a Pathfinder coach would take roughly seventy-two hours. My Cerebral Palsy would be subjected to severe, unrelenting spasticity from the cramped seating and the constant vibration of the diesel engine. 

The logical solution was to fly. I could easily afford two round-trip tickets out of Norfolk International Airport directly to Seattle-Tacoma. Every other week, Jessica deposited money into a secret bank account for me, ensuring I always had a financial safety net. It was a lifeline that kept me connected to my family. 

But as I looked at Delores, the danger of that logic became terrifyingly clear. 

If I offered to buy plane tickets, I would expose the secret account. Delores would instantly demand to know where the money came from. She would demand access to it, and she would drain the only security Jessica had managed to build for me. The cost of a flight wasn't just the price of the ticket; it was the total destruction of my financial autonomy. 

"Okay," I said quietly, forcing my facial expression to remain entirely neutral. "A bus trip sounds nice."

"It's going to be great," she insisted, entirely satisfied with my submission. "We leave this Saturday."

My processor threw a minor error flag. *Saturday.* 

"Saturday?" I repeated, my mind instantly referencing the Hampton Roads transit grid. "The CVTA Route 85 doesn't run on Saturdays. How are we getting to the terminal?"

Delores waved her hand dismissively. "My mom is going to drop us off. I'm telling her we're just going to Virginia Beach to stay at Ellen's house for a little bit."

I frowned, the pieces of the puzzle feeling jagged and wrong. "You're going to lie to Robyn?"

"It's none of her business," Delores snapped, the sharp, defensive edge instantly returning to her voice. "I don't need my mother questioning me. We're just going to pack some bags, she'll drop us off, and we'll get on the bus. That's it."

She turned and opened the closet door, already beginning to pull clothes off the hangers. 

I sat on the edge of the bed, a cold knot tightening in my stomach. She was lying to her mother, she was hiding our true destination, and she was willingly trapping us on a commercial bus for three days. But as long as it kept my secret bank account hidden, I had no choice but to go along with it. 

I thought it was just a vacation. I had absolutely no idea she was packing for a one-way trip.
