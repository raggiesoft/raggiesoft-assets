The two-hour drone up Interstate 64 was a grim preview of the physical toll the next four days would demand. The heavy vibration of the Pathfinder chassis translated directly into the floorboards, radiating up through my boots and settling deep into my joints. Every time the bus hit a seam in the concrete, my spine compressed against the rigid, unyielding fabric of the seat. 

By the time the air brakes hissed and we pulled into the Richmond transit terminal, my CP spasticity was flaring in my lower back, tight and burning. 

"We have a layover," Delores announced, standing up immediately and blocking the narrow aisle. "We change buses here for the westbound route. Get the carry-on bags and meet me inside."

I waited for the aisle to clear before forcing myself upright, my knee buckling slightly as the blood flow rushed back into my cramped legs. I grabbed our small duffel bags from the overhead rack and limped down the rubber steps into the cold midday air. 

The Richmond terminal was older and significantly more crowded than Norfolk. We immediately separated to use the restrooms—Delores marching off toward the women's room while I slipped into the men's room to splash freezing water on my face. Staring at my pale reflection in the scratched mirror, the sheer, crushing scale of what we were doing finally began to set in. We were only two hours into a ninety-six-hour marathon, and my systems were already starting to lag. 

When I walked back out into the main concourse, Delores was standing near the entrance of the small, brightly lit cafeteria attached to the terminal. She was clutching her purse, staring at the laminated menus taped to the glass with a tight, frustrated expression. 

I walked over, the heavy duffel bags cutting into my shoulder. "Are you hungry?"

"I don't have the money to eat at a restaurant," she said sharply, her tone defensive. She only had a few loose dollars to her name, a fact that always manifested as a prickly, hostile insecurity. 

"I'll pay for it," I offered quietly, pulling my wallet from my pocket. "Let's just sit down and eat a real lunch. We have the time before the westbound bus boards."

Her posture relaxed slightly, the tension bleeding out of her shoulders as she accepted the financial bailout. She didn't say thank you. She just pushed through the glass doors and claimed a small formica booth near the window. 

I ordered us two plates of hot food. For myself, I got a cheeseburger—no onions, no tomato—and a side of fries. When I reached into the glass cooler by the register to grab a carton of milk, Delores immediately shot her hand out and slapped my wrist. 

"Put that back," she snapped, glaring at the carton. "I am lactose intolerant."

I stared at her, my processor freezing for a split second. I was paying for my own drink, with my own money, but her medical restrictions were somehow a universal law that governed my diet as well. Arguing was pointless. I quietly returned the milk to the shelf and pulled out a plastic bottle of apple juice instead, paying for the entire meal with the Quantum payroll money she knew nothing about. 
I stared at her, my processor freezing for a split second. I was paying for my own drink, with my own debit card, but her medical restrictions were somehow a universal law that governed my diet as well. Arguing was pointless. I quietly returned the milk to the shelf and pulled out a plastic bottle of apple juice instead, swiping my debit card to pay for the entire meal with the Quantum payroll money she knew nothing about. 

We sat in relative silence, the clatter of silverware and the low hum of the cafeteria masking the tension between us. The food was greasy and heavily salted, but it was hot. 

I chewed the bitter burger, staring out the cafeteria window at the massive silver buses idling in the boarding lanes. We were only a few hours into the trip, and my lower back was already locked in a dull, throbbing ache. Delores ate quickly, completely unbothered by the fact that I had just subsidized her transit experience. 

I washed the food down with the apple juice, silently calculating how many more times I could safely tap into the Quantum payroll deposits before she started asking questions. The westbound bus would be boarding soon, and the hardest miles were still ahead of us.
