The layover in Kansas City lasted for three exhausting hours. We sat on hard plastic terminal chairs in the dead of night, waiting for the Denver-bound express. 

When the PA system finally barked out the boarding call at two in the morning, Delores marched onto the new coach, leaving me to drag the luggage across the concrete bay and heave it into the undercarriage. My right knee was radiating a dull, sickening heat, the spasticity so severe I had to physically lift my own leg with my hands just to clear the steep rubber steps of the bus. 

I collapsed into the aisle seat next to her, out of breath and trembling. 

The air brakes released, and the heavy coach lurched forward, navigating the downtown streets of Kansas City, Missouri. I leaned my head against the cold glass, watching the sodium-vapor streetlights bleed past. 

A few minutes later, the bus crested a massive steel bridge spanning a wide, black expanse of water. A green highway sign flashed past my window in the darkness: *Missouri River*. 

Right behind it was another, larger sign: *Welcome to Kansas.* 

I blinked, my exhausted brain struggling to process the geographical data. I stared out the window as the bus descended into a sprawling, illuminated downtown grid that looked almost identical to the one we had just left. 

*Kansas City, Kansas?*

My processor stuttered. For my entire life, the public school system in Virginia had hammered it into my head that Kansas City was in Missouri. It was a geographic absolute. Now, I was crossing a river and pulling into an entirely different municipality, in an entirely different state, that shared the exact same name. The sheer absurdity of it temporarily broke through the fog of my physical pain. I wanted to ask someone about it, to verify that my map data wasn't corrupt, but the woman sitting next to me was still a hostile, unapproachable wall of ice. 

So I kept the geographic anomaly to myself, watching the duplicate city fade away as the Pathfinder merged onto Interstate 70 West. 

The transition from the metropolitan glow to the absolute, crushing darkness of the Great Plains was almost instantaneous. There were no suburbs, no rolling hills, no ambient light. There was only a perfectly flat, pitch-black horizon stretching out into infinity. 

The bus settled into a heavy, vibrating drone. Delores pulled her jacket tight and closed her eyes, maintaining her punishing silence even in sleep.
