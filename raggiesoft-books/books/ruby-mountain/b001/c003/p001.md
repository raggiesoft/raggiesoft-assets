# Part 1: The Diagnostics (08:00)

The heavy oak doors of The Prospector's Keep were propped wide open, letting the crisp, thawing spring air flood into the 1890s lobby. Out on the cobblestone turnaround, a fleet of heavy-duty commercial work trucks had replaced the exiled generation's luxury SUVs.

Master architects, structural engineers, and master plumbers filed into the Great Hall, carrying heavy clipboards and diagnostic equipment. They looked up at the towering Douglas fir columns and the massive stone fireplaces, visibly awed by the scale of the Klondike-era construction .

Victoria Vance stood in the center of the Persian rug, dressed in a sharp, utilitarian blazer and dark slacks. She didn't offer them a hospitality greeting; she offered them a mandate.

"Gentlemen," Victoria addressed the lead contractor, her voice echoing clearly in the cavernous space. "You are here because the previous owners prioritized cosmetic patches over fundamental infrastructure . That era is over. I want every inch of this building evaluated."

The lead structural engineer nodded, pulling out a set of old blueprints. "Ms. Vance, depending on what we find behind these walls, a full diagnostic could take us weeks. We can expedite the surface-level checks if you're trying to meet a summer opening deadline."

"There is no summer deadline," Victoria countered smoothly, her tone absolute. "The Prospector's Keep is officially closed for the 1999 season. We are not rushing this."

She stepped closer to the contractor, her eyes locking onto his with terrifying, sovereign intensity.

"I would rather you take twice as long if it means you uncover something," Victoria instructed. "If you suspect a pipe is corroded, you do not patch it—you tear the wall down to the bedrock and trace the line. Do not hide the rot to save me time. Find the vulnerabilities, map them, and tell me exactly what it will cost to rip them out. You have the run of the building."

The contractors exchanged a look of deep, professional respect. They rarely encountered owners willing to sacrifice a season of revenue to do the job right.

"Understood, Ms. Vance," the foreman said, gesturing for his crew to fan out. "We'll start with the basement boiler lines and work our way up."

## The Digital Classroom (09:15)

While the contractors began systematically dismantling the failing infrastructure of the first floor, the younger cousins were completely insulated from the chaos.

Up in the unrenovated North Wing—the temporary sanctuary the pack had claimed the night before —the minors were gathered around a cluster of heavy oak tables, deeply immersed in their daily routines. The eviction of their parents hadn't derailed their education; Victoria's logistical architecture ensured that the pack's academic momentum never paused.

Victoria had already engineered an ironclad Alternative Learning Experience (ALE) agreement with the local rural school district. To bypass the unreliability of the mountain's fragile satellite internet, Victoria digitized all the educational software and syllabi, hosting everything locally on the Keep's Quantum server.

The room was quiet, save for the rhythmic clicking of their standard two-button optical mice as the cousins logged into their workstations. Operating entirely on the internal localized network, they accessed their coursework at blazing, uninterrupted LAN speeds.

Fourteen-year-old Amanda sat next to Johnny, quietly working through an algebra module, while the younger cousins—Emma, Grace, Chloe, and Mia—focused intently on their own screens. There was no panic about submitting their digital worksheets or losing a connection.

They operated under Victoria's highly efficient "Batch Upload" protocol. The cousins simply saved their work to the local server directory. At the end of the week, Victoria would collect the zipped files and transmit them directly to the school district via a secure FTP client during a stable connectivity window.

The adults were gone, the walls downstairs were coming down, and the mountain was finally theirs. Sitting in the quiet hum of their digital classroom, the youngest members of the Vance-Whitaker pack went to work.
