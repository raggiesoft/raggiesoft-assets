# Part 4: The Island Anomaly (17:50)

The heavy, metallic groan of the Evergreen ferry docking at Friday Harbor reverberated through the passenger deck, signaling the end of the crossing.

When the pedestrian doors finally opened, the air that hit Johnny’s face was even thicker than it had been in Anacortes. The marine layer had settled low over the San Juan Islands as evening approached. Every breath required a conscious, deliberate flex of his intercostal muscles. His blood oxygen levels were steadily dropping, the sea-level atmosphere acting as a slow, invisible poison.

Vanessa pushed his wheelchair down the steel gangway and out into the bustling Friday Harbor terminal. The town was a picturesque, maritime postcard—lined with seafood restaurants, tourist boutiques, and moored sailboats.

But Vanessa didn't steer him toward the commercial district, nor did she look for a waiting DSHS transport van. Instead, she pushed him up a steep, paved incline, moving away from the waterfront and into a densely packed residential grid of older, weathered apartment complexes.

Johnny’s dark eyes tracked the changing environment, his internal logic engine flashing red. Residential zoning. No commercial signage. No ADA-compliant facility infrastructure. Ten minutes later, Vanessa stopped in front of a drab, two-story apartment building. The paint was peeling around the exterior stairwells, and the parking lot was cracked. She wheeled him up a short, concrete ramp to a ground-floor unit, pulled a standard brass key from her purse, and unlocked the door.

She pushed him over the threshold and kicked the door shut behind them, throwing the deadbolt.

Johnny stared at his surroundings. The apartment was cramped and dim, smelling faintly of stale coffee and cheap vanilla air freshener. There was a worn sofa, a small television, and a kitchenette cluttered with unwashed mugs.

There was absolutely no medical equipment. There were no observation cameras. There wasn't even a desk for a caseworker.

This is a civilian domicile, Johnny concluded, his brow furrowing in profound confusion. Why is a state-mandated twenty-four-hour independent living assessment being conducted in a private residence?

Vanessa dropped her purse on the kitchen counter with a heavy sigh, as if shedding the last remaining layer of her professional disguise. She walked back over to him, and once again, that unsettling, glazed look returned to her eyes. The clinical distance of a transition nurse was entirely gone.

"Home sweet home," Vanessa murmured, her voice taking on that same sickeningly sweet, romantic cadence she had used in the windowless clinic back on the mainland.

She stepped behind his wheelchair and bypassed the living room entirely, pushing him straight down a short, narrow hallway. The walls were too close together; his wheel rims nearly scraped the cheap drywall.

She guided him into a small, cluttered bedroom. A queen-sized bed took up most of the floor space. The single window overlooking the parking lot had its blinds pulled tightly shut.

Johnny’s chest heaved as he fought the heavy air, his core spasticity twitching in his legs. His brain was desperately trying to map a grid of logic over a situation that possessed none. His family had promised him that this was a medical necessity. Victoria had reviewed the paperwork. Therefore, this woman was a state authority. If the state authority required the assessment to take place in a private bedroom, then fighting the protocol would only jeopardize his family's legal standing.

He didn't have his Quantum XN to ask questions. He didn't have the lung capacity to physically resist. He had a lifetime of conditioning that taught him to trust the adults managing his care. He decided to simply endure it. He would go with the flow, log the data, and wait for noon tomorrow when Victoria would return.

Vanessa locked the bedroom wheels of his chair, then walked over to the nightstand and switched on a small, dim lamp.

She turned back to him, her eyes tracing his exhausted frame with a twisted, predatory affection. She sat down on the edge of the mattress, directly in front of his chair, and leaned in close. The smell of her vanilla perfume was suffocating in the small room.

"You've been so brave today, Johnny," Vanessa whispered, reaching out to gently cup the side of his face. Her thumb stroked his cheekbone. "I know this is all new for you. But I promised you this was going to be special, didn't I? A secret just for us."

She leaned closer, her hands moving down to the collar of his shirt, entirely abandoning the final shreds of medical protocol. She was going to show him what "real life" was like away from his family.

Johnny stared at the dim lamp over her shoulder, his analytical mind quietly shutting down as the system violation began.
