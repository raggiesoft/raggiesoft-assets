# Part 1: The Dial-Up Escape (08:45)

The air at sea level didn't just feel heavy; it felt toxic.

Sitting in the cramped living room of the Friday Harbor apartment, Johnny Vance took a shallow, measured breath. The dense, salty humidity rolling off the San Juan Channel felt like a wet woolen blanket draped over his lungs. Every time he tried to inhale deeply, his chest muscles burned with a strange, exhausting friction. A vice-like headache had been pounding behind his eyes since they got off the ferry last night, and his fingers—usually perfectly steady on a keyboard—were fighting a terrifying, microscopic tremor.

His body, permanently conditioned for the crisp, thin, 7,000-foot oxygen of the North Cascades, was actively screaming at him. He didn't know exactly what the dense marine air was doing to his blood, but his primal instincts were flashing red: *If you stay in this room, you are going to die.*

But his mind was perfectly sharp. And right now, his mind was focused entirely on the clock.

"Vanessa," Johnny typed, his stiff, trembling fingers moving with deliberate precision across the mechanical keyboard of his heavy Quantum XN laptop. He hit the macro key. The text-to-speech software cut through the suffocating quiet of the apartment. "This is not an assessment facility. There is no barometric step-down room. You need to take me back to Anacortes. The Guardian ad Litem evaluation is supposed to start in an hour."

Vanessa Hayes didn't look up from her magazine. She was sitting on the beige velvet sofa across from his wheelchair, her bare feet tucked underneath her.

"Oh, Johnny, we don't have to worry about the court today," Vanessa sighed, her voice dripping with a sickly, patronizing sweetness. She finally looked at him, and Johnny felt his stomach drop.

She didn't look like a dedicated state healthcare provider. She looked at him with a glazed, deeply unsettling romantic possessiveness. She had promised him and his family that this 24-hour program was a medical necessity to help him build independence.

So why had she lit candles in the apartment last night? Why had she asked him about his dating history?

Johnny’s chest tightened as the suffocating memory of the previous evening surfaced, compounding the heavy, toxic weight of the marine air. Why had she crossed the room when the lights were low, sitting far too close to his chair when his tired arms were too weak to push his wheels backward? She had taken his clothes off when it wasn't his scheduled time to bathe. She had touched him in ways that defied every established rule of medical care, making his skin crawl and his stomach turn, whispering the entire time that it was a "special secret just for them."

Johnny did not possess the social or experiential vocabulary to define exactly what had happened in the dark. His world on the mountain was mathematically precise, built entirely on physical boundaries, respect, and clinical routines. But his brilliant, analytical brain recognized a profound, terrifying system violation. He didn't know the legal terminology for what she had done, but he knew with sickening clarity that she had breached his physical sovereignty while he was entirely helpless to stop her.

Johnny’s jaw tightened. He hadn't agreed to this 24-hour coastal evaluation because he trusted her. He was only sitting in this miserable apartment because of what she had done in the Bellingham hospital hallway last Friday. She had cornered him with a sealed Whatcom County Superior Court order. She had whispered that Adult Protective Services was going to strip Victoria of her Plenary Guardianship and seize the mountain unless he proved his independence at an Anacortes transition facility.

Johnny had willingly walked into the trap to protect his family's legal sovereignty. But the moment Victoria had dropped him off at the Anacortes commercial park yesterday, Vanessa had unplugged the phones, forced him out the back door, and dragged him onto a ferry to an island he couldn't escape.

Vanessa stood up, walking over to his wheelchair. Johnny forced himself not to flinch as she reached out. Her fingers brushed the hair out of his eyes, trailing down his cheek in an intimate, violating gesture that made his skin crawl.

"You don't need your aunt anymore, sweetie," Vanessa murmured, tapping the top of his laptop screen. "She just wants to keep you locked up on that freezing mountain. You and me... we're going to build a real life down here. A quiet life. We don't need to call them."

She reached behind his laptop. With a sharp tug, she pulled the gray RJ-11 phone cord out of the modem jack, disconnecting him from the apartment's landline.

She smiled, looping the cord around her hand like a trophy.

"I'm going to jump in the shower," Vanessa announced, completely satisfied that she had won by simply removing his ability to communicate with the outside world. She looked at his flushed skin and the shallow, exhausted rise of his chest, assuming she had completely broken his spirit. "When I get out, I'll make us some breakfast. Why don't you just watch the boats out the window for a bit?"

She walked down the narrow hallway. A moment later, the bathroom door clicked shut, the lock sliding into place. The pipes groaned, and the heavy hiss of the shower water began to run.

Johnny sat perfectly still in his wheelchair for exactly thirty seconds.

He listened to the water. He listened to the faint, distant horn of the Evergreen Marine ferry docking at the Friday Harbor terminal down the hill.

Then, his left hand dropped down to the side of his custom wheelchair cushion.

He wedged his fingers into the tight seam of the upholstery, feeling around blindly until his knuckles brushed against smooth plastic. He gripped it and pulled.

It was a spare, three-foot, black RJ-11 phone cable. He had stolen it from a drawer in the kitchen at 3:00 AM while she was sleeping.

Vanessa thought he was a helpless victim. She thought taking away one wire severed him from the world. She didn't understand that while his body was confined to this chair, his mind belonged to a family of apex predators who built fortresses for a living.

Johnny leaned forward, his core muscles screaming in protest, his head swimming dizzily in the heavy air. He reached behind his Quantum XN, his fingers fumbling blindly for the small square port on the back of the heavy machine.

*Click.*

He plugged the other end directly into the secondary phone jack on the wall baseboard that Vanessa didn't know he could reach.

He fell back into his chair, breathing heavily, his lungs burning. He ignored the pain. He pulled up his custom dialer interface, completely bypassing the local ISP Vanessa used. Instead, he engaged the laptop's internal 56k modem, double-checking the hardware settings to ensure the speaker was completely muted. He typed in a heavily encrypted, toll-free 1-800 number—a dedicated enterprise line set up by the family that routed directly into the primary RRAS server rack sitting 7,000 feet up a mountain in Whatcom County.

He hit ENTER.

The room remained dead silent. There was no crackle from the laptop speaker, no agonizing, static-laced screech of a 56k modem handshake filling the apartment.

To anyone else, a muted dial-up connection felt agonizingly blind. But Johnny just watched the small, gray dialogue box in the center of his screen.

*Dialing 1-800... Connecting... Verifying username and password...*

He shot a glance at the closed bathroom door. The shower was still running.

The progress bar filled. The dialogue box vanished. Down in the bottom right corner of his taskbar, the tiny icon of two overlapping computer monitors lit up blue.

SECURE CONNECTION ESTABLISHED. WELCOME TO PROSPECTOR_QUANTUM_NET. USER: J_VANCE.

Johnny pulled up his command terminal. He bypassed standard email, using the core q-send command. He couldn't use the global \[\*\] wildcard and trigger a panic among the civilian staff in the lodge. He targeted the secure, partitioned family domain.

q-send /domain:VANCE_WHITAKER I need extraction. The court order was a forgery. There is no assessment. I have been kidnapped.

He hit ENTER.

Two hundred miles away, at 7,000 feet, the effect was surgical and instantaneous. Behind closed doors, every single active Quantum XN monitor logged into the family's administrative domain froze. Victoria’s screen in the Owner's Quarters, Morgan’s diagnostics terminal in the boiler room, and Amanda's logistics manifest were hijacked by a harsh, grey dialog box.

Ten seconds later, a reply box popped up over Johnny’s terminal window. It was from Sarah, the twenty-year-old cousin who managed his guardianship paperwork.

\[SYSTEM ALERT from S_FOSTER\]: What?! Johnny, Victoria is looking at the Guardian ad Litem paperwork right now! Where is the facility?!

Johnny typed furiously, his trembling fingers slipping on the keys, fighting the agonizing brain fog.

q-send \[S_FOSTER\] The paperwork is fake. I am not in Anacortes. The moment Victoria drove away, Vanessa forced me onto a ferry. I am in Friday Harbor. I am trapped on the island.

A fifteen-second pause. Johnny could almost feel the sheer, unadulterated terror and rage erupting inside the Keep.

\[SYSTEM ALERT from S_FOSTER\]: FRIDAY HARBOR?! Johnny, my god. Victoria is mobilizing the heavy transport. We are coming to the island. Lock yourself in a room.

q-send \[S_FOSTER\] NO. Do not come to the apartment. The air here is wrong. My chest hurts. I cannot stay here. I am leaving now. There is a ferry leaving Friday Harbor at 10:55 AM. It arrives in Anacortes at 12:05. I am rolling myself onto that boat. Meet me on the mainland.

\[SYSTEM ALERT from S_FOSTER\]: Johnny, are you sure you have the strength to make it to the terminal?

q-send \[S_FOSTER\] Yes. But I need ticket money.

\[SYSTEM ALERT from S_FOSTER\]: Done. Transferring \$100 to your debit card right now from the Trust. Give me ten seconds... Okay, it's cleared. Johnny, please. If she catches you...

q-send \[S_FOSTER\] She will not. Tell Amanda to bring the breathing machine. I love you, Sarah. I am coming home.

\[SYSTEM ALERT from S_FOSTER\]: We will be waiting at the dock. Go.

Johnny killed the connection sequence. He pulled the black RJ-11 cable from his laptop and shoved it back under the cushion. He needed to leave zero digital footprint.

Through the thin walls, the shower was still running.

Johnny spun his chair around, his racing heart hammering painfully against his ribs. He unlocked the apartment's deadbolt and slipped out into the crisp, heavy marine layer of the San Juan Islands.

The door clicked shut behind him. Fighting the blinding headache and the toxic, heavy air, he engaged the high gear on his chair's rims and started the brutal, ten-minute push toward the water.
