# Part 4: The Wrong Schedule (09:15)

The water shut off, and the sudden silence in the apartment was heavy. Vanessa stepped out of the bathroom, aggressively rubbing her hair with a towel. The steam billowed out into the hallway behind her.

"Alright, Johnny," she called out, her voice dripping with artificial authority. "I hope you spent the last twenty minutes thinking about your attitude, because I am not doing this today."

Silence.

She walked into the living room, tossing the towel onto the cheap sofa. She stopped. The space next to the kitchen counter was empty.

"Johnny?"

She spun around. The bedroom door was open. She took three steps and looked inside. The bed was made, but the space underneath it, where he kept his black canvas backpack, was empty.

A cold spike of genuine terror hit Vanessa’s stomach. She rushed back into the kitchen. The landline phone sat perfectly in its cradle, the RJ-11 cord resting innocuously against the wall exactly where it always was. She didn't give it a second glance. Her eyes flew to the front door. The deadbolt was turned to the vertical, unlocked position.

*No. He wouldn't.*

He did.

Vanessa’s mind went entirely blank for a fraction of a second before the reality of the situation crashed down on her. If Johnny got on a boat, he would call his family. If he called his family, Victoria Vance would realize he wasn't in Anacortes. And if Victoria Vance realized she had lied and trapped a legally incapacitated dependent on an island to milk his Trust and state benefits...

"She is going to kill me," Vanessa whispered to the empty room. "She is going to put me in federal prison."

Panic fully took the wheel. She lunged for the kitchen drawer, ripping it open and pulling out a crumpled, tri-fold **Evergreen Marine** paper schedule. Her hands were shaking so badly she could barely track the columns. She flipped it to the Fall timetable, her eyes darting across the grid.

*9:35 AM.*

She saw the numbers and her heart hammered against her ribs. She didn't realize, in her blind panic, that her finger was tracing the *Westbound* schedule for the Anacortes-to-Lopez run. She just saw the time. She looked at the microwave clock.

*09:23 AM.*

"Dammit!"

She threw the schedule on the counter, grabbed a pair of sweatpants, shoved her bare feet into her sneakers, and bolted out the front door.

## 09:30 AM - The Friday Harbor Terminal

Vanessa sprinted the last block down to the waterfront, her lungs burning, her wet hair plastering to the side of her face in the freezing October wind. She cut across the crosswalk, ignoring a honking delivery truck, and vaulted onto the wooden planks of the ferry terminal.

She stopped, chest heaving, scanning the water.

Nothing.

The massive wooden pylons of the slip were empty. There was no giant green-and-white vessel loading cars. The staging lanes were mostly empty, save for a few local pickup trucks parked with their engines off. The toll booth agent was reading a newspaper.

Vanessa paced frantically in a tight circle, her hands pulling at the roots of her hair. *Where is it? Did it leave early? Did he make it on?* She stared out at the grey, choppy waters of the Salish Sea, completely blind to the fact that the actual Eastbound boat wasn't due for another hour and twenty minutes.

Directly across the street, sitting in the warm, espresso-scented air of the corner coffee shop, Johnny watched her.

His arms and shoulders carried a dull ache from the physical exertion of pushing his heavy manual wheelchair up the town's coastal inclines, but sitting here now, the burn felt like a trophy. He took another slow bite of his hashbrown.

Through the plate-glass window, he watched Vanessa pacing on the dock like a trapped animal. She looked wildly up and down the empty staging lanes, interrogating the horizon. Not once did she turn around. Not once did she think to walk across the street and check the local businesses. She was so convinced of her own superiority, so certain that Johnny was just a helpless, panicked kid trying to flee, that it never occurred to her he might be executing a methodical, calculated wait right behind her back.

Johnny picked up his cup of hot chocolate, took a sip, and let a small, defiant smile touch the corners of his mouth.

*Checkmate,* he thought.
