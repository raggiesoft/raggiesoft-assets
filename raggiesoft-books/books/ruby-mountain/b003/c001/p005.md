# Part 5: The Free Passage (10:35)

For an hour, Johnny sat in the warmth of the coffee shop, watching the frantic, erratic movements of his abuser through the plate-glass window.

Around 9:50 AM, Vanessa had finally walked up to the toll booth and spoken to the agent. Johnny watched her shoulders slump as she clearly received the news about the actual 10:55 AM departure time. Realizing she had an hour to kill and assuming Johnny was still somewhere in the town trying to navigate the hills, she turned her back on the water. She sprinted up Spring Street, disappearing into the town to frantically search the library and the local streets, completely abandoning her post at the dock.

She had cleared his path.

Johnny waited until 10:35 AM. Evergreen Marine usually began boarding walk-on and roll-on passengers about fifteen minutes before departure, and he wanted to be at the front of the line, minimizing his time exposed on the street.

He tossed his empty cup into the trash, exited the coffee shop, and pushed his way across the crosswalk toward the terminal. The freezing October wind whipped off the Salish Sea, stinging his face, but he didn't stop. He bypassed the massive, empty staging lanes meant for vehicles and followed the blue painted lines curving to the left, directing pedestrian traffic toward the sheltered walk-on entrance.

A small line of locals with backpacks and commuting gear had formed at the ticket window. Johnny took his place in the queue, his heart hammering a steady, nervous rhythm against his ribs. He pulled his Trust-issued debit card from his wallet, resting his thumb over the embossed numbers.

He was so close. The massive green-and-white Evergreen Marine vessel, the *M/V Yakima*, was already pulling into the slip, its massive diesel engines rumbling so deeply he could feel the vibrations in the frame of his wheelchair.

The line moved quickly. When it was his turn, Johnny rolled up to the glass window.

His fingers flew across his keyboard. "One ticket to Anacortes, please," the text-to-speech software announced clearly. He held up the debit card.

The ticket agent, a woman in a heavy fleece Evergreen Marine jacket, looked down from her booth and offered a warm, easy smile. She didn't reach for the card.

"Put your money away, sweetheart," she said, waving a hand. "You don't need a ticket."

Johnny froze, his brow furrowing in confusion. He quickly typed, "I do not understand. I am going to the mainland."

"I know," the agent chuckled kindly. "But Evergreen Marine only charges fares Westbound. Getting onto the island costs money. Leaving the islands to go back to Anacortes is totally free for passengers. You just walk right on through."

She reached out and pressed a button inside her booth. The heavy metal turnstile clicked, the locking mechanism disengaging.

Johnny stared at the open gate, the sheer weight of the moment washing over him. Vanessa had built a prison of lies. She had made him believe every single aspect of his life, his care, and his movement required her permission, her management, and a transaction. She had made the island feel like an inescapable fortress.

But the fortress had an open door. The state wasn't keeping him here. The water wasn't a wall. It was completely free to leave.

He slipped his debit card back into his wallet.

"Thank you," his computer spoke, though the digitized voice couldn't capture the overwhelming swell of relief in his chest.

"Safe travels," she replied.

Johnny pushed the wheels of his chair forward, rolling smoothly through the turnstile and out onto the covered wooden walkway leading to the ferry's passenger deck. Behind him, the turnstile clicked shut. He didn't look back at the town. He kept his eyes locked straight ahead on the massive steel boat that was about to take him home.
