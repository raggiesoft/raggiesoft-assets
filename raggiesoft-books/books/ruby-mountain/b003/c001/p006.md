# Part 6: The Widening Gap (10:55)

The *M/V Yakima* let out a deep, bone-rattling blast from its massive air horn. Deep in the hull, the heavy diesel engines shifted, sending a powerful vibration up through the steel decks and right into the frame of Johnny’s wheelchair.

He was parked at a large viewing window in the forward passenger lounge, his hands resting lightly on his wheels.

Slowly, the massive green-and-white vessel began to reverse out of the wooden slip. The water below churned into a violent, frothy white as the propellers bit into the Salish Sea.

Movement on the dock caught his eye.

Sprinting past the toll booth, completely ignoring the pedestrian walkway, was Vanessa. Her hair was wild, her clothes thrown on haphazardly. She reached the edge of the boarding gate just as the deckhands secured the heavy steel chains.

She was too late. The ferry was already fifty feet out, the gap of dark, freezing water widening by the second.

Even through the thick, salt-streaked glass of the lounge, Johnny could see the absolute rage and despair on her face. She grabbed the chain-link fence, staring at the departing boat. He watched her pull that crumpled Evergreen Marine schedule from her pocket—the one she had misread in the apartment. He watched the exact moment her eyes found the correct column for the Eastbound sailings, and the realization hit her: The next direct boat to Anacortes wasn't until 1:55 PM.

She was stranded.

Johnny let out a long, shaky breath. For the first time in months, the iron-tight knot of tension between his shoulder blades completely dissolved. He leaned back against the canvas support of his chair. The geographical moat that Vanessa had used to imprison him was now his ultimate shield. She couldn't touch him.

He had exactly an hour until the boat docked on the mainland.

He had been on Evergreen Marine ferries before with Amanda and the older cousins for summer day-trips down to Island County, but he had never navigated one alone. He took a few minutes just to revel in the sheer autonomy of it. He rolled away from the window, maneuvering easily through the wide, ADA-compliant aisles of the passenger cabin. He took the elevator up to the sun deck, braving the freezing, howling October wind for just a minute to feel the sea spray on his face and watch Friday Harbor shrink into a tiny, insignificant speck against the shoreline.

When the cold became too much, he retreated back into the heated cabin and found a quiet booth in the corner.

He opened his backpack and pulled out his Quantum XN laptop. He didn't have a dial-up connection out here on the water, but he didn't need one. He opened a blank word processing document and placed his hands over his custom keyboard.

He had made a promise to himself: He was going to tell them *everything*.

He knew how his twin sister operated. Amanda was fiercely protective, and when she saw him, she was going to be an emotional wreck. If he tried to explain the last few months using his text-to-speech software in real-time, it would take hours, and they would constantly interrupt him with questions and tears.

So, he typed.

For forty-five minutes, as the ferry cruised smoothly through the San Juan archipelago, Johnny documented the entire nightmare. He typed out the truth about the phantom "Anacortes facility," the subsidized apartment, the isolation, the gaslighting, and Vanessa's aggressive control over his communications. He laid out the entire timeline of her fraud.

By the time the captain's voice crackled over the overhead PA system, announcing their final approach into Anacortes, Johnny had a three-page document saved to his desktop.

He closed the laptop, slid it back into his bag, and rolled toward the front passenger doors. Through the forward windows, the mainland of Washington State was growing larger, the dark timber of the Cascade foothills looming in the distance.

He was ready.
