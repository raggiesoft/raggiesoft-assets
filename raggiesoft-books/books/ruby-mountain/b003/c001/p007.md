# Part 7: The Terminal (11:54)

The heavy tires of the Holt Workmaster van crunched against the gravel as Ashleigh bypassed the staging lanes and threw the vehicle into a temporary loading zone directly adjacent to the Anacortes terminal's main passenger exit. She shoved the gearshift into park and killed the engine.

It was 11:54 AM. They had beaten the boat by nine minutes.

Inside the cavernous van, the silence of the stopped engine was immediately filled with the tense, chaotic energy of the cousins trying to unravel the sheer scale of the fraud.

"I still don't understand the logistics," Sarah said, leaning over the middle row of seats, her hands nervously twisting the fabric of her coat. "If she was living in public housing on the island, what was the address on her state caregiver filings? How did she intercept the mail from the Trust?"

"She probably set up a ghost P.O. Box in Anacortes and forwarded it," Ashleigh replied, her eyes locked on the empty terminal doors. "She used the mainland address to keep us away, then just rode the Evergreen ferry back and forth to collect the checks."

In the third row, Victoria ignored the speculation. Her Quantum laptop was closed, and she was meticulously reading over the freshly printed, four-page *ex parte* restraining order she had faxed to a Whatcom County judge from a rest stop twenty minutes ago. The ink was barely dry, but the legal trap was fully set. She pulled a black fountain pen from her lapel, made one final, aggressive underline beneath Vanessa's name, and snapped the legal binder shut.

In the passenger seat, Amanda wasn't listening to Sarah's theories or watching Victoria's legal preparations. She was staring blankly out the windshield at the deep, grey waters of the Salish Sea, and she was crying.

The tears fell silently at first, but as the clock on the dashboard ticked to 12:00 PM, a jagged sob broke through her chest. She pressed the heels of her hands against her eyes. She didn't care about the forged documents. She didn't care about the state checks. She was terrified. Johnny was her fraternal twin; they shared a bond that superseded everything else. The thought of him trapped in a small apartment, stripped of his communication, and forced to navigate a maritime escape entirely on his own was tearing her apart.

"Hey," Ashleigh said softly, reaching across the center console to grip Amanda's shoulder. "He got himself to the boat. He's safe."

"I just need to see him," Amanda choked out, her voice trembling. "I need to see him right now."

At 12:03 PM, a blast from a massive air horn shook the windows of the van.

The *M/V Yakima* glided around the edge of the breakwater. The sheer size of the Evergreen Marine vessel was staggering, its green-and-white superstructure blocking out the horizon as the captain expertly reversed the massive engines, churning the water white to guide the boat into the wooden slip.

"How long?" Amanda asked, wiping her face, her hand already gripping the door handle.

"Three to five minutes," Victoria stated with calm authority from the back seat. "They have to secure the mooring lines and lower the overhead pedestrian ramp. Walk-on passengers disembark first, before the car deck is cleared."

The waiting was agonizing. The cousins sat in the van, watching the deckhands in high-visibility jackets throw the heavy ropes over the massive steel cleats. The hydraulic whine of the passenger ramp lowering into place echoed across the parking lot.

At 12:07 PM, the glass doors of the terminal building finally slid open.

A wave of commuters, tourists, and locals poured out onto the pavement. Ashleigh, Sarah, and Amanda held their breath, their eyes frantically scanning the crowd.

And then, the crowd parted slightly.

Moving smoothly down the center of the concrete ramp, his black canvas backpack slung over the handles of his manual wheelchair, was Johnny. He looked exhausted, the freezing wind whipping at his jacket, but the moment he cleared the terminal doors and saw the massive Holt Workmaster van idling in the loading zone, he stopped.

He didn't hit his text-to-speech keyboard. He didn't wave. He just looked at the van, and his shoulders completely dropped in a silent, overwhelming exhale of relief.

Amanda threw the passenger door open and hit the pavement running.
