# Part 6: The Rationalization (17:15)

Vanessa sat on the edge of the cheap sofa, the silence of the Friday Harbor apartment pressing against her eardrums. Outside, the sun was beginning its early descent, casting long, cold shadows across the linoleum floor.

She hadn't gone back to the docks. She hadn't looked at the ferry schedules. She had simply walked back up the hill, locked the deadbolt, and collapsed.

For hours, her mind had spun through catastrophic legal scenarios, but as the afternoon wore on, a desperate, hollow rationalization began to take root.

*They have him,* she told herself, staring at the blank television screen. *That’s all Victoria Vance really cares about. She got her precious nephew back to her ridiculous mountain fortress.*

Why would a multi-millionaire lawyer waste her time dragging a subsidized state caregiver into a Whatcom County courtroom? It would mean dragging Johnny into the public eye, putting his condition on the record, and the Vance-Whitakers guarded their privacy like paranoid shut-ins. They didn't want a messy public trial.

*It's going to blow over,* Vanessa decided, the delusion solidifying into a twisted sense of relief. *They'll cut my access to the Trust, cancel the state checks, and just lock their gates. I just need to lay low.*

A sharp, dull cramp in her lower abdomen suddenly broke her train of thought.

Vanessa groaned, leaning forward and resting her forehead against her knees. The adrenaline crash from the morning's panic was already making her feel physically ill, and now her body was signaling the start of her cycle.

"Perfect," she muttered to the empty room. "Just perfect."

She pushed herself off the sofa and walked down the short hallway to the bathroom. She flicked on the harsh overhead vanity light, splashing some cold water on her face before dealing with the physical reality of the cramps.

But a moment later, she frowned in confusion.

It was incredibly light. Barely a spot.

She sat there for a moment, her annoyance shifting into a dull, tired frustration. This wasn't how her cycle normally started. It was weak, inconsistent, and the color was entirely wrong. It didn't make any sense.

Vanessa sighed heavily, pressing the heels of her hands into her eyes. *Stress,* she concluded immediately.

Her heart had been hammering against her ribs since she stepped out of the shower at nine o'clock that morning. Her cortisol levels were probably through the roof. The sheer panic of staring down the barrel of a kidnapping charge, the furious sprint to the docks, the realization that her financial pipeline was severed—it was more than enough to throw her entire biological system out of rhythm. Her body was just reacting to the trauma of the day.

She dealt with it, washed her hands, and aggressively flicked off the bathroom light.

She walked back into the darkening living room, feeling a strange, empty sense of finality. The physical irregularity was just another annoying symptom of a disastrous twenty-four hours. She pulled a blanket off the back of the sofa and curled into the cushions, completely convinced that the worst of the storm had finally passed.
