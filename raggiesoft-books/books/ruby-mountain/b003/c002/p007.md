# Part 7: Johnny is Home (17:25)

"Don't take the Owner's gate," Amanda said suddenly from the passenger seat, leaning forward as the Holt Workmaster van crested the final ridge. "Take the main drive. Let him come through the front doors."

Ashleigh glanced in the rearview mirror, caught Victoria’s approving nod, and banked the heavy van toward the colossal timber porte-cochère of the main hotel entrance.

The Prospector's Keep was bustling with the late-summer rush. The massive circular driveway was a chaotic, perfectly choreographed dance of luxury SUVs, bellhops pushing brass luggage carts, and wealthy tourists returning from the alpine hiking trails. The air was crisp with the approaching autumn, filled with the steady, ambient energy of high-end mountain hospitality.

Ashleigh bypassed the line of waiting cars, pulling the massive van right up to the VIP loading zone.

The heavy sliding door rolled open.

Ashleigh immediately jumped out and moved to the rear cargo doors, pulling Johnny’s manual wheelchair free and unfolding it with a sharp, metallic clack. She rolled it right up to the edge of the van's floorboard and locked the brakes.

Amanda stepped out first. With the effortless, synchronized routine of a twin who had done this ten thousand times, she leaned in, wrapped her arms securely under Johnny’s arms, and smoothly transferred his weight from the bench seat down into his chair.

The second he was seated, the crisp, highly oxygenated mountain air hit Johnny’s face.

He unzipped his jacket a few inches and took a massive, effortless breath. He looked up at the towering, four-story stone chimneys and the colossal Douglas fir logs. For the weekend, his world had been defined by cramped drywall and the heavy, suffocating marine layer of the coast. Now, he was sitting at the threshold of a fortress.

Amanda clicked his brake levers off, gripped the worn rubber handles of his chair, and pushed him forward over the cobblestones.

The massive glass-and-iron front doors of the lobby were propped wide open. Standing just inside the threshold, completely ignoring the paying guests swirling around them, was a wall of twelve women.

The rest of the Pack.

Diana, the 25-year-old Head Chef, was still wearing her flour-dusted apron. Morgan and Megan, the Whitaker electrician and plumber, were in their heavy canvas work jackets. Clara and Maya, the Vance mechanics, were flanking the younger teenagers who were practically vibrating with anticipation.

The second Amanda pushed Johnny’s front casters over the brass threshold, the entire group surged forward.

For Johnny, his autism meant that crowds, loud noises, and sudden physical contact were usually deeply overwhelming. He required strict boundaries and quiet spaces to regulate his nervous system. But this was the one, profound exception. This wasn't a crowd. This was his pack.

They swarmed his wheelchair.

Diana reached him first, dropping to her knees right there in the lobby to wrap her arms around his neck in a massive, crushing hug. "Look at you, kid," she choked out, burying her face in his jacket. "We got you. We got you."

Then came Clara and Maya, wrapping him in arms that smelled of boiler grease and pine resin. Then Morgan, Grace, Sarah, and the younger girls. The hugs came in a relentless, tearful, overwhelming wave. Johnny didn't flinch. He didn't retreat. He leaned into them, resting his head against shoulders, his face buried in the familiar scents of woodsmoke, coffee, and mountain air. He closed his eyes, letting the sheer, terrifying weight of his three-month isolation completely dissolve.

"Okay, okay, give him some air," Victoria commanded gently, stepping through the doors behind them with his Quantum laptop tucked under her arm. "Let him see his house."

The sea of cousins parted, wiping their eyes and laughing, allowing Amanda to push Johnny fully into the center of the room.

The sensory impact of the lobby was staggering.

The Great Hall of The Prospector's Keep was a masterpiece of 1903 pioneer architecture. The late afternoon sun streamed through the massive frosted windowpanes, catching the dust motes dancing in the high-vaulted ceilings. Only one of the three colossal, walk-in fireplaces was lit to cut the early September chill, casting a warm, flickering light over the antique leather furniture. Waitstaff moved efficiently through the room, offering high tea and coffee to the hikers and tourists lounging in the armchairs.

Every surface gleamed. Every detail was flawless. It was a monument to their family's century of survival on the mountain.

Johnny sat in his chair, his cousins forming a protective, impenetrable ring around him to keep the oblivious tourists at bay. He looked at the massive grandfather clock ticking steadily near the reception desk. Johnny looked up over his shoulder at Amanda, a massive, uncontainable smile breaking across his face.

He was in his natural habitat. He was home.
