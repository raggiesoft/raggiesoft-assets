# Part 1: The Proxy (10:00)

The morning light filtering through the reinforced windows of the second-floor sanctuary was entirely different from the grey, marine haze of Friday Harbor. Here, at 7,000 feet, the light was sharp, brilliant, and completely unobstructed.

Johnny Vance sat at his custom-built workstation, his manual wheelchair locked securely into place. Resting in front of him was his heavy, black Quantum XN laptop. The machine was booted up, running smoothly on the Keep's localized network, and the screen displayed a PDF from the Bellingham Community College registrar.

The heavy oak door to the suite clicked open, and Victoria walked in. The Matriarch was carrying a sleek, silver travel mug of black coffee and a manila file folder. She pulled up a chair next to his desk, her presence radiating a calm, absolute authority.

"I've reviewed the BCC syllabus you pulled off the server," Victoria said, opening the manila folder and laying the printed documents flat on the desk. "The coursework is rigorous. The networking protocols align perfectly with what Morgan is doing for her own degree at Northwest Pacific University. Let's get the proxy finalized."

The Vance-Whitaker pack ran a strict, two-tier educational system to survive the winter isolation. The minor-aged cousins, like Emma, operated under a K-12 Alternative Learning Experience (ALE) contract negotiated with the local rural school district in Concrete, Washington. But the adults—Johnny, Sarah, Morgan—operated in the realm of higher education. They utilized Bellingham Community College for their foundational degrees, eventually transferring to Northwest Pacific University's remote learning portal to finish their advanced coursework.

Johnny’s fingers flew across his mechanical keyboard.

"I need the Disability Services Accommodation Form and the Enrollment Proxy signed," his text-to-speech engine announced in its flat, digitized cadence. "If it is not submitted by Friday, I forfeit the Fall Quarter."

Victoria didn't hesitate, and she certainly didn't patronize him. Because she held Plenary Guardianship and Conservatorship, she had absolute legal jurisdiction over Johnny's educational estate. She didn't need a caseworker's permission, and she certainly didn't need Vanessa.

Victoria pulled her heavy black fountain pen from her blazer pocket. She verified the course load, flipped to the final page of the proxy, and signed her name with a sweeping, aggressive signature that carried the full, terrifying weight of the Vance-Whitaker Trust.

"You aren't forfeiting anything," Victoria stated, sliding the signed paper over to him. "You are going to class."

Johnny took the paper, placing it carefully onto the flatbed scanner next to his desk. The machine hummed, converting her signature into a high-resolution digital file.

With his paperwork complete, Johnny initiated the Keep's specialized "Batch Upload" protocol. Because the family would soon be cut off from the planet by thirty feet of snow, standard daily internet usage could be temperamental during massive blizzards. Instead of relying on a constant live connection, Johnny zipped the encrypted enrollment files and routed them through the encrypted satellite uplink, sending them directly to the BCC registrar via a secure FTP client.

He hit ENTER. The progress bar flashed green.

He didn't have to worry about winter storms disrupting his midterms, either. Victoria had aggressively negotiated a "High-Altitude Distance Exception" with both BCC and Northwest Pacific University. The state colleges were legally bound to grant Johnny and his cousins rolling extensions on coursework if a Cascade blizzard knocked out their satellite connection for a week.

The progress bar hit one hundred percent. *TRANSMISSION COMPLETE.*

Johnny looked up at Victoria. The agonizing anxiety of the Friday Harbor lie was gone. His academic timeline was secure, safely hosted on the massive Quantum OS 2003 Server humming eighty feet below them in the bedrock.

His left hand dropped to his keyboard.

"Enrollment confirmed," the synthetic voice chimed into the quiet room. "I am ready for the winter."

Victoria smiled, picking up her coffee. "Good man. Class starts in two weeks. Take the rest of the day off; Amanda is threatening to drag you down to the Great Hall to help with the Gala prep."
