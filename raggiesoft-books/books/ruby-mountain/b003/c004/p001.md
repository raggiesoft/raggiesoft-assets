# Part 1: Pro Se (08:50)

The heavy double doors of Courtroom 3B swung open, and Vanessa Hayes stepped onto the polished linoleum floor of the Whatcom County Superior Court.

She paused just inside the doorway, adjusting the lapels of her modest beige suit. She was exhausted, her shoulders tight with tension. Because the county transit buses didn't run on Sundays, she had been forced to leave San Juan Island on Saturday. She had spent the last two nights trapped in a cheap, sixty-dollar-a-night motel three blocks from the courthouse, eating vending machine food and watching local television.

She had put the entire weekend on a credit card that was already maxed out. But she wasn't worried. In her mind, the motel was an investment. Once the judge granted her custody this morning, she would finally gain access to Johnny's state benefits and Social Security backpay. She would pay off the card, take the boy back to Friday Harbor, and finally be in control.

She took a breath, trying to project the image of a deeply dedicated, overworked healthcare professional.

Beside her, public defender Arthur Davies scanned the room, his worn leather briefcase clutched in his hand.

"Alright, Vanessa, listen to me," Davies murmured, leaning in close. "We are going up against serious generational wealth here. Do not let them intimidate you. We stick to the script: you are the certified professional, and this restraining order is a massive overreaction by an isolated, eccentric family."

Vanessa nodded tightly, scanning the room.

The courtroom was mostly empty, save for the first two rows of the public gallery. Sitting dead center in the polished wooden pews was the Vance-Whitaker pack.

They didn't look like people who had spent their weekend navigating bus schedules. At 6:00 AM that morning, Victoria, Amanda, Johnny, Sarah, and Ashleigh had loaded into the Keep’s heavy transport—a massive, matte-black, 4x4 commercial van. Because Morgan had hermetically sealed and pressurized the passenger cabin to perfectly match the 7,000-foot altitude of Ruby Mountain, Johnny hadn't felt the grueling atmospheric drop to sea level at all. They had simply driven out of the Cascades in a rolling fortress, arriving at the courthouse completely rested, perfectly tailored, and unbothered.

Amanda sat with her arm draped protectively across the back of Johnny’s manual wheelchair, her hand resting just inches from the portable RespiraCore Compressor securely mounted to the heavy steel frame. Flanking them on either side were Sarah and Ashleigh Foster, both wearing sharp, dark sweaters, their eyes tracking Vanessa with the synchronized, unsettling intensity of apex predators.

Johnny didn't look terrified. He didn't look like a victim who had just spent time trapped in a subsidized apartment. He was completely relaxed, the quiet, rhythmic *hiss-click* of the compressor feeding a steady flow of pressurized air through a discreet nasal cannula, compensating for the grueling drop to sea level. His stiff fingers rested lightly on the keyboard of his Quantum XN laptop, ignoring Vanessa entirely.

Davies followed Vanessa’s gaze, but his eyes bypassed the gallery and landed on the petitioner’s counsel table at the front of the room.

He stopped walking. A confused frown creased his forehead.

Sitting completely alone at the heavy oak table was Victoria Vance. She wore a tailored charcoal blazer, her posture impossibly straight. She wasn't nervously flipping through papers or whispering frantically to a legal team. There was no legal team. She was simply aligning a single leather-bound portfolio and a silver fountain pen perfectly parallel to the edge of the table.

"Where is her representation?" Vanessa whispered, a sudden spike of anxiety hitting her chest. "Did they settle? Did they back down?"

Davies let out a short, incredulous scoff. His shoulders instantly dropped, the tension completely leaving his body.

"No, Vanessa. Look at the table," Davies said, a smug, patronizing smile creeping onto his face. "She didn't hire counsel. She’s appearing *pro se*."

"Pro se?" Vanessa asked, unfamiliar with the term.

"It means she is representing herself," Davies explained, guiding Vanessa toward the respondent’s table. "It happens all the time with arrogant, wealthy people. They think because they run a successful business, they know more than a judge. It is the single biggest mistake you can make in court. Judges absolutely despise *pro se* litigants because they don't know the rules of evidence and they waste the court's time."

Vanessa let out a long, shaky breath, a triumphant smirk finally breaking through her manufactured anxiety.

She sat down at the table, looking across the aisle at Victoria. The Matriarch didn't look back. She simply sat in silence, radiating a cold, impenetrable calm.

*She's too cheap to hire a lawyer,* Vanessa thought, her confidence surging. *She actually thinks she can just talk her way out of this.*

At 9:00 AM, the heavy wooden door behind the bench opened.

"All rise!" the bailiff barked.

The Honorable Judge Harrison took his seat at the elevated bench, adjusting his reading glasses as he opened the thick case file in front of him.

"Be seated," Judge Harrison commanded. The courtroom settled with a collective rustle of clothing and creaking wood. "We are here on the matter of the Vance-Whitaker Trust versus Vanessa Hayes. Cause number 03-4-01289-2. This is a Show Cause hearing to determine why the emergency *ex parte* restraining order and temporary injunctions issued this past weekend against the respondent, Ms. Hayes, should not remain in full and permanent effect."

The judge looked down at the respondent’s table. "Counsel, state your appearance for the record."

Davies stood up, buttoning his suit jacket. "Arthur Davies, Your Honor, appearing on behalf of the respondent, Ms. Vanessa Hayes."

"Thank you, Mr. Davies," the judge said, making a note. He then shifted his gaze across the aisle to the solitary figure sitting at the petitioner's table. He frowned slightly, mirroring Davies' earlier confusion.

"Ms. Vance," Judge Harrison said, his tone carrying a hint of judicial impatience. "I see you are seated at the counsel table. Is your attorney delayed in traffic, or are you intending to proceed *pro se* this morning? Because I must warn you, these proceedings are highly technical, and the court expects proper legal procedure when deciding on a permanent injunction of this magnitude."

Victoria stood up. The movement was smooth, deliberate, and commanded the absolute attention of every person in the room.

"Neither, Your Honor," Victoria stated. Her voice was perfectly modulated, carrying easily to the back of the gallery where Johnny was watching. "I am not appearing *pro se*, nor am I waiting for counsel."

Davies let out a quiet, mocking sigh, leaning back in his chair.

Victoria picked up a single document from her leather portfolio.

"Victoria Vance," she stated, providing her official Washington State Bar Association admission number clearly for the court reporter to log. "I am appearing in my capacity as lead counsel for the Vance-Whitaker Trust, and as the fully credentialed, Plenary legal representative for the protected party. We are ready to proceed."

Davies’ smug smile instantly vanished. He sat bolt upright, staring at her.

Judge Harrison paused, looking down at the credential filing in his folder. He adjusted his glasses, verifying the state bar admission. A look of distinct realization washed over his face. He wasn't dealing with an arrogant hotel owner. He was dealing with an officer of the court.

"I see," Judge Harrison said, his tone shifting immediately from patronizing to strictly professional. "Very well, Counselor. The burden of proof is on the petitioner. Ms. Vance, you may begin your argument."

Davies swallowed hard, his throat suddenly dry.

Vanessa, completely oblivious to the massive shift in the room's legal gravity, adjusted her beige suit one last time. She looked at Victoria, ready to play the victim.
