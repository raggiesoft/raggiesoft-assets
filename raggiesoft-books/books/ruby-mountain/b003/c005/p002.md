# Part 2: The Sovereign Blueprint (14:30)

The low, rumbling vibration of the boiler room was interrupted by a quiet, rhythmic tapping. Johnny’s stiff fingers hit a pre-programmed macro key on his laptop, sending a single, sharp chime through his software.

Morgan paused mid-sip, lowering her coffee mug. She read his body language instantly—the subtle shift in his posture, the slight tightening of his jaw.

"Biological clock?" Morgan asked casually, already standing up from her metal desk.

Johnny offered a single, tight nod.

"Alright, let's get it done before Victoria starts pacing a hole in the floorboards upstairs," Morgan said.

She walked around the server rack, unplugging his ethernet tether and closing his Quantum XN laptop, sliding it safely into the custom pouch on the back of his chair. She unlocked his brakes and wheeled him out of the main engineering bay and into the adjacent washroom.

It was a single, heavy-duty industrial room, retrofitted with reinforced ADA grab bars and a commercial sink, the walls lined with the same 1903 bedrock as the rest of the lower level. Inside, the environment shifted to pure, clinical efficiency. The Vance-Whitaker pack operated without the rigid, arbitrary boundaries of the civilian world.

Morgan locked his wheelchair brakes next to the toilet. She didn't hesitate or ask if he was ready. She simply leaned down, wrapped her strong, grease-smudged arms securely around his torso, and hoisted his dead weight upward in a flawless, effortless deadlift carry. Johnny’s arms naturally looped around her neck, his core spasticity melting away in the absolute security of her grip.

She transferred him to the porcelain seat, adjusting his clothing with brisk, dignified efficiency.

Then, Morgan dropped to one knee directly in front of him. She rested her hands lightly on his knees to keep his legs from spasming outward, and she locked her gaze entirely onto his dark eyes.

This was the absolute, non-negotiable rule of the mountain. Johnny was prone to absence seizures—silent, terrifying neurological misfires where his brain would simply pause. If one triggered while he was sitting unsupported on the toilet, his body would instantly go slack, pitching him forward onto the hard tile before he could even make a sound.

Morgan didn't look at the walls. She didn't check her watch. She held his gaze with the fierce, unwavering intensity of a Vanguard holding a perimeter.

"So, I was thinking about the WSDOT crew for the Gala," Morgan said, her voice completely casual, contrasting the intense, life-saving lock of her eyes. "If we seat them near the east radiators, they're going to roast in their dress uniforms. We might need to ask Diana to bump their table closer to the drafty windows."

Johnny blinked in acknowledgment, his analytical brain engaging with the logistical puzzle, completely comfortable in the extreme vulnerability of the moment. He was safe. This was exactly how his world was supposed to work.

When he was finished, Morgan stood up, hoisting him once more and seamlessly transferring him back into the customized foam of his wheelchair seat.

With Johnny safely secured and his brakes locked, Morgan simply turned around, unfastened her coveralls, and used the toilet herself. There was no hesitation, no request for privacy, and no embarrassment. It was just biology.

"Honestly, I'm just glad the heavy equipment is running smoothly," Morgan continued seamlessly over the sound of the flushing water, stepping over to the commercial sink to wash her hands with heavy-duty pumice soap. "If that boiler acts up on the night of the Centennial, Victoria will personally feed me to the wolves."

Morgan dried her hands, walked back over to his chair, and gave his shoulder a firm squeeze.

"Alright, boss," she smiled. "Let's get you upstairs."

Morgan pushed him out of the washroom and toward the heavy, iron-grated freight elevator. Waiting for them at the massive steel doors was Victoria Vance.

The Matriarch of Ruby Mountain looked immaculate. She was wearing a tailored charcoal blazer and dark slacks, the picture of absolute, unshakeable authority. But the moment the elevator doors rattled open and she saw Johnny, the hard, legal armor she wore for the civilian world softened entirely.

Morgan locked the brakes on the chair. "Delivered in one piece," she grinned, leaning down to wrap her arms around Johnny in a tight, grease-smelling hug. "I'll see you at dinner, kid."

Johnny leaned into the embrace, immensely grateful for the Lead Engineer.

As Morgan headed back to the boiler room, Victoria stepped forward. She didn't offer a brisk pat on the shoulder. She dropped to a crouch, wrapping her arms around Johnny’s shoulders and pressing her cheek firmly against the side of his head. It was a deep, grounding hold—a physical reaffirmation that the nightmare in Friday Harbor was over, that the fortress had held, and that her cousin was exactly where he belonged.

Johnny closed his eyes, resting his head against Victoria’s shoulder, his breathing perfectly steady in the crisp, highly oxygenated mountain air.

"Welcome home, Johnny," Victoria murmured quietly, her voice carrying the absolute weight of the Vance-Whitaker Trust. She pulled back, her sharp eyes scanning his face to ensure he was fully present. "Ready to get to work?"

Johnny gave a firm, enthusiastic nod.

Victoria stood up, stepping behind his wheelchair and pushing him out of the freight bay. They moved through the opulent, bustling corridors of the Keep, bypassing the civilian staff and the wealthy VIPs entirely, until they reached the heavy oak doors of the Owner's Quarters.

Victoria’s office was a sanctuary of power. The walls were lined with floor-to-ceiling bookshelves housing a century of legal ledgers, tax codes, and architectural blueprints. A massive, roaring fireplace commanded one wall, while a sprawling mahogany desk sat in front of a bay window overlooking the golden, larch-covered valley below.

Victoria wheeled him directly up to the side of the mahogany desk, giving him equal standing at the table. She pulled his Quantum XN laptop from the pouch, setting it in front of him and plugging it into the room's dedicated ethernet port.

The desk was completely buried under stacks of paper: seating charts, WSDOT highway clearing invoices, and Diana’s multi-course catering manifests.

"We are exactly thirty days out from the Halloween Gala," Victoria said, taking her high-backed leather chair and sliding a thick, leather-bound binder toward him. "Four hundred guests. The centennial toast at 20:00 hours, followed by the End-of-Season lockdown procedures on November 1st."

She leaned forward, resting her elbows on the desk, treating him not as a dependent, but as her brilliant, analytical Second-in-Command.

"I have the civilian logistics locked down," Victoria continued, her eyes sharp. "But I need your eyes on the internal timeline. WSDOT is predicting a late November closure. Based on Morgan's telemetry today, do we trust their timeline? Or do I need to order Emma's perimeter crew to start moving the heavy, non-perishable equipment down to the Mount Vernon storage units next week just in case the weather turns?"

Johnny’s fingers hovered over his mechanical keyboard, a profound sense of purpose settling into his chest. This was his domain. He wasn't just surviving the mountain; he was helping to command it. He looked at the WSDOT timeline, cross-referenced it with the flawless 1018 millibar reading Morgan had just shown him, and began to type.
