# Part 7: The Final Perimeter (19:15)

By the time the last of the food was gone, Johnny’s eyes were heavy, his blinks growing slow and uncoordinated.

Even with the heavy, grounding weight of Amanda’s arm across his chest and the caloric comfort of Diana’s cooking, the sheer mental exhaustion of the day had finally caught up with him. He had spent his afternoon mapping out supply chain logistics and reviewing the legal execution of his abuser. His processor was completely out of power. He was ready to shut down.

Emma recognized the shift in his breathing instantly. She quietly slid off the edge of the mattress, gathering the heavy ceramic bowls and the empty glass tumblers onto the wooden serving tray.

"I'll take these down to the kitchens," Emma whispered, balancing the tray on her hip. She stepped up to the side of the bed, leaning over to press a soft, affectionate kiss to Johnny’s forehead. "Goodnight, boss. We've got the watch."

Johnny offered a small, exhausted blink of acknowledgment. Emma slipped out the door, the heavy latch clicking silently shut behind her.

Amanda sat up, reaching over to the nightstand. She pulled open her own laptop, tapping into the localized Vance-Whitaker network. She pulled up the secure chat window she shared with the Co-Guardians.

**\[A_VANCE\]:** *Initiating lockdown. Johnny is tapped out. When you two come up for the night, please observe strict acoustic protocols. Do not wake him.*

**\[S_FOSTER\]:** *Copy that. We are wrapping up the federal dossier now. We will be ghosts.*

Amanda closed the laptop. She turned back to Johnny, offering a gentle, tired smile. "Alright, kid. Let's get you squared away."

Because Amanda lacked the brute physical strength of Morgan or Ashleigh, she couldn't execute a flawless deadlift carry. Instead, their transfers were a masterclass in deeply practiced, lifelong coordination. Amanda retrieved his wheelchair and locked it tightly against the side of the heavy oak bed. She slid a polished wooden transfer board beneath him. Moving slowly and deliberately, she supported his torso while he used his remaining upper-body strength to slide across the board and into the chair.

In the master bathroom, the routine was equally measured. Amanda assisted him with a quiet, dignified efficiency that completely erased the horrors of the island apartment. There was no hesitation, no lingering, and absolutely no violation of protocol. She helped him wash his face with warm water and the familiar eucalyptus soap, then guided him into soft, loose-fitting cotton pajamas that wouldn't agitate his spasticity during the night.

By the time they navigated the transfer back into the center of the massive oak bed, Johnny was practically asleep.

Amanda pulled the heavy down comforter up over his shoulders. She climbed in beside him, pulling him flush against her chest once more, resuming the deep-pressure hold that anchored his nervous system to the mountain.

"I've got you," Amanda whispered into the dark room, resting her chin on the top of his head. "You're safe."

Johnny didn't have the energy to type or nod. He simply let out a long, slow breath, his rigid muscles finally, completely surrendering to the mattress. He was asleep within minutes.

Two hours later, the heavy oak door of the suite clicked open with agonizing care.

Ashleigh and Sarah slipped into the room, stepping out of their boots by the door to avoid making a sound on the hardwood floor. The room was bathed in the faint, silver glow of the moonlight filtering through the frosted windowpanes.

Sarah paused at the foot of the twins' bed.

Johnny was fast asleep, his breathing slow and rhythmic. Amanda was wrapped tightly around him, her arm thrown protectively across his chest, her own eyes closed in deep, exhausted slumber. They looked exactly as they were supposed to—a single, unbroken unit, safely locked inside a fortress that loved them.

Ashleigh stepped up beside her sister, a soft, incredibly protective smile touching her lips. The legal war was paused for the night. The federal dossiers were printed. The grifter was rotting in a cell.

They had done their jobs.

Moving in absolute, synchronized silence, the Co-Guardians crossed the room to the second massive oak bed. They changed into their own sleepwear, pulled the heavy quilts back, and climbed in side-by-side.

Sarah reached over to the bedside lamp. She looked across the quiet, peaceful room one last time, ensuring the perimeter was flawless.

*Click.*

The light went out. The 1903 boiler rumbled deep beneath the floorboards, keeping the winter at bay, and the Prospector’s Keep slept.
