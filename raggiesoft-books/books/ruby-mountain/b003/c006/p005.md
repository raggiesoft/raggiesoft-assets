# Part 5: The Tactical Seed (12:45)

The heavy oak doors of the Owner's Quarters swung open, and the roaring warmth of Victoria’s massive fireplace washed over them.

After the heavy, suffocating humidity of the Skagit gorge and the biting, wet wind of the loading dock, the climate-controlled sanctuary of the Owner's Quarters felt like an absolute triumph of engineering. Johnny took a deep, unassisted breath of the dry, highly oxygenated alpine air. His core spasticity, already calming from the ascent, finally settled back into its comfortable baseline.

Victoria was sitting behind her mahogany desk, reviewing a stack of WSDOT highway clearing manifests. She looked up as Morgan wheeled Johnny up to his designated position at her right flank.

"I assume the Château St. Claire survived the air-gap?" Victoria asked, setting her fountain pen down.

"Not a single shattered bottle," Morgan confirmed, dropping into one of the heavy leather chairs opposite the desk. "Diana's crew has it secured in the cellar. And the Lower Motorpool is locked down tight."

Victoria gave a single, satisfied nod. She hadn't actually needed a status report on the subterranean bunker. "Emma holds the line. I never doubted it."

Johnny let his stiff hands rest on his keyboard. He looked out the massive bay window behind Victoria’s desk. Up here at seven thousand feet, the tropical moisture of the Pineapple Express was already beginning to freeze as it collided with the high-altitude air. The sky wasn't just dumping snow; it was violently erasing the horizon.

Johnny had just seen the sheer panic in the eyes of a professional 18-wheeler driver at a mere twelve hundred feet. He had felt the crushing weight of the atmospheric river in his own lungs.

His analytical processor began to run the tactical probabilities. The math was starting to look increasingly hostile.

Johnny’s fingers moved deliberately over his mechanical keyboard. He hit the macro key.

"The perimeter is currently secure," his text-to-speech software announced smoothly into the quiet office. "But the atmospheric variables are degrading faster than the historical models predict. Victoria, I recommend we begin running contingency logistics."

Victoria leaned forward, her sharp, intelligent eyes locking onto her Second-in-Command. "Contingency logistics for what, Johnny?"

"For the Centennial Gala," Johnny’s software replied, his dark eyes meeting hers. "If WSDOT cannot maintain the pioneer trench against this loading event, October 31st is a mathematical liability. We should reconsider the viability of the Halloween timeline."

It was a bold, massive strategic suggestion. Moving a four-hundred-person luxury event was a logistical nightmare that most commercial resorts wouldn't even entertain until the state explicitly forced their hand. But Johnny didn't care about hospitality industry norms. He cared about the physical safety of the Vanguard.

Victoria didn't dismiss him. She didn't offer a patronizing reassurance that everything would be fine. She treated his assessment with the profound, absolute respect it deserved.

The Matriarch looked out the window at the howling whiteout, her jaw tightening.

"I am not ready to declare a full retreat from the Halloween timeline yet," Victoria said, her voice dropping into its cold, sovereign register. "We are thirty days out. But your tactical assessment is noted, and it is entirely valid. I will not trap this family behind a wall of ice for the sake of a party."

Victoria turned her gaze to her Lead Engineer.

"Morgan," Victoria commanded. "You own the sky. I want you tracking that Gulf anomaly every single hour. Do not take your eyes off the PK-7015 feed."

Morgan sat forward, her posture shifting from casual mechanic to apex forecaster.

"I won't," Morgan promised, her eyes flicking between Victoria and Johnny. "Miller at WSDOT is watching the same B.C. arctic front we are. If that barometric pressure blinks, or if the Snow Water Equivalent starts pushing past the safe loading threshold for the Liberty Bell chutes... you two will be the very first to know."

Johnny gave a firm nod, leaning back into the custom foam of his wheelchair.

The tactical seed was planted. The timeline was officially on notice. He closed his eyes, immensely relieved to be safely locked inside the impenetrable stone walls of his mountain, while the Fog of War raged outside.
