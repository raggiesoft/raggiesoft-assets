# Part 1: The Backchannel (10:30)

For the last forty-eight hours, the subterranean engineering bay had operated in a state of hyper-focused, clinical tension.

The flawless 1018 millibar baseline was a ghost. PK-7015, the Keep's military-grade weather station, was currently reading 1004 mb and dropping. The air in the boiler room felt thick and charged, mirroring the atmospheric violence brewing thousands of miles away.

Morgan Foster sat at her heavy metal desk, staring at the raw data scrolling down her CRT monitor. Beside her, Johnny’s rigid hands were resting over his mechanical keyboard, his own screen mirrored to the Northwest Avalanche Center’s secondary servers.

Then, the heavy, encrypted satellite phone mounted to the server rack began to ring.

It wasn't the standard digital trill of the civilian landlines upstairs. It was a harsh, mechanical burst—the dedicated frequency used strictly by the state authorities.

Morgan didn't hesitate. She snatched the heavy receiver off the cradle and slammed her thumb onto the speakerphone button so Johnny could hear.

"Keep Telemetry, Foster speaking," Morgan answered, her voice dropping into its cold, authoritative register.

*"Morgan. It’s Miller at WSDOT Seattle."*

The voice of the lead regional forecaster echoed through the cavernous boiler room. He didn't sound like a state official. He sounded exhausted, terrified, and deeply informal. This wasn't an official dispatch. This was a quiet, off-the-record backchannel between two heavy-weight meteorologists who knew exactly what the data meant.

"Talk to me, Miller," Morgan said, her eyes narrowing at her screen. "Tell me the Gulf stall held."

*"It didn't,"* Miller exhaled sharply into the receiver. *"The 30-day models just completely collapsed on our end. That Pineapple Express you flagged on Tuesday? It finally picked a trajectory. It’s moving inland, fast, and it is dragging an ocean of warm water with it."*

Johnny’s dark eyes darted to his screen. His stiff fingers hit a macro key, bringing up the northern tracking grids. He saw it immediately.

"We are tracking a secondary variable," Johnny’s text-to-speech software cut through the static of the speakerphone. "British Columbia radar indicates a massive arctic front dropping south across the border."

*"Your second-in-command is right on the money,"* Miller said grimly. *"That's why I'm calling you on the secure line, Morgan. If that warm atmospheric river collides with the B.C. freeze, the collision zone is going to stall directly over the Cascade Crest. Right on top of Ruby Mountain."*

Morgan leaned forward, her jaw tightening. "Give me the loading projection."

*"It’s going to be a historic, catastrophic loading event,"* Miller replied bluntly. *"The temperature differentials are going to create 'Cascades Concrete' at an unprecedented rate. Morgan, my snowplow operators are good, but if the Liberty Bell avalanche chutes load the way my new models are suggesting... I cannot make any promises that State Route 20 stays open past the third week of October. We might have to drop the gates a month early."*

A heavy, absolute silence fell over the engineering bay, save for the rhythmic roaring of the 1903 boiler.

A mid-November closure was standard. A late-October closure was a logistical nightmare. But the *third* week of October? That meant the mountain was about to violently slam shut, locking four hundred billionaires, caterers, and politicians behind millions of tons of impenetrable ice.

"Understood, Miller," Morgan said quietly, the sovereign power of the Vance dynasty bleeding into her tone. "We appreciate the unofficial heads-up. Keep monitoring the Snow Water Equivalents on my feed. We'll handle our side."

*"Good luck, Keep. Seattle out."*

The line clicked dead.

Morgan reached up and hung the receiver back on the rack. She didn't swear. She didn't panic. She simply turned her chair to face Johnny.

"The math just broke," Morgan said softly.

Johnny stared at the WSDOT timeline on his screen. The October 31st Centennial Gala wasn't just logistically unfeasible anymore; it was a lethal liability. If Victoria waited until Halloween to host the VIPs, the Vanguard would spend the next six months trying to feed and house four hundred panicked civilians while WSDOT tried to blast through forty-foot walls of concrete snow.

Johnny pulled up the localized Quantum OS chat interface. He bypassed his own terminal and handed the floor entirely to the Lead Engineer. This was her domain. Her data.

Morgan pulled her keyboard closer. She didn't type an update. She typed a war declaration.

**q-send /domain:VANCE_WHITAKER \[V_VANCE\]** *Priority Override. WSDOT Seattle backchannel confirms 30-day model collapse. Imminent collision of tropical moisture and arctic freeze directly over the Keep. SR-20 closure timeline severely compromised. WSDOT guarantees zero open-asphalt access by late October.*

Morgan paused for a split second, looking over at Johnny. He gave her a single, firm nod.

She added the final, devastating recommendation to the Matriarch.

*Recommendation: The Halloween timeline is dead. Prepare to compress the Centennial Gala operations immediately.*

Morgan hit enter.

Now, the entire weight of the mountain shifted upward to the third floor. They had given Victoria the raw, uncompromising truth of the atmosphere. Now, they had to wait and see if the Matriarch was willing to do the impossible: move a massive, four-hundred-person luxury event up by two weeks, entirely on the fly, without letting the billionaires know they were running for their lives.
