# Part 2: The War Council (10:35)

Morgan’s fingers had barely left the mechanical keyboard when the localized Quantum OS chat interface chimed sharply in the quiet rumble of the engineering bay.

Victoria did not type a measured, administrative response. The Matriarch’s reply was instantaneous and absolute.

**\[SYSTEM ALERT from V_VANCE\]:** *I am coming down to the engineering bay. Right now. I am bringing Diana and Emma.*

Morgan exhaled a long, steady breath, reaching up to rub the grease from her forehead. She looked over at Johnny, who was already running automated predictive models on the B.C. arctic front on his secondary screen.

"Brace yourself, boss," Morgan said quietly. "The Vanguard is assembling."

Less than three minutes later, the deep, mechanical rattle of the heavy freight elevator echoed through the subterranean corridor. The heavy iron grates slammed open. Footsteps marched purposefully over the concrete floors.

They didn't knock. The heavy metal door to the engineering bay was already propped open to vent the intense, dry heat of the 1903 boiler, and the three women strode directly into the operational heart of the Keep.

Victoria Vance led the formation, dressed in a sharp, tailored navy blazer, her expression forged from cold, impenetrable iron. Flanking her right was Diana, still wearing her double-breasted chef’s coat, a pen tucked tightly behind her ear. On Victoria’s left was seventeen-year-old Emma, dressed in her heavy, insulated Iron-Weave Apparel perimeter jacket, a radio clipped to her harness.

Victoria stopped dead in the center of the room. She looked at the harsh, glowing data scrolling across Morgan’s CRT monitors, and then she looked at her Lead Engineer.

"Please," Victoria said, her voice dropping into a low, sovereign command that demanded a specific reality, "give us some good news, Morgan."

But Victoria already knew the answer. Morgan’s posture—rigid, arms crossed tightly over her chest, eyes dark and unyielding—broadcasted the absolute failure of the atmosphere.

"There isn't any, Victoria," Morgan said flatly, refusing to sugarcoat the atmospheric physics for the Matriarch. She tapped the glass of her screen, pointing to the massive, swirling thermal anomaly Johnny had isolated. "The Gulf stall broke. The Pineapple Express is pulling a staggering amount of tropical moisture inland, and it is going to collide directly with an arctic freeze dropping out of British Columbia."

Diana stepped forward, her culinary mind instantly translating the weather into logistical nightmares. "What does a collision mean for the snowpack?"

"It means we aren't getting powder," Johnny’s text-to-speech software chimed flawlessly, his analytical brain engaging with the tactical briefing. "We are getting high-density, hyper-compacted moisture. 'Cascades Concrete.' The loading weight on the Liberty Bell avalanche chutes will increase exponentially."

Morgan nodded at her Second-in-Command, then looked back at Victoria. "WSDOT just gave me the unofficial heads-up on the backchannel. Their 30-day models completely collapsed. If that storm hits the Cascade Crest, their rotary plows will not be able to clear the avalanche debris. They cannot guarantee State Route 20 stays open past the third week of October."

Emma’s hands dropped to her radio, her jaw tightening. "The Halloween timeline requires the highway gates to stay open until November 1st. If WSDOT drops the gates early..."

"If they drop the gates early," Victoria finished, her voice terrifyingly calm, "we will have four hundred billionaires, fifty catering staff, and an armada of delivery trucks trapped at seven thousand feet for the next six months."

The engineering bay fell dead silent, the weight of the mathematical reality pressing down on the Vanguard. The Keep was an impenetrable fortress, but it was not designed to harbor a civilian city. If the billionaires panicked, the sovereign peace of the mountain would shatter.

"So, what is the play?" Diana asked, her dark eyes flashing. "I have three pallets of Château St. Claire and six hundred pounds of venison scheduled for delivery on the 28th."

Morgan leaned back against the server rack, crossing her arms again. She delivered the absolute, uncompromising law of the mountain.

"The play is that the mountain is no longer negotiating," Morgan stated. "We have to beat the Fog of War. I cannot give you an exact date of impact yet, because the storm is still moving over the ocean. But I need all of you to be prepared to move the Centennial Gala up at a moment's notice."

Victoria stared at the glowing weather grids, her brilliant legal and administrative mind running millions of calculations per second. To compress a massive, heavily publicized luxury event by two weeks without triggering mass cancellations or panic required a logistical flex of unprecedented proportions.

But Victoria Vance did not lose to the state, and she did not lose to the weather.

"Understood," Victoria said, her voice hardening into pure titanium. She looked at her pack, her brilliant legal and administrative mind instantly structuring a contingency plan.

"Diana, contact your primary suppliers. Tell them to prep for an expedited timeline, but do not give them a new delivery date yet. Put them on a twenty-four-hour standby."

"Yes, Victoria," Diana nodded, already mentally reorganizing the commercial kitchens.

"Emma, mobilize your perimeter crew," Victoria commanded. "If we have to compress this timeline, WSDOT is going to be swamped. I want your team ready to run convoy escorts for the delivery trucks the second they hit the lower highway."

"I'll fuel the Kodiak plows today," Emma confirmed, resting her hand on her radio.

Victoria turned her gaze back to the glowing CRT monitors, looking at the massive, swirling thermal anomaly Johnny had isolated on the screen. The weight of the Keep rested entirely on the math.

"Morgan, Johnny... keep your eyes on that barometric pressure," Victoria said, her tone absolute. "If it drops by so much as a single millibar, or if that arctic front crosses the border, you tell me immediately. Day or night."

The Matriarch turned on her heel, her tailored blazer snapping sharply as she headed back toward the heavy iron grates of the freight elevator.

"We are not getting buried," Victoria declared over her shoulder, her voice echoing in the heavy industrial space. "Draw up the compressed blueprints. The second that storm commits, we move the Gala."
