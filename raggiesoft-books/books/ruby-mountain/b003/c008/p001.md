# Part 1: All Hands (06:30)

The Fog of War had finally broken, replaced by the crushing, undeniable reality of the atmosphere.

At 4:00 AM, the first major pulse of the collision zone had slammed into the Cascade Crest. It did not arrive as a graceful, swirling alpine flurry. It arrived as a violent atmospheric dumping of "Cascades Concrete"—eight inches of hyper-dense, water-laden snow falling in a terrifying six-hour window.

By 6:30 AM, the Owner's Quarters of the Prospector’s Keep was packed to absolute capacity.

The heavy mahogany desk had been pushed back. Every single chair, sofa, and leather ottoman in Victoria’s sprawling office was occupied. For the first time since the Nisqually Earthquake, all seventeen members of the Vance-Whitaker pack were gathered in a single room. From the twenty-seven-year-old Lead Engineer down to twelve-year-old Lily, the Vanguard was assembled.

The roaring fireplace cast flickering shadows over the faces of the heavy operations mechanics, the legal guardians, and the perimeter sentries. Nobody was panicking. They were waiting for their orders.

At the front of the room, Victoria stood beside a massive, rolling CRT monitor that Johnny had tethered directly to the Keep's Quantum OS. Johnny was parked directly to her right in his manual wheelchair, his rigid fingers resting over his keyboard. Amanda stood immediately behind him, her hands resting firmly on his shoulders, providing the deep, grounding pressure he needed in a room filled with this much kinetic energy.

Morgan Foster stood on Victoria’s left, holding a thick printout of the morning telemetry.

The heavy, encrypted satellite phone on the desk was on speaker.

*"The pioneer trench is holding for now,"* the exhausted voice of Miller, the WSDOT Regional Director, echoed through the quiet room. *"But my Kodiak rotaries are already chewing through a foot of solid ice in the lower chutes. The B.C. arctic front accelerated overnight, Morgan. It hit the tropical moisture directly over your airspace."*

"We felt the impact, Miller," Morgan said flatly. "Give us the revised timeline."

There was a heavy pause on the line.

*"The Halloween models are dead,"* Miller stated. *"NWAC is officially projecting a historic loading event on the Liberty Bell ridges. I am revising the SR-20 closure estimate from mid-November to the first week of November. And honestly, Victoria? If the SWE (Snow Water Equivalent) keeps climbing at this rate, it might be late October. I cannot guarantee safe civilian passage past the 25th."*

Victoria Vance looked at the glowing radar loop on Johnny’s screen. The massive red and purple anomaly was locked in a terrifying stall directly over their coordinates.

She calculated the operational liability. If she waited until October 31st, she was risking the lives of four hundred VIP guests, fifty caterers, and a fleet of commercial delivery drivers. If the highway gates dropped early, the Prospector's Keep would become a refugee camp for panicking billionaires.

It was an unacceptable breach of her sovereign control. She refused to let the mountain dictate her operational liability.

"Understood, Miller," Victoria said, her voice cutting through the ambient noise of the storm rattling the thick windowpanes. "Keep the plows running. We will handle our perimeter. Keep Telemetry out."

Morgan reached over and cut the connection.

Victoria turned to face the sixteen women and her Second-in-Command. The Matriarch’s eyes were cold, calculating, and utterly unshakeable.

"The mountain just moved the finish line," Victoria announced, her voice carrying the absolute, uncompromising weight of a century-old dynasty. "We are not getting trapped, and we are not canceling. We are moving the 100th Anniversary Gala up."

A ripple of intense, focused energy swept through the room.

Victoria turned to Johnny. "Run the absolute earliest logistical date we can execute."

Johnny’s mind was already three steps ahead. His fingers fired across the mechanical keyboard, his text-to-speech software seamlessly bridging the gap between his brilliant processor and the room.

"Cross-referencing vendor transit times with WSDOT clearing schedules," Johnny's synthetic voice stated cleanly. "Assuming maximum acceleration of Diana's kitchen prep and Emma's perimeter escorts, the most mathematically sound execution date is Thursday, October 16th."

*Five days.* They had exactly five days to compress three weeks of high-end luxury event planning.

Victoria didn't flinch. She looked at Diana, who was standing by the fireplace in her chef’s coat. "Can you feed four hundred people a five-course venison dinner on Thursday night?"

Diana’s dark eyes flashed with pure, culinary adrenaline. "I will have to run the prep cooks on twenty-four-hour shifts, and I need Clara and Maya to haul the dry goods up from the sub-basement right now. But yes. I can plate it by 19:00 hours."

Victoria turned to Emma, the seventeen-year-old Perimeter Architect. "The delivery trucks won't be able to handle the ice. You are taking the Kodiak."

"I'll take Grace and Chloe," Emma nodded sharply, already pulling her radio from her belt. "We will intercept the commercial trucks at the base of the highway and physically drag them up the mountain if we have to."

Victoria looked at Ashleigh and Sarah, the legal engine of the family. "I need the narrative spun immediately. The billionaires cannot know they are outrunning a catastrophic avalanche. They will panic."

"We will rebrand the date change this morning," Sarah confirmed, her legal mind already drafting the communications. "We'll call it an 'Exclusive Early Winter Premiere.' We'll spin the storm as a luxury aesthetic. They get to sip champagne while the first major snows fall. They’ll eat it up. No one will cancel."

"Good," Victoria said. She placed her hands flat on her mahogany desk, leaning forward to look at her family.

"This is what we were built for," the Matriarch told them, the fierce pride radiating from her every word. "The civilians down in Seattle are going to freeze and panic. But up here, we hold the line. Execute the pivot."

The pack broke instantly. The younger cousins sprinted for the heavy operations bays. Diana marched out the door to command the kitchens. Ashleigh and Sarah headed straight for the communications array.

The Fog of War was thick, but the Vanguard was moving faster.
