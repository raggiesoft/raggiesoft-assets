# Part 2: The Rebrand (07:00)

As the heavy oak doors of the Owner's Quarters clicked shut behind the departing Vanguard, the intense, kinetic energy of the room instantly shifted into cold, administrative precision.

Victoria walked behind her massive mahogany desk and sat down. She didn't take a moment to breathe or process the panic of the collapsing weather models. She simply pulled a fresh, blank legal pad to the center of her blotter and uncapped her fountain pen.

Johnny remained parked directly to her right. He hadn't followed Amanda out the door. He was the Second-in-Command, and he knew that while Morgan fought the physical war downstairs, the psychological and logistical war was fought right here. He wanted to watch the Matriarch work.

"Plug in, Johnny," Victoria commanded, not looking up as she began writing a rapid series of bullet points. "We need to update the public-facing servers immediately. The narrative has to beat the weather reports."

Johnny’s fingers flew over his mechanical keyboard, pulling up the Keep’s customized HTML backend on his Quantum XN laptop.

"Drafting the primary homepage banner," Johnny’s text-to-speech software chimed quietly. "Awaiting copy."

"Header: *The Prospector’s Keep Announces an Exclusive Early Winter Premiere for the Centennial Gala,*" Victoria dictated, her voice smooth and entirely devoid of the anxiety that had gripped WSDOT. "Body text: *Due to an unprecedented, historic early snowfall, we are offering our VIP guests the rare opportunity to witness the North Cascades transition into winter from the absolute luxury of the Keep. The Centennial Gala has been expedited to Thursday, October 16th, to align with the first major snows of the century.*"

Johnny typed the copy flawlessly, his analytical mind appreciating the sheer brilliance of the spin. She wasn't telling the billionaires they were outrunning a catastrophic avalanche. She was telling them they were getting exclusive, front-row seats to a private, meteorological spectacle.

"Banner is live," Johnny confirmed, hitting the deployment macro.

Victoria immediately picked up her desk phone, dialing the front-of-house concierge desk.

"This is Vance," Victoria said the moment the line connected. "Initiate the Centennial communications protocol. You are going to personally call every single VIP on the Halloween guest list. You will use the 'Early Winter Premiere' script. If they balk at the compressed timeline, offer them a complimentary helicopter transit from Seattle for next summer. But hold the line on the Gala date. It is October 16th, or it is nothing."

Victoria hung up and looked at Johnny. "We will lose a few of the international guests. The flight logistics from Europe won't compress in five days. Acceptable losses. The Seattle and Vancouver billionaires will drive up just for the bragging rights."

She drew a sharp, horizontal line across her legal pad. The VIPs were handled. Now came the true logistical triage.

"Pull up the civilian registry," Victoria ordered. "The standard guest list."

Johnny rapidly cross-referenced the Keep’s booking software. "We currently have eighty-two non-Gala guests occupying the standard suites. Their checkout dates range from tomorrow through October 25th."

"The hard cutoff is Tuesday, October 14th at 11:00 hours," Victoria stated, her voice turning ruthless. She did not care about the hospitality metrics of the standard rooms. She cared about the structural liability of human bodies trapped behind a wall of ice.

"Draft an email to the standard registry," Victoria commanded. "Any guest with a reservation extending past October 14th is to be evacuated. Offer them their choice: a complete, one-hundred-percent refund of their stay, or double the value in credit for the 2004 summer season."

Johnny paused, his fingers hovering over the keys. He calculated the financial loss of the directive.

"Clarification," Johnny typed, his software echoing his thought. "If a guest arrived on October 10th, and is scheduled to check out on October 15th... do we prorate the refund for the nights they were not impacted?"

"No," Victoria said, her eyes locking onto his. "If their booking is impacted by even a single hour, you refund the entire stay. Zero proration. We are not going to argue with them at the front desk over fractional room rates while WSDOT is losing the highway. I want the money back in their accounts so they pack their bags and get off my mountain without a fight."

Johnny nodded, deeply respecting the math. The financial hit was irrelevant compared to the operational disaster of housing eighty-two panicking civilians during a six-month winter siege. He deployed the mass email, watching the server logs as the notifications hit the civilian inboxes.

Victoria drew a second sharp line across her legal pad.

"Now," the Matriarch said, her voice dropping into a dangerous, absolute register. "The alcohol."

She picked up her phone again, bypassing the front desk and dialing the logistics bay in the sub-basement.

"Clara," Victoria said, addressing her twenty-one-year-old heavy operations mechanic. "I need you and Maya to initiate a full sweep of the wine cellars and the secondary storage units. I want an exact inventory of every single drop of alcohol on this property."

Victoria looked out the bay window of her office. The sky over the golden larches was already darkening into a bruised, terrifying gray as the tropical moisture collided with the freezing air.

"Separate the exact manifests Diana needs for Thursday night's Centennial Gala," Victoria instructed Clara. "Everything else—every extra bottle of champagne, every keg, every crate of scotch—gets loaded onto the outbound box trucks this afternoon. If it isn't being uncorked on Thursday night, it goes down the mountain today."

Johnny listened, the ironclad law of the Vanguard resonating in his chest. The billionaires could drink themselves into a stupor on Thursday. But the moment the highway gates dropped, the Keep would revert to a sovereign, military-grade survival outpost. And a survival outpost had to be absolutely, unconditionally dry.

Victoria hung up the phone. In less than twenty minutes, she had completely rewritten the operational reality of a multi-million-dollar resort.

She looked at her Second-in-Command. The legal pad in front of her was covered in sharp, decisive ink.

"The board is clear, Johnny," Victoria said quietly, the adrenaline of the compressed timeline sharpening her features. "Now, we just have to beat the snow."
