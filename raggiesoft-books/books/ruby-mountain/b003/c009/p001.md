# Part 1: The Breaking Point (05:45)

The subterranean engineering bay of The Prospector’s Keep felt entirely detached from the howling chaos occurring seven thousand feet above it. Down here, the 1903 boiler hummed with a deep, methodical rhythm, filling the bedrock chamber with dry, reliable heat.

But on Morgan Foster’s primary CRT monitor, the mountain was screaming.

Morgan leaned into her desk, her elbows braced against the metal edge, her eyes locked onto a terminal window that was flashing a stark, aggressive red. This wasn't standard meteorological modeling. This was the raw, unpolished data stream feeding directly from the sovereign telemetry array legally mounted to the sheer granite face of Liberty Bell.

Beside her, Johnny Vance was perfectly still in his manual wheelchair. His dark eyes darted across his Quantum XN laptop screen, rapidly cross-referencing Morgan’s data with the sub-surface thermistor arrays.

"The hydrostatic bladders are maxing out," Morgan muttered, the grease smudges on her forehead catching the red glow of the screen. "The SWE is climbing exponentially. That isn't snow loading into the chutes, Johnny. That's pure, unadulterated water weight."

Johnny’s stiff fingers moved over his mechanical keyboard, hitting the macro key.

"Thermistor array confirms a catastrophic temperature gradient," his text-to-speech software chimed, a synthetic voice delivering a terrifying reality. "The tropical moisture is sitting directly on top of yesterday's arctic freeze. It is forming a massive persistent slab. Structural friction is degrading at a rate of four percent per hour."

Morgan didn't hesitate. The time for observation was over. The physical trigger point had been reached.

She reached up and pulled the heavy receiver of the encrypted satellite phone from its cradle. Her fingers punched in a specialized twelve-digit routing number—the emergency inter-agency coordination line—and slammed the speakerphone button so Johnny remained fully in the loop.

The line trilled once before three distinct clicks signaled the connection.

*"NWAC Operations, Seattle. We're here, Keep."*

*"NWS Seattle Lead Desk, online."*

*"Miller, WSDOT. Morgan, tell me your hardware is malfunctioning."*

Morgan leaned back in her chair, projecting the cold, untouchable authority of a Certified Consulting Meteorologist directly into the ears of the federal government.

"The hardware is perfectly calibrated, Miller," Morgan stated flatly. "I am officially declaring a redline on the Liberty Bell eastern chutes. Our SWE pillows are registering a load weight that exceeds the 1999 baseline by thirty percent. Johnny just pulled the thermistor data; you have a massive persistent slab forming under the new snowfall. The structural friction is gone."

A heavy, collective silence hung over the speakerphone, broken only by the sound of frantic typing from the NWS desk in Seattle.

*"She's right,"* the NWAC forecaster confirmed, his voice tight with stress. *"Morgan, your feed is pinging our primary servers now. The sheer tonnage of water sitting in that start-zone is staggering. If that chute releases, it's going to wipe State Route 20 clean off the map."*

*"My Kodiak rotaries are already running twenty-four-hour shifts just to keep the pioneer trench open,"* Miller from WSDOT rasped, the exhaustion evident in his voice. *"Morgan, if that slab breaks, my plows cannot clear the debris. The concrete will be forty feet deep. We are going to lose the highway."*

"We know," Morgan said calmly. "That is why Victoria is taking the civilian liability off your board."

*"What do you mean?"* the NWS forecaster asked.

Johnny typed rapidly, allowing Morgan to drink her coffee while he delivered the Matriarch's tactical strike.

"The Prospector's Keep will not allow its logistics to be dictated by an avalanche," Johnny’s software announced cleanly over the line. "Victoria Vance has officially compressed the Centennial Gala timeline. The event has been moved from October 31st to Thursday, October 16th."

The silence on the line was profound. For a government bureaucracy that required weeks to approve a simple permit, the idea of moving a massive, four-hundred-person luxury event by two weeks in the span of twenty-four hours was completely incomprehensible.

*"You're moving it..."* Miller breathed, a profound sense of relief washing over his words. *"You're actually moving it. Morgan, do you have any idea what a nightmare it was going to be trying to evacuate four hundred panicking billionaires from Milepost 134 when those gates dropped?"*

"Victoria does the math, Miller," Morgan replied, a fierce smirk touching her lips. "By Friday morning, every single VIP guest, catering truck, and commercial vendor will be driven back through your gates and safely down into the lowlands. The mountain will be completely clear of civilian targets."

*"Thank God for Victoria Vance,"* the NWAC forecaster muttered.

"Keep your plows running until Thursday night, Miller," Morgan commanded, returning to the immediate tactical reality. "Emma is running our Kodiak rigs from the Lower Motorpool to the summit to drag the delivery trucks up, but she needs you to keep the asphalt open from Diablo to Milepost 138."

*"You have my word, Keep,"* Miller promised fiercely. *"We will hold the trench until your last VIP is off the mountain. After that... I'm dropping the gates. We'll lock you in."*

"Understood," Morgan said. "We'll see you in the spring. Keep Telemetry out."

She hit the disconnect button, cutting the feed.

Morgan looked over at Johnny, who was already running a fresh set of delivery timelines against the degrading weather models. The government was officially warned. The highway was living on borrowed time.

"Well, boss," Morgan said, pushing her chair back and grabbing a heavy brass wrench. "The fuse is officially lit. Let's make sure the boiler can handle the blast."
