# Part 3: The Switchback & The Sanctuary (16:00)

The Cousin Council officially disbanded as the grandfather clock chimed four times. Diana had to get back to the commercial kitchens to oversee the Gala prep, and Morgan and Megan were paged by the resort staff to double-check the ballroom lighting grid.

Johnny backed his wheelchair away from the oak table. He looked up at Amanda, Sarah, and Ashleigh—the "Core Four."

*"I want to see our room,"* he typed, the text-to-speech engine echoing softly in the emptying dining hall.

"Let's go," Amanda smiled, stepping behind his chair and gripping the worn rubber handles. Sarah and Ashleigh immediately flanked them.

They left the cavernous, 18-foot ceilings of the Great Room and moved into the private owner's corridor. To get to the second floor, they could have used the modernized, wood-paneled elevator, but Johnny didn't want the elevator. He wanted the ramp.

When Victoria ordered the massive 1999 renovations, she explicitly demanded that Johnny’s mobility would never be treated as an afterthought or a clinical add-on. To overcome the staggering 18-foot vertical climb to the second floor, contractors had completely gutted a massive corridor to build a 216-foot ADA-compliant switchback ramp.

Amanda pushed him onto the gentle incline.

It was a masterpiece of architectural blending. Instead of sterile steel or medical-grade linoleum, the ramp was built entirely from reclaimed old-growth timber, secured by heavy mortise-and-tenon joinery. The custom-forged iron railings felt cool and solid under Johnny's hands. As Amanda pushed him smoothly up the sweeping switchbacks, it didn't feel like a medical necessity; it felt like walking the grand halls of an 1890s timber baron. It was a physical manifestation of his family's fierce, protective love.

They reached the second-floor landing. The hallway here was lined with massive, reinforced windows. Right now, they looked out over the jagged peaks, but Johnny knew that in a few months, when the colossal Cascade blizzards hit, those windows would sit exactly flush with the twenty-foot winter snowpack, turning the second floor into a glowing, snow-bound submarine.

Amanda navigated the wide, custom doorframes and pushed him into the corner suite.

The heavy door clicked shut behind Sarah and Ashleigh, and Johnny let out a breath he felt like he had been holding for three months.

For an autistic brain, sudden changes, unfamiliar environments, and broken routines are a source of profound, physical anxiety. Vanessa had ripped him from his world and shoved him into a cheap, unpredictable apartment.

But this room was exactly as his brain expected it.

The extended-stay suite was massive but fiercely cozy. The small kitchenette to the right was perfectly organized. In the main room, the two heavy, wood-framed queen beds were made with the exact heavy quilts they always used. Sarah and Ashleigh’s bed was by the door. Johnny and Amanda’s bed was by the window.

His side of the room was a shrine to predictability. His heavy charging dock for the Quantum XN laptop was sitting in the exact center of his nightstand. The lighting was the soft, warm amber he preferred. Even the smell of the room—a faint mixture of cedar wood, Amanda’s lavender laundry detergent, and the crisp ozone of the mountain air—was completely, flawlessly intact.

Amanda clicked his wheel brakes on near the foot of their bed.

"We didn't touch a single thing," Sarah said softly, tossing her canvas jacket over her own bed. "We just dusted. We wanted it ready for when you came back."

Johnny didn't type anything. He just looked at the heavy quilt on his bed, the perfectly aligned charging cables, and his two 20-year-old cousins kicking their boots off on the other side of the room. He felt the tension completely drain from his spine.

There were no arbitrary rules here. No forced isolation. No unpredictable sensory nightmares. He was surrounded by the three women who had ripped him from his abuser, inside a fortress that was about to be sealed off from the rest of the world.

He unclipped his Quantum laptop from his wheelchair mount and set it carefully on the charging dock. He didn't need words right now. The pattern was restored.
