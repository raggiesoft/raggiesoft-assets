# Part 1: The Waking Fortress (08:15)

The morning light bleeding through the reinforced windows of the second-floor sanctuary was a blinding, crisp white.

Down below, vibrating faintly through the heated floorboards, Johnny could feel the deep, rhythmic hum of the massive industrial boilers that Clara and Maya had fired up for the morning rush. Out in the grand hallway, he could hear the muffled, rapid-fire squeal of brass luggage carts as the last wave of paying guests prepared to check out.

The acoustic signature of the room was a symphony of perfectly synchronized chaos. The clock was ticking. Tonight was the 100th Anniversary Gala, and tomorrow at 1:00 PM, the WSDOT would drop the steel barricades.

But inside the suite, the world was perfectly still.

Johnny slowly blinked his eyes open. The first thing he registered was the smell of melting butter and toasted bread. The second thing he registered was the absolute, immovable weight of Amanda’s arms wrapped tightly around his chest.

She was already awake. She had been awake for hours.

Amanda knew exactly what Johnny had revealed the night before, and she knew exactly how his autistic brain processed trauma. If he woke up disoriented, even for a fraction of a second, his mind might drag him back to the terrifying, isolated mornings in the Friday Harbor apartment. So, she hadn't moved an inch. She stayed anchored to him, her chin resting gently on his shoulder, breathing in time with him so that the very first sensory input he received upon waking was the undeniable proof that he was safe.

"Morning, kid," Amanda whispered, feeling the shift in his breathing. She squeezed him gently. "I've got you. We're home."

Johnny let out a long, slow exhale, the residual tension of his nightmares dissolving into the heavy quilts.

Across the room, the source of the warm smells became clear. Sarah and Ashleigh were up, both wearing oversized flannel pajamas. They were moving quietly around the suite's kitchenette. Sarah was flipping eggs in a skillet, while Ashleigh was carefully buttering four slices of uniform, perfectly browned toast.

It was only when Johnny deliberately shifted his weight, turning his head to look at the kitchenette and signaling that he was fully grounded, that Amanda finally let go.

She sat up, stretching her arms, her own oversized pajama shirt wrinkling. "Alright," she announced to the room. "The VIP is awake. Plate it up."

Ashleigh brought over a tray, setting it carefully on Johnny’s lap desk. It was exactly what he needed: a perfectly predictable, low-sensory breakfast surrounded by the three women who had pulled him out of hell.

For the next twenty minutes, the Core Four sat in their pajamas in the amber light of the suite, eating breakfast and talking quietly about the logistics of the day. There was no mention of Vanessa. There was no mention of the tears from the night before. The wall had been rebuilt.

Once the plates were cleared, the tempo shifted. It was time to go to work.

Amanda, Sarah, and Ashleigh changed into their sharp, black "Gala prep" uniforms, while Amanda helped Johnny into a thick, comfortable, sensory-friendly sweater. She transferred him smoothly into his manual wheelchair, clicked his Quantum XN laptop into its heavy mount, and pushed him out into the hall.

They bypassed the elevator and took the sweeping, 216-foot timber switchback ramp. As they descended, the noise of the resort amplified. It wasn't the chaotic, overwhelming noise of a crowded city; it was the purposeful, rhythmic noise of a sovereign pack operating at peak efficiency.

They reached the bottom of the ramp and slipped through the private oak doors, emerging onto the second-story interior balcony that overlooked the Great Hall.

Johnny looked down.

The lobby was spectacular. Massive crimson and gold velvet banners hung from the rafters. The three walk-in fireplaces were roaring. A twelve-piece jazz orchestra was doing a soundcheck in the corner, the brass instruments flashing under the iron chandeliers.

And standing in the direct center of the floor, wearing a flawless tailored suit and flanked by the towering figures of Clara and Maya, was Victoria.

She looked like she had slept for a full eight hours, betraying absolutely none of the apocalyptic legal warfare she had waged in the middle of the night. She was currently dismantling a nervous-looking event coordinator with nothing but a clipboard and a raised eyebrow.

Johnny rested his hands on his armrests.

*"I want to go down,"* his Quantum laptop announced softly.

Amanda smiled, clicking off his brakes. "Let's go join the command center."

She pushed him down the final hallway and out into the main lobby. The second the heavy wheels of his chair hit the polished stone of the Great Hall, the energy of the room seemed to pivot toward him.

Clara and Maya immediately broke off from Victoria's side, jogging over to flank his chair like massive, soot-stained bodyguards, aggressively waving off a pair of wealthy tourists who were walking too close with their luggage.

Victoria finished her instructions to the event coordinator, tapped her pen on her clipboard, and turned around. The Matriarch's eyes locked onto Johnny. A sharp, genuine smile broke across her face—a rare, unguarded expression that she reserved entirely for her bloodline.

"Good morning, Johnny," Victoria said, stepping over the chaotic lobby floor to meet them.

Johnny looked at his older cousin. He thought about the physical barricade in his bed, the warm breakfast, and the absolute certainty that the woman standing in front of him had handled the monster on the island.

He didn't use the text-to-speech engine. He just looked Victoria directly in the eye, gave a sharp, deliberate nod, and offered a wide, relaxed smile.

"Good," Victoria said, tapping the edge of his Quantum screen with her pen. "Because I need my second-in-command. WSDOT predicts the gates will close Saturday, and we have 400 people to feed, entertain, and subsequently evict before the snow buries the highway."
