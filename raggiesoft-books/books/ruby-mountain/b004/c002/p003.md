# Part 3: The Heat of the Kitchen (09:15)

The transition from the Underworld to the commercial kitchens was a shift from dry, thrumming industrial heat to a humid, chaotic inferno of culinary precision.

Clara and Maya hit the massive stainless-steel double doors, pushing them wide open. "Make a hole!" Maya called out over the din of clattering pans and shouting line cooks. "Quality Control coming through!"

Amanda pushed Johnny into the sprawling expanse of the Keep’s primary kitchen. It was a masterpiece of stainless steel, white tile, and roaring gas burners. The air was thick with the scent of roasting garlic, reducing wine, and caramelized sugar.

In the center of it all, holding a stainless-steel clipboard, was twenty-five-year-old Diana Whitaker. Her white chef’s coat was immaculate, the Vance-Whitaker crest embroidered in gold on the breast. Her dark hair was pulled back into a tight, no-nonsense bun, and she was currently walking shoulder-to-shoulder with a nervous-looking man wearing a Whatcom County Health Department polo.

"The walk-in freezers are holding at negative ten," the inspector said, checking off a box on his form. "Sanitization stations are perfectly diluted. Honestly, Chef Whitaker, I don't know why my supervisor insists on sending me up here on an unannounced drop-in. This kitchen is cleaner than the hospital in Bellingham. Zero infractions. You pass with flying colors."

"The mountain has a standard, David," Diana replied smoothly, offering a polite but firm smile. "We’ll see you in the spring."

She escorted him to the back service elevator. The second the elevator doors closed, Diana's polite smile vanished. Her eyes snapped across the kitchen, locking onto the pastry and baking annex in the far corner.

Johnny watched her posture completely change. Diana was usually the warmest of the Whitaker sisters, the one who snuck him extra desserts and let him listen to jazz in the pantry. But right now, the Matriarch's blood was showing.

"Clara, Maya, hold him here," Amanda murmured quietly, stepping back slightly from Johnny's chair. "We are in the blast zone."

Diana marched across the kitchen toward the marble prep tables. A seasonal pastry chef—a guy Victoria had contracted specifically for the heavy lifting of the winter season—was frantically trying to plate a massive tray of miniature tarts. His station was a mess. There were smears of raspberry coulis on the marble, and worse, he was using a dirty rag to wipe the edges of the serving platters.

Diana didn't scream. She didn't throw a pan. That wasn't how the Vance-Whitakers operated.

She walked up behind him, reached out, and calmly turned off his induction burner.

The pastry chef jumped, spinning around. "Chef! I'm just finishing—"

"Put the rag down, Julian," Diana said. Her voice was terrifyingly quiet, easily cutting through the noise of the kitchen. "My office. Right now."

She didn't wait for a response. She turned on her heel and walked into the small, glass-walled inventory office at the back of the kitchen. Julian swallowed hard, took off his apron, and followed her inside, pulling the door shut.

Because the walls were glass, Johnny could see exactly what was happening, even if he couldn't hear the words. Diana stood behind her desk, her posture rigid. She pointed to the messy station, then pointed at Julian, speaking in short, clipped sentences. Julian tried to gesture defensively. Diana simply shook her head once, reached into her desk, pulled out a pre-cut final paycheck, and slid it across the glass surface.

The entire interaction took less than forty seconds.

Julian picked up the check, his shoulders slumped. He walked out of the office, bypassed his messy station entirely, pushed through the rear exit doors, and was gone.

Diana stood in the office for another few seconds. She took a deep, steadying breath, pressing her fingers to her temples. Then, she looked through the glass and saw Johnny’s wheelchair parked near the center island.

The lethal, icy chef instantly melted.

She pushed out of the office and jogged over to him, grabbing a fresh tasting spoon from a sterilized cup on her way. "Sorry you had to see that, kid," Diana sighed, wiping a non-existent smudge off the pristine stainless steel counter next to him. "If you cut corners on sanitation on the day of the Centennial, you don't belong on this mountain."

Johnny looked at her, then looked at the abandoned pastry station. He reached for his keyboard.

*"The standard is the standard,"* his Quantum laptop announced.

"Exactly," Diana grinned, her eyes crinkling. "And speaking of the standard, I need my executive palate. Julian was supposed to be managing the L’Orange Royale glaze for the petit fours, but clearly, I need to do it myself. I need to know if the orange reduction is too sharp."

She reached for a small, gleaming copper saucepan on a nearby burner, dipped the tasting spoon, and brought it over. She held her hand under it to catch any drips, offering it to Johnny.

He leaned forward and tasted it. The flavor was explosive—rich, buttery, with a sharp, bright hit of citrus that immediately balanced out the sugar. It was flawless.

He swallowed, gave Diana a firm nod, and hit the macro key on his laptop that triggered a pre-programmed sound file he only used for the kitchen: a loud, ringing ding of a service bell.

"Perfect. That's a wrap on the glaze," Diana laughed, tossing the spoon into the wash bin. She looked up at Amanda, Maya, and Clara. "Alright, get him out of here before the lunch rush hits and I put you all to work chopping onions. I've got 400 people to feed."

Clara playfully saluted her. "Yes, Chef."

Amanda gripped the handles of Johnny's wheelchair and backed him away from the heat of the ovens. The infrastructure was solid. The food was secure. The fortress was operating exactly as it was designed to.
