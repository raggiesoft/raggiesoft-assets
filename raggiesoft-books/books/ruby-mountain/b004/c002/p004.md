# Part 4: The Five-Mile Moat (09:45)

Amanda pushed Johnny out of the sweltering heat of the commercial kitchens and back into the cool, wood-paneled corridors of the main floor.

*"The perimeter,"* Johnny typed, glancing at the reinforced windows.

"Front gates it is," Amanda nodded. Maya and Clara broke off to head back down to the boiler rooms, leaving Amanda to navigate Johnny through the maze of the 1903 architecture.

They bypassed the crowded Great Hall, taking a private staff corridor that opened directly onto the Keep’s massive, timber-framed front portico.

The second the heavy oak doors opened, the freezing, pine-scented air of the Cascades hit them. It was a staggering contrast to the heat of the ovens.

The storm hadn't arrived yet. The sky over the peaks was an absolute, blinding, crystalline blue. The sun was brilliant, reflecting off the distant glaciers, but the air temperature was hovering just above freezing. It was a flawless, razor-sharp North Cascades morning.

The portico overlooked the absolute edge of Victoria Vance’s empire.

Stretching out from the grand cobblestone turnaround was **Prospector Road**, the family's private, five-mile asphalt artery that snaked its way down the mountain to connect with State Route 20. Running parallel to it was the beautifully maintained paved biking and hiking trail that Victoria left open for the public.

Standing at the edge of the portico, entirely unfazed by the biting wind and holding a heavy radio and a clipboard, was seventeen-year-old Emma Bennett. She was wearing a thick black peacoat, her breath pluming in the cold air as she directed a fleet of luxury shuttles with the ruthless efficiency of an air traffic controller.

"Move shuttle three down to the lower lot!" Emma barked into her radio, squinting against the bright sun. "We have seventy guests arriving from Seattle in twenty minutes, and I want the turnaround clear!"

Emma clicked the radio off, turned around, and saw Johnny and Amanda. A massive grin broke across her face.

"Inspector general on deck," Emma laughed, walking over and bumping her shoulder affectionately against Amanda’s. She tapped Johnny’s armrest. "Good morning, Johnny. You came out just in time to watch the drawbridge go up."

Johnny looked past her, down the long, winding descent of Prospector Road. The asphalt was completely dry and clear under the sun.

*"Status,"* he requested.

"The public buffer is officially sealed," Emma reported, flipping a page on her clipboard. "Victoria had her private security sweep the Happy Creek, Thunder Creek, and Fourth of July trailheads an hour ago. We officially closed the paved trail on Prospector Road for 'private event staging.' There is not a single civilian hiker left on the mountain."

Johnny felt a deep wave of satisfaction. The "benevolent" public access was shut down. The mountain was theirs again.

"As for the Gala," Emma continued, pointing her pen down the five-mile road. "The weather is holding perfectly. We have exactly 400 VIPs, local politicians, and historical society members making the drive up from SR-20 right now. They get to party like it's 1903 tonight under a clear sky. But..." Emma’s smile turned feral, highlighting the Vance-Whitaker bloodline. "...at 10:00 AM tomorrow, the storm hits and I start packing them onto the shuttles. We ship every single one of them back down Prospector Road before WSDOT drops the steel gate on Saturday."

Johnny looked at his younger cousin. She was a seventeen-year-old girl orchestrating the movement of hundreds of millionaires and politicians just to ensure her family's fortress was empty before the highway vanished.

Vanessa Hayes couldn't even figure out a bus schedule in Friday Harbor.

Johnny reached out and hit a key.

*"Flawless."*

"I try," Emma winked, pulling her collar up against the wind. "The valets have the exterior locked down, Johnny. The perimeter is entirely secure. Why don't you head inside and check on the Grand Ballroom? The decorators are starting to panic."

Amanda pulled the wheelchair backward, away from the biting wind of the portico. "Good work, Em. Keep them moving."

"I have Vance blood!" Emma shouted back over the wind, already turning her radio back on as a black SUV crested the top of the hill. "I run on logistics!"
