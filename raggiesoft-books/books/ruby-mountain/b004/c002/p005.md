# Part 5: The 1903 Illusion (10:15)

Amanda pushed Johnny’s wheelchair through the grand mahogany double doors, transitioning from the wide corridors into the absolute crown jewel of The Prospector's Keep: The Grand Ballroom.

If the Underworld was the thrumming, iron heart of the mountain, the Ballroom was its soul.

It was a cavernous, sprawling expanse of polished herringbone oak floors and fifty-foot ceilings. Suspended from the massive timber rafters were six original 1903 crystal chandeliers, casting a warm, fractured golden light across the room. The walls were draped in heavy crimson and gold velvet, and massive stone fireplaces anchored opposite ends of the hall.

It smelled of beeswax, fresh pine garlands, and expensive floor wax.

Currently, the room was a hive of frantic, high-stress activity. Dozens of outside caterers and decorators—hired from Seattle specifically for the Centennial—were rushing around, setting up the 400 chairs, polishing the brass flatware, and laying out the miles of heavy white tablecloths.

Standing in the center of the dance floor, her hands on her hips, was twenty-year-old Sarah Foster.

She was still wearing her sharp, black "Gala prep" uniform, but she looked incredibly annoyed. She was currently standing toe-to-toe with the lead event designer, a frantic man named Preston who looked like he had drank four espressos and was severely regretting taking a contract at a remote mountain fortress.

"I am telling you," Preston pleaded, gesturing wildly to a rolling cart holding three massive, modern, LED-lit ice sculptures of swans. "We need these on the head tables! It provides verticality! It provides modern elegance!"

"And I am telling *you*," Sarah replied, her voice eerily calm—a trait she had absolutely inherited from Victoria. "That this is a 1903 Centennial. Not a 1990s cruise ship. We don't do neon swans."

"But the lighting—"

"Will be handled by the chandeliers and the brass lanterns," Sarah interrupted smoothly.

Amanda pushed Johnny right up to the edge of the argument.

Sarah glanced over, saw Johnny, and let out a long sigh of relief. The icy, authoritative demeanor dropped instantly, replaced by a warm, exhausted smile. "Thank God. The cavalry."

Preston spun around, looking at Johnny’s wheelchair, the Quantum XN, and Amanda's unamused expression. "Look, we just need to finalize the centerpieces—"

"Preston," Sarah cut him off, pointing a thumb at Johnny. "This is Johnny Vance. He is the Matriarch's second-in-command. Whatever he says, goes."

Preston swallowed hard. He looked at the teenager in the wheelchair, suddenly realizing he was speaking to a direct heir of the mountain.

Johnny looked at the massive, glowing LED ice swans. They were hideous. They clashed violently with the heavy oak, the velvet, and the deeply historical weight of the Keep. He looked at Sarah, who was rolling her eyes, and then looked back at Preston.

He slowly brought his hand down to the keyboard.

*"Melt them,"* the synthetic voice announced, echoing flatly in the massive room.

Sarah let out a sharp bark of laughter, clapping a hand over her mouth to muffle the sound.

Preston's shoulders completely deflated. "Right. Okay. No swans. Brass lanterns it is. I'll... I'll just go tell the team to put these in the walk-in." He grabbed the rolling cart and scurried away toward the service doors.

"You are a lifesaver," Sarah sighed, walking over and resting her forearms on the back of Johnny's wheelchair next to Amanda. "If I had to look at those plastic LED monstrosities all night, I was going to throw them into the fireplace."

Johnny smiled, looking out over the massive room.

"It looks incredible, Sarah," Amanda said softly, taking in the sheer scale of the historical recreation. "Victoria is going to be thrilled."

"It's going to be perfect," Sarah agreed, her voice dropping to a quieter, more protective register. She leaned down slightly, resting her chin near Johnny's shoulder, echoing Amanda's exact posture from that morning. "And it's completely locked down, Johnny. Emma has the exterior, Morgan has the grid, Victoria has the VIPs, and I have the floor. Nobody gets in here tonight unless they are vetted, cleared, and on the list. You just get to enjoy the party."

Johnny let out a long, slow breath. The anxiety that usually accompanied massive crowds wasn't there. For the first time in months, he wasn't trapped. He was standing in the center of an impenetrable fortress, surrounded by an army of women who loved him.

Tonight was going to be magnificent.
