# Part 2: The Brutal Awakening (09:00 - 10:00)

On the third floor, Sarah and Ashleigh stepped out of the service elevator. They had traded their velvet gala gowns for heavy Iron-Weave Apparel canvas pants, wool sweaters, and steel-toed boots. They each held a master keycard and a clipboard.

A young housekeeper was standing in the hallway, clutching a stack of fresh towels, looking absolutely terrified by the emergency bells.

"Drop the linens, Maria!" Sarah ordered instantly, pointing a firm finger down the hall. "The rooms stay dirty. Grab your coat and get downstairs. Your seat on the shuttle is waiting."

The housekeeper dropped the towels right onto the pristine herringbone oak and ran for the stairs.

A door further down the hall flew open. Representative Elias Thorne—the state politician Victoria had thoroughly dismantled the night before—stumbled out into the corridor. He was wearing a silk bathrobe, his hair disheveled, pressing a hand to his temple as a vintage-champagne hangover hammered against his skull.

"What is the meaning of this?!" Thorne shouted over the clanging bells, completely ignoring the fleeing housekeeper. "Turn that racket off! I demand to speak to the concierge!"

Sarah didn’t slow her stride. She walked right up to the State Representative, her clipboard pressed firmly against her chest, her expression entirely devoid of customer-service warmth.

"The concierge is currently packing his own survival bag, Representative," Sarah stated loudly, her voice cutting through the noise. "There is an extreme avalanche warning on Liberty Bell. WSDOT is closing the highway in less than four hours. If you are not in the lobby with your luggage in exactly fifteen minutes, you will be left behind."

Thorne blinked, the alcohol fog instantly evaporating. "Avalanche? But... the roads..."

"The plows are holding the road open for our shuttles," Ashleigh cut in, stepping past him to hammer her fist against the next door. "Put your boots on, Elias. The party is over."

By 09:15 AM, the Great Hall was pure chaos.

The elite of Washington State and the hotel's working-class staff were now mashed together in a frantic, disoriented mob. Tech executives were dragging rolling suitcases across the floor alongside panicked sous-chefs in civilian coats.

They were expecting the standard luxury hotel departure. Instead, they hit the absolute, unforgiving wall of Diana’s logistics.

Diana had transformed the front of the Great Hall into a military mess line. The silver chafing dishes were gone. In their place were three massive, ten-gallon industrial urns of scalding black coffee and hundreds of heavy, foil-wrapped breakfast burritos stacked like sandbags.

"No plates! No cream! Keep moving!" Diana commanded, standing behind the tables in a stained canvas apron. She shoved a hot, foil-wrapped burrito into the hands of a bewildered county commissioner, then immediately shoved another into the hands of a fleeing bellhop. "Take the calories and get to the vestibule. Eat on the road."

At the front portico, the reality of the situation finally hit the evacuees.

Seventeen-year-old Emma stood in the glass-enclosed vestibule, flanked by three towering Vance cousins acting as heavy security. Emma pushed the heavy oak front doors open, and the "Cascades Concrete" roared into the lobby.

The temperature outside was twelve degrees. The wind was howling at sixty miles an hour, driving heavy, wet ice directly into the faces of the departing crowd.

"Shuttle One is loading!" Emma screamed over the wind, her voice amplified by a heavy megaphone. "Staff and guests mix! Twelve passengers! Throw your bags in the back and get in the seat! We are not waiting for stragglers!"

Up on the second floor, in the quiet sanctuary of the Owner's Quarters, Johnny sat at his Quantum terminal. He was watching the live feeds from the security cameras mounted in the Great Hall and the portico.

He watched Emma, barely seventeen, completely commanding a room full of millionaires. He watched Sarah ruthlessly clear the third floor, ensuring not a single person remained in their rooms. He watched Diana fueling the exodus with pure, high-calorie efficiency.

His family didn’t just run a hotel. They ran a sovereign operation. And the outsiders, with all their money and political influence, were entirely dependent on the Vance pack to survive.

Amanda walked up behind him, handing him a mug of hot tea. She looked at the security feed, watching Representative Thorne awkwardly scrambling into the back of a freezing 4x4 van, his silk bathrobe peeking out from under his winter coat.

"Well," Amanda smirked, taking a sip of her own coffee. "I don't think we're getting a five-star review on InfoLink America for this morning's service."

Johnny’s left hand dropped to his keyboard.

*"They are getting off the mountain alive,"* the text-to-speech engine announced flatly. *"That is a six-star service."*

Amanda laughed, a sharp, bright sound in the quiet suite. "Damn right it is."

She reached down and tapped the edge of his lap desk. "Keep your eyes on the telemetry, kid. Emma has the first wave rolling, but that WSDOT clock is ticking."

Johnny nodded. He pulled up Morgan's AWS data stream on his secondary monitor.

09:45 AM. TIME TO WSDOT CLOSURE: 03H:15M.

The first convoy of chained vans lurched forward, following the rotary plows directly into the whiteout. The evacuation had begun.
