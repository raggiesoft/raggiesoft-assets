# Part 5: The Steel Drops (12:42)

The drive back up Prospector Road was the most dangerous five miles of Emma Bennett’s life.

With the Lower Motorpool blast doors sealed and the civilian convoy racing west behind the Washington State Patrol, Emma was the absolute last person outside the fortress. She threw her heavy 4x4 maintenance truck into low gear and began the brutal, blinding ascent.

The "Cascades Concrete" was falling at over three inches an hour now. The trenches that Maya’s rotary plow had cut just an hour earlier were already filling with heavy, wet drifts. Emma gripped the steering wheel, her knuckles white, feeling the heavy chained tires slipping and biting into the ice as she navigated the blind switchbacks.

The wind screamed against the reinforced glass of the cab, rocking the heavy truck on its suspension. But she didn't stop. She pushed the roaring diesel engine to its absolute limit until the towering, dark stone silhouette of The Prospector’s Keep finally emerged from the whiteout.

Emma pulled the truck under the heavy timber portico, threw it into park, and killed the engine.

She stepped out into the freezing hurricane, grabbed the heavy iron handle of the Keep's front oak doors, and hauled them open. She slipped inside, throwing her entire body weight against the wood to force the doors shut against the wind.

*Click. Clack.* She threw the massive deadbolts, locking the fortress from the inside.

The acoustic shift was instant and staggering. The violent, deafening roar of the blizzard was immediately muted by the two-foot-thick stone walls of the 1903 architecture, reduced to a low, heavy thrumming that vibrated in the floorboards.

Emma stood in the vestibule, breathing heavily, shaking the snow off her black peacoat.

She walked into the Great Hall.

The four hundred VIP guests were gone. The rolling suitcases, the frantic politicians, the spilled coffee—all of it had been swept away. The massive room had returned to its intended state: a quiet, impenetrable sanctuary. A roaring fire was burning in the ten-foot hearth, casting a warm, flickering orange light across the herringbone oak floors.

Gathered around the fire was the pack.

Morgan, Megan, Clara, and Maya were shedding their snow-caked canvas coveralls. Diana was handing out heavy ceramic mugs of hot cider to the younger cousins. Sarah and Ashleigh were sitting on the leather Chesterfield sofas, completely exhausted but smiling.

Standing at the center of the room, flanked by Amanda and Johnny’s wheelchair, was Victoria Vance.

The Matriarch wasn't holding a clipboard. She didn't need one. Her eyes swept across the room in a practiced, calculating arc, doing the only inventory that actually mattered to her. She counted the heavy trades crew. She counted the logistics team. She counted the younger cousins. She looked down at Johnny.

Seventeen.

The bloodline was whole.

Victoria walked over to a heavy mahogany side table and picked up her encrypted satellite phone. She looked at the grandfather clock ticking against the far wall.

It was **12:42 PM**.

She hit the WSDOT Regional Director’s direct dial. The static-laced connection went through almost immediately.

"Director," Victoria stated, her voice carrying clearly across the quiet Great Hall. Every cousin in the room stopped talking and turned to listen. "My perimeter engineer is back inside the Keep. The Lower Motorpool is sealed. The ADA convoy is on the asphalt, and WSP is escorting the last of the civilian vehicles to the gate."

*"I have dispatch confirming visual on the State Patrol rear guard right now,"* the Director replied. The sheer, exhausting relief in his voice was palpable even through the satellite distortion. *"They are passing Milepost 134. Victoria, your mountain is entirely clear of civilians."*

"The Keep is sterile," Victoria confirmed, a fiercely satisfied smile touching the corners of her mouth. "Only Vance blood remains."

*"Understood. We are issuing the final closure order. The steel is dropping now."*

Fifteen miles away, at the base of the canyon, WSDOT crews unlatched the massive, heavy-duty steel swing gates. With the blinding snow swirling around them, they pushed the barricades across both lanes of State Route 20, driving the heavy locking pins deep into the asphalt. They hung the reflective red signs that would not be moved for half a year: **ROAD CLOSED. AVALANCHE DANGER.**

*"The highway is locked, Victoria,"* the Director breathed. *"Stay warm up there. See you in the spring."*

"See you in the spring, Director."

Victoria ended the call. She set the heavy phone down on the mahogany table.

She turned to face her family. Seventeen apex predators, completely cut off from the eastern medical grid, local law enforcement, and the rest of the world. Outside, millions of tons of snow were preparing to bury their private road under an impassable wall of ice.

Victoria looked down at Johnny. His hands were resting on his Quantum XN keyboard, his shoulders relaxed, completely devoid of the tension he had carried in Friday Harbor.

"Well," Victoria announced, her voice echoing warmly in the cavernous hall. "The guests have checked out. Morgan, lock down the grid. Diana, figure out what we're having for dinner."

Johnny smiled. The siege had officially begun, and he had never felt safer in his entire life.
