# Part 4: The Pack (16:00)

Amanda grabbed the glass shaker of crushed red pepper flakes from the center of the table and handed it to Johnny. With slow, deliberate movements of his stiff left hand, he dusted his slice, the bright red flakes sinking instantly into the hot, bubbling oil of the melted mozzarella.

The massive, scarred oak table at the back of the kitchen was usually reserved for the staff’s shift meals. Today, it was the absolute center of the Vance-Whitaker universe.

All seventeen members of the family had migrated down from the upper floors, drawn by the smell of woodsmoke, roasting garlic, and baking bread. They dragged industrial steel stools and wooden prep chairs to the table, packing in shoulder-to-shoulder.

There was no seating chart. There was no jazz orchestra. There was only the loud, chaotic, overlapping conversation of a pack that finally had its territory entirely to itself.

Diana dropped into a chair across from Johnny, completely shedding her strict CTE Educator persona. She grabbed a massive slice of pizza, folded it in half perfectly down the center crease, and took a bite, closing her eyes in sheer relief.

"Okay," Diana mumbled happily through the food. "The kids did good. I can officially retire for the night."

"You earned it, Chef," Morgan called out from the end of the table. The Keep’s head mechanic was still wearing her heavy canvas work pants, her hands stained faintly with grease from checking the subterranean fuel lines. She lifted her bottle of sparkling water in a mock toast. "I just ran the final telemetry check. The primary diesel generators are topped off, the wood cribs are full, and the boilers are running at a perfect sixty PSI. The mountain can throw whatever it wants at us. We aren't feeling a thing."

Outside, the mountain was certainly trying. The wind had escalated from a howl to a sustained, violent scream. Through the high, reinforced clerestory windows of the kitchen, all they could see was a blinding, swirling wall of white ice and snow violently impacting the glass. The "Cascades Concrete" was burying them alive.

But inside, the temperature was a perfect, comfortable seventy degrees. The radiant heat from the massive brick pizza oven warmed the entire room, and the heavy stone walls of the 1903 fortress absorbed the impact of the storm, reducing the violent blizzard to a low, rhythmic thud.

Victoria sat at the head of the table. The Matriarch, who had orchestrated the political and legal subjugation of the state's elite just eighteen hours ago, was leaning back in her wooden chair, eating a slice of pepperoni pizza with her bare hands.

She wasn't looking at spreadsheets or legal briefs. She was watching her family.

She watched Sarah and Ashleigh arguing playfully over the last slice of the basil pie. She watched the younger cousins—Zoe, Maya, and Clara—glowing with pride as their older relatives demolished the food they had made. And most importantly, she watched Johnny.

Johnny took another bite of his pizza. The heat of the chili flakes flared brilliantly against the sweet acidity of the San Marzano tomatoes.

He thought about Friday Harbor. He thought about the sterile, quiet apartment. He thought about Vanessa Hayes forcing him to eat lukewarm, perfectly portioned, tasteless food while she controlled every single aspect of his environment. He had spent three months in absolute, terrified silence.

He looked at the scarred oak table. It was loud. It was messy. Flour was dusting the floor, and seventeen people were talking over each other in a cacophony of absolute, unapologetic life.

He loved it.

He rested his hand on his lap desk, his fingers moving across the Quantum XN keyboard. He didn't broadcast it to the room. He just sent a direct, localized ping to the small monitor sitting next to Victoria’s plate.

*\>\> J_VANCE: The drawbridge is up. The fortress holds.*

Victoria paused. She read the green text glowing on the small screen. She looked down the length of the long oak table, her eyes meeting Johnny’s. The Matriarch smiled—a genuine, fiercely protective expression that she reserved entirely for her pack. She raised her slice of pizza in a silent, acknowledging salute.

"Alright, eat up and listen close," Amanda announced, bumping her shoulder gently against Johnny’s arm. She wiped a smudge of flour off the sleeve of her flannel shirt, raising her voice to address the entire table. "Because once this kitchen is clean, we have a mandate. Morgan and I pulled five king-sized mattresses from the VIP suites while the kids were baking."

Sarah stopped mid-chew, her eyes lighting up. "We are doing the Great Hall bivouac?"

"Oh, we are doing the Great Hall bivouac," Amanda confirmed, grinning. "We've got the projector set up, and Morgan wired the surround sound directly into the 1903 acoustics. We are going to build a fortress inside the fortress, light the ten-foot fireplace, and not move until tomorrow morning. But there are rules."

Amanda pointed a flour-dusted finger at the younger cousins, then swept it to include her older peers.

"The evacuation is over. The guests are gone. Which means work mode is officially offline," Amanda declared. "Mandatory uniform for the Bivouac is pajamas. I don't want to see a single pair of jeans, no work boots, and absolutely no grease-stained canvas pants, Morgan. Everyone hits the showers, gets into their winter sleepwear, and meets in the Great Hall at 18:00 hours."

A cheer went up from the younger cousins.

Johnny let out a quiet, raspy exhale of a laugh. The Gala they had been planning for the guests would have been a masterpiece of illusion, but this—this chaotic, warm, fiercely guarded isolation—this was the real magic of The Prospector’s Keep.
