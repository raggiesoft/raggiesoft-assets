# Part 1: The Pajama Protocol (18:15)

The 1897 Great Hall was the architectural crown jewel of The Prospector’s Keep. It was a cavernous, three-story expanse of exposed old-growth timber, intricate ironwork, and floor-to-ceiling reinforced glass that looked directly out over the Skagit Gorge.

Usually, the room demanded a certain level of high-end, old-world formality. But tonight, the pack had completely conquered it.

Morgan and Amanda had pushed the heavy leather chesterfield sofas and antique coffee tables to the perimeter. In the dead center of the massive Persian rug, directly in front of the roaring ten-foot stone fireplace, they had constructed a sprawling sea of five king-sized mattresses, buried under a mountain of heavy down comforters, sleeping bags, and pillows.

At exactly 18:15, the massive oak doors of the Great Hall swung open, and the pack began to filter in, strictly adhering to Amanda’s mandate.

The transformation was striking. The tactical, hyper-competent resort operators of the morning had completely vanished. Diana, having scrubbed the flour and kitchen stress from her skin, was wearing a thick, oversized set of matched fleece pajamas. Morgan had traded her grease-stained mechanic’s canvas for a worn-out gray hoodie and flannel sleep pants. The younger cousins—Zoe, Maya, and Clara—tumbled onto the mattresses in heavy thermal sets, immediately fighting over the best pillows.

Even Victoria Vance, the undisputed commander of the mountain, had shed her tailored wool slacks. She walked into the hall carrying a mug of herbal tea, dressed in a set of elegant, heavy silk winter pajamas and thick cashmere socks, looking entirely relaxed.

Amanda pushed Johnny’s wheelchair through the doors last.

Johnny was wearing incredibly soft, heavy-knit cotton sleep pants and a long-sleeved thermal shirt. For the first time in three months, his body wasn't coiled tight with anxiety or fighting the heavy barometric pressure of the coast. He was clean, he was warm, and the thin 7,000-foot air was rushing effortlessly through his lungs.

"Alright, end of the line," Amanda said, locking the brakes on his chair right at the edge of the mattress sea. "You want the left flank or the center?"

Johnny didn't even need to use his keyboard. He just pointed a stiff finger toward the center of the mattresses, directly in the blast radius of the massive stone fireplace.

"Center it is," Amanda smiled.

With practiced, effortless synchronization, she helped him transfer out of the chair. He didn't have the core strength to navigate the shifting terrain of five mattresses on his own, so Amanda simply kept her arms wrapped under his shoulders, sliding with him until they reached the dead center of the Bivouac. She propped him up against a small mountain of heavy feather pillows, completely burying his legs under a massive, heated down comforter.

The heat from the ten-foot fire washed over his face. The ambient glow of the flames danced across the vaulted timber ceilings.

"I've got the perimeter," Sarah announced, walking around the mattresses and clicking off the heavy antique lamps one by one.

The Great Hall plunged into darkness, illuminated only by the roaring fire and the bright blue standby screen of the digital projector Morgan had rigged to the upper balcony. Outside, the blizzard was throwing thousands of pounds of ice against the reinforced glass, the wind shrieking like a freight train. But inside, the acoustics of the heavy timber and stone swallowed the noise, turning the deadly storm into nothing more than a localized lullaby.

Amanda crawled under the heavy comforter next to Johnny, immediately shifting her weight to press her shoulder and hip flush against his side. It was the Anchor Protocol, seamless and automatic. Johnny let out a long, slow exhale, leaning his head against her shoulder as the younger cousins settled down around them.

Morgan hit a button on a remote control, and the projector beam flickered to life, throwing the opening credits of a movie across a massive canvas screen they had dropped over the main staircase.

Johnny didn't care what movie was playing. He let his eyes slide shut, the deep, rumbling bass of the surround sound vibrating through the mattresses. He was surrounded by a wall of flannel, fleece, and the absolute, uncompromising safety of his family. The mountain was locked down, and the pack was exactly where it was supposed to be.
