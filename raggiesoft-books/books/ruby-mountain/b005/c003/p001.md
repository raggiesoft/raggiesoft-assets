# Part 1: The Whiteout (07:00)

The deep, heavy sleep of the Bivouac was broken by the sound of the 1897 grandfather clock striking seven in the corner of the Great Hall.

Johnny opened his eyes slowly. The massive stone fireplace had burned down to a deep, glowing bed of orange embers, casting a faint, warm light across the sea of mattresses. Amanda was still asleep next to him, her breathing slow and even, her arm thrown securely across his chest.

Usually, at seven in the morning, the Great Hall was flooded with the sharp, blinding, high-altitude sunlight of the Cascades.

Today, it was completely, claustrophobically dark.

Johnny shifted his head against the pillows, looking toward the colossal, three-story, floor-to-ceiling windows that overlooked the Skagit Gorge. He couldn't see the gorge. He couldn't see the sky. The reinforced glass was entirely covered by a solid, impenetrable wall of blinding white ice and snow. The storm had completely buried the first floor of the Keep.

He didn't panic. The air in the room was warm, the heavy down comforter was heavy and safe, and the floor wasn't vibrating with the howling wind anymore. The massive snowpack had effectively insulated the building, plunging the Great Hall into the quiet, muffled stillness of a subterranean bunker.

A few mattresses over, Morgan Whitaker sat up.

She rubbed her eyes, ran a hand through her messy hair, and immediately looked at the buried windows. The Head Mechanic didn't say a word. She quietly slid out of her sleeping bag, grabbed her heavy gray hoodie from the floor, and padded across the Persian rug in her socks toward the Grand Foyer.

Twenty minutes later, the massive oak doors of the Great Hall pushed open.

Morgan walked back in, carrying a large Heat-Vault Jug of black coffee. She was followed by Clara and Maya. The heavy operations team had officially clocked in.

Amanda stirred next to Johnny, blinking the sleep out of her eyes. She felt the immediate shift in the room's energy. She sat up, pulling the heavy comforter up to her chin.

"Status?" Victoria’s voice came from the far side of the mattresses. The Matriarch was already awake, sitting cross-legged in her silk pajamas, holding a leather-bound notebook.

Morgan unscrewed the cap of her Heat-Vault Jug, taking a long drink of the scalding coffee.

"The mountain didn't just hit us, Vic. It dropped the entire ocean on us," Morgan reported, her voice low to avoid waking the younger cousins. "I just checked the exterior telemetry. We took on forty-two inches of Cascade Concrete overnight, and it's still coming down at about three inches an hour."

Amanda looked at the completely buried windows. "Are the trusses holding?"

"The '99 steel is laughing at it," Clara answered, her voice carrying the quiet, absolute confidence of a structural engineer. "I checked the strain gauges in the attic. We are well within the load-bearing tolerances. The old 1903 timber would have snapped at 4 AM, but the skeleton is holding the weight perfectly. We don't need to shovel the roof."

"But?" Victoria asked, catching the slight hesitation in Morgan's posture.

Morgan sighed, leaning against the back of a heavy leather chesterfield sofa.

"But the vent heaters are losing the war," Morgan admitted. "The coils on the primary diesel exhaust and the boiler flues are running at maximum voltage, but the snow is too wet and it's falling too fast. The heaters are just turning the snow into slush, and the ambient temperature is instantly freezing it into massive ice dams."

"If the boiler vents ice over, the exhaust backfires, and the system auto-shuts down," Maya added, her expression serious. "We lose the radiant heat."

Victoria didn't flinch. She just nodded, making a note in her leather book. The romantic, cozy isolation of the Bivouac was over. The survival logistics had begun.

"Understood," Victoria said calmly. "Morgan, Clara, Maya. Get dressed for heavy exposure. I want those vents manually cleared and chipped out before 09:00. Tether up before you go out the second-story service doors. Do not let the wind take you."

"Yes, ma'am," the three women replied in unison.

"Amanda," Victoria said, shifting her gaze to the center of the mattresses. "Wake the rest of the pack. Get Johnny dressed and moved to the kitchen. Have Diana start breakfast. We need hot calories in the heavy ops team before they go out into the freeze."

Amanda nodded, throwing the comforter off her shoulders. The transition was instantaneous.

"Alright, kid," Amanda said, turning to Johnny with a warm, focused smile. "Morning time. Let's get you into your chair. We've got a mountain to fight."
