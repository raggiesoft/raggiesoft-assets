# Part 3: The Anchor and the Kitchen (07:30)

While Morgan’s heavy ops team was gearing up in the second-floor service corridor to fight the mountain, Amanda pushed Johnny’s wheelchair out of the Great Hall and toward the private residential wing.

Sarah and Ashleigh walked on either side of the chair. The twenty-year-old Foster twins shared legal guardianship of Johnny with Victoria, and they shared a massive, second-story suite with Amanda and Johnny.

Amanda bypassed the heavy oak staircase and pushed the chair directly into the private residential elevator, hitting the button for the second floor.

When the elevator doors slid open, the acoustic reality of the Keep shifted violently. Down on the first floor, the massive snowpack had insulated the building, turning it into a quiet bunker. But up here on the second floor, the architecture was fully exposed to the storm.

Amanda pushed the chair down the hall and unlocked the door to their suite.

The room was set up like a sprawling, high-end extended-stay apartment. A small kitchenette sat to the left, opening into a comfortable living area, with two massive queen-sized beds dominating the back half of the room.

The noise was terrifying. The reinforced, floor-to-ceiling windows weren't buried in darkness like the Great Hall. Instead, they were taking the absolute, point-blank fury of the blizzard. The glass vibrated continuously in the frames as eighty-mile-an-hour wind gusts slammed sheets of blinding white ice against the panes. The light bleeding into the room wasn't the warm glow of morning; it was a harsh, icy, grayish-blue.

"Alright," Sarah announced, ignoring the shrieking wind against the glass. She stepped immediately into the massive, ADA-compliant bathroom attached to the suite and cranked the thermostat on the wall, turning on the high-voltage ceiling heat lamps. "Bathroom ambient temp is rising. I'm pulling the waterproof transfer bench."

"I've got the clothing pull," Ashleigh added, walking toward the heavy cedar dressers. Since they were officially snowed in until May, formal wear was entirely obsolete. She pulled out a pair of incredibly soft, heavy-knit fleece track pants and a thick, long-sleeved thermal henley for Johnny, tossing them onto the freshly made bed he shared with Amanda.

Amanda stepped in front of Johnny’s chair.

There was no hesitation, no awkwardness, and no verbal instructions needed. This was the Care Mandate. The next forty-five minutes were a masterclass in the quiet, uncompromising dignity of the Vance-Whitaker pack.

They moved through the morning hygiene protocols with the practiced, clinical efficiency of a specialized medical unit. Amanda managed his physical transfers, her arms locked securely under his shoulders to support his core weight. Sarah and Ashleigh operated as the ultimate logistical support, managing the water temperature, the towels, and the accessibility hardware.

Johnny trusted them implicitly. In Friday Harbor, Vanessa had used his physical vulnerability as a weapon to humiliate and control him. But here, with his twin sister and his cousins, his physical limitations were just a logistical puzzle that the pack solved together every single morning.

Once Johnny was bathed, dried, and safely resting on the edge of the mattress under the glow of a warm lamp, the three women took their turns rotating through the massive walk-in shower.

By 08:15, the suite was ready.

Sarah and Ashleigh emerged from the bathroom wearing comfortable, oversized wool sweaters and thick winter leggings. Amanda finished helping Johnny pull his arms through the sleeves of his thermal henley, making sure the fabric wasn't bunched or pulling aggressively against his sensory boundaries.

"Comfortable?" Amanda asked, kneeling to slip a pair of heavy, fleece-lined slippers onto his feet.

Johnny let out a quiet, relaxed exhale. He looked past Amanda's shoulder at the shrieking, chaotic whiteout trying to break through the second-story glass. He dropped his head slightly to bump his forehead against his sister's shoulder in a silent *yes*. He was warm, he was clean, and the fortress was holding.

Amanda smiled, standing up and grabbing the handles of his chair. "Let's head downstairs and see what the kitchen is up to."

They navigated the second-floor corridor, rode the elevator back down to the insulated quiet of the first floor, and pushed through the massive, swinging wooden doors of the commercial kitchen.

The air smelled incredibly of roasting bacon, dark roast coffee, and melting butter.

Diana was standing at the head of the stainless-steel prep island. She had traded her fleece pajamas for her crisp, white Master Chef’s apron. Standing at attention on the other side of the counter were the "Indoor Logistics" team—fifteen-year-old Chloe, fourteen-year-old Zoe, and twelve-year-old Lily.

They were wearing their own pristine white aprons over their winter clothes, their notebooks open on the steel counter.

"Breakfast service is the most dangerous meal of the day if you lose your focus," Diana was saying, tapping her dry-erase marker against the whiteboard. She had drawn a massive diagram of an egg. "Why?"

"Because eggs are a primary vector for Salmonella Enteritidis," Chloe answered immediately, reading from her notes. "The pathogen can survive inside the intact shell."

"Exactly," Diana nodded, pointing the marker at her. "Which means our temperature control has to be flawless. We do not leave cartons of eggs sitting on the prep counter while we cook the bacon. They stay in the walk-in until the exact second they hit the heat. And what is the minimum safe internal cooking temperature for pooled eggs?"

"155 degrees Fahrenheit," Zoe answered proudly.

"Spot on," Diana smiled. "Now, let's talk about plating. We have three heavy ops mechanics currently outside freezing to death to save our boiler vents. When they walk through those doors, their core temperatures are going to be in the basement. How do we serve them?"

Diana pulled a heavy, ceramic plate from a lower cabinet and set it on the steel counter.

"If you take hot, perfectly cooked eggs and put them on a cold, room-temperature ceramic plate, the ceramic instantly absorbs the heat," Diana explained, running her hand over the cold plate. "The eggs go cold in sixty seconds. The fat congeals. It's disgusting."

She pointed to the warming ovens beneath the massive brick hearth.

"Lily, pull the plates," Diana ordered.

The twelve-year-old grabbed a pair of thick, insulated oven mitts, opened the warming drawer, and pulled out a stack of ceramic plates that had been resting at a constant 140 degrees. She set them carefully on the pass.

"Hot food goes on hot plates," Diana instructed. "When Morgan, Clara, and Maya sit down, their food needs to be radiating heat from the top and the bottom. We are feeding their internal furnaces. Understood?"

"Yes, Chef," the three younger cousins replied in perfect unison.

Amanda parked Johnny at the end of the prep island, leaning against the counter with a grin.

"I don't know, Diana," Sarah teased, grabbing a piece of perfectly crispy bacon off a cooling rack. "You might be over-training them. By December, Chloe is going to be critiquing my knife skills."

"Someone has to," Diana shot back without missing a beat, though her eyes were smiling. She looked over at Johnny, noticing the relaxed slope of his shoulders and the thick, warm clothes. "Morning, kid. Hot chocolate?"

Johnny raised his left hand to his lap desk, his fingers tapping across the Quantum XN keyboard.

*"With milk. While it lasts,"* the flat, synthetic voice replied. *"And whatever Lily is cooking."*

Lily beamed with absolute pride at the compliment. She immediately turned to the massive walk-in refrigerator, executing Diana's protocols flawlessly. She didn't leave the door propped open; she stepped inside, grabbed a gallon of whole milk, and stepped back out, letting the heavy door click shut to preserve the thermal envelope.

She poured exactly the right amount into a heavy stainless-steel saucepan and set it on the induction burner. She didn't blast the heat—she warmed it slowly so the milk wouldn't scald, whisking in the dark cocoa powder until it was perfectly smooth and steaming.

She grabbed a warmed ceramic mug from the pass, poured the rich hot chocolate into it, and carried it over to his lap desk with extreme care.

"Fifteen minutes until the heavy ops team gets back," Diana announced, looking at the industrial clock on the wall. "Logistics team, hit the line. Let's build the breakfast."
