# Part 4: The Thaw (08:35)

The massive, swinging wooden doors of the kitchen didn't just open; they were pushed hard by three heavily insulated, exhausted bodies.

Morgan, Clara, and Maya stumbled into the warm, bright, bacon-scented air of the commercial kitchen. The ambient seventy-degree heat hit them like a physical wall, immediately causing the thick layer of Cascade Concrete packed onto their canvas coveralls to begin melting into dirty puddles on the tile floor.

The heavy operations team looked like they had just survived a war. Their faces were flushed a violent, wind-burned red, their safety goggles were caked in ice, and Clara was visibly shivering, her teeth clicking together in a rapid, involuntary rhythm.

"Vents are clear," Morgan rasped, her voice completely stripped of its usual commanding volume. She reached up with stiff, shaking fingers, struggling to unclip the frozen carabiner on her harness. "The boiler is breathing. We bought ourselves at least twenty-four hours before it builds back up."

"Get that gear off," Diana ordered immediately, abandoning her whiteboard and stepping into the role of den mother. "Drop the coveralls right there, I don't care about the floor. Get to the staff table."

Sarah and Ashleigh immediately stepped away from Johnny's chair, moving to help Clara and Maya peel out of the heavy, soaking wet thermal layers. Freed from the freezing canvas, the three heavy ops mechanics collapsed into the wooden chairs around the scarred oak staff table. They were wearing only their base layers, exhausted, their internal core temperatures dangerously low.

"Line, listen up!" Diana called out, clapping her hands sharply. The shift in her tone was electric. "We have frozen operators on the deck. We are firing the breakfast. Execute!"

The younger cousins didn't panic. They had just spent twenty minutes running the theoretical drills, and now it was time for the practical application.

Twelve-year-old Lily moved first. She grabbed the heavy insulated mitts and pulled three heavy ceramic plates from the 140-degree warming drawer. She set them precisely on the stainless-steel pass, the heat radiating visibly off the porcelain.

Fifteen-year-old Chloe stepped up with a massive stainless-steel skillet. The scrambled eggs inside were cooked to a flawless, soft-curd 155 degrees—safe, but not rubbery. Using a wide spatula, she portioned the eggs onto the center of each hot plate, using the back of the utensil to create a tight, visually appealing mound rather than a sloppy spread.

Fourteen-year-old Zoe followed right behind her with the resting racks of thick-cut, hearth-baked bacon.

"Height builds appeal," Zoe muttered to herself, echoing one of Diana’s previous lessons. Instead of laying the bacon flat beside the eggs, she leaned three thick slices against the mound of eggs like a structural teepee, creating a visually striking, three-dimensional profile.

Lily stepped in for the final touch. She grabbed a small pair of culinary tweezers and precisely dropped a pinch of finely minced, bright green chives over the pale yellow eggs, creating an instant, vibrant color contrast. Finally, she took a clean linen towel and ran it quickly around the outer rim of all three plates, ensuring there were no smudges, grease marks, or stray crumbs.

It didn't look like a survival ration slapped together in a snowed-in bunker. It looked like a fifty-dollar breakfast service at a high-end Seattle bistro.

"Service!" Chloe called out, her voice cracking slightly with pride.

"Run them," Diana smiled, stepping back to let them finish the job.

Chloe, Zoe, and Lily carefully picked up the heavy, radiating ceramic plates. They walked over to the staff table and set them down directly in front of Morgan, Clara, and Maya.

Clara immediately wrapped her freezing, shaking hands around the edges of the ceramic plate. The 140-degree heat transferred instantly from the porcelain into her skin. She let out a long, shuddering gasp, closing her eyes as the deep, comforting warmth flooded her nervous system.

"Oh, my god," Clara whispered, opening her eyes to look at the beautifully plated food. "You guys... this is incredible."

"The plates are heated to maintain the thermal envelope of the proteins," Lily explained proudly, standing at attention with her hands behind her back, exactly the way she had seen Diana do it.

Morgan let out a raspy laugh, picking up her fork. She took her first bite of the eggs and bacon. The rich, salty fat, the perfect texture, and the sheer, aggressive heat of the food hit her empty stomach like a furnace igniting.

"Chef," Morgan said, looking past the younger cousins to Diana, who was pouring three massive mugs of dark roast coffee. "Promote them. All of them. This is the best thing I have ever eaten in my entire life."

Zoe and Chloe grinned at each other, their shoulders dropping with relief. The pressure of the kitchen was intense, but the validation of feeding their frozen family members was worth every second of the stress.

Amanda leaned against the counter next to Johnny's wheelchair, watching Clara’s violent shivering slowly subside as the hot food worked its magic.

Johnny rested his left hand on his lap desk. He took a slow sip of the hot chocolate Lily had made for him, the rich, whole milk coating his throat. He looked at the heavy ops team, safe and thawing out. He looked at the younger cousins, beaming with pride. And he looked at Diana, the undisputed master of the Keep's internal morale.

He didn't type anything. He didn't need to. The blizzard outside was shrieking against the second-story glass, completely burying the first floor under thousands of tons of Cascade Concrete, but it didn't matter. The fortress was warm. The pack was fed. And the mountain was losing.
