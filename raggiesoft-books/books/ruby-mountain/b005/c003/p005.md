# Part 5: The Deep Clean (09:30)

By nine-thirty, the adrenaline of the morning had completely burned off, replaced by the sheer, imposing reality of the physical labor ahead of them.

Victoria ordered the heavy ops team to remain by the Great Hall fireplace to recover their core temperatures, and Diana locked herself in the kitchen to begin auditing the walk-in freezers for their strict six-month rationing protocol. That left the remaining able-bodied members of the pack to face the aftermath of Friday’s frantic evacuation.

Three hundred luxury suites had been abandoned in an absolute panic.

Seventeen-year-old Emma Bennett stood at the base of the grand staircase, holding a heavy clipboard. She was in her element. As the master logistician of the Bennett branch, organizing chaos was her specialty.

"Listen up," Emma addressed the cleaning crew, which consisted of Amanda, Sarah, Ashleigh, Grace, Mia, and the younger Vance sisters. "We are not doing this in one day. We have six months, so pace yourselves. We are officially transitioning the Keep from a commercial enterprise into a dormant fortress. We strip the beds, bag the trash, and lock the doors. We are starting with the third floor."

Amanda pushed Johnny’s wheelchair into the commercial elevator, following the Foster twins up to the third-floor guest wing.

When the elevator doors slid open, the hallway looked like the site of a minor disaster. The wealthy evacuees had left a trail of chaos: a stray cashmere scarf discarded on the carpet, an overturned room service cart, and half-empty glasses of flat vintage champagne sitting on the mahogany console tables.

Amanda pushed Johnny down the center of the wide corridor and locked his brakes directly across from the heavy brass doors of the industrial laundry chute.

"Alright, kid, you're our command center," Amanda said, pulling a heavy, coiled gray CAT5 Ethernet cable from a linen closet. She plugged one end into the wall port and handed the other to Johnny.

Johnny plugged the cable into the side of his Quantum XN laptop, instantly linking into the Keep’s localized 2003 server network. He pulled up the master room inventory database. His physical limitations meant he couldn't strip a king-sized mattress, but his mind was the architectural anchor of the pack. He was going to act as Dispatch, tracking exactly which rooms were clear, which required maintenance, and how many loads of laundry were stacking up in the basement.

"You good here?" Amanda asked, leaning down to wrap her arms around his shoulders in a tight, warm hug.

Johnny nodded, leaning into the embrace and giving her a small thumbs-up with his stiff left hand. He wasn't alone. Amanda, Sarah, and Ashleigh propped open the doors to Rooms 301, 302, and 303, creating a triangle of activity perfectly centered around his wheelchair. They were never more than fifteen feet away, their voices carrying easily into the hallway.

"Oh, you have got to be kidding me," Sarah's voice echoed in disgust from Room 302—the suite formerly occupied by Representative Elias Thorne.

Amanda poked her head out of 301, holding a bundle of white sheets. "What did he leave?"

Sarah walked out into the hallway, holding up a heavy, crystal ice bucket. Floating in the melted water was a half-eaten breakfast burrito and a soggy, incredibly expensive silk necktie.

"He tried to flush a cloth napkin down the toilet and threw his tie in the ice bucket," Sarah sighed, dumping the bucket into the rolling housekeeping cart. She walked over to Johnny, tossing her arms around his neck for a quick, affectionate squeeze before grabbing a fresh trash bag. "I'm formally requesting that we revoke his reservation privileges for the 2004 season."

Johnny’s fingers moved across his keyboard.

*"Room 302 logged. Maintenance required. Plumbing obstruction,"* the text-to-speech engine chimed flatly down the corridor. *"And Thorne is permanently blacklisted in the system."*

Ashleigh laughed from the doorway of 303, tossing a massive bundle of heavy down comforters into the hallway. "Good call, boss. Send it down."

Amanda picked up the bundle of bedding, hauled open the heavy brass door of the laundry chute, and shoved the linens inside. A deep, satisfying *whoosh* echoed up the shaft as the fabric plummeted three stories down into the subterranean laundry facility.

"Load one away," Amanda called out.

Johnny tapped his keyboard, updating the basement queue.

They fell into a steady, comfortable rhythm. There was no pressure to turn the rooms over for new guests. There were no managers checking their watches. It was just the pack, reclaiming their territory room by room. Grace and Mia swept through behind the older girls, bagging up trash and unplugging the mini-fridges to conserve the mountain's diesel power grid.

The hallway became a revolving door of pack affection. The physical tactile grounding was constant. Every ten minutes, Amanda would walk out of a room, drop a load of laundry down the chute, and check in with Johnny. She would offer him a sip of water, adjust the heavy fleece blanket over his legs to ensure the drafty hallway wasn't chilling him, and lean down to press her cheek against his temple in a long, lingering hug. When Grace walked by with an armful of empty champagne bottles, she would pause to rest her head on his shoulder for a few seconds. When Ashleigh moved the cart, she ruffled his hair.

For Johnny, it was absolute sensory perfection. The steady flow of deep-pressure hugs kept his nervous system entirely regulated, wrapping him in an unbreakable web of familial safety while he managed the digital logistics of the fortress.

By 11:30 AM, the entire east wing of the third floor was completely stripped, sterilized, and locked down. The heavy oak doors were shut, plunging that section of the hotel into a deep, dormant hibernation that wouldn't be disturbed until the spring thaw.

Johnny looked at the spreadsheet glowing on his Quantum monitor. They had cleared forty rooms. The mountain was howling outside the hallway windows, but inside, the Keep was slowly becoming cleaner, smaller, and infinitely more theirs.

*"East Wing Secure,"* Johnny broadcasted through his speakers. *"Transitioning to West Wing."*

"Heard that, Dispatch," Amanda smiled, walking out of the final room and dusting her hands off on her Iron-Weave Apparel pants. She walked up behind his chair, wrapping her arms around his chest from behind in one final, heavy squeeze before unlocking the brakes. "Let's roll. We've got a lot more beds to strip."
