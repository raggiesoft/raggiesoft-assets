# Part 6: The West Wing (11:45)

Amanda navigated the manual wheelchair across the central landing, the rubber tires moving silently over the thick Persian runner. They left the secured, darkened East Wing behind and pushed through the heavy double doors into the West Wing of the third floor.

"Alright, new staging ground," Amanda announced, parking Johnny directly beside the brass laundry chute door for this corridor. She uncoiled the heavy gray CAT5 cable, plugged it into the wall jack, and handed the connector to Johnny.

Johnny snapped the cable into his Quantum XN laptop. The localized network pinged, and his master inventory spreadsheet instantly refreshed. He tapped a command, turning the grid for the West Wing from red (Occupied) to yellow (In Progress).

"Pace yourselves, team," Emma’s voice drifted up from the grand staircase, where she was auditing the second-floor logistics. "We are not burning out on day one. Finish the third floor and we officially call it a day for the housekeeping detail. The fourth floor can wait until next week."

"Music to my ears," Ashleigh groaned, stretching her arms over her head. "I've stripped so many duvet covers I feel like I'm drowning in down feathers."

"Stop complaining and grab a trash bag," Sarah laughed, throwing a roll of heavy black plastic at her sister. "Room 340. Let's go."

The pack settled back into their methodical, unhurried rhythm. Without the pressure of the WSDOT deadline or the impending avalanche, the work felt entirely different. It wasn't a frantic evacuation; it was the careful, deliberate winterization of their home.

Grace and Mia moved methodically from room to room, executing their deep-cleaning protocols. They unplugged the heavy mini-fridges, propped the small doors open to prevent mildew, and pulled the heavy blackout curtains shut to insulate the glass against the howling blizzard outside.

Every time a cart rolled past the command center, the physical grounding continued.

Ashleigh emerged from Room 342 holding a stack of abandoned glossy magazines. She dropped them on the housekeeping cart, walked over to Johnny, and leaned down to wrap her arms around his shoulders, burying her face in the crook of his neck for a long, exaggerated sigh.

"Rich people read the most boring articles," Ashleigh muttered against his thermal shirt. She gave him a tight squeeze, pressed a kiss to his temple, and pulled back. "You doing okay, boss? Need me to grab you another hot chocolate from the kitchen?"

Johnny shook his head, a soft smile on his face. He lifted his stiff left hand and patted her forearm.

*"I am optimal. Keep stripping beds, Foster,"* the text-to-speech engine commanded dryly.

Ashleigh saluted him with a grin and marched back into the suite.

Ten minutes later, Amanda walked out of Room 345 carrying a mountain of tangled white sheets. She hauled open the brass door, shoved the linens down the chute, and turned to Johnny. She didn't say a word. She just knelt beside his chair, adjusted the heavy fleece blanket covering his legs to completely block out the hallway draft, and wrapped her arms tightly around his waist.

Johnny let out a long, slow exhale, leaning his weight into his twin sister's embrace. The deep pressure instantly neutralized any lingering sensory static from the chaotic morning. The storm outside was screaming, violently throwing ice against the third-story glass at the end of the hall, but the violent weather only made the warmth of Amanda's hug feel more absolute.

"Almost done, kid," Amanda murmured against his chest. She squeezed him one last time, stood up, and brushed a stray lock of hair out of his eyes. "Five rooms left."

By 1:15 PM, the heavy rolling carts were overflowing with trash, and the laundry chute had swallowed the last of the West Wing's linens.

Sarah walked out of the final suite at the end of the corridor. She reached up, grabbed the heavy oak doors, and pulled them shut with a heavy, echoing *THUD*. She pulled the master keycard from her pocket, swiped the lock, and engaged the deadbolt.

The small light on the electronic lock flashed from green to a solid, dormant red.

Johnny hit the final keystroke on his Quantum XN. The entire third-floor grid on his monitor shifted from yellow to a sterile, uniform gray.

*"Third floor is completely sterilized and locked down,"* Johnny's speakers announced. *"Eighty-two rooms offline."*

"And with that, the housekeeping shift is officially over," Amanda declared, wiping her brow. She unhooked the CAT5 cable from Johnny’s laptop and began coiling it up. "We did good. Half the hotel is asleep."

Sarah leaned against the wall, looking down the long, dim, empty hallway. The frantic energy of the VIP guests was completely gone. The Keep felt massive, quiet, and deeply secure.

"So," Ashleigh said, leaning against her sister and looking toward the elevator. "Diana said something about rationing, right? Do you think she rationed the leftover pastries from the Gala brunch, or do you think they're sitting in the kitchen waiting for us?"

Johnny’s fingers danced across his keyboard.

*"Diana explicitly locked the almond croissants in the walk-in,"* the computer chimed. *"But Maya knows the override code for the electronic padlock."*

Amanda burst out laughing, unlocking the brakes on his manual wheelchair. "You are an absolute menace, Johnny Vance. Let's go orchestrate a heist."

She spun the chair around, and the pack headed toward the elevator, leaving the third floor to sleep through the winter.
