# Part 1: The First Summit (09:00)

The Vanguard convoy moved down the unplowed void of State Route 20 like a mechanized military column.

At the head of the formation was the apex predator: the fifty-ton, high-visibility orange Kodiak rotary plow. Seventeen-year-old Emma Bennett sat in the captain's chair, the 500-horsepower diesel engine roaring as she pushed the massive, spiked augers into four feet of fresh, heavy snow. She carved a flawless, sheer-walled trench down the center of the highway, throwing a fifty-foot geyser of pulverized ice over the sheer drop of the Skagit gorge.

Clara and Maya Vance sat in the oversized passenger seats, fully geared up in grease-stained Iron-Weave Apparel and heavy Talon-Cleats, entirely unfazed by the terrifying drop-offs.

Directly behind the Kodiak, perfectly sheltered in Emma's slipstream and driving over flattened asphalt, were two of the Keep’s heavy Ridgeback 4x4 transit vans.

Because Victoria’s legal sovereignty relied on absolute, unassailable adherence to state law, Maya and Emma could not touch the cargo in the back of those vans. It was commercial alcohol—fifty cases of vintage Bordeaux and summer champagne worth forty thousand dollars. Washington State law mandated that commercial liquor transfer could only be handled by personnel twenty-one or older.

Therefore, Ashleigh (23) piloted the first Ridgeback, and Megan (22) drove the second.

As Emma rounded the final, sweeping curve toward Milepost 134, the expansive, snow-packed expanse of the Ross Dam Trailhead parking lot came into view. Just past the lot sat the massive steel WSDOT barricade, locked tight against the mountain.

The first Mountain Summit of the season had officially convened.

Waiting on the paved, frozen ice rink of the civilian side were three idling commercial vehicles: an Aero-Transit step-van, a heavy Pro-Serve Foodways flatbed, and an armored, climate-controlled transport from the Mount Vernon secure storage facility. The lowland drivers were huddled in their cabs, terrified of the wind shear.

But on the mountain side of the steel, the Brotherhood was already gathered.

Idling next to the gate was the bright yellow Seattle City Light Alpine-Cat. Flanking it were two heavy-duty tracked ATVs belonging to Ellis and the Ross Lake Resort caretakers. Tucked just behind them was a small, heated utility tractor driven by the two grizzled watchmen from the half-built NCI construction site.

Emma brought the 50-ton Kodiak to a hissing, pneumatic halt mere inches from the steel. The two Ridgebacks parked directly behind her.

The Vanguard had arrived.

Emma killed the rotary blades, leaving the diesel engine roaring. Clara and Maya dropped down onto the packed ice, instantly vaulting the crossbeams of the WSDOT gate. They landed on the civilian side with heavy thuds, their Talon-Cleats biting into the ice. Ashleigh and Megan followed, climbing the steel with clinical efficiency.

Henderson, the veteran SCL engineer, walked up to the gate from the mountain side, grinning through his frost-covered beard. "Right on time, girls! Thought Morgan said this storm was going to bury us by tomorrow!"

"It is," Maya yelled over the wind, reaching back over the gate. "Which is why we're front-loading the calories!"

Emma, still in the cab of the Kodiak, shoved a massive, industrial-grade thermal Poly-Store Bin out the window. Ellis from Ross Lake caught it, practically groaning at the weight.

Maya popped the heavy vacuum seals. The freezing air was instantly filled with the staggering, rich scent of Diana Whitaker’s heavy elk and barley stew, accompanied by foil-wrapped bricks of fresh-baked sourdough and Heat-Vault jug of spiced, boiling cider.

"Diana said the elk is from the deep freeze!" Maya announced to the gathered mountain men. "High protein, maximum density! Eat it while it's boiling!"

The NCI watchmen, who had been surviving on canned beans in a freezing construction trailer for three days, looked like they were ready to drop to their knees and worship the Poly-Store Bin. The food wasn't just lunch; it was the Vanguard's currency, their way of cementing the psychological anchor of the Air-Gap.

While the mountain men tore into the sourdough, Ashleigh and Megan marched across the icy parking lot to the armored Mount Vernon transport truck.

The driver rolled down his window, shivering uncontrollably.

"Manifest transfer," Ashleigh yelled over the howling wind, pulling a clipboard from her jacket. "Fifty cases of commercial vintage. You need to keep the cargo bay at exactly fifty-five degrees all the way to Skagit County, or the corks will pop."

The driver looked at the twenty-three-year-old girl, then at the towering, terrifying avalanche chutes above them. He signed the manifest with a shaking hand.

Ashleigh, Megan, Clara, and Maya formed a highly synchronized, brutal physical chain. For twenty minutes, they manually hauled fifty heavy wooden crates of wine out of the Ridgebacks, over the top of the steel WSDOT gate, across the freezing parking lot, and into the lowland transport. They didn't slow down. They didn't complain. They worked with the terrifying, tireless stamina of industrial athletes.

The moment the last case of Bordeaux was secured, Clara pounded her fist on the side of the transport truck. "Get out of here!" she ordered the driver. "The highway is going to ice over in twenty minutes!"

The driver didn't need to be told twice. He threw the truck into gear and fled for the lowlands.

Next was the inbound cargo.

Clara marched over to the terrified Aero-Transit driver. "Priority medical."

The driver hurriedly handed a heavy, insulated white cooler through the window. Clara checked the seals. It was Johnny’s scheduled medication drop. She immediately hauled it back across the ice, passing it carefully over the gate to Emma, who secured it inside the heated Kodiak cab. Without that box, their cousin’s baseline autonomic functions would fail. The mountain demanded self-reliance, but chemistry demanded the lowlands.

Finally, the Vanguard and the Brotherhood converged on the Pro-Serve Foodways flatbed.

This was Diana's war chest. Four hundred pounds of Monarch Mills, sixty gallons of high-smoke-point cooking oil, and two hundred pounds of root vegetables.

Henderson and Ellis didn't just stand by. They left their stew and marched across the gate to help. The mountain men and the Vance women worked shoulder-to-shoulder, hauling massive, fifty-pound sacks of flour across the ice rink, passing them over the steel barricade, and loading them into the now-empty Ridgeback vans.

When the Pro-Serve Foodways flatbed was finally empty, the terrified driver reversed out of the lot, leaving the caretakers entirely alone in the absolute, freezing silence of the closure zone.

The cross-load was complete.

Henderson wiped the flour off his high-vis jacket and picked up his Heat-Vault jug of spiced cider. He looked through the steel bars at Ashleigh and Clara.

"You tell Victoria the grid is stable down at the dam," Henderson said, his voice dropping to a solemn, professional register. "And you tell Diana she saved our lives this week."

"We'll pass it along, Henderson," Ashleigh said, vaulting back over the gate to the mountain side. "Keep your radios on Morgan's frequency. When this low-pressure system hits tomorrow, the telemetry says it's going to be a complete whiteout."

"We'll be listening," Ellis agreed, climbing onto his tracked ATV.

The Brotherhood scattered. The NCI watchmen and the Ross Lake caretakers peeled off toward their respective access trails. Henderson threw the Alpine-Cat into gear, plunging down the steep access road toward the hydroelectric dam.

Emma tapped the Kodiak’s air horn one final time in a gesture of mountain solidarity.

Megan and Ashleigh climbed back into the heavily loaded Ridgebacks. Clara and Maya swung back up into the massive passenger seats of the Kodiak.

"Medical is secure. Calories are secure," Clara reported, locking the heavy cab door.

Emma looked at the heavy, dark gray clouds spilling over the jagged peaks of Liberty Bell Mountain. Morgan’s storm was arriving right on schedule.

"Locking the vault," Emma said quietly.

She threw the fifty-ton machine into drive. The massive rotary blades screamed to life, and the Vanguard convoy turned their backs on the lowlands, pushing deeper into the high-altitude isolation to return to their fortress.
