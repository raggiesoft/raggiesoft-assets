# Part 1: The Broadcast (09:00)

By late November, State Route 20 was a tomb of hyper-compacted ice. The Washington State Department of Transportation had dropped the heavy steel gates at Milepost 134 three weeks ago. The Prospector’s Keep was legally, physically, and completely severed from the civilian grid.

But Victoria Vance did not need a highway to wage war. She only needed bandwidth.

The Great Hall of the Keep had been transformed from a pioneer-era luxury lobby into a broadcast studio of devastating psychological power. The central, two-story river-rock fireplace was roaring, casting a brilliant, warm light across the antique leather furniture and the soaring Douglas fir columns. Outside the massive frosted windowpanes, a furious alpine blizzard was hammering the mountain, but inside, the atmosphere was perfectly, immaculately controlled.

Morgan and Megan Whitaker, the heavy industrial trades of the family, were operating as a military-grade AV unit.

"Uplink is stabilized," Morgan announced, stepping back from a heavy, black Aegis Transport Case resting on an antique mahogany table. She had physically spliced a state-of-the-art Omni-Link Uplink video-conferencing unit directly into the Keep’s encrypted satellite array. "Johnny routed the data packets through the Quantum OS servers. We have zero latency. No pixelation. They are going to see you in flawless high-definition."

Megan, standing atop a small rolling scaffold, adjusted a set of professional-grade tungsten photography lights, angling them to bounce softly off the vaulted wooden ceiling. She was ensuring there were no harsh shadows on the Matriarch’s face.

"Lighting is set," Megan confirmed, climbing down. "You look like royalty, Victoria."

Victoria Vance stood in the center of the Great Hall, inspecting her reflection in a large, silver-backed mirror. She wore a tailored, dark charcoal suit, her posture forged from cold, impenetrable iron. She did not look like a grieving relative. She looked like an executioner.

"Perfect," Victoria said quietly. She turned her gaze to the center of the staging area.

Parked perfectly in the center of the camera’s frame, directly beside Victoria’s leather armchair, was Johnny. He was wearing a crisp, dark navy sweater. The rhythmic *hiss-click* of his RespiraCore Compressor machine was notably absent; up here in the freezing, highly oxygenated alpine air, his lungs operated with effortless strength. He rested his stiff hands on the mechanical keyboard of his laptop.

Standing immediately behind his wheelchair, her hands resting fiercely on his shoulders, was his twin sister, Amanda. She wore a dark blazer over a black turtleneck, her expression radiating absolute, lethal protection.

"Are you ready, boss?" Amanda murmured, her fingers squeezing his shoulders.

Johnny offered a single, rigid nod. *System secure.*

"Go live," Victoria commanded, taking her seat.

One hundred and fifty miles away, the Whatcom County Superior Court in Bellingham was dreary, damp, and suffocatingly gray. The relentless November rain pounded against the cheap municipal windows.

Vanessa Hayes sat at the defense table. She was wearing a standard-issue, faded orange county jumpsuit. Her wrists were shackled to the heavy oak table. Her attorney, a public defender who looked entirely overwhelmed by the sheer volume of the prosecution's evidence, sat nervously beside her.

Vanessa was terrified, but a small, arrogant part of her ego still clung to the delusion of control. She expected the victims to be wheeled into this miserable room. She expected to see Johnny trembling. When the judge announced they would be receiving remote testimony due to the highway closure, Vanessa smirked. She expected a grainy, pathetic, stuttering webcam feed of a frightened family trapped in a cabin.

The bailiff picked up a remote control and turned on the massive, 40-inch CRT television mounted at the front of the courtroom.

The screen flickered, and the collective breath of the Bellingham courtroom caught in their throats.

It wasn't a grainy webcam. It was a flawless, cinema-quality broadcast.

The courtroom was instantly forced to look into the roaring, colossal heart of The Prospector's Keep. They saw the absolute luxury, the towering timber, the blazing fire, and the driving snow against the glass. The disparity in power was visually staggering. Vanessa was sitting in a fluorescent-lit municipal cage; her victims were sitting in a multi-million-dollar mountain fortress.

And right in the center of the screen, looking directly down the camera lens with terrifying, sovereign authority, was Victoria Vance.

Vanessa’s smirk vanished. The blood drained completely from her face.

The Honorable Judge Reynolds cleared his throat, clearly taken aback by the sheer production value of the uplink. "Miss Vance. The court recognizes you. We are proceeding with the victim impact statement regarding the charges, notably RCW 9A.44.050, Rape in the Second Degree."

"Thank you, Your Honor," Victoria’s voice echoed through the courtroom speakers. Because of Johnny’s routing architecture, there wasn't a millisecond of delay. Her voice was crystal clear, cold, and absolute.

Victoria did not look at the judge. She stared directly into the lens, ensuring her eyes were locked dead onto the television screen sitting in front of the defense table. She was looking right at Vanessa.

"The defense will undoubtedly attempt to argue diminished capacity or circumstantial confusion," Victoria began, her voice ringing like struck steel. "They will note that my cousin, Johnny Vance, is autistic, non-verbal, and physically restricted to a manual wheelchair. The defendant, Vanessa Hayes, knew this. She did not stumble into an ambiguous situation. She systematically isolated a physically helpless individual to bypass his safeguards."

Victoria reached over, her manicured hand resting gently but firmly on the armrest of Johnny’s wheelchair.

"She weaponized his silence," Victoria stated, the venom in her voice dropping the temperature of the Bellingham courtroom. "She calculated that because he could not speak, he could not fight back. She believed that because he could not walk, he could not escape her."

On the television screen, Amanda Vance’s grip on her brother’s shoulders visibly tightened, her eyes burning holes through the camera. Johnny sat perfectly still, his dark eyes locked on the monitor on his end, watching Vanessa shrink into her plastic chair.

"But the defendant made a catastrophic miscalculation," Victoria continued, leaning forward slightly, the firelight catching the sharp angles of her face. "Johnny is not silent. He speaks with the full, uncompromising voice of his family. And he is no longer isolated."

Victoria paused, letting the silence hang in the courtroom, allowing the visual reality of the fortress behind her to crush the last remnants of Vanessa's hope.

"He is sitting in a sovereign stronghold," Victoria delivered the final blow, "surrounded by a pack that will ensure Vanessa Hayes never sees the outside of the Washington Corrections Center for Women for the remainder of her natural life. We demand the absolute maximum sentence under RCW 9A.44.050. The mountain demands it."

Victoria stopped speaking. She didn't break eye contact with the camera.

In the Whatcom County courtroom, Vanessa Hayes lowered her head, staring at the shackles on her wrists. The psychological warfare was over. Victoria Vance had reached across a hundred and fifty miles of avalanche debris, through the digital void, and utterly destroyed her.
