# Part 2: The Sentencing (09:15)

The silence that followed Victoria’s declaration was absolute. Inside the Whatcom County Superior Court, the only sound was the heavy November rain lashing against the municipal windows.

On the massive CRT television at the front of the room, the feed from The Prospector's Keep remained flawlessly stable. Victoria Vance sat in perfect, terrifying stillness. Beside her, Johnny’s dark eyes stared through the camera lens, watching the woman who had tried to break him completely unravel.

The Honorable Judge Reynolds broke the silence. He adjusted his glasses, looking away from the high-definition screen and down at the defense table.

"Ms. Hayes," Judge Reynolds said, his voice stripped of any judicial warmth. "You have entered a plea of guilty to the amended criminal information. The court notes for the record that your previous counsel withdrew citing irreconcilable ethical conflicts, and your current public defender has rightfully advised you that proceeding to trial against the sheer volume of forensic evidence provided by the Vance-Whitaker Trust would be an exercise in futility."

Vanessa’s new public defender sat rigidly beside her, staring at his legal pad. He had seen the encrypted digital routing logs, the SS7 telecom trace, and the meticulous financial audits Victoria had gift-wrapped for the District Attorney. He had told Vanessa in the holding cell that if she didn't take the blind plea, a jury would bury her under the jail.

The Whatcom County District Attorney stood up. He didn't need to grandstand; Victoria Vance had already executed the psychological kill shot.

"Your Honor, the State concurs with Ms. Vance's impact statement," the DA announced, opening the massive evidentiary dossier Victoria had compiled. "The defendant's actions were entirely predatory. She forged a Whatcom County Superior Court seal to coerce a family into surrendering a dependent. She committed Commercial Fraud and Theft of Services to secure an abandoned clinic in Anacortes, creating a phantom transition facility. She then transported the victim across county lines, isolating him in a subsidized apartment in Friday Harbor."

The DA flipped a page, his tone hardening. "That constitutes Unlawful Imprisonment. Furthermore, by dragging a victim biologically acclimated to seven thousand feet down to sea level without transitioning protocols or medical hardware, she committed Criminal Mistreatment in the First Degree."

The DA looked directly at Vanessa. "But the State’s primary focus is the violation of RCW 9A.44.050. The defendant utilized her geographic isolation and the victim's physical immobility to systematically strip his autonomy, engaging in Sexual Abuse of a Vulnerable Adult. The State requests consecutive sentencing on all counts. We request the absolute maximum."

Judge Reynolds nodded slowly. He closed the thick file folder on his bench and folded his hands.

"Vanessa Hayes, stand up," the judge commanded.

Vanessa’s knees shook as she pushed herself up from the heavy oak table. The shackles chains around her wrists rattled loudly in the quiet room. She couldn't bring herself to look at the television screen. She stared firmly at the judge's wooden gavel.

"In my twenty years on the bench, I have rarely seen a grift so profoundly cruel, nor one so spectacularly ill-conceived," Judge Reynolds stated, his voice echoing with absolute authority. "You targeted a young man because you believed his disability made him an easy mark. You believed his silence meant he had no voice."

The judge gestured toward the glowing screen, where the roaring fireplace of the Keep framed the Vanguard.

"But you failed to realize that his voice is backed by an entire dynasty," Judge Reynolds continued. "You didn't just break the law, Ms. Hayes. You walked into a fortress and attempted to steal from a family that possesses the resources, the intelligence, and the absolute sovereign will to dismantle your life down to the studs."

Vanessa squeezed her eyes shut, a single tear cutting through the exhaustion on her pale face.

"On the count of Forgery of a Superior Court Seal, I sentence you to five years. On the counts of Commercial Fraud, Theft of Services, and Unlawful Imprisonment, I sentence you to ten years. On the count of Criminal Mistreatment in the First Degree, I sentence you to ten years."

Judge Reynolds paused, letting the staggering weight of the time sink into the Bellingham courtroom.

"And on the charge of Rape in the Second Degree under RCW 9A.44.050, I sentence you to the statutory maximum of one hundred and forty-four months. These sentences are to be served consecutively. You are remanded immediately to the custody of the Department of Corrections to begin your term at the Washington Corrections Center for Women."

Vanessa’s knees buckled. Her public defender had to catch her by the elbow to keep her from collapsing completely onto the linoleum floor. Decades. She was going away for decades.

"And Ms. Hayes?" Judge Reynolds added, his tone carrying a final, chilling note of finality. "The court has been informed that the Federal Office of the Inspector General is currently reviewing your fraudulent Representative Payee applications and Medicaid filings. When the State of Washington is finished with you, the federal government will be waiting at the gates."

The judge slammed his gavel. "Court is adjourned."

The two Whatcom County Sheriff's deputies moved in instantly, grabbing Vanessa by the arms and physically turning her away from the defense table. As they hauled her toward the side door leading to the holding cells, Vanessa finally looked up at the television screen.

She looked at Victoria Vance, who sat with the cold, unbothered posture of a ruling monarch. She looked at Amanda, whose hands rested fiercely on Johnny's shoulders.

And finally, she looked at Johnny.

He wasn't trembling. He wasn't afraid. His stiff hand moved deliberately over the mechanical keyboard of his Quantum XN laptop. He hit a single macro key.

The Omni-Link Uplink feed severed instantly.

The massive CRT television in the Bellingham courtroom went completely black, plunging Vanessa Hayes into the dark as the heavy steel door of the holding cell slammed shut behind her.

One hundred and fifty miles away, at seven thousand feet, the Vance-Whitaker pack sat in the warm, golden light of the Great Hall. The blizzard screamed against the reinforced glass outside, burying the Keep under thousands of tons of Cascade Concrete.

Victoria closed her leather portfolio. The legal war was over. The mountain was secure.
