# Part 2: The Vanguard Assembly (10:45)

Twenty minutes after hanging up the satellite phone, Victoria Vance walked out of the Owner's Quarters and stepped up to the heavy timber railing of the second-floor mezzanine. She looked down at the Great Hall.

"Vanguard," Victoria called out, her voice carrying effortlessly over the crackle of the massive river-rock fireplace. "Assemble."

The response was immediate. Within five minutes, all seventeen cousins were gathered in the Great Hall. Clara and Maya came up from the boiler room, wiping grease from their hands. Diana stepped out of the kitchens. Amanda wheeled Johnny out of the office and parked him directly at the edge of the large Persian rug, while the younger cousins—Chloe, Zoe, Mia, and twelve-year-old Lily—took seats on the heavy leather sofas.

Victoria walked slowly down the sweeping staircase, projecting absolute calm. A panicked pack was a dead pack.

"We have a localized bureaucratic situation," Victoria began, taking her place at the head of the assembly. "A rogue State Environmental Inspector has driven up to the Ross Dam gate at Milepost 134. He brought his own padlock and locked the steel."

Emma Bennett sat forward instantly, her eyes narrowing. As the master logistician who commanded the Lower Motorpool, the gates were her territory. "He locked the gate? He grounded my Kodiak?"

"He did," Victoria confirmed.

Clara folded her arms, her jaw setting. "I can take a cordless angle grinder down there and cut that padlock in ten minutes, Victoria."

"Absolutely not," Victoria said smoothly. "We do not break the law, Clara. We weaponize it."

Victoria turned her attention to the youngest cousins. Lily and Mia were looking nervous. They understood the harsh physical reality of the winter isolation, but the legal architecture of their survival was still a complex web to them.

"Lily, Mia, listen to me," Victoria said, shifting into her role as the Matriarch and teacher. "When WSDOT drops the steel gates in November, they officially abandon the highway. By law, civilians are barred from crossing. If a civilian gets trapped in an avalanche past that gate, the state is liable."

The young girls nodded slowly.

"But we cannot survive for six months without heavy supplies," Victoria continued, pacing slowly in front of the fire. "So, we operate under a 'Winter Administrative Access Permit.' It is a federally recognized legal exemption. It gives Emma the legal authority to drive the rotary plows down into the avalanche zone to meet the delivery trucks at the gate, perform the cross-load, and bring the cargo back up. It is the absolute legal lifeline of this mountain."

"And the Inspector pulled it?" Mia asked, her voice tight. "So we can't get deliveries?"

"He didn't just pull ours," Victoria smiled, a cold, sharp expression that made the older cousins immediately sit up straighter. "He pulled the blanket exemption for the entire corridor. He thought he was surgically targeting The Prospector’s Keep to freeze us out."

Morgan Foster let out a sudden, dark laugh from the back of the room. She was a Certified Consulting Meteorologist; she knew exactly who else operated in the Skagit gorge. "Oh, no. Tell me he didn't."

"He did," Victoria confirmed, her smile widening just a fraction. "He didn't read the fine print. That exact same administrative permit is used by the caretakers at Ross Lake Resort to get their off-grid diesel. And more importantly, it is the exact same permit used by Seattle City Light to run their municipal engineers up to Ross Dam."

The realization swept through the room like a shockwave. Sarah and Ashleigh, the legal engine of the family, exchanged a look of pure, unadulterated awe.

"He just blockaded the Seattle power grid," Sarah whispered.

"Precisely," Victoria nodded. "The Inspector has filed an emergency injunction in Olympia for tomorrow morning to make his padlock permanent. He expects me to show up and fight him for our hotel."

"Are you going to?" Emma asked, a fierce grin starting to form.

"I won't have to say a single word," Victoria replied. "I just got off the phone with Marcus Cole, the Director of Winter Operations for Seattle City Light. Tomorrow morning, when that rogue inspector walks into the courtroom, he is going to be met by the Mayor of Seattle's entire municipal litigation team, and the federal lawyers for the National Park Service."

A cheer went up from Clara and Maya. Johnny’s hands flew across his keyboard, his text-to-speech software chiming perfectly over the noise.

"Checkmate," the synthetic voice announced.

Victoria held up a hand, bringing the room back to a focused quiet.

"The mountain does not belong to the state," Victoria reminded them, her voice ringing with sovereign pride. "It belongs to the people who are strong enough to survive it. The caretakers of the air-gap protect their own. Emma, keep your Kodiak plows chained and fueled. Clara, keep the grinder in the toolbox. By tomorrow afternoon, the City of Seattle will have handed us the keys to that gate."

The Vanguard Assembly broke with a renewed, electric energy, the panic completely neutralized by the sheer brilliance of the Matriarch's proxy war.
