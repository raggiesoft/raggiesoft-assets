# Part 1: The Civilian Instinct (14:15)

The maintenance catwalk near the primary turbine intake of Ross Dam was encased in three inches of solid, black ice.

Henderson and Barrett were two hours into a brutal, freezing shift, trying to manually clear a jammed hydraulic gate. The air was a biting negative ten degrees, and the roar of the Skagit River churning through the spillways below was deafening.

"Hand me the heavy pry bar!" Henderson yelled over the rushing water, bracing his boots against the steel grating.

Barrett turned to grab the tool from the yellow Tundra-Track parked on the access road. He didn't see the ice fracture.

A sharp, violent *CRACK* echoed over the water. A massive shelf of accumulated ice and sheared granite broke loose from the cliff face directly above the intake. It plummeted thirty feet, slamming directly into the catwalk.

The impact threw Barrett to the ground. When he scrambled to his knees, his breath caught in his throat.

The heavy steel railing had buckled entirely, pinning Henderson beneath a massive, immovable slab of ice and twisted metal. Henderson’s face was completely drained of color, his teeth gritted in absolute, blinding agony. His right leg was trapped, the angle of his femur sickeningly unnatural.

Panic—pure, unadulterated civilian panic—flooded Barrett’s system.

"Henderson!" Barrett screamed, scrambling over the ice and desperately tearing at the steel railing. It wouldn't budge. The slab weighed over a ton. "Hold on! I'm getting help!"

Operating entirely on lowland instinct, Barrett shoved a trembling, thick-gloved hand into his heavy canvas jacket and ripped out his cell phone.

He flipped it open, his thumb instantly mashing 9-1-1.

He stared at the screen. *NO SIGNAL.* "Come on, come on!" Barrett yelled, holding the phone up to the sky, desperately trying to catch a cellular bounce from the valley.

"Barrett..." Henderson groaned, his voice weak, his breath pluming in the freezing air.

"It's dead!" Barrett panicked, dropping the phone into the snow. "I'm going to the maintenance shed! I'll use the landline! I'll call Seattle dispatch, they can send a medevac helicopter from—"

"Barrett, stop!" Henderson roared, the sheer pain making his voice ragged.

Barrett froze, staring wide-eyed at his senior engineer.

"Listen to me," Henderson ground out, his head falling back against the freezing grating. "If an engineer at Ross Dam suffers a severe medical emergency during one of these whiteouts, municipal helicopters cannot fly, and Seattle City Light Tundra-Tracks cannot breach the ice walls. 911 is on the other side of a forty-foot wall of Cascades Concrete. The state is not coming for us."

Barrett stared at him, the horrifying reality of the Air-Gap finally, truly sinking in. The safety nets of civilization did not exist here.

"Then who do we call?" Barrett asked, his voice trembling.

Henderson forced his eyes open, looking up at the jagged, snow-blind peaks of Ruby Mountain. "The only entity capable of executing a rescue is the Vance-Whitaker pack. Get to the Tundra-Track. Use the encrypted VHF radio."
