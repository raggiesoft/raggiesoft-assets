## The Traitor

The Great Hall of The Prospector's Keep felt less like a luxury alpine lodge and more like a besieged bunker. The massive, 1890s timber-framed room was littered with open, half-packed designer suitcases and overstuffed leather duffel bags.

The air was toxic, thick with the bitter, desperate rage of the older generation. The parents, aunts, and uncles who had spent the last two decades bleeding Laurent Vance's fortune dry were reluctantly throwing their possessions into their bags.

Standing near the unlit hearth, fuming in the shadows, was Richard Vance. He glared across the room at the thirty-six-year-old woman who had just legally annihilated them.

Victoria Vance stood perfectly still near the grand mahogany reception desk. She wasn't packing. She was watching them, her posture radiating absolute, sovereign authority.

"This is a mistake, Victoria, and you know it," Richard spat, slamming his hand against the stone mantle. "You think you can just seize the estate? We are the senior bloodline. We built the modern era of this family!"

"You bankrupted this family, Richard," Victoria corrected, her voice cold and clinically detached. "You ran this resort into the ground, and when you ran out of liquidity, you brokered a backdoor deal to clear-cut five thousand acres of our old-growth buffer to a commercial logging conglomerate".

"It was a business decision!" another uncle shouted from the mezzanine stairs. "We needed the capital!"

"It was a fiduciary breach," Victoria shot back, her eyes narrowing into a terrifying, predatory glare. "And it violated the original 1897 covenant that legally demanded the forest remain untouched in perpetuity. You tried to sell our armor to pay for your lowland country clubs".

Richard sneered, turning his venom toward the woman standing quietly by the massive river-stone fireplace. Eleanor Whitaker.

"We wouldn't be in this mess if it wasn't for the rat," Richard hissed, taking a step toward his sister. "You stole the contracts from our offices, Eleanor. You handed them directly to her".

Eleanor didn't flinch. She was the mother of the Whitaker trades crew, and she possessed a rugged, unshakeable integrity that her siblings lacked. "I protected the mountain, Richard," Eleanor stated firmly. "You were going to strip the perimeter bare. I wasn't going to let you sell the ground out from under my daughters."

"You sold out your own brothers and sisters!" Richard roared, his face flushing red. He whipped back around to face Victoria. "And the name change? Are you out of your mind, Victoria? 'The Vance-Whitaker Trust'? You are legally amending Laurent Vance's historic charter just to honor a snitch?"

"I am legally amending the charter to permanently honor the only adult in your generation who possessed a shred of integrity," Victoria said, her voice dropping into a deadly, uncompromising register. "It is a daily reminder to the cousins you are leaving behind: Blood gets you on the mountain, but loyalty is what keeps you here".

The adults grumbled, zipping up their luggage with furious, jerky movements.

"We aren't leaving," one of the aunts declared stubbornly, crossing her arms. "You can wave that judge's gavel around all you want, Victoria. We are physically snowed in. The state highway is closed. We aren't going anywhere."

Victoria let out a short, dark laugh that carried no humor.

"You haven't been listening to the WSDOT radio frequency, have you?" Victoria asked softly. She looked at the grandfather clock ticking in the corner. "The rotary plows are clearing the final avalanche chute right now. The state is unlocking the steel gates at Milepost 134 tomorrow morning".

The blood drained from Richard's face.

"The Whatcom County Sheriff has already been briefed, and he holds the eviction order," Victoria promised, her eyes burning with an apocalyptic finality. "The absolute second those gates swing open on State Route 20 tomorrow, the cruisers are driving straight up Prospector Road. You can either pack your bags tonight and drive your Apex Mountaineers out of my motorpool with a shred of dignity, or you can wait for the Sheriff to physically drag you off my property in handcuffs".

Victoria turned her back on them, an ultimate dismissal.

"Have your bags packed by dawn," the Matriarch commanded. "Your era is over."

## The Vision

The heavy oak doors of The Prospector's Keep slammed shut, cutting off the furious exhaust of the departing luxury SUVs. The echo reverberated through the cavernous 1890s timber of the Great Hall, followed by a profound, ringing silence.

The screaming was finally over.

Amanda immediately knelt beside Johnny's manual wheelchair. At fourteen years old, her protective instincts were already absolute. She wrapped her arms around his shoulders, burying her face against his neck to provide the deep pressure she knew his nervous system desperately needed after the chaotic, high-decibel confrontation with their exiled relatives.

Victoria Vance took a slow, steadying breath. She had just successfully excised the rot from her family tree. She walked over to the twins, her imposing, ironclad posture softening the moment she reached them. She placed a warm, grounded hand on the back of Johnny's neck.

"It's done," Victoria murmured fiercely. "They are never coming back."

"Let's get out of the lobby," Amanda whispered to her brother.

Amanda unlocked the brakes on his chair and pushed him away from the grand staircase, navigating the drafty, echoing corridors of the first floor. Currently, the sixteen remaining cousins were scattered across the sprawling, unrenovated guest wings of the Keep.

Johnny and Amanda’s assigned room was an ADA-accessible guest suite. It was cramped and fundamentally unsuited for permanent living. It held a single Queen bed, which the twins shared, and pushed tightly against the faded wallpaper was a narrow rollaway bed where Victoria slept.

Amanda parked the chair and helped Johnny transfer onto the edge of the mattress. The room was freezing. Outside, the May thaw was beginning, but inside, the archaic 1890s plumbing hissed and clanked violently within the walls. A cold draft bled straight through the poorly sealed windowpanes.

Victoria stepped into the room, pulling the heavy door shut. She didn't sit down on the rollaway bed. Instead, she stood in the center of the cramped space, looking at the peeling wallpaper, the rattling radiator, and the water-stained ceiling tiles. For decades, the older generation had bled the resort dry, subjecting the grand, historic interiors to cheap, ugly cosmetic patches while the fundamental infrastructure actively failed.

"This isn't going to work," Victoria stated, her voice dropping into a cold, calculating register.

Amanda paused pulling the heavy quilt over Johnny's legs. "The room?"

"The entire mountain," Victoria corrected. She crossed her arms, her brilliant legal and administrative mind shifting from the courtroom to the architecture. "We cannot live scattered across three hundred failing hotel rooms. We are a pack. We need a sanctuary. A dedicated, impenetrable wing built entirely for us."

Johnny rested his stiff hand on his lap desk. He watched his older cousin pace the narrow floor.

"I am not opening the resort this season," Victoria announced, the decision hardening into absolute law in real-time. "We are going to gut it. Like the Truman White House".

Amanda's eyes widened. "Gut the Keep? But the summer revenue—"

"Is irrelevant if the roof collapses on us next winter," Victoria interrupted smoothly. "We are going to tear out every rotting internal wall, every failing wire, and every corroded pipe. We will drop a modern, seismically sound steel skeleton inside this historic shell. And when we rebuild the interior, we are going to section off a premium, multi-story block of this lodge to serve as our permanent Family Quarters".

Victoria looked at Johnny, her eyes shining with a fierce, visionary light.

"You won't have to navigate narrow hotel doors or rely on these miserable, drafty rooms ever again. I am going to build you an Owner's Quarters with 18-foot ceilings, an indoor therapeutic pool, and a private commercial elevator".

Johnny let out a slow, raspy exhale. The sheer scale of what she was proposing was staggering, but looking at Victoria Vance, he didn't doubt the execution for a single second.

Amanda climbed onto the bed, wrapping her arms securely around Johnny to anchor him. She looked at her older cousin, the undisputed Matriarch of the mountain.

"Where do we start?" Amanda asked.

Victoria pulled a small leather notebook from her blazer pocket and uncapped her fountain pen.

"We start by drawing the borders," Victoria said, looking out the drafty window at the dark, towering peaks of the Cascades. "The guests can have the rest of the hotel. But this side of the mountain belongs to us."

## The Migration

The drafty, peeling confines of Room N314 felt entirely different now. It was no longer just a miserable hotel suite on the third floor of the North Wing; it was Ground Zero for the Vance-Whitaker empire.

Victoria turned away from the freezing window overlooking the dark, jagged silhouette of the Cascades. She looked at Amanda, who was still sitting protectively behind Johnny on the edge of the Queen bed, and then stepped toward the door.

"I am officially establishing the perimeter," Victoria announced, her tone shifting from visionary architect back to tactical commander. "Tonight will be the absolute last night this family sleeps scattered across three different wings of a failing hotel. Once the Sheriff clears the property tomorrow morning, I am claiming Room N316 right next door. It will serve as my temporary office and command center while the contractors gut the rest of the building."

Victoria opened the heavy oak door to the hallway. Standing just outside, having silently gathered during Victoria's declaration, were Morgan, Diana, Sarah, and Ashleigh. The older cousins had naturally gravitated toward Johnny's room the moment the screaming in the Great Hall had stopped.

Victoria looked at her nieces. "Tomorrow afternoon, I want all of you out of the East and West wings. Claim the suites surrounding N314 and N316. The North Wing has the premium views of Ross Lake, and more importantly, it puts us all in a single, defensible corridor. The rooms are fully furnished; just pack your clothing and toiletries. We consolidate the pack tomorrow."

Twenty-three-year-old Morgan Foster leaned against the doorframe. She looked at her younger cousins crammed into the drafty room, and then looked down the long, dimly lit corridor of the North Wing.

Morgan shook her head. "No."

Victoria paused, her sharp eyes narrowing slightly at the rare defiance from her lead mechanic. "Excuse me?"

"We aren't waiting for tomorrow," Morgan stated flatly, pushing off the doorframe. She looked at twenty-year-old Diana, who was already nodding in absolute agreement. Morgan turned back to Victoria. "We aren't spending one more night sleeping down the hall from people who tried to sell us out. We're moving right now."

Victoria stared at the twenty-three-year-old. The Matriarch didn't discipline the insubordination; a fiercely proud, razor-sharp smile broke across her face.

"Execute," Victoria commanded.

The pack mobilized with terrifying, synchronized speed.

It wasn't a methodical, careful packing job. It was a tactical raid on their own childhoods. Because The Prospector's Keep operated as a commercial resort, every single room was already outfitted with heavy oak armoires, thick mattresses, and heavy quilts. They didn't need to move a single piece of furniture.

Within ten minutes, the third-floor corridors of the East and West wings were chaotic with motion. The cousins stormed into their old, assigned rooms, ripping their clothes out of drawers, shoving toiletries into canvas bags, and throwing their winter coats over their shoulders.

In the West Wing, Richard Vance stepped out of his suite, drawn by the sudden, heavy thud of combat boots and frantic movement. He watched in absolute disbelief as his fifteen-year-old twin daughters, Sarah and Ashleigh, marched past him carrying armfuls of clothes and textbooks.

"What do you think you're doing?" Richard barked, his face flushing with a fresh wave of apoplectic rage. "Get back in your room! We are leaving tomorrow, and you are coming with us!"

Sarah didn't even break her stride. She didn't flinch, and she didn't look at him. She just kept walking.

"Did you hear me?!" Richard roared, stepping into the center of the hallway to physically block their path.

Before he could even raise his hand, Morgan Foster materialized from the adjacent stairwell. The twenty-three-year-old heavy mechanic didn't yell. She simply walked directly into Richard’s airspace, using her height and her broad, grease-stained shoulders to physically force him to take a step backward.

"They don't take orders from you anymore, Richard," Morgan said, her voice a low, lethal hum that echoed the 1890s timber. "Go pack your bags. Your era is over."

Richard stared at his niece, his fists trembling, but he didn't push back. The power dynamic hadn't just shifted; it had been entirely severed. Defeated, he retreated back into his suite, slamming the door.

The migration flowed entirely into the North Wing.

Johnny sat in his manual wheelchair in the open doorway of N314, his hands resting quietly on his lap desk. He watched the absolute, unapologetic takeover of the corridor.

Thirteen-year-old Emma Bennett marched into Room N312, immediately claiming the bed nearest the window. Diana took N315 across the hall, carrying a crate of her favorite culinary textbooks. The younger cousins—Mia, Zoe, Chloe, and Lily—spilled into the surrounding suites, their laughter and overlapping voices filling the drafty hallway with a sudden, overwhelming sense of life.

Through the massive, frosted windowpanes at the end of the corridor, the dark, freezing expanse of Ross Lake was faintly visible under the moonlight. The North Wing was cold, and the wallpaper was peeling, but as Johnny watched his family barricade themselves into a single, unified perimeter, the aesthetic completely ceased to matter.

Amanda stepped out of N314, holding two heavy down comforters she had liberated from an empty linen closet. She draped one carefully over Johnny’s lap, tucking the edges in to block the draft, and smiled down at him.

"Well, boss," Amanda murmured, looking down the bustling hallway where Morgan and Clara were already establishing who got which side of the closet in N310. "I think we just claimed the high ground."

Johnny looked up at his twin sister. He raised his stiff left hand and offered a sharp, deliberate thumbs-up.

The biological parents were fuming in the dark on the other side of the Keep, but their anger was utterly irrelevant. The fortress had officially fallen to the Vanguard, and the pack was finally together.
