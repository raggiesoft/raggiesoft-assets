## The Diagnostics

The heavy oak doors of The Prospector's Keep were propped wide open, letting the crisp, thawing spring air flood into the 1890s lobby. Out on the cobblestone turnaround, a fleet of heavy-duty commercial work trucks had replaced the exiled generation's luxury SUVs.

Master architects, structural engineers, and master plumbers filed into the Great Hall, carrying heavy clipboards and diagnostic equipment. They looked up at the towering Douglas fir columns and the massive stone fireplaces, visibly awed by the scale of the Klondike-era construction .

Victoria Vance stood in the center of the Persian rug, dressed in a sharp, utilitarian blazer and dark slacks. She didn't offer them a hospitality greeting; she offered them a mandate.

"Gentlemen," Victoria addressed the lead contractor, her voice echoing clearly in the cavernous space. "You are here because the previous owners prioritized cosmetic patches over fundamental infrastructure . That era is over. I want every inch of this building evaluated."

The lead structural engineer nodded, pulling out a set of old blueprints. "Ms. Vance, depending on what we find behind these walls, a full diagnostic could take us weeks. We can expedite the surface-level checks if you're trying to meet a summer opening deadline."

"There is no summer deadline," Victoria countered smoothly, her tone absolute. "The Prospector's Keep is officially closed for the 1999 season. We are not rushing this."

She stepped closer to the contractor, her eyes locking onto his with terrifying, sovereign intensity.

"I would rather you take twice as long if it means you uncover something," Victoria instructed. "If you suspect a pipe is corroded, you do not patch it—you tear the wall down to the bedrock and trace the line. Do not hide the rot to save me time. Find the vulnerabilities, map them, and tell me exactly what it will cost to rip them out. You have the run of the building."

The contractors exchanged a look of deep, professional respect. They rarely encountered owners willing to sacrifice a season of revenue to do the job right.

"Understood, Ms. Vance," the foreman said, gesturing for his crew to fan out. "We'll start with the basement boiler lines and work our way up."


While the contractors began systematically dismantling the failing infrastructure of the first floor, the younger cousins were completely insulated from the chaos.

Up in the unrenovated North Wing—the temporary sanctuary the pack had claimed the night before —the minors were gathered around a cluster of heavy oak tables, deeply immersed in their daily routines. The eviction of their parents hadn't derailed their education; Victoria's logistical architecture ensured that the pack's academic momentum never paused.

Victoria had already engineered an ironclad Alternative Learning Experience (ALE) agreement with the local rural school district. To bypass the unreliability of the mountain's fragile satellite internet, Victoria digitized all the educational software and syllabi, hosting everything locally on the Keep's Quantum server.

The room was quiet, save for the rhythmic clicking of their standard two-button optical mice as the cousins logged into their workstations. Operating entirely on the internal localized network, they accessed their coursework at blazing, uninterrupted LAN speeds.

Fourteen-year-old Amanda sat next to Johnny, quietly working through an algebra module, while the younger cousins—Emma, Grace, Chloe, and Mia—focused intently on their own screens. There was no panic about submitting their digital worksheets or losing a connection.

They operated under Victoria's highly efficient "Batch Upload" protocol. The cousins simply saved their work to the local server directory. At the end of the week, Victoria would collect the zipped files and transmit them directly to the school district via a secure FTP client during a stable connectivity window.

The adults were gone, the walls downstairs were coming down, and the mountain was finally theirs. Sitting in the quiet hum of their digital classroom, the youngest members of the Vance-Whitaker pack went to work.

## The Truman Execution

The lead structural engineer walked back into the Great Hall just after two o'clock in the afternoon. His hands were coated in a thick layer of black, century-old dust, and his expression was grim.

Victoria Vance was sitting at the mahogany reception desk, reviewing the estate's financial ledgers. She looked up, recognizing the heavy posture of a man carrying bad news.

"You didn't need weeks, did you?" Victoria asked quietly, setting her pen down.

The engineer shook his head, laying a flashlight on the desk. "Ms. Vance, we pulled back the cosmetic paneling in the East Wing and took core samples of the primary load-bearing joists. The dry rot is extensive. The 1890s plumbing isn't just leaking; it is actively corroding the foundational timber."

He leaned forward, his professional demeanor entirely stripped of any customer-service softening. "You are on borrowed time. The building is currently supporting its own weight, but if a severe winter storm pushes the snow load past its absolute limit, or if a significant seismic event hits the Cascades, this lodge will pancake. It is fundamentally unsafe."

Victoria didn't gasp, and she didn't mourn the loss of her revenue. She simply absorbed the physics of the mountain. She possessed the independent wealth to fix it, and she possessed the sovereign will to prioritize the survival of her pack over commercial profit.

"Then we do not patch it," Victoria stated, her voice hardening into absolute, unbreakable titanium. "We tear it out."

The engineer blinked. "Tear what out?"

"Everything," Victoria commanded. "Every rotting internal wall, every failing wire, and every corroded pipe. We are going to execute a complete structural gutting. I want a modern, seismically sound steel framework dropped directly inside this historic wooden shell".

It was a staggering, multi-million dollar architectural directive. Victoria was explicitly modeling her vision on the famous "Truman Reconstruction" of the White House in the 1950s: the historic exterior stone and old-growth timber shell would be meticulously braced and preserved, while the entire failing interior was hollowed out and completely replaced .

When the dust settled, she would lay miles of brand-new, high-pressure copper piping, state-of-the-art commercial electrical grids, and a massive network of Ethernet cable. To the naked eye, the lodge would remain a perfectly preserved Klondike-era artifact, but beneath the surface, it would become a heavily armored, modern survival bunker .

"A total gut and rebuild..." the engineer breathed out, doing the mental math on the sheer scale of the labor. "Ms. Vance, working around the winter snowpack, a project of that magnitude will take years."

"Then it takes years," Victoria replied without a second of hesitation. "My family's safety supersedes your timeline, and it supersedes my profit margins. Get your architects on the phone and start drafting the steel."

The engineer nodded, a deep, profound respect washing over his face. He turned and walked rapidly back toward the West Wing to inform his crew that they weren't doing an inspection anymore; they were drawing up a fortress.

Victoria turned to the heavy Quantum OS terminal sitting behind the reception desk.

She opened the administrative backend for The Prospector's Keep public website. The flashing cursor waited for her command in the primary homepage banner field. She didn't consult a PR firm, and she didn't offer a corporate apology.

With sharp, deliberate keystrokes, Victoria typed the death warrant for the 1999 season:

*To Our Valued Guests,*

*For nearly a century, The Prospector's Keep has stood as a monument to the North Cascades. To ensure it stands for a century more, we are prioritizing the absolute safety and structural integrity of our lodge above all else. Effective immediately, The Prospector's Keep will undergo a massive, multi-year historic restoration and seismic modernization.* *We thank you for your enduring loyalty. All current reservations will be fully refunded. There is currently no timeline for our reopening. We will welcome you back to the mountain when the work is done.*

She hit the deploy macro.

The website updated instantly across the globe. Victoria Vance closed the terminal. The public era of the Keep was officially paused, and the era of the Vanguard had begun.
