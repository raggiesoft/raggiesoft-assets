## The Escort

The rhythmic *hiss-click* of Johnny’s RespiraCore Compressor machine was suddenly drowned out by the deafening, pneumatic blast of commercial air brakes.

A massive, fifty-three-foot refrigerated 18-wheeler groaned under the concrete archway of the Lower Motorpool. The cab was caked in a thick layer of heavy, wet slush, and the massive tire chains rattled violently against the pristine floor of the subterranean bunker before the rig hissed to a complete stop in the primary loading bay.

The driver’s side door swung open, and a burly man in a high-visibility vest climbed down. He looked pale, exhausted, and utterly terrified. He took one look past the concrete archway at the brutal, sheer incline of Prospector Road disappearing into the blizzard.

"I'm not doing it," the driver announced, holding his hands up as Emma approached with her clipboard. "I don't care what the routing slip says. I am not driving this rig up that goat path. It's a suicide run. You can call my dispatcher, you can refuse the load, but I am dropping this trailer right here."

Emma stopped a few feet away, her expression perfectly calm. She didn't argue. She simply tapped her pen against her clipboard.

"Take a breath, man. You don't have to," Emma said, her voice a soothing contrast to the echoing diesel engine. "Read your contract again. You aren't contracted for the summit. Your destination is the Lower Motorpool. You are exactly where you are supposed to be."

The driver blinked, the panic slowly draining from his face. "I am?"

"Prospector Road has a strict wheelbase limit," Emma explained smoothly. "An 18-wheeler would jackknife on turn two. Pop the back doors. We’ll take it from here."

The driver let out a massive breath of relief and practically sprinted to unlatch the heavy rear doors of the trailer.

Emma turned to her lowland valets, who were already staging one of the Keep’s agile, chained box trucks back-to-back with the 18-wheeler.

"Alright, listen up," Emma called out, her voice echoing off the concrete. "That is three pallets of vintage Château St. Claire. It is highly fragile, and because I am seventeen years old, I am legally barred from touching a single bottle of it. Underage protocol is in effect. Adult staff only on the forklift. Cross-load it, secure it, and do not drop Victoria's champagne."

The adult logistics crew moved in instantly, the electric forklift whirring to life to carefully transfer the tightly wrapped pallets from the commercial rig into the Vance-Whitaker box truck. Emma stood back with her hands in her pockets, supervising the perimeter without ever crossing the legal boundary.

Johnny watched from his wheelchair, his chest rising and falling in time with the mechanical lung. He loved the absolute, uncompromising rigidity of the Vanguard's rulebook. Victoria Vance left zero legal vulnerabilities, even down in the basement.

"Alright, boss," Morgan said, stepping out from behind the heavy Ridgeback van. "The cargo is secured. It's time to get you back above the clouds."

Johnny wheeled his chair forward. Emma met him halfway, her stern operational face melting into a warm, protective smile. She leaned down, careful not to bump the RespiraCore Compressor tubing, and wrapped her arms around his shoulders in a tight, grounding hug.

"I'll hold the line down here," Emma whispered near his ear. "Tell Victoria the Motorpool is locked down. Nobody gets past the gates."

Johnny squeezed his eyes shut, leaning his head against her shoulder. *System secure.* Morgan took over, unlocking the hydraulic ramp on the Ridgeback. She seamlessly transferred Johnny inside, locking his chair into the heavy floor anchors. She closed the heavy side door, instantly sealing out the roar of the idling diesel engines.

Morgan climbed into the driver's seat and keyed her radio.

"Box Truck One, this is Escort Lead," Morgan transmitted. "I am pacing you. Keep your bumper ten feet off my taillights. If you lose traction, you hit the horn and hold your brakes. I will pull you up."

*"Copy that, Lead,"* the driver of the box truck radioed back.

Morgan shifted the Ridgeback into four-wheel-drive low. The amber strobe lights on the roof flared to life, casting rhythmic yellow flashes against the concrete walls. She hit the gas, and the heavy van surged forward, out of the bunker and straight into the teeth of the blizzard.

The ascent was brutal. The tropical moisture from the Pineapple Express was actively colliding with the arctic freeze, dumping snow at an astronomical rate. Morgan drove with terrifying, surgical precision. She hit the hairpins wide, letting the heavy tires of the Ridgeback break the fresh snowpack, creating a perfect, rutted track for the champagne truck following closely behind them.

As they climbed past four thousand feet, the environment began to shift. The heavy, damp, Skagit marine layer was left behind in the valley. The temperature plummeted, and the air thinned out into crisp, freezing alpine oxygen.

Johnny felt the pressure lift from his chest. His intercostal muscles stopped fighting the humidity. He reached up, unfastened the straps of the RespiraCore Compressor mask, and pulled it off his face, taking a deep, unassisted breath of the freezing mountain air.

"Better?" Morgan asked, keeping her eyes locked on the whiteout conditions ahead.

"Much," Johnny’s text-to-speech chimed quietly.

Thirty minutes later, the Ridgeback rounded the final switchback and leveled out. The massive, stone facade of The Prospector’s Keep loomed out of the storm, its windows glowing with warm, golden light against the darkening sky.

Morgan bypassed the civilian valet circle entirely, driving the Ridgeback and its trailing box truck directly around to the reinforced service portico at the rear of the kitchens.

The heavy loading doors were already rolled open. Diana’s kitchen staff—flanked by Clara and Maya—were standing on the concrete dock, bundled in heavy coats, ready to receive the cargo.

Morgan parked the Ridgeback and killed the engine. The delivery had made it through the air-gap.

"Welcome back to the summit, Johnny," Morgan smiled, unbuckling her seatbelt. "Let's go tell Victoria we bought her a few more hours."

## The Tactical Seed

The heavy oak doors of the Owner's Quarters swung open, and the roaring warmth of Victoria’s massive fireplace washed over them.

After the heavy, suffocating humidity of the Skagit gorge and the biting, wet wind of the loading dock, the climate-controlled sanctuary of the Owner's Quarters felt like an absolute triumph of engineering. Johnny took a deep, unassisted breath of the dry, highly oxygenated alpine air. His core spasticity, already calming from the ascent, finally settled back into its comfortable baseline.

Victoria was sitting behind her mahogany desk, reviewing a stack of WSDOT highway clearing manifests. She looked up as Morgan wheeled Johnny up to his designated position at her right flank.

"I assume the Château St. Claire survived the air-gap?" Victoria asked, setting her fountain pen down.

"Not a single shattered bottle," Morgan confirmed, dropping into one of the heavy leather chairs opposite the desk. "Diana's crew has it secured in the cellar. And the Lower Motorpool is locked down tight."

Victoria gave a single, satisfied nod. She hadn't actually needed a status report on the subterranean bunker. "Emma holds the line. I never doubted it."

Johnny let his stiff hands rest on his keyboard. He looked out the massive bay window behind Victoria’s desk. Up here at seven thousand feet, the tropical moisture of the Pineapple Express was already beginning to freeze as it collided with the high-altitude air. The sky wasn't just dumping snow; it was violently erasing the horizon.

Johnny had just seen the sheer panic in the eyes of a professional 18-wheeler driver at a mere twelve hundred feet. He had felt the crushing weight of the atmospheric river in his own lungs.

His analytical processor began to run the tactical probabilities. The math was starting to look increasingly hostile.

Johnny’s fingers moved deliberately over his mechanical keyboard. He hit the macro key.

"The perimeter is currently secure," his text-to-speech software announced smoothly into the quiet office. "But the atmospheric variables are degrading faster than the historical models predict. Victoria, I recommend we begin running contingency logistics."

Victoria leaned forward, her sharp, intelligent eyes locking onto her Second-in-Command. "Contingency logistics for what, Johnny?"

"For the Centennial Gala," Johnny’s software replied, his dark eyes meeting hers. "If WSDOT cannot maintain the pioneer trench against this loading event, October 31st is a mathematical liability. We should reconsider the viability of the Halloween timeline."

It was a bold, massive strategic suggestion. Moving a four-hundred-person luxury event was a logistical nightmare that most commercial resorts wouldn't even entertain until the state explicitly forced their hand. But Johnny didn't care about hospitality industry norms. He cared about the physical safety of the Vanguard.

Victoria didn't dismiss him. She didn't offer a patronizing reassurance that everything would be fine. She treated his assessment with the profound, absolute respect it deserved.

The Matriarch looked out the window at the howling whiteout, her jaw tightening.

"I am not ready to declare a full retreat from the Halloween timeline yet," Victoria said, her voice dropping into its cold, sovereign register. "We are thirty days out. But your tactical assessment is noted, and it is entirely valid. I will not trap this family behind a wall of ice for the sake of a party."

Victoria turned her gaze to her Lead Engineer.

"Morgan," Victoria commanded. "You own the sky. I want you tracking that Gulf anomaly every single hour. Do not take your eyes off the PK-7015 feed."

Morgan sat forward, her posture shifting from casual mechanic to apex forecaster.

"I won't," Morgan promised, her eyes flicking between Victoria and Johnny. "Miller at WSDOT is watching the same B.C. arctic front we are. If that barometric pressure blinks, or if the Snow Water Equivalent starts pushing past the safe loading threshold for the Liberty Bell chutes... you two will be the very first to know."

Johnny gave a firm nod, leaning back into the custom foam of his wheelchair.

The tactical seed was planted. The timeline was officially on notice. He closed his eyes, immensely relieved to be safely locked inside the impenetrable stone walls of his mountain, while the Fog of War raged outside.
