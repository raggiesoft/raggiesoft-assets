## All Hands

The Fog of War had finally broken, replaced by the crushing, undeniable reality of the atmosphere.

At 4:00 AM, the first major pulse of the collision zone had slammed into the Cascade Crest. It did not arrive as a graceful, swirling alpine flurry. It arrived as a violent atmospheric dumping of "Cascades Concrete"—eight inches of hyper-dense, water-laden snow falling in a terrifying six-hour window.

By 6:30 AM, the Owner's Quarters of the Prospector’s Keep was packed to absolute capacity.

The heavy mahogany desk had been pushed back. Every single chair, sofa, and leather ottoman in Victoria’s sprawling office was occupied. For the first time since the Nisqually Earthquake, all seventeen members of the Vance-Whitaker pack were gathered in a single room. From the twenty-seven-year-old Lead Engineer down to twelve-year-old Lily, the Vanguard was assembled.

The roaring fireplace cast flickering shadows over the faces of the heavy operations mechanics, the legal guardians, and the perimeter sentries. Nobody was panicking. They were waiting for their orders.

At the front of the room, Victoria stood beside a massive, rolling CRT monitor that Johnny had tethered directly to the Keep's Quantum OS. Johnny was parked directly to her right in his manual wheelchair, his rigid fingers resting over his keyboard. Amanda stood immediately behind him, her hands resting firmly on his shoulders, providing the deep, grounding pressure he needed in a room filled with this much kinetic energy.

Morgan Foster stood on Victoria’s left, holding a thick printout of the morning telemetry.

The heavy, encrypted satellite phone on the desk was on speaker.

*"The pioneer trench is holding for now,"* the exhausted voice of Miller, the WSDOT Regional Director, echoed through the quiet room. *"But my Kodiak rotaries are already chewing through a foot of solid ice in the lower chutes. The B.C. arctic front accelerated overnight, Morgan. It hit the tropical moisture directly over your airspace."*

"We felt the impact, Miller," Morgan said flatly. "Give us the revised timeline."

There was a heavy pause on the line.

*"The Halloween models are dead,"* Miller stated. *"NWAC is officially projecting a historic loading event on the Liberty Bell ridges. I am revising the SR-20 closure estimate from mid-November to the first week of November. And honestly, Victoria? If the SWE (Snow Water Equivalent) keeps climbing at this rate, it might be late October. I cannot guarantee safe civilian passage past the 25th."*

Victoria Vance looked at the glowing radar loop on Johnny’s screen. The massive red and purple anomaly was locked in a terrifying stall directly over their coordinates.

She calculated the operational liability. If she waited until October 31st, she was risking the lives of four hundred VIP guests, fifty caterers, and a fleet of commercial delivery drivers. If the highway gates dropped early, the Prospector's Keep would become a refugee camp for panicking billionaires.

It was an unacceptable breach of her sovereign control. She refused to let the mountain dictate her operational liability.

"Understood, Miller," Victoria said, her voice cutting through the ambient noise of the storm rattling the thick windowpanes. "Keep the plows running. We will handle our perimeter. Keep Telemetry out."

Morgan reached over and cut the connection.

Victoria turned to face the sixteen women and her Second-in-Command. The Matriarch’s eyes were cold, calculating, and utterly unshakeable.

"The mountain just moved the finish line," Victoria announced, her voice carrying the absolute, uncompromising weight of a century-old dynasty. "We are not getting trapped, and we are not canceling. We are moving the 100th Anniversary Gala up."

A ripple of intense, focused energy swept through the room.

Victoria turned to Johnny. "Run the absolute earliest logistical date we can execute."

Johnny’s mind was already three steps ahead. His fingers fired across the mechanical keyboard, his text-to-speech software seamlessly bridging the gap between his brilliant processor and the room.

"Cross-referencing vendor transit times with WSDOT clearing schedules," Johnny's synthetic voice stated cleanly. "Assuming maximum acceleration of Diana's kitchen prep and Emma's perimeter escorts, the most mathematically sound execution date is Thursday, October 16th."

*Five days.* They had exactly five days to compress three weeks of high-end luxury event planning.

Victoria didn't flinch. She looked at Diana, who was standing by the fireplace in her chef’s coat. "Can you feed four hundred people a five-course venison dinner on Thursday night?"

Diana’s dark eyes flashed with pure, culinary adrenaline. "I will have to run the prep cooks on twenty-four-hour shifts, and I need Clara and Maya to haul the dry goods up from the sub-basement right now. But yes. I can plate it by 19:00 hours."

Victoria turned to Emma, the seventeen-year-old Perimeter Architect. "The delivery trucks won't be able to handle the ice. You are taking the Kodiak."

"I'll take Grace and Chloe," Emma nodded sharply, already pulling her radio from her belt. "We will intercept the commercial trucks at the base of the highway and physically drag them up the mountain if we have to."

Victoria looked at Ashleigh and Sarah, the legal engine of the family. "I need the narrative spun immediately. The billionaires cannot know they are outrunning a catastrophic avalanche. They will panic."

"We will rebrand the date change this morning," Sarah confirmed, her legal mind already drafting the communications. "We'll call it an 'Exclusive Early Winter Premiere.' We'll spin the storm as a luxury aesthetic. They get to sip champagne while the first major snows fall. They’ll eat it up. No one will cancel."

"Good," Victoria said. She placed her hands flat on her mahogany desk, leaning forward to look at her family.

"This is what we were built for," the Matriarch told them, the fierce pride radiating from her every word. "The civilians down in Seattle are going to freeze and panic. But up here, we hold the line. Execute the pivot."

The pack broke instantly. The younger cousins sprinted for the heavy operations bays. Diana marched out the door to command the kitchens. Ashleigh and Sarah headed straight for the communications array.

The Fog of War was thick, but the Vanguard was moving faster.

## The Rebrand

As the heavy oak doors of the Owner's Quarters clicked shut behind the departing Vanguard, the intense, kinetic energy of the room instantly shifted into cold, administrative precision.

Victoria walked behind her massive mahogany desk and sat down. She didn't take a moment to breathe or process the panic of the collapsing weather models. She simply pulled a fresh, blank legal pad to the center of her blotter and uncapped her fountain pen.

Johnny remained parked directly to her right. He hadn't followed Amanda out the door. He was the Second-in-Command, and he knew that while Morgan fought the physical war downstairs, the psychological and logistical war was fought right here. He wanted to watch the Matriarch work.

"Plug in, Johnny," Victoria commanded, not looking up as she began writing a rapid series of bullet points. "We need to update the public-facing servers immediately. The narrative has to beat the weather reports."

Johnny’s fingers flew over his mechanical keyboard, pulling up the Keep’s customized HTML backend on his Quantum XN laptop.

"Drafting the primary homepage banner," Johnny’s text-to-speech software chimed quietly. "Awaiting copy."

"Header: *The Prospector’s Keep Announces an Exclusive Early Winter Premiere for the Centennial Gala,*" Victoria dictated, her voice smooth and entirely devoid of the anxiety that had gripped WSDOT. "Body text: *Due to an unprecedented, historic early snowfall, we are offering our VIP guests the rare opportunity to witness the North Cascades transition into winter from the absolute luxury of the Keep. The Centennial Gala has been expedited to Thursday, October 16th, to align with the first major snows of the century.*"

Johnny typed the copy flawlessly, his analytical mind appreciating the sheer brilliance of the spin. She wasn't telling the billionaires they were outrunning a catastrophic avalanche. She was telling them they were getting exclusive, front-row seats to a private, meteorological spectacle.

"Banner is live," Johnny confirmed, hitting the deployment macro.

Victoria immediately picked up her desk phone, dialing the front-of-house concierge desk.

"This is Vance," Victoria said the moment the line connected. "Initiate the Centennial communications protocol. You are going to personally call every single VIP on the Halloween guest list. You will use the 'Early Winter Premiere' script. If they balk at the compressed timeline, offer them a complimentary helicopter transit from Seattle for next summer. But hold the line on the Gala date. It is October 16th, or it is nothing."

Victoria hung up and looked at Johnny. "We will lose a few of the international guests. The flight logistics from Europe won't compress in five days. Acceptable losses. The Seattle and Vancouver billionaires will drive up just for the bragging rights."

She drew a sharp, horizontal line across her legal pad. The VIPs were handled. Now came the true logistical triage.

"Pull up the civilian registry," Victoria ordered. "The standard guest list."

Johnny rapidly cross-referenced the Keep’s booking software. "We currently have eighty-two non-Gala guests occupying the standard suites. Their checkout dates range from tomorrow through October 25th."

"The hard cutoff is Tuesday, October 14th at 11:00 hours," Victoria stated, her voice turning ruthless. She did not care about the hospitality metrics of the standard rooms. She cared about the structural liability of human bodies trapped behind a wall of ice.

"Draft an email to the standard registry," Victoria commanded. "Any guest with a reservation extending past October 14th is to be evacuated. Offer them their choice: a complete, one-hundred-percent refund of their stay, or double the value in credit for the 2004 summer season."

Johnny paused, his fingers hovering over the keys. He calculated the financial loss of the directive.

"Clarification," Johnny typed, his software echoing his thought. "If a guest arrived on October 10th, and is scheduled to check out on October 15th... do we prorate the refund for the nights they were not impacted?"

"No," Victoria said, her eyes locking onto his. "If their booking is impacted by even a single hour, you refund the entire stay. Zero proration. We are not going to argue with them at the front desk over fractional room rates while WSDOT is losing the highway. I want the money back in their accounts so they pack their bags and get off my mountain without a fight."

Johnny nodded, deeply respecting the math. The financial hit was irrelevant compared to the operational disaster of housing eighty-two panicking civilians during a six-month winter siege. He deployed the mass email, watching the server logs as the notifications hit the civilian inboxes.

Victoria drew a second sharp line across her legal pad.

"Now," the Matriarch said, her voice dropping into a dangerous, absolute register. "The alcohol."

She picked up her phone again, bypassing the front desk and dialing the logistics bay in the sub-basement.

"Clara," Victoria said, addressing her twenty-one-year-old heavy operations mechanic. "I need you and Maya to initiate a full sweep of the wine cellars and the secondary storage units. I want an exact inventory of every single drop of alcohol on this property."

Victoria looked out the bay window of her office. The sky over the golden larches was already darkening into a bruised, terrifying gray as the tropical moisture collided with the freezing air.

"Separate the exact manifests Diana needs for Thursday night's Centennial Gala," Victoria instructed Clara. "Everything else—every extra bottle of champagne, every keg, every crate of scotch—gets loaded onto the outbound box trucks this afternoon. If it isn't being uncorked on Thursday night, it goes down the mountain today."

Johnny listened, the ironclad law of the Vanguard resonating in his chest. The billionaires could drink themselves into a stupor on Thursday. But the moment the highway gates dropped, the Keep would revert to a sovereign, military-grade survival outpost. And a survival outpost had to be absolutely, unconditionally dry.

Victoria hung up the phone. In less than twenty minutes, she had completely rewritten the operational reality of a multi-million-dollar resort.

She looked at her Second-in-Command. The legal pad in front of her was covered in sharp, decisive ink.

"The board is clear, Johnny," Victoria said quietly, the adrenaline of the compressed timeline sharpening her features. "Now, we just have to beat the snow."

## The Line of Fire

The lobby of The Prospector’s Keep was a masterpiece of pioneer-era opulence. Massive Douglas fir columns supported the soaring ceiling, and the two-story river-rock fireplaces roared with heat. Usually, the space hummed with the relaxed, ambient murmur of wealthy civilians enjoying their alpine sanctuary.

This morning, the energy had violently shifted.

The automated evacuation emails had hit the civilian registry at 07:15. By 08:30, the front desk was a chaotic bottleneck of rolling luggage, ringing phones, and anxious conversations. Through the massive, frosted windowpanes of the Great Hall, the sky was a bruised, terrifying charcoal. The first heavy, wet flakes of the storm’s secondary pulse were beginning to smear against the glass.

Victoria Vance walked down the grand, sweeping staircase, her tailored navy blazer sharp and immaculate. She did not manage her mountain from the shadows when the pressure spiked. If she was going to order the evacuation of eighty-two civilians, she was going to stand directly in the line of fire.

She stepped smoothly behind the polished mahogany of the front desk. The civilian concierge staff, a team of college-aged seasonal workers from the lowlands, looked visibly relieved as the Matriarch took her position at the center terminal.

Victoria didn't offer a pep talk. She simply pulled up the master folio registry on the screen and looked up at the line.

"Next," she called out, her voice cutting cleanly through the nervous chatter of the lobby.

A middle-aged couple from Seattle stepped up, glancing nervously at the windows.

"We got the email," the man said, sliding his keycards across the marble counter. "We're actually grateful. We heard on the radio that the passes were getting slick, and my wife didn't want to risk the drive tomorrow anyway. We'll take the refund."

"A wise decision," Victoria said smoothly. Her fingers flew across the keyboard, processing the complete reversal of their charges in seconds. "Your folio is cleared. Have a safe drive down the mountain. The lower elevations are still seeing rain, but I advise leaving within the hour."

They nodded their thanks and hurried toward the valet.

Victoria processed the next four parties with identical, clinical efficiency. Most of the civilians possessed basic survival instincts. They took one look at the hostile, darkening sky, accepted the massive financial concession of a full refund, and happily fled back to the safety of the Seattle grid.

But not everyone respected the atmospheric physics.

A tall man in a cashmere sweater and a luxury ski jacket stepped up to Victoria’s terminal. His face was flushed with the specific, arrogant indignation of a man who believed his wealth could purchase the weather.

"This is completely unacceptable," the man demanded, slapping his hand flat on the marble counter. "My wife and I booked the Grand Larch suite through Wednesday the 15th. We drove all the way up from Portland. I am not cutting my vacation short just because of a little early snow. I drive an all-wheel-drive Apex Mountaineer. We are staying until Wednesday."

The young concierge clerk standing next to Victoria physically flinched.

Victoria did not flinch. She slowly took her hands off the keyboard, folded them neatly on the desk, and looked the man directly in the eyes. Her expression was forged from cold, impenetrable iron.

"I appreciate your confidence in your vehicle, sir," Victoria said, her tone perfectly polite but carrying the terrifying, absolute weight of a sovereign ruler. "However, the issue is not your traction tires. The issue is the millions of tons of 'Cascades Concrete' currently loading into the Liberty Bell avalanche chutes directly above State Route 20."

The man opened his mouth to argue, but Victoria simply spoke over him, her voice dropping into a deadly, uncompromising register.

"The Washington State Department of Transportation is officially projecting a historic loading event," Victoria continued. "If you attempt to leave on Wednesday the 15th, you will not be driving through a little early snow. You will be parked behind a forty-foot-tall wall of hyper-compacted ice, shattered timber, and granite boulders. And because this facility will be under lockdown protocols for the Centennial Gala, you will not be permitted back inside."

The man stared at her, the sheer, unapologetic brutality of the data finally piercing his ego.

"The hard cutoff for all civilian departures is Tuesday, October 14th, at 11:00 AM," Victoria stated, tapping her manicured finger against the marble to punctuate the timestamp. "There are absolutely no exceptions. You can take a one-hundred-percent refund right now and drive home safely tomorrow morning, or you can refuse, and my perimeter security team will physically escort you to your vehicle on Tuesday at 11:01 AM."

She held his gaze, waiting for the reality to set in. The mountain did not negotiate.

The man swallowed hard, the flush draining from his face as he looked out the window at the heavy, wet snow piling onto the asphalt. His Apex Mountaineer suddenly looked very small.

"We'll... we'll take the refund," he muttered, his posture collapsing. "We will check out on Monday morning."

"An excellent choice," Victoria smiled, the titanium armor instantly shifting back to flawless hospitality. She processed the folio and handed him a printed receipt. "Drive safely, sir."

He walked away, completely neutralized.

Victoria turned to the young concierge clerk beside her, who was staring at the Matriarch in absolute awe.

"When they push back," Victoria instructed the clerk quietly, her eyes already scanning the rest of the line, "you do not apologize for the weather. You give them the data, and you give them the deadline. Tuesday at 11:00 AM. We hold the line."

She turned back to her terminal, her hands hovering over the keys.

"Next," the Matriarch commanded.

The evacuation of Ruby Mountain continued.
