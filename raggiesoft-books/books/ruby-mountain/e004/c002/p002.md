## The 1903 Illusion

Amanda pushed Johnny’s wheelchair through the grand mahogany double doors, transitioning from the wide corridors into the absolute crown jewel of The Prospector's Keep: The Grand Ballroom.

If the Underworld was the thrumming, iron heart of the mountain, the Ballroom was its soul.

It was a cavernous, sprawling expanse of polished herringbone oak floors and fifty-foot ceilings. Suspended from the massive timber rafters were six original 1903 crystal chandeliers, casting a warm, fractured golden light across the room. The walls were draped in heavy crimson and gold velvet, and massive stone fireplaces anchored opposite ends of the hall.

It smelled of beeswax, fresh pine garlands, and expensive floor wax.

Currently, the room was a hive of frantic, high-stress activity. Dozens of outside caterers and decorators—hired from Seattle specifically for the Centennial—were rushing around, setting up the 400 chairs, polishing the brass flatware, and laying out the miles of heavy white tablecloths.

Standing in the center of the dance floor, her hands on her hips, was twenty-year-old Sarah Foster.

She was still wearing her sharp, black "Gala prep" uniform, but she looked incredibly annoyed. She was currently standing toe-to-toe with the lead event designer, a frantic man named Preston who looked like he had drank four espressos and was severely regretting taking a contract at a remote mountain fortress.

"I am telling you," Preston pleaded, gesturing wildly to a rolling cart holding three massive, modern, LED-lit ice sculptures of swans. "We need these on the head tables! It provides verticality! It provides modern elegance!"

"And I am telling *you*," Sarah replied, her voice eerily calm—a trait she had absolutely inherited from Victoria. "That this is a 1903 Centennial. Not a 1990s cruise ship. We don't do neon swans."

"But the lighting—"

"Will be handled by the chandeliers and the brass lanterns," Sarah interrupted smoothly.

Amanda pushed Johnny right up to the edge of the argument.

Sarah glanced over, saw Johnny, and let out a long sigh of relief. The icy, authoritative demeanor dropped instantly, replaced by a warm, exhausted smile. "Thank God. The cavalry."

Preston spun around, looking at Johnny’s wheelchair, the Quantum XN, and Amanda's unamused expression. "Look, we just need to finalize the centerpieces—"

"Preston," Sarah cut him off, pointing a thumb at Johnny. "This is Johnny Vance. He is the Matriarch's second-in-command. Whatever he says, goes."

Preston swallowed hard. He looked at the teenager in the wheelchair, suddenly realizing he was speaking to a direct heir of the mountain.

Johnny looked at the massive, glowing LED ice swans. They were hideous. They clashed violently with the heavy oak, the velvet, and the deeply historical weight of the Keep. He looked at Sarah, who was rolling her eyes, and then looked back at Preston.

He slowly brought his hand down to the keyboard.

*"Melt them,"* the synthetic voice announced, echoing flatly in the massive room.

Sarah let out a sharp bark of laughter, clapping a hand over her mouth to muffle the sound.

Preston's shoulders completely deflated. "Right. Okay. No swans. Brass lanterns it is. I'll... I'll just go tell the team to put these in the walk-in." He grabbed the rolling cart and scurried away toward the service doors.

"You are a lifesaver," Sarah sighed, walking over and resting her forearms on the back of Johnny's wheelchair next to Amanda. "If I had to look at those plastic LED monstrosities all night, I was going to throw them into the fireplace."

Johnny smiled, looking out over the massive room.

"It looks incredible, Sarah," Amanda said softly, taking in the sheer scale of the historical recreation. "Victoria is going to be thrilled."

"It's going to be perfect," Sarah agreed, her voice dropping to a quieter, more protective register. She leaned down slightly, resting her chin near Johnny's shoulder, echoing Amanda's exact posture from that morning. "And it's completely locked down, Johnny. Emma has the exterior, Morgan has the grid, Victoria has the VIPs, and I have the floor. Nobody gets in here tonight unless they are vetted, cleared, and on the list. You just get to enjoy the party."

Johnny let out a long, slow breath. The anxiety that usually accompanied massive crowds wasn't there. For the first time in months, he wasn't trapped. He was standing in the center of an impenetrable fortress, surrounded by an army of women who loved him.

Tonight was going to be magnificent.

## The Golden Hour

By five o'clock in the evening, the flawless blue sky over the North Cascades had been completely swallowed by a bruised, heavy expanse of purple clouds. The temperature plummeted into the twenties, and the first distinct, swirling flurries of snow began to fall across the cobblestone portico of The Prospector's Keep.

It was the visual warning shot. Down in the valleys, WSDOT had just issued their final bulletin for the weekend: *SR-20 to be barricaded at Milepost 134 Saturday night due to catastrophic avalanche danger.* The deadline was absolute. But inside the fortress, the panic of the deadline was replaced by the electric, humming tension of a flawlessly executed plan.

Up in the second-floor sanctuary, the "Gala prep" uniforms had been discarded. The room smelled of expensive hairspray, polished leather, and the faint, comforting scent of the eucalyptus soap they had used that morning.

Ashleigh was kneeling in front of Johnny’s wheelchair, her hands moving deftly as she tied a dark crimson silk tie around his collar.

"How's the collar, kid?" Ashleigh asked softly, adjusting the knot. "Too tight?"

Johnny shook his head once. He was wearing a custom-tailored charcoal suit. Victoria hadn't just bought it off the rack; she had commissioned it entirely from sensory-friendly, ultra-soft brushed cotton and silk linings, ensuring there were no stiff tags or scratchy wool seams to trigger a sensory overload during the long night.

"He looks like a Kennedy," Sarah announced, stepping out of the bathroom.

Sarah had traded her utilitarian prep uniform for a stunning, structured black velvet evening gown that looked like armor. Her blonde hair was swept up into a severe, elegant twist. Behind her, Amanda emerged wearing a deep emerald green dress that caught the ambient light of the suite, her expression entirely relaxed as she clipped a silver watch to her wrist.

The radio sitting on the kitchenette counter suddenly crackled to life.

*"Command, this is Emma on Perimeter,"* the seventeen-year-old’s voice buzzed through the static. *"Lower Motorpool confirms the first wave of Ridgeback 4x4s is loaded and past the lower gates. They are making the ascent. ETA to the summit portico is twenty minutes. The snow is starting to stick to the asphalt."*

Sarah walked over and pressed the talk button. "Copy that, Emma. Are the plows on standby?"

*"We have two rotaries staged at the Motorpool,"* Emma replied confidently. *"They'll run sweeps behind the shuttles all night if they have to. Prospector Road stays open until 10:00 AM tomorrow. After that, we evacuate."*

"Understood. We're heading down to the floor," Sarah said, clicking the radio off. She turned to the room, her eyes sweeping over her sister, her cousin, and finally landing on Johnny.

The Core Four held a moment of absolute, silent solidarity. Last night, this room had been the site of a devastating revelation. Tonight, it was the staging ground for their absolute triumph.

"Alright, pack," Sarah breathed out, a rare, genuine smile cracking her severe features. "Let's go show these people whose mountain they're standing on."

Amanda clicked off Johnny’s wheel brakes and pushed him out into the timber-framed hallway.

As they rolled onto the sweeping switchback ramp, Johnny realized that the Keep had completely transformed. The "Golden Hour" had arrived. The harsh daylight was gone, replaced entirely by the fractured, mesmerizing glow of the six massive crystal chandeliers and the roaring, crackling heat of the walk-in fireplaces. The velvet banners soaked up the light, turning the entire Great Hall into a cavern of crimson and gold.

The jazz orchestra was already on the floor, playing a low, sultry instrumental warm-up.

Standing at the absolute center of the room, waiting for them, was Victoria.

The Matriarch had traded her tailored business suit for an immaculate, floor-length, 1903-inspired evening gown in deep sapphire blue. With her dark hair pinned back and a diamond necklace catching the firelight, she looked less like a corporate lawyer and more like a ruling monarch.

She watched her cousins descend the ramp, her eyes tracking the precise, unified formation of Amanda, Sarah, and Ashleigh flanking Johnny’s chair.

"Flawless," Victoria murmured as they reached the polished oak of the Ballroom floor. She stepped forward, resting a hand gently on Johnny’s shoulder. "WSDOT just confirmed the closure. 7:00 PM Saturday. The trap is set, the timeline is locked, and Vanessa Hayes is currently fighting for her life in a San Juan County interrogation room. The fortress is secure."

Johnny looked up at his older cousin. He raised his left hand, offering her a crisp, deliberate thumbs-up.

Victoria's smile widened. "I agree."

Before anyone could say another word, the heavy, muffled crunch of specialized snow tires on cobblestone echoed through the thick exterior walls.

The first shuttles had arrived.

"Positions," Victoria ordered, her voice dropping an octave into pure command.

Sarah and Ashleigh immediately broke off, moving to flank the entrance of the Grand Ballroom to manage the guest flow. Amanda pushed Johnny to his designated station near the primary fireplace—a position that kept him warm, gave him a perfect tactical view of the entire room, and ensured he was heavily guarded by the architecture.

Victoria walked to the grand mahogany double doors at the front of the Great Hall.

She gave a sharp nod to the two valets standing by the handles. They gripped the brass rings and pulled the massive doors inward.

A blast of freezing, pine-scented air and swirling white snow blew into the opulent lobby, instantly clashing with the heat of the fires. Through the open doors, a fleet of heavy, black Ridgeback 4x4 vans idled in the turnaround, their headlights cutting through the falling snow.

The guests—billionaires, politicians, and historical society elites—began to step out of the shuttles, shivering in their tuxedos and gowns, their eyes widening in absolute awe as they looked up at the roaring fireplaces and the velvet banners.

Victoria Vance stepped forward into the cold air, completely unfazed by the snow landing on her sapphire gown.

"Welcome to The Prospector's Keep," Victoria announced, her voice ringing out into the storm. "Let us celebrate a century."
