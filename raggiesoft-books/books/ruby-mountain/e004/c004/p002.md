## The Handoff

At 12:20 PM, Maya’s massive orange rotary plow broke through the final snowdrift at the base of Prospector Road, its spinning steel augers chewing through the ice and spitting it into the tree line.

Behind her, the twelve civilian sedans of the ADA escort crawled out of the blinding whiteout and onto the heavily salted, relatively clear pavement of State Route 20. Morgan’s tow rig brought up the rear, sealing the convoy.

Waiting for them at the highway junction were three Washington State Patrol cruisers, their lightbars cutting frantic, strobing flashes of red and blue through the blizzard.

Morgan grabbed her radio. "Perimeter, this is Engineering. The ADA package is delivered. WSP has them."

Up in the Owner's Quarters at the Keep, Victoria immediately picked up her satellite phone and hit the WSDOT Regional Director's direct dial. He answered on the first ring.

"The Heavy Escort is clear of the private road," Victoria reported flatly. "Your troopers have them on the asphalt."

*"I see them on the dispatch board,"* the Director replied, his voice clipped and tight with stress. *"Victoria, my avalanche forecasters at Sand Point are losing their minds. PK-7015 is showing a twenty percent water equivalent. Liberty Bell is groaning. WSP is pushing your ADA guests west at top speed, but I make no promises past 12:45. The road needs to close. Where is your final wave?"*

"Pulling into the Lower Motorpool right now," Victoria said. "We will not be the reason your troopers get buried, Director."

She hung up, her eyes flicking to Johnny’s secondary monitor.

It was **12:23 PM**. Thirty-seven minutes until the steel dropped.

Five miles down the mountain, the Lower Motorpool was a scene of absolute, diesel-choked madness. The facility was a massive, subterranean concrete bunker built into the side of the mountain, designed to keep the guests' luxury cars safely out of the alpine elements.

Right now, it looked like a military evacuation zone.

The final four Ridgeback 4x4 shuttles roared down the concrete ramp, their chained tires sparking against the floor. They slammed to a halt, the side doors sliding open before the vehicles had even completely stopped.

"Out! Everybody out!" Emma Bennett screamed over the echoing roar of engines. She was standing on the hood of a parked maintenance truck, using her megaphone to project over the chaos. "Find your vehicle! Keys are in the ignitions! Do not pack your trunks, throw your bags in the back seat, and get in line!"

A pair of WSP Troopers, dressed in heavy winter gear and high-visibility vests, had driven their cruisers directly into the subterranean lot to take command of the civilian exodus. They weren't acting like polite traffic cops; they were acting like drill sergeants.

"Move it, people! Let's go, let's go!" a State Trooper barked, slamming his gloved hand against the hood of a sleek silver *Horizon Avalon* as the terrified driver fumbled with his door handle. "You have fifteen miles to drive to get past the barricades! Put it in drive and do not stop for anything!"

Suddenly, a wealthy tech executive in a tailored cashmere overcoat stopped dead at the bumper of his idling sedan and spun around. "Wait! My leather weekender is still in the shuttle!"

He actually turned back, jogging directly into the traffic lane toward the idling Ridgeback van, completely ignoring the screaming engines and the exhaust fumes.

"Leave it!" Emma yelled through the megaphone, pointing a gloved finger directly at him. "Get in the car! WSDOT is dropping the gates!"

The executive ignored her. He reached the van, yanking the heavy rear door open and lunging inside to grab his designer bag.

He didn't make it back to his car on his own.

A WSP Trooper, his high-visibility vest plastered with freezing slush, materialized behind the executive. The Trooper grabbed the man by the collar of his expensive coat, physically hauling him backward, bag and all.

"Are you out of your mind?!" the Trooper roared, his voice echoing off the concrete as he violently shoved the executive toward the open door of his sedan. "Your bag isn't going to survive thirty feet of snow, sir! The mountain is coming down! You get in that vehicle and drive, or I will arrest you, leave you in my cruiser, and we can both get buried! Move!"

The sheer, physical aggression of the state police officer finally broke through the elite entitlement. The realization that his wealth could not stop an avalanche hit the executive like a physical blow. He threw the bag into the passenger seat and slammed the door.

Engines roared to life across the entire cavern. Headlights snapped on, blinding in the dim light.

The WSP Troopers stood at the exit ramp, waving the cars out onto the highway with frantic, sweeping motions of their glowing traffic wands. They weren't checking licenses; they were just shoving metal off the mountain.

*Screeech.*

The last civilian vehicle—the tech executive's sedan—burned rubber on the concrete floor, fishtailing slightly before rocketing up the exit ramp and merging onto State Route 20, chasing the taillights of the convoy ahead of it.

The WSP Troopers immediately jumped into their cruisers. The lead trooper rolled his window down, looking up at Emma on the maintenance truck. He pointed a gloved finger at her.

"Lock your bunker, kid!" the Trooper shouted over the howling wind outside. "We are clearing the canyon!"

The cruisers threw their sirens on, the wailing sound quickly fading into the whiteout as they tore off down the highway, acting as the rear guard for the massive civilian convoy.

Emma jumped down from the hood of the truck. The Lower Motorpool was suddenly, overwhelmingly empty. The air was thick with the smell of unburned fuel and burnt rubber, but the frantic screaming was gone.

She keyed her radio, her chest heaving as the adrenaline slowly began to recede.

"Command, this is Perimeter," Emma said, her voice shaking slightly with sheer relief. "The Lower Motorpool is empty. Troopers are clear. Initiating lockdown."

Emma walked over to the heavy industrial breaker panel on the wall. She grabbed the massive red lever that controlled the primary blast doors of the motorpool and pulled it down.

With a deep, grinding groan of hydraulic pressure, the two-foot-thick steel and concrete reinforced doors began to slide shut, sealing the subterranean garage off from the highway.

*CLANG.*

The locking bolts fired into place.

Emma looked at her watch. It was **12:37 PM**.

Up in the sanctuary, Johnny closed his eyes. The green dots on his map were entirely gone, replaced by the solid, unbroken perimeter line of The Prospector's Keep.

Amanda leaned her head against his shoulder, letting out a long, shuddering sigh.

"She did it," Amanda whispered.

Victoria stood by the window, looking out into the blinding white storm. The Matriarch's posture finally relaxed, the iron rigidity leaving her shoulders. She placed her hand against the freezing reinforced glass.

The mountain was theirs.

## The Steel Drops

The drive back up Prospector Road was the most dangerous five miles of Emma Bennett’s life.

With the Lower Motorpool blast doors sealed and the civilian convoy racing west behind the Washington State Patrol, Emma was the absolute last person outside the fortress. She threw her heavy 4x4 maintenance truck into low gear and began the brutal, blinding ascent.

The "Cascades Concrete" was falling at over three inches an hour now. The trenches that Maya’s rotary plow had cut just an hour earlier were already filling with heavy, wet drifts. Emma gripped the steering wheel, her knuckles white, feeling the heavy chained tires slipping and biting into the ice as she navigated the blind switchbacks.

The wind screamed against the reinforced glass of the cab, rocking the heavy truck on its suspension. But she didn't stop. She pushed the roaring diesel engine to its absolute limit until the towering, dark stone silhouette of The Prospector’s Keep finally emerged from the whiteout.

Emma pulled the truck under the heavy timber portico, threw it into park, and killed the engine.

She stepped out into the freezing hurricane, grabbed the heavy iron handle of the Keep's front oak doors, and hauled them open. She slipped inside, throwing her entire body weight against the wood to force the doors shut against the wind.

*Click. Clack.* She threw the massive deadbolts, locking the fortress from the inside.

The acoustic shift was instant and staggering. The violent, deafening roar of the blizzard was immediately muted by the two-foot-thick stone walls of the 1903 architecture, reduced to a low, heavy thrumming that vibrated in the floorboards.

Emma stood in the vestibule, breathing heavily, shaking the snow off her black peacoat.

She walked into the Great Hall.

The four hundred VIP guests were gone. The rolling suitcases, the frantic politicians, the spilled coffee—all of it had been swept away. The massive room had returned to its intended state: a quiet, impenetrable sanctuary. A roaring fire was burning in the ten-foot hearth, casting a warm, flickering orange light across the herringbone oak floors.

Gathered around the fire was the pack.

Morgan, Megan, Clara, and Maya were shedding their snow-caked canvas coveralls. Diana was handing out heavy ceramic mugs of hot cider to the younger cousins. Sarah and Ashleigh were sitting on the leather Chesterfield sofas, completely exhausted but smiling.

Standing at the center of the room, flanked by Amanda and Johnny’s wheelchair, was Victoria Vance.

The Matriarch wasn't holding a clipboard. She didn't need one. Her eyes swept across the room in a practiced, calculating arc, doing the only inventory that actually mattered to her. She counted the heavy trades crew. She counted the logistics team. She counted the younger cousins. She looked down at Johnny.

Seventeen.

The bloodline was whole.

Victoria walked over to a heavy mahogany side table and picked up her encrypted satellite phone. She looked at the grandfather clock ticking against the far wall.

It was **12:42 PM**.

She hit the WSDOT Regional Director’s direct dial. The static-laced connection went through almost immediately.

"Director," Victoria stated, her voice carrying clearly across the quiet Great Hall. Every cousin in the room stopped talking and turned to listen. "My perimeter engineer is back inside the Keep. The Lower Motorpool is sealed. The ADA convoy is on the asphalt, and WSP is escorting the last of the civilian vehicles to the gate."

*"I have dispatch confirming visual on the State Patrol rear guard right now,"* the Director replied. The sheer, exhausting relief in his voice was palpable even through the satellite distortion. *"They are passing Milepost 134. Victoria, your mountain is entirely clear of civilians."*

"The Keep is sterile," Victoria confirmed, a fiercely satisfied smile touching the corners of her mouth. "Only Vance blood remains."

*"Understood. We are issuing the final closure order. The steel is dropping now."*

Fifteen miles away, at the base of the canyon, WSDOT crews unlatched the massive, heavy-duty steel swing gates. With the blinding snow swirling around them, they pushed the barricades across both lanes of State Route 20, driving the heavy locking pins deep into the asphalt. They hung the reflective red signs that would not be moved for half a year: **ROAD CLOSED. AVALANCHE DANGER.**

*"The highway is locked, Victoria,"* the Director breathed. *"Stay warm up there. See you in the spring."*

"See you in the spring, Director."

Victoria ended the call. She set the heavy phone down on the mahogany table.

She turned to face her family. Seventeen apex predators, completely cut off from the eastern medical grid, local law enforcement, and the rest of the world. Outside, millions of tons of snow were preparing to bury their private road under an impassable wall of ice.

Victoria looked down at Johnny. His hands were resting on his Quantum XN keyboard, his shoulders relaxed, completely devoid of the tension he had carried in Friday Harbor.

"Well," Victoria announced, her voice echoing warmly in the cavernous hall. "The guests have checked out. Morgan, lock down the grid. Diana, figure out what we're having for dinner."

Johnny smiled. The siege had officially begun, and he had never felt safer in his entire life.
