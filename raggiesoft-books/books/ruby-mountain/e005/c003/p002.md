## The Thaw

The massive, swinging wooden doors of the kitchen didn't just open; they were pushed hard by three heavily insulated, exhausted bodies.

Morgan, Clara, and Maya stumbled into the warm, bright, bacon-scented air of the commercial kitchen. The ambient seventy-degree heat hit them like a physical wall, immediately causing the thick layer of Cascade Concrete packed onto their canvas coveralls to begin melting into dirty puddles on the tile floor.

The heavy operations team looked like they had just survived a war. Their faces were flushed a violent, wind-burned red, their safety goggles were caked in ice, and Clara was visibly shivering, her teeth clicking together in a rapid, involuntary rhythm.

"Vents are clear," Morgan rasped, her voice completely stripped of its usual commanding volume. She reached up with stiff, shaking fingers, struggling to unclip the frozen carabiner on her harness. "The boiler is breathing. We bought ourselves at least twenty-four hours before it builds back up."

"Get that gear off," Diana ordered immediately, abandoning her whiteboard and stepping into the role of den mother. "Drop the coveralls right there, I don't care about the floor. Get to the staff table."

Sarah and Ashleigh immediately stepped away from Johnny's chair, moving to help Clara and Maya peel out of the heavy, soaking wet thermal layers. Freed from the freezing canvas, the three heavy ops mechanics collapsed into the wooden chairs around the scarred oak staff table. They were wearing only their base layers, exhausted, their internal core temperatures dangerously low.

"Line, listen up!" Diana called out, clapping her hands sharply. The shift in her tone was electric. "We have frozen operators on the deck. We are firing the breakfast. Execute!"

The younger cousins didn't panic. They had just spent twenty minutes running the theoretical drills, and now it was time for the practical application.

Twelve-year-old Lily moved first. She grabbed the heavy insulated mitts and pulled three heavy ceramic plates from the 140-degree warming drawer. She set them precisely on the stainless-steel pass, the heat radiating visibly off the porcelain.

Fifteen-year-old Chloe stepped up with a massive stainless-steel skillet. The scrambled eggs inside were cooked to a flawless, soft-curd 155 degrees—safe, but not rubbery. Using a wide spatula, she portioned the eggs onto the center of each hot plate, using the back of the utensil to create a tight, visually appealing mound rather than a sloppy spread.

Fourteen-year-old Zoe followed right behind her with the resting racks of thick-cut, hearth-baked bacon.

"Height builds appeal," Zoe muttered to herself, echoing one of Diana’s previous lessons. Instead of laying the bacon flat beside the eggs, she leaned three thick slices against the mound of eggs like a structural teepee, creating a visually striking, three-dimensional profile.

Lily stepped in for the final touch. She grabbed a small pair of culinary tweezers and precisely dropped a pinch of finely minced, bright green chives over the pale yellow eggs, creating an instant, vibrant color contrast. Finally, she took a clean linen towel and ran it quickly around the outer rim of all three plates, ensuring there were no smudges, grease marks, or stray crumbs.

It didn't look like a survival ration slapped together in a snowed-in bunker. It looked like a fifty-dollar breakfast service at a high-end Seattle bistro.

"Service!" Chloe called out, her voice cracking slightly with pride.

"Run them," Diana smiled, stepping back to let them finish the job.

Chloe, Zoe, and Lily carefully picked up the heavy, radiating ceramic plates. They walked over to the staff table and set them down directly in front of Morgan, Clara, and Maya.

Clara immediately wrapped her freezing, shaking hands around the edges of the ceramic plate. The 140-degree heat transferred instantly from the porcelain into her skin. She let out a long, shuddering gasp, closing her eyes as the deep, comforting warmth flooded her nervous system.

"Oh, my god," Clara whispered, opening her eyes to look at the beautifully plated food. "You guys... this is incredible."

"The plates are heated to maintain the thermal envelope of the proteins," Lily explained proudly, standing at attention with her hands behind her back, exactly the way she had seen Diana do it.

Morgan let out a raspy laugh, picking up her fork. She took her first bite of the eggs and bacon. The rich, salty fat, the perfect texture, and the sheer, aggressive heat of the food hit her empty stomach like a furnace igniting.

"Chef," Morgan said, looking past the younger cousins to Diana, who was pouring three massive mugs of dark roast coffee. "Promote them. All of them. This is the best thing I have ever eaten in my entire life."

Zoe and Chloe grinned at each other, their shoulders dropping with relief. The pressure of the kitchen was intense, but the validation of feeding their frozen family members was worth every second of the stress.

Amanda leaned against the counter next to Johnny's wheelchair, watching Clara’s violent shivering slowly subside as the hot food worked its magic.

Johnny rested his left hand on his lap desk. He took a slow sip of the hot chocolate Lily had made for him, the rich, whole milk coating his throat. He looked at the heavy ops team, safe and thawing out. He looked at the younger cousins, beaming with pride. And he looked at Diana, the undisputed master of the Keep's internal morale.

He didn't type anything. He didn't need to. The blizzard outside was shrieking against the second-story glass, completely burying the first floor under thousands of tons of Cascade Concrete, but it didn't matter. The fortress was warm. The pack was fed. And the mountain was losing.

## The Deep Clean

By nine-thirty, the adrenaline of the morning had completely burned off, replaced by the sheer, imposing reality of the physical labor ahead of them.

Victoria ordered the heavy ops team to remain by the Great Hall fireplace to recover their core temperatures, and Diana locked herself in the kitchen to begin auditing the walk-in freezers for their strict six-month rationing protocol. That left the remaining able-bodied members of the pack to face the aftermath of Friday’s frantic evacuation.

Three hundred luxury suites had been abandoned in an absolute panic.

Seventeen-year-old Emma Bennett stood at the base of the grand staircase, holding a heavy clipboard. She was in her element. As the master logistician of the Bennett branch, organizing chaos was her specialty.

"Listen up," Emma addressed the cleaning crew, which consisted of Amanda, Sarah, Ashleigh, Grace, Mia, and the younger Vance sisters. "We are not doing this in one day. We have six months, so pace yourselves. We are officially transitioning the Keep from a commercial enterprise into a dormant fortress. We strip the beds, bag the trash, and lock the doors. We are starting with the third floor."

Amanda pushed Johnny’s wheelchair into the commercial elevator, following the Foster twins up to the third-floor guest wing.

When the elevator doors slid open, the hallway looked like the site of a minor disaster. The wealthy evacuees had left a trail of chaos: a stray cashmere scarf discarded on the carpet, an overturned room service cart, and half-empty glasses of flat vintage champagne sitting on the mahogany console tables.

Amanda pushed Johnny down the center of the wide corridor and locked his brakes directly across from the heavy brass doors of the industrial laundry chute.

"Alright, kid, you're our command center," Amanda said, pulling a heavy, coiled gray CAT5 Ethernet cable from a linen closet. She plugged one end into the wall port and handed the other to Johnny.

Johnny plugged the cable into the side of his Quantum XN laptop, instantly linking into the Keep’s localized 2003 server network. He pulled up the master room inventory database. His physical limitations meant he couldn't strip a king-sized mattress, but his mind was the architectural anchor of the pack. He was going to act as Dispatch, tracking exactly which rooms were clear, which required maintenance, and how many loads of laundry were stacking up in the basement.

"You good here?" Amanda asked, leaning down to wrap her arms around his shoulders in a tight, warm hug.

Johnny nodded, leaning into the embrace and giving her a small thumbs-up with his stiff left hand. He wasn't alone. Amanda, Sarah, and Ashleigh propped open the doors to Rooms 301, 302, and 303, creating a triangle of activity perfectly centered around his wheelchair. They were never more than fifteen feet away, their voices carrying easily into the hallway.

"Oh, you have got to be kidding me," Sarah's voice echoed in disgust from Room 302—the suite formerly occupied by Representative Elias Thorne.

Amanda poked her head out of 301, holding a bundle of white sheets. "What did he leave?"

Sarah walked out into the hallway, holding up a heavy, crystal ice bucket. Floating in the melted water was a half-eaten breakfast burrito and a soggy, incredibly expensive silk necktie.

"He tried to flush a cloth napkin down the toilet and threw his tie in the ice bucket," Sarah sighed, dumping the bucket into the rolling housekeeping cart. She walked over to Johnny, tossing her arms around his neck for a quick, affectionate squeeze before grabbing a fresh trash bag. "I'm formally requesting that we revoke his reservation privileges for the 2004 season."

Johnny’s fingers moved across his keyboard.

*"Room 302 logged. Maintenance required. Plumbing obstruction,"* the text-to-speech engine chimed flatly down the corridor. *"And Thorne is permanently blacklisted in the system."*

Ashleigh laughed from the doorway of 303, tossing a massive bundle of heavy down comforters into the hallway. "Good call, boss. Send it down."

Amanda picked up the bundle of bedding, hauled open the heavy brass door of the laundry chute, and shoved the linens inside. A deep, satisfying *whoosh* echoed up the shaft as the fabric plummeted three stories down into the subterranean laundry facility.

"Load one away," Amanda called out.

Johnny tapped his keyboard, updating the basement queue.

They fell into a steady, comfortable rhythm. There was no pressure to turn the rooms over for new guests. There were no managers checking their watches. It was just the pack, reclaiming their territory room by room. Grace and Mia swept through behind the older girls, bagging up trash and unplugging the mini-fridges to conserve the mountain's diesel power grid.

The hallway became a revolving door of pack affection. The physical tactile grounding was constant. Every ten minutes, Amanda would walk out of a room, drop a load of laundry down the chute, and check in with Johnny. She would offer him a sip of water, adjust the heavy fleece blanket over his legs to ensure the drafty hallway wasn't chilling him, and lean down to press her cheek against his temple in a long, lingering hug. When Grace walked by with an armful of empty champagne bottles, she would pause to rest her head on his shoulder for a few seconds. When Ashleigh moved the cart, she ruffled his hair.

For Johnny, it was absolute sensory perfection. The steady flow of deep-pressure hugs kept his nervous system entirely regulated, wrapping him in an unbreakable web of familial safety while he managed the digital logistics of the fortress.

By 11:30 AM, the entire east wing of the third floor was completely stripped, sterilized, and locked down. The heavy oak doors were shut, plunging that section of the hotel into a deep, dormant hibernation that wouldn't be disturbed until the spring thaw.

Johnny looked at the spreadsheet glowing on his Quantum monitor. They had cleared forty rooms. The mountain was howling outside the hallway windows, but inside, the Keep was slowly becoming cleaner, smaller, and infinitely more theirs.

*"East Wing Secure,"* Johnny broadcasted through his speakers. *"Transitioning to West Wing."*

"Heard that, Dispatch," Amanda smiled, walking out of the final room and dusting her hands off on her Iron-Weave Apparel pants. She walked up behind his chair, wrapping her arms around his chest from behind in one final, heavy squeeze before unlocking the brakes. "Let's roll. We've got a lot more beds to strip."

## The West Wing

Amanda navigated the manual wheelchair across the central landing, the rubber tires moving silently over the thick Persian runner. They left the secured, darkened East Wing behind and pushed through the heavy double doors into the West Wing of the third floor.

"Alright, new staging ground," Amanda announced, parking Johnny directly beside the brass laundry chute door for this corridor. She uncoiled the heavy gray CAT5 cable, plugged it into the wall jack, and handed the connector to Johnny.

Johnny snapped the cable into his Quantum XN laptop. The localized network pinged, and his master inventory spreadsheet instantly refreshed. He tapped a command, turning the grid for the West Wing from red (Occupied) to yellow (In Progress).

"Pace yourselves, team," Emma’s voice drifted up from the grand staircase, where she was auditing the second-floor logistics. "We are not burning out on day one. Finish the third floor and we officially call it a day for the housekeeping detail. The fourth floor can wait until next week."

"Music to my ears," Ashleigh groaned, stretching her arms over her head. "I've stripped so many duvet covers I feel like I'm drowning in down feathers."

"Stop complaining and grab a trash bag," Sarah laughed, throwing a roll of heavy black plastic at her sister. "Room 340. Let's go."

The pack settled back into their methodical, unhurried rhythm. Without the pressure of the WSDOT deadline or the impending avalanche, the work felt entirely different. It wasn't a frantic evacuation; it was the careful, deliberate winterization of their home.

Grace and Mia moved methodically from room to room, executing their deep-cleaning protocols. They unplugged the heavy mini-fridges, propped the small doors open to prevent mildew, and pulled the heavy blackout curtains shut to insulate the glass against the howling blizzard outside.

Every time a cart rolled past the command center, the physical grounding continued.

Ashleigh emerged from Room 342 holding a stack of abandoned glossy magazines. She dropped them on the housekeeping cart, walked over to Johnny, and leaned down to wrap her arms around his shoulders, burying her face in the crook of his neck for a long, exaggerated sigh.

"Rich people read the most boring articles," Ashleigh muttered against his thermal shirt. She gave him a tight squeeze, pressed a kiss to his temple, and pulled back. "You doing okay, boss? Need me to grab you another hot chocolate from the kitchen?"

Johnny shook his head, a soft smile on his face. He lifted his stiff left hand and patted her forearm.

*"I am optimal. Keep stripping beds, Foster,"* the text-to-speech engine commanded dryly.

Ashleigh saluted him with a grin and marched back into the suite.

Ten minutes later, Amanda walked out of Room 345 carrying a mountain of tangled white sheets. She hauled open the brass door, shoved the linens down the chute, and turned to Johnny. She didn't say a word. She just knelt beside his chair, adjusted the heavy fleece blanket covering his legs to completely block out the hallway draft, and wrapped her arms tightly around his waist.

Johnny let out a long, slow exhale, leaning his weight into his twin sister's embrace. The deep pressure instantly neutralized any lingering sensory static from the chaotic morning. The storm outside was screaming, violently throwing ice against the third-story glass at the end of the hall, but the violent weather only made the warmth of Amanda's hug feel more absolute.

"Almost done, kid," Amanda murmured against his chest. She squeezed him one last time, stood up, and brushed a stray lock of hair out of his eyes. "Five rooms left."

By 1:15 PM, the heavy rolling carts were overflowing with trash, and the laundry chute had swallowed the last of the West Wing's linens.

Sarah walked out of the final suite at the end of the corridor. She reached up, grabbed the heavy oak doors, and pulled them shut with a heavy, echoing *THUD*. She pulled the master keycard from her pocket, swiped the lock, and engaged the deadbolt.

The small light on the electronic lock flashed from green to a solid, dormant red.

Johnny hit the final keystroke on his Quantum XN. The entire third-floor grid on his monitor shifted from yellow to a sterile, uniform gray.

*"Third floor is completely sterilized and locked down,"* Johnny's speakers announced. *"Eighty-two rooms offline."*

"And with that, the housekeeping shift is officially over," Amanda declared, wiping her brow. She unhooked the CAT5 cable from Johnny’s laptop and began coiling it up. "We did good. Half the hotel is asleep."

Sarah leaned against the wall, looking down the long, dim, empty hallway. The frantic energy of the VIP guests was completely gone. The Keep felt massive, quiet, and deeply secure.

"So," Ashleigh said, leaning against her sister and looking toward the elevator. "Diana said something about rationing, right? Do you think she rationed the leftover pastries from the Gala brunch, or do you think they're sitting in the kitchen waiting for us?"

Johnny’s fingers danced across his keyboard.

*"Diana explicitly locked the almond croissants in the walk-in,"* the computer chimed. *"But Maya knows the override code for the electronic padlock."*

Amanda burst out laughing, unlocking the brakes on his manual wheelchair. "You are an absolute menace, Johnny Vance. Let's go orchestrate a heist."

She spun the chair around, and the pack headed toward the elevator, leaving the third floor to sleep through the winter.
