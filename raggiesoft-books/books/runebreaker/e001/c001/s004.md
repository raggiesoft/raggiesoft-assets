Ocea woke to the sensation of cool, dewy blades of grass tickling the soles of her bare feet.

She gasped, pushing herself up on her palms and blinking against a sky that could never have belonged to Earth. Overhead, sweeping curtains of emerald and turquoise aurora light rippled across a velvet canopy of oversized, jewel-bright stars. Floating islands of mossy stone drifted lazily across the horizon, tethered to the earth below by cascading waterfalls that dissolved into glowing mist before they ever touched the ground.

She looked down at herself. She was still wearing her soft cotton t-shirt and her faded 1992 denim jeans, completely barefoot, exactly as she had been sitting on her living room carpet minutes—or lifetimes—ago. She had no steel armor, no broadsword, and no medieval cloak. Against the lush, painterly sixteen-bit grandeur of this fantasy realm, she looked hopelessly, stubbornly out of place.

"Tay..." she whispered, her voice trembling in the vast quiet of the meadow.

Panic surged in her throat as the memory of the rift crashed over her—the towering sorcerer in purple runes, the fabricated illusion of her own death, and the sound of her twin brother screaming her name as he was dragged toward the dark fortress.

Ocea shoved her hand into the front pocket of her jeans. Her fingers closed around the fire-engine red half of their summer-camp clay medallion. When she pulled it out into the starlight, she gasped. The cheap acrylic paint she had brushed onto the clay years ago was pulsing with a steady, living heartbeat of warm golden light.

When she held the jagged ceramic edge toward the north—where a monolithic spire of black obsidian and violet lightning pierced the distant clouds—the golden pulse beat faster, tugging gently at her palm like a compass needle pointing toward its missing half.

He was alive. He was inside that fortress, and the monster who ruled it had convinced him that she was dead.

Ocea stood up on the luminous grass, clenching the warm red clay shard tightly in her fist. She didn't care that she didn't have armor or shoes, and she didn't care what kind of dark magic governed this world. Tay had spent eighteen years being her safe harbor. Now it was her turn to cross the map and bring him home.

In the valley just below the ridge, the warm amber lanterns of a small, timber-framed settlement flickered between towering spires of protective blue quartz. Taking a deep breath of the crisp, mana-charged night air, Ocea stepped onto the footpath and began to walk.
