That evening, the innkeeper of the Sanctuary Inn refused to accept a single coin for the corner room under the eaves, insisting that anyone brave enough to walk toward the Obsidian Spire deserved the softest feather mattress in the valley.

Ocea sat curled up on the wide window seat, her bare feet warmed by a braided wool rug as she looked out past the village's glowing blue crystal barrier toward the dark northern horizon. Downstairs in the common room, a gentle, looping lullaby of acoustic guitar and soft wooden flute drifted up through the floorboards—the universal sound of a safe save point before the final dungeon.

On the small oak table beside her rested her canvas satchel. Tucked neatly inside was the coarse, scratchy wool tunic she had taken from the guardhouse clothesline, ready to be pulled over her head once she breached the Spire's outer perimeter so she could blend in among the Wizard's isolated servants. Beneath her sleeve, the lightweight *Runebreaker* gauntlet hummed with a barely perceptible golden warmth against her wrist.

Ocea rested her chin on her knees, holding the fire-engine red half of the clay medallion against her palm.

Back home in 1992, whenever a late-night thunderstorm rolled across town and rattled the glass of their bedroom windows, she and Tay would drag their blankets together and talk in the dark until the thunder passed. Right now, miles away inside a freezing fortress of black stone, Tay was facing the worst storm of his life completely alone—convinced that she was dead, and that obeying the monster who held him captive was all he had left.

"Hang on just one more night, Tay," Ocea whispered to the glowing red clay in her hand. "I'm coming in tomorrow. And I'm not leaving without you."

She blew out the brass oil lamp on the nightstand, climbed beneath the warm quilt in her t-shirt and jeans, and let the soothing melody of the Sanctuary Inn carry her into a deep, restorative sleep.
