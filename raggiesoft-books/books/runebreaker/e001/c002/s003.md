The interior of the Crystal Sage's observatory looked like a cross between an medieval alchemist's tower and an electronics repair shop. Towering brass astrolabes and rotating quartz prisms shared workbench space with spools of copper wire, glass vacuum tubes filled with dancing golden fireflies, and ancient scrying monitors humming in resonant sixteen-bit harmony.

At the center of the room stood the Crystal Sage himself, his long silver beard tucked into a leather tool belt as he peered through a jeweler's loupe at a fractured shard of sapphire.

"Don't tell me, don't tell me!" the Sage called out without turning around as Ocea's bare feet padded across the polished quartz floor. "By the lack of leather boot-heels and the unmistakable electrostatic signature of late-twentieth-century cotton-poly blend, you're the variable who fell through the CRT rift at twenty-one hundred hours last night!"

Ocea blinked, stopping beside the workbench. "You saw us fall through?"

The Sage spun around on his stool, pushing three different pairs of magnifying spectacles up onto his forehead. His eyes softened with immediate, grandfatherly sympathy when he saw the raw determination—and the sleepless worry—etched across her face.

"I saw two signatures enter the atmosphere of the Dream Kingdom on the Twenty-Eighth Day of the Last Frost, Year 404 of the Era of the Crystal," the Sage said gravely, gesturing toward a holographic projection of the Obsidian Spire hovering over his table. "A twin resonance—two halves of a single harmonic frequency. The Grand Wizard intercepted the stream. He has spent decades trying to write the *Grand Decree*, a master spell designed to format the entire directory of our world and erase every unpredictable emotion, memory, and free will."

Ocea's stomach dropped. "Why did he take Tay?"

"Because the Grand Decree requires an analog human heart to compile," the Sage explained, tapping the projection where a faint, freezing blue pulse throbbed deep within the Spire's subterranean dungeons. "Pure joy or love would reject the dark purple code instantly. So the Wizard fabricated a memory of your death. He is using your brother's grief—the unbearable weight of believing he failed to protect his twin sister—as an icy conduit to channel the dark runes."

Tears stung the corners of Ocea's eyes, hot and fierce. She reached into her jeans pocket and slammed the fire-engine red half of their clay summer-camp medallion onto the workbench.

"He's ten minutes older than me, and he's the best big brother in any world," Ocea said, her voice steady despite the tightness in her chest. "I don't care what kind of spell the Wizard put on him. Tell me how to break it."

The Crystal Sage stared down at the jagged piece of painted summer-camp clay. Slowly, he lowered his jeweler's loupe, his hand trembling as the quartz instruments all around the laboratory began to hum in harmonic resonance with the cheap red acrylic paint.

"Four hundred and four years..." the Sage whispered in utter astonishment. "Our entire age was named the *Era of the Crystal* after a prophecy of two sundered halves—one red as fire, one blue as the sea. Generations of miners dug through every mountain in the Realm looking for it, and when nothing was ever found, the Kingdom dismissed the Twin Crystal as a fairy tale. No wonder we never found it in our soil! It wasn't mined from rock—it was sculpted at a summer camp in another world by the two of you!"
