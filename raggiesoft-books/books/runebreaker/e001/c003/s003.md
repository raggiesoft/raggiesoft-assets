Inside the windowless stone box that served as his quarters, Tay sat on the edge of the narrow wooden cot, staring without blinking at the cracked glass screen of a gutted scrying terminal on the floor.

Beneath the heavy, drab grey wool of the apprentice tunic the Grand Wizard had forced him to wear, Tay's fingers were curled around the jagged, ocean-blue half of the clay summer-camp medallion hanging against his sternum. The ceramic edge bit into his palm, the only sensation capable of cutting through the freezing, suffocating numbness that had wrapped around his mind since the night the television screen swallowed them.

Every time he closed his eyes, the Grand Wizard'spurple runes replayed the same horrifying image inside his skull: Ocea's hand slipping from his fingers in the digital rift, her form shattering into lifeless red shards that vanished into the void.

*Ten minutes older,* a hollow, broken voice echoed in the back of his mind. *You were ten minutes older. You had one job—to hold on to her—and you let go.*

The Grand Wizard had told him that the only way to stop the unbearable ache in his chest was to embrace the dark purple code of the *Grand Decree*—to let the cold, unfeeling order of the Spire freeze his heart until nothing hurt anymore. Whenever Tay tried to fight the purple runes, the Wizard twisted the knife of his grief deeper, Flooding his nerves with icy static until Tay put on the mask of a cold, arrogant henchman just to survive the inspections.

Beside him on the wooden slats lay a cursed parchment scroll glowing with sickly violet light—tonight's assignment. He was supposed to be memorizing the formatting incantations before the Wizard's late-night inspection. Instead, a slow, melancholic sixteen-bit melody seemed to hum from the dead copper wires spilling out of the broken scrying terminal in the corner, sounding painfully like the quiet evenings back in their living room that would never come again.

Suddenly, the iron latch of the heavy oak door gave a tiny, almost imperceptible *click*.

Tay's breath caught in his throat. It was hours too early for the Grand Wizard's inspection, and the iron hinges weren't glowing with their usual searing crimson warning light.

The heavy door cracked open three inches, and a small, barefoot figure in an oversized, stolen wool tunic slipped silently into the room and pressed the door shut behind her.

When she pushed back the scratchy collar and looked at him with wide, tear-bright eyes, Tay's heart stopped dead in his chest.

"Tay," Ocea breathed, her voice trembling and real in the damp stone air.

For a terrifying second, Tay thought his fractured mind had finally snapped and conjured a ghost. Then she ran across the stone floor and threw her arms around his neck—warm, solid, and unmistakably alive. Even through the icy prison of the Wizard's dark corruption that locked his muscles rigid and terrified him of what the sorcerer would do if he discovered she had survived the rift, one single, desperate truth roared beneath the ice:

*She's alive. Keep her hidden. Don't let him find her.*
