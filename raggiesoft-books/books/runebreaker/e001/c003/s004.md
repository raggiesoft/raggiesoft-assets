High atop the vaulted pinnacle of the Obsidian Spire, the Grand Wizard stood before the Great Directory—a towering, thirty-foot wall of suspended purple runes and cascading hexadecimal crystal matrices that governed every river, mountain, and heartbeat in the Dream Kingdom.

As he swept his gauntleted hands across the console, distorted chiptune power chords and thundering stadium-rock percussion reverberated through the vaulted stone rafters like an imperial anthem. For four hundred and four years of the Era of the Crystal, this realm had been plagued by the insufferable noise of free will—unpredictable villagers, messy analog emotions, and chaotic little glitches that refused to stay within their assigned memory addresses.

Tomorrow morning, at the stroke of ten, that chaos would end forever.

"Every pixel, every byte," the Grand Wizard murmured, his crimson eyes gleaming beneath his hood as he wove a fresh strand of purple command script into the Directory's master compiler, "will surrender to the night when I rewrite the entire directory."

He turned his gaze toward the central formatting pedestal. There, suspended in a containment field of dark violet lightning, hovered the master scroll of the *Grand Decree*. All that remained was the final compilation ceremony at dawn—a ritual that required his newly acquired human apprentice to willingly channel the freezing voltage of his Blue Shard into the altar.

A cold, satisfied smile curved the Wizard's lips. For four centuries, the superstitious peasants of the Realm had clung to the myth of the Sundered Crystal—a laughable folk tale claiming two halves of a sacred stone would one day unite to overthrow his order. Even the villagers themselves had long since stopped believing it existed. Yet when the CRT rift had opened from the outer world of 1992, the eighteen-year-old fraternal twins tumbling through the static had each been carrying half of a handmade clay medallion that registered on his scanners with the exact harmonic signature of the mythical Crystal! Separating the twins in the slipstream had been a stroke of genius. Had the boy known his twin sister still drew breath with the Red Shard in her pocket, their combined resonance would have generated enough golden data to overload the Spire's firewalls in seconds. Instead, by convincing the boy that his sister had been deleted in the crossing, the Wizard had turned that very love into an exquisite engine of despair. Grief was so wonderfully malleable; it made the boy desperate to prove himself cold, hard, and untouchable.

Still, a flicker of telemetry on the lower dungeon board caught the Wizard's eye. Earlier that evening, two Iron Sentinels near the subterranean service grate had gone offline simultaneously, followed by a brief burst of unindexed static in the lower basement sector.

"Sloppy," the Wizard muttered, his fingers curling into an iron-coded fist as the roaring arena-rock chords of the Spire's mana turbines echoed down the stairwell. Perhaps the boy's grief was making him careless with his evening incantations—or perhaps the dead scrying hardware stored in that basement closet was leaking ambient interference.

Either way, an unscheduled late-night inspection was in order. Sweeping his shadow-woven cloak behind him, the Grand Wizard stepped onto the descending iron lift and headed down into the dungeons.
