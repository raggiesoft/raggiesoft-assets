A slow, descending sixteen-bit arpeggio—like the sound of a console losing power—echoed softly through the windowless stone holding cell high in the eastern tower.

Ocea sat slumped on the freezing stone floor with her knees drawn up to her chest. Before locking the cell door to prepare the cathedral below, the Citadel's automaton wardens had forced a grotesque, pixelated ceremonial gown over her shoulders—a stiff, suffocating dress woven from itchy silver-and-violet static wire meant to mark her as the sacrifice of the Corrupted Ceremony. In her stubborn defiance, Ocea had refused to let them take her real clothes, keeping her cotton t-shirt and faded denim jeans on right underneath the hideous gown, and kicking away the glass slippers they tried to force onto her bare feet.

Still, as she looked down at her right wrist, a crushing weight of despair settled over her chest.

The quartz lens of the *Runebreaker* gauntlet was fractured down the center, its silver conduits dark and cold. Her ribs ached from the Guardian's blow, the cell door was triple-barred with purple runes, and in just over an hour, the Grand Wizard was going to use Tay's broken heart to erase the entire Dream Kingdom.

For the first time since the television had pulled them out of their living room, Ocea let her forehead drop onto her knees and whispered the two words every player dreaded:

"It's over."

The lonely, melancholic notes of her own theme—slowed down and drained of color—seemed to drift through the cold air of the cell. She had crossed the Crystal Plains, learned to channel golden data, and snuck into the heart of the dungeon, and it still hadn't been enough. The Wizard was too strong. The code was too heavy.

Then, in the pitch-black shadow between her knees, Ocea saw a tiny light at the end of the tunnel.

Down inside the front pocket of the denim jeans hidden beneath the ceremonial gown, a single, stubborn pinpoint of warm golden light flickered to life.

Ocea lifted her head, her breath catching. Slowly, she slipped her hand under the itchy skirt of the ceremonial dress and pulled the fire-engine red half of their summer-camp clay medallion from her jeans pocket.

The red clay wasn't dark. Along its jagged ceramic edge, a tiny golden spark was pulsing in a steady, rhythmic pattern: *tap-tap-pause-tap*.

Ocea recognized that rhythm instantly. It was the secret four-beat knock she and Tay had used on each other's bedroom doors back home since they were six years old. Down in the cathedral vaults below, Tay wasn't surrendering to the purple runes anymore. Now that he knew his twin sister was alive, he was tapping against his ocean-blue half of the medallion with everything he had, sending a tiny heartbeat of hope straight up through the stone floors of the Spire to tell her he hadn't given up.

And as the golden spark touched the cracked quartz lens of her *Runebreaker* gauntlet, the fracture lines didn't fail—they filled with liquid gold, glowing twice as bright as before.

Ocea stared at the warm light in her palm, and a fierce, unstoppable smile broke through her tears.

She wasn't out of continues. Not as long as her brother was still calling for Player Two.

Ocea stood up on her bare feet, brushed the dungeon dust off her shoulders, and squared her jaw as the footsteps of the ceremonial guard approached the cell door to escort her to the cathedral. They wanted her at the altar? Fine. She was going to crash the ceremony.
