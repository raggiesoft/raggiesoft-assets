The moment Ocea's palm struck his chest, a tidal wave of warm, golden light surged straight into Tay's heart, and the freezing purple cage inside his skull shattered into a million pieces.

Tay gasped, sucking in a lungful of air that tasted like autumn wind and living room carpet instead of dungeon sulfur. All the toxic lies the Grand Wizard had poured into his mind—the fabricated image of Ocea shattering in the rift, the suffocating guilt, the icy numbness he had worn like armor—evaporated in the radiance of his twin sister's golden pixel magic.

She was standing right in front of him in her t-shirt and jeans, barefoot and fiercely alive, her eyes shining with the stubborn, unconditional love that had carried her across an entire world to rescue him.

"No!" the Grand Wizard roared, slamming the butt of his staff onto the altar to force the compilation through by brute strength. "Obey the Decree, apprentice! Your grief belongs to me!"

"I'm not your apprentice," Tay said, his voice ringing clear and steady across the cathedral as a bright, heroic synth melody clashed directly against the Wizard's dark chords.

With a surge of raw strength, Tay wrenched his wrists free from the fracturing violet chains and snatched the ocean-blue half of their clay summer-camp medallion out of the altar's crystal vise.

"And she's not dead!" Tay shouted.

He reached forward at the exact same instant Ocea extended her hand. Right above the formatting pedestal, they slammed the fire-engine red half and the ocean-blue half of the clay medallion together.

*CLICK.*

The jagged ceramic teeth locked together seamlessly. For a heartbeat, there was absolute silence. Then, where the cheap summer-camp acrylic paint met along the zigzag seam, a blinding pillar of united gold-and-sapphire light erupted straight through the roof of the Grand Cathedral.

The formatting pedestal exploded outward in a shower of harmless sparks. Across the thirty-foot wall of the Great Directory, the Grand Wizard's purple command runes began to unravel at blistering speed, his master scroll tearing itself to ribbons as the very architecture of the Obsidian Spire groaned and fractured around them. Massive obsidian pillars cracked from floor to ceiling, shedding chunks of corrupted stone that dissolved into swirling sixteen-bit noise before hitting the ground.

Before the shockwave could throw Ocea off balance, Tay stepped in and wrapped both arms tightly around his twin sister in the warm, breathless bear hug he had been starving to give her for days, lifting her clear off her bare feet for a second before setting her down beside him.

"Missed you, Ocea," Tay breathed, his voice thick with emotion as he kept one hand locked firmly around hers over the united medallion.

"There's my brother," Ocea grinned, wiping a tear from her cheek with her free wrist as the floor of the cathedral gave way beneath them, dropping them both onto the floating battle-platforms of the Wizard's inner lair below.
