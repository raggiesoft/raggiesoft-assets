She waited three agonizing seconds, then rapidly punched in the phone number printed on the payphone's metal placard. She slammed the receiver back onto the hook and sprinted back to the car, sliding into the backseat and pulling the door shut just seconds before the men walked out of the store.

She reached over and grabbed my trembling hand, squeezing it with a fierce, grounding pressure. *They're coming,* the pressure said. *I promise.*

Richard put the car in drive, and we pushed deeper into the mountains.

## 8:15 PM — Off the Grid

The road turned from asphalt to dirt, and then to pure, uneven gravel.

We were completely off the grid. The dense canopy of extreme southeastern Kentucky blocked out whatever remained of the evening sun. We pulled up to a decaying cinderblock cabin sitting on the edge of a black, still body of water. Fern Lake.

The isolation was suffocating. There were no streetlights, no neighboring houses, no sounds except the oppressive hum of cicadas.

The men dragged us inside. The interior of the cabin smelled of mildew and stale beer. They shoved Samantha and me into a small, windowless bedroom at the end of the hall. The heavy wooden door slammed shut, and the deadbolt clicked.

My breathing hitched, short and erratic. My jaw was clenched so tight my teeth ached.

## 9:45 PM — The Cellar Floor

The heavy wooden door slammed shut. The deadbolt clicked into place with a terrifying, metallic finality.

I was left lying on the filthy, mildewed carpet. My brain was completely short-circuiting, sending wild, misfiring electrical signals to every muscle in my body. My limbs were locked in a brutal, rigid spasm. I couldn't pull my arms inward. I couldn't push myself up. Thomas ripped his arm away with a grunt of surprise, shoving her violently back by her shoulders. Samantha stumbled, hitting the floor, but she instantly bared her teeth, scrambling right back to her knees, ready to throw herself at them again.

Richard looked down at her, wiping a bead of sweat from his forehead. A sickening, amused smirk crept across his face.

"Oh, feisty, huh?" Richard sneered, his breath reeking of stale alcohol and cheap cigarettes. "You want to fight? Sit down and shut up, or we'll figure out a use for you, too."

He didn't hit her. Predators look for the easiest prey, and Samantha was making it blindingly clear that she would gouge their eyes out if they tried to pin her down. They didn't want a fight; they wanted absolute control, and my locked, paralyzed body gave them exactly that.

Richard and Thomas turned their backs on us and walked out into the hall.

The moment the door locked, Samantha scrambled across the dark room. She dropped to her knees beside me and pulled my thrashing, rigid body into her lap. She wrapped her arms completely around my chest, locking her hands together and applying a desperate, heavy deep-pressure grip.

"I've got you," she sobbed, burying her face into my neck. "Toby, I've got you. Breathe. Just breathe with me."

Her compression slowly fought through the neurological static. My spine stopped trying to arch backward. The violent tremors in my hands began to subside, leaving me entirely drained, exhausted, and broken.

I rested my head against her chest. We sat there in the pitch-black room for what felt like an eternity.

The only sound in the cabin was the clinking of glass bottles from the main room. The drinking was heavy. Their voices grew louder, slurring together as the alcohol stripped away whatever thin layer of humanity they had left. Every time a bottle clattered against a table, my shoulders flinched. Samantha just squeezed me tighter, rocking me back and forth.

"They're coming," Samantha whispered, over and over again like a prayer into the dark. "Abbey and Sarah are coming. I promised you. They are coming."

The illuminated gap at the bottom of the door was our only metric for time. I watched the dust motes drift through the thin slice of yellow light. I didn't know what time it was, but I knew the night was stretching endlessly.

## 11:15 PM — The Threshold

Richard and Thomas stepped over the threshold, their eyes locked on me.

Before either of them could take another step, a sound echoed from the front of the cabin. It wasn't a knock. It was a violent, heavy rattling against the front door, barely audible over the sudden, deafening crack of thunder outside. The storm had rolled in, and torrential rain was hammering against the cabin's tin roof.
