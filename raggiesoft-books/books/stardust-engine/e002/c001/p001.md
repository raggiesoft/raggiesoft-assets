## October 12, 1990 (`19:30 CDT`) — The Thirty-Minute Box & The Sabotaged Mix

To this day, **Ryan**, **Cassidy**, **Holly**, **Evan**, and **Tyler** all hate [***Live in Chicago***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago).

If it were up to their musical pride alone, the two-inch master reels recorded at **The Midwest Horizon Arena** (*Midwest Horizon*) on the night of **October 12, 1990** would have stayed locked at the bottom of the Blacksburg vault forever. The only reason the seven-track concert remains in the public **Engine Room Records** catalog is for strict historical and legal record-keeping: fifteen seconds into Track 1 ([**"The Stardust Engine"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-01-the-stardust-engine-live-in-chicago)), twenty-three-year-old Ryan steps up to the center microphone in front of eighteen thousand people and shouts:

> *"Hello Chicago! I'm Ryan O'Connell! And this is my sister, Cassidy! And we are The Stardust Engine!"*

Two years later, that timestamped magnetic tape would become Exhibit A in a Los Angeles courtroom—the "Smoking Gun" proving that **Apex Records** executive Julian Vance never bothered to listen to a single minute of the band's live tapes before trying to market Ryan and Cassidy as a married couple during the *Friction* sessions.

Everything else about that night in Chicago was a war zone.

The only reason an opening act from Montgomery County, Virginia was playing an eighteen-thousand-seat indoor arena outside O'Hare Airport on the fall extension of the *Neon Hearts* Tour was because the headliners on the bill were close family friends who loved the O'Connells like younger siblings. Weeks before the Chicago stop, the headliners had called Apex Records' tour department in Los Angeles with a simple request: they wanted Ryan, Cassidy, Evan, and Tyler to come back out during the headlining encore with two drum kits and jam together to close down the night.

Apex Records' executives had given a two-word answer: ***"Absolutely not."***

In the label's eyes, **The Stardust Engine** was a synth-pop product designed to sell cassettes of [***Neon Hearts***](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1989-neon-hearts) to teenagers—not a rock band allowed to trade seven-minute guitar and synthesizer solos with arena legends. Apex locked the band into a rigid **thirty-minute opening-act box**, stationed a label tour representative named **Arthur Pembrook** in the Stage-Right wings tapping a gold stopwatch, and threatened to pull the main stage breakers at `29:59` if they ran thirty seconds over.

Because the show was being recorded to twenty-four-track tape for a promotional tour EP, nineteen-year-old **Holly O'Connell**—managing the band between her coursework at **Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute**—could not stay backstage. During the entire thirty-minute set, Holly stationed herself one hundred and twenty feet out on the arena floor inside the barricaded **Front-of-House (`FOH`) mixing and recording island**, standing directly behind the label-hired FOH sound engineer with a legal pad in one hand and a calibrated stopwatch in the other.

Within ninety seconds of the opening downbeat of [**"The Stardust Engine"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-01-the-stardust-engine-live-in-chicago), Holly saw why she needed to be there.

Standing at the FOH console, the label's mixing engineer deliberately pulled down the faders on Cassidy's upper synthesizer tier—burying her iconic cosmic *"Stardust"* bell-synth leitmotif so deep beneath the drums and rhythm guitars that the call-and-response heart of the song was practically inaudible on the house PA and the two-track reference print. When Holly leaned over the doghouse of the mixing console and demanded that he bring Channels 9 and 10 back up to unity gain, the engineer pointed to a handwritten memo taped to the meter bridge from L.A. A&R: *Keep Track 1 guitar-dry; push synth hooks ONLY on the Neon Hearts singles.*

Up on stage, hearing the hollow, lopsided house mix bouncing back off the rear concrete bowl of **Midwest Horizon**, Evan and Tyler responded the only way the Wright twins knew how: stripped of a studio click-track, they played fifteen beats per minute faster and twice as heavy, turning the glossy pop arrangements of [**"Neon Hearts"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-02-neon-hearts-live-in-chicago) and [**"Dancing in the Neon"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-03-dancing-in-the-neon-live-in-chicago) into a ferocious garage-rock sprint as they raced the label's thirty-minute clock.

And then came Track 4.

## `19:41 CDT` — Track 4 (*"If You Walked Away"*), Track 5 (*"Light Up the Night"*) & The Clear-Com Alert

To the marketing department in Los Angeles, [**"If You Walked Away"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-04-if-you-walked-away-live-in-chicago) was a slow-dance breakup ballad meant to get eighteen thousand kids holding lighters in the air.

To twenty-four-year-old **Cassidy O'Connell**, who was autistic and experienced every word she sang with literal, photographic intensity, the song was not a metaphor. It was a waking nightmare.

Standing behind her three-tier keyboard rig under the harsh pink and teal arena par-cans, already sensory-overloaded by the ruined monitor mix and the label rep barking time warnings from the Stage-Right curtain, Cassidy looked across the stage at Ryan as she sang the third verse and final chorus:

> *"They build these walls so high and steep*
> *With promises they never keep*
> *But we have a fortress, strong and true*
> *I'm safe as long as I have you...*
> *If you walked away, the sky would lose its blue*
> *I wouldn't know what to do, I'd be lost in the gray..."*

Halfway through the bridge, Ryan heard it in his floor wedge.

It wasn't a vocal ornament. Cassidy's breath was catching in her throat, her vibrato fracturing into the high, thin tremor of acute autistic panic. Every night on tour, singing those lyrics forced her brain to simulate the one scenario she could not survive—Ryan leaving her behind in the noise—and tonight, trapped under eighteen thousand screaming strangers and a hostile crew, the simulation had become real.

When the final piano chord faded and Cassidy whispered into the microphone—*"Don't walk away... Thank you so much"*—her voice broke on the last word.

Ryan took half a step toward her riser to call an audible, ready to cut the set right there and walk her off the stage.

From the Stage-Right wings, Arthur Pembrook jabbed two fingers violently at his gold watch and mouthed across the deck: *THREE MORE SONGS. PLAY THE SINGLE OR YOU'RE IN BREACH.*

Behind her keyboards, Cassidy saw the label rep's gesture. Terrified that stopping the show would get Ryan sued or punished by the label, she locked her knees, stared straight down at the black-and-white ivory keys, and threw every ounce of her cognitive energy into an iron wall of autistic masking.

When Tyler counted off Track 5—[**"Light Up the Night"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-05-light-up-the-night-live-in-chicago), the synth-pop single Apex had bet their entire quarterly marketing budget on—Ryan shouted *"Are you ready to light up the night, Chicago?!"* as loud as his lungs could manage, stalking to the very front lip of the stage and windmilling his guitar chords for one reason only: to pull all thirty-six thousand eyes in the arena onto himself so nobody would look at his sister.

To the crowd out on the floor, Cassidy sounded note-perfect on *"Light Up the Night."* Seventeen years of classical piano muscle memory carried her fingers across the synthesizer arpeggios, and her voice hit every harmony on the chorus.

Out at the Front-of-House mixing pit one hundred and twenty feet away, Holly could hear the mechanical tightness in Cassidy's phrasing, and her stomach knotted—but through the haze of arena theatrical smoke and eighteen thousand teenagers jumping on the floor, she couldn't see Cassidy's face behind the towering synthesizer rack.

Someone ten feet away from Cassidy, however, could both see her face and hear what the house PA was muting.

Just inside the **Stage-Left wings** at **Monitor World**, twelve feet from Cassidy's keyboard riser, the headlining band's veteran monitor engineer, **Gordy Vance**, was mixing the stage wedges in a pair of closed-back studio headphones while the headliner's frontman—the O'Connells' close family friend—stood right beside his console watching the kids play.

Out in the arena, a noise gate on the FOH board automatically muted Cassidy's vocal microphone whenever she wasn't singing. Inside Gordy's pre-fader cue headphones at Monitor World, there was no noise gate.

Between every single chorus of *"Light Up the Night,"* the second Cassidy turned her mouth half an inch off-axis from the microphone capsule, Gordy heard her gasping for air—shallow, ragged hyperventilation punctuated by tiny, choked whimpers in the back of her throat as tears spilled down her cheeks onto the top synthesizer tier.

"Look at her hands," the headliner said quietly to Gordy from the curtain, his jaw clenching as he watched Cassidy's left wrist shaking uncontrollably between chords. "She's in freefall. The only reason she hasn't collapsed on that riser is because she doesn't want the suit on Stage-Right to go after her brother."

As the final chord of *"Light Up the Night"* rang out and the crowd roared (`03:15`), Cassidy's knees buckled an inch behind the keyboard stand. She gripped the aluminum frame of her top synthesizer with both hands to stay upright, her chest heaving, staring wide-eyed at Ryan across the stage in sheer, silent terror.

Ryan didn't look at Arthur Pembrook in the Stage-Right wing. He didn't care about the teleprompter or the stage blocking.

Walking straight across the center deck to Cassidy's riser, Ryan stepped up to his microphone, looked directly at his sister, and spoke over the roar of eighteen thousand people to introduce Track 6 ([**"My Anchor Holds"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-06-my-anchor-holds-live-in-chicago)):

> *"This is a song for that one person who keeps you sane... the one who's your anchor."*

Through the first two verses and choruses, Ryan stayed within arm's reach of her keyboard stand, singing every line of the chorus straight to her face (*"But when the world is spinning out of my control / You're the one true thing that I can hold / Through the static and the driving rain / You're the one who keeps me sane"*). Then, as the bridge gave way to the soaring guitar solo (`02:12`), Ryan saw Cassidy's shoulders begin to convulse as her masking wall cracked.

Without missing a sixteenth-note on his fretboard, Ryan stepped right onto her keyboard riser, turned around, and pressed his spine **flush against Cassidy's back**—locking his shoulder blades against hers in a standing, back-to-back deep-pressure brace right in front of eighteen thousand people.

To the teenagers in the stands, it looked like a classic 1980s arena-rock stage pose between guitar player and keyboardist.

In reality, Ryan was physically holding his sister upright with his own spine so she wouldn't fall to the floor, letting the low-frequency resonance of his body and his guitar neck vibrate straight through her ribs to ground her nervous system through the final ninety seconds of the song.

In the Stage-Left wing, watching Ryan brace Cassidy's trembling back with his own, the headliner turned to his monitor engineer and his personal head of security—a six-foot-five, two-hundred-and-eighty-pound former Chicago offensive lineman named **Marcus "Mac" Kowalski**.

"Gordy, hit the FOH call beacon and get Holly off the floor right now," the headliner ordered, his voice steel. "Mac, clear the backstage corridor to Dressing Room 3. The second those kids come through the curtain after their closer, nobody touches that door."

Gordy slammed his thumb down on the red **`CALL`** button of the hardwired **`Clear-Com`** party-line beltpack linking Stage-Left Monitor World to the Front-of-House pit.

One hundred and twenty feet out in the arena crowd, the white xenon strobe light on the FOH intercom box flashed wildly. Holly snatched the single-ear headset off the console rail and pressed the heavy foam earcup against her left ear while plugging her right ear with her finger.

*"Holly, it's Gordy at Stage-Left Monitor World—listen to me!"* the monitor engineer's voice crackled urgently over the roar of the crowd as *"My Anchor Holds"* ended (`04:15`). *"Cassidy is having a massive emergency up here. I've been hearing her hyperventilating in my cue cans ever since Track 4. That back-to-back move Ryan just did on the solo wasn't for show—he was holding her up so her legs wouldn't give out. Pembrook's screaming at them from Stage-Right to finish the last song, and she's going to shatter the exact second they clear the curtain. Leave the FOH board right now and get back to Dressing Room 3! Mac is clearing the hallway for you—GO!"*

Holly didn't waste half a second asking a question.

As Ryan shouted *"Thank you, Chicago! You've been beautiful! This is our last song!"* and kicked into the frantic, high-speed opening riff of Track 7 ([**"Electric Color"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-07-electric-color-live-in-chicago)), nineteen-year-old Holly shoved her legal pad and stopwatch into her leather bag, vaulted the gap in the FOH steel barricade, and sprinted up the concrete vomitory tunnel underneath the lower bowl of **Midwest Horizon**.

## `19:57 CDT` — Behind the Curtain: The Perimeter & The Vow

Up on stage, the three minutes and twenty-seven seconds of [**"Electric Color"**](https://music.raggiesoft.com/the-stardust-engine/1990-live-in-chicago?track=1-07-electric-color-live-in-chicago) were pure, desperate survival.

Ryan never left the edge of Cassidy's riser. Anchored by her brother standing two feet from her right shoulder and Evan and Tyler driving the rhythm section like a freight train behind them, Cassidy poured the very last drops of her adrenaline into the keys. To anyone listening to the tape, the performance sounds almost manic in its joy and speed—because all four of them were racing toward the finish line to get Cassidy off that stage alive.

At `03:12`, they slammed down on the final chord.

> *"Thank you! Goodnight, everybody! We're The Stardust Engine!"*

As eighteen thousand fans erupted into a deafening standing ovation (`03:20–03:27`), Ryan didn't take a bow. The instant the stage lights dipped to blue for the changeover, he yanked the quarter-inch cable out of his guitar jack, handed the instrument blindly to Gordy at the edge of the Stage-Left wing, and caught Cassidy around the waist the exact millisecond they stepped behind the heavy black velour side-stage curtain.

The mask didn't just slip; it shattered into a million pieces.

Before her sneakers even touched the rubber floor mat at the bottom of the Stage-Left stairs, Cassidy's knees buckled completely. A raw, ragged sob tore out of her chest as both hands flew up over her ears, her entire body shaking so violently her teeth clicked together.

"I've got you, Cass, I've got you," Ryan breathed, taking almost all of her weight against his chest while Evan and Tyler cleared the drum riser in two bounds and flanked them on either side—forming an impenetrable four-person moving shield down the concrete backstage corridor.

Thirty feet down the hall, the door to **Dressing Room 3** was already wide open.

Having beat them to the room by forty seconds, Holly had already flipped off the harsh, buzzing overhead fluorescent ballast tubes—leaving only a single warm 40-watt incandescent table lamp glowing in the corner—and pulled the heavy curtains shut across the mirror so there wouldn't be any visual reflections to overwhelm her sister.

Ryan carried Cassidy straight through the doorway, with Evan and Tyler stepping in right behind them.

Holly immediately pulled the heavy steel dressing-room door shut and threw the deadbolt.

**Inside the room were Ryan, Cassidy, Holly, Evan, and Tyler—and nobody else.**

Five seconds later, out in the concrete corridor, Arthur Pembrook came charging down the hallway from Stage-Right with his clipboard raised, red-faced and ready to berate the band about their tempo and demand that Ryan and Cassidy come out to the VIP lounge to shake hands with regional radio distributors.

Pembrook didn't get within six feet of the door handle.

Standing squarely across the doorframe of Dressing Room 3 with his massive arms folded over his chest was **Mac Kowalski**—the headlining family friend's six-foot-five, two-hundred-and-eighty-pound personal bodyguard.

"Out of the way," Pembrook snapped, reaching for the brass handle. "That's my opening act in there, and I have fifteen radio program directors waiting in Hospitality—"

Mac didn't uncross his arms. He simply shifted half a step forward, blotting out the hallway lights above Pembrook's head like a concrete overpass.

"Nobody goes through this door," Mac said, his voice a low, flat Chicago rumble that carried zero room for negotiation. "Only the five kids inside. Boss's orders."

"Your boss doesn't sign their checks, Apex Records does!" Pembrook barked. "Open that door!"

"Take one more step toward this latch, suit," Mac replied calmly, looking down at the label rep's chest, "and I'm going to pick you up by your lapels and carry you out to the loading dock dumpsters myself. Turn around and walk away."

Pembrook stared up at the two-hundred-and-eighty-pound wall of muscle blocking the doorway, swallowed hard, and backed down the corridor without another word.

Inside the quiet, dimly lit sanctuary of Dressing Room 3, the muffled voices in the hallway faded away completely.

On the low fabric sofa against the far wall, Ryan sat with his back braced against the corner cushions and twenty-four-year-old Cassidy curled tightly in his lap and arms, her knees drawn up to her chest and her face buried against the collar of his sweat-damp stage shirt.

She was crying hard—deep, body-wracking sobs that shook her ribcage as twenty minutes of trapped terror, sensory overload, and the visceral phantom nightmare of *"If You Walked Away"* finally poured out of her.

Nobody in the room told her to calm down, and nobody asked her to speak. They knew the protocol they had practiced since childhood on Draper Road and Progress Street.

Holly knelt quietly on the carpet beside the sofa, unlaced Cassidy's tight stage shoes, slipped them off her feet, and draped Ryan's heavy wool touring coat over Cassidy's trembling shoulders like a weighted blanket before sitting right beside Ryan's left knee with her hand resting steadily on Cassidy's ankle. Across the narrow room, Evan and Tyler sat silently side-by-side on two black road cases just inside the locked door, keeping watch over the room's inner perimeter so their cousins didn't have to process a single stray sound.

Ryan wrapped both of his long arms completely around Cassidy's shoulders and back, locking his wrists together to give her firm, unyielding **deep-pressure input**. Pressing his cheek against the top of her hair, he rocked her slowly at a steady sixty beats per minute—the exact resting heartbeat tempo they had used since 1978—and whispered the same anchor phrase over and over against her ear:

"Family sticks together, Cass. I'm right here. Feel my arms? I'm right here. Family sticks together. I'm never walking away from you. Never."

For fifteen minutes, then twenty, Cassidy cried into his shirt, her fingers twisting tightly into the fabric over his sternum while Ryan held the pressure steady and unbroken. Slowly, degree by degree, the violent shivering in her shoulders began to subside, her ragged gasps evening out to match the slow rise and fall of Ryan's chest.

When her breathing finally steadied, Ryan didn't let go. Instead, looking over the top of Cassidy's head at Holly, Evan, and Tyler in the warm glow of the corner lamp, Ryan's jaw set with quiet, absolute finality.

"Holly," Ryan said softly, his voice vibrating through his chest against Cassidy's ear.

"I'm listening, Ry," Holly answered from beside his knee.

"Take *'If You Walked Away'* off the setlist," Ryan said.

Then he looked down at his sister curled in his arms.

"Cass, listen to me," Ryan said gently, his voice steady and clear. "I don't care what Apex says. I don't care if they fine us, I don't care if they threaten to pull tour support, and I don't care if it's on the record. **We are never playing *'If You Walked Away'* live again if it can possibly be helped.** Never again. No song is worth what that just did to you."

Curled up in his lap beneath the heavy wool coat, Cassidy lifted her head from his collarbone.

Her eyes were red and still brimming with tears in the dim amber lamplight, her cheeks wet—but the panicked terror that had gripped her since the fourth song on stage was gone.

As she looked up into her brother's eyes, Ryan tightened his hug grip firmly around her shoulders, pulling her even closer against his chest so she could feel the solid, immovable certainty of his promise.

With tears glistening on her eyelashes, Cassidy looked up at him and gave a small, deep, appreciative nod—before resting her forehead right back down against his heart.
