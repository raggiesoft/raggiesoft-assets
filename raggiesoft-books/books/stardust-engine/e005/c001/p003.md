## February 4, 2016 • 12:10 PM — Why Five Musicians from Progress Street Ride the Bus

"Watch that brass threshold strip, Cass," Ryan O'Connell said quietly over his left shoulder as he pressed his palms forward along the anodized titanium handrims of his twenty-four-inch rear wheels, guiding his manual wheelchair into the accessible third-floor gallery of the Virginia House of Delegates in Richmond.

Right behind his backrest, his forty-eight-year-old sister Cassidy kept both hands wrapped firmly around the rubberized rear push-handles mounted to Ryan's titanium frame, using the forward momentum of her older brother's wheelchair like a rolling walker to steady her cerebral palsy gait as her left foot cleared the polished brass sill onto the gallery carpet. Flanking them in the aisle were their youngest sister Holly, carrying a burgundy leather legal portfolio under her arm, and their forty-eight-year-old identical twin cousins Evan and Tyler Wright.

Pinned to all five of their winter coats were bright blue *RIDE TRANSIT VIRGINIA* lapel stickers.

To anyone outside Montgomery County who only knew The Stardust Engine from platinum album sleeves or stadium lighting rigs, seeing all five members of the band and their chief executive rolling through the Virginia State Capitol at noon on a Thursday in February to lobby for public bus funding might have looked surprising. Back home in Blacksburg and Christiansburg, however, it was simply how they lived.

Whenever the weather was clear and they weren't hauling flight cases or braving cold mountain rain in Unit 02, Ryan, Cassidy, Holly, Evan, and Tyler took the Blue Ridge Transit buses around town just like everyone else on Progress Street NE. After decades of touring in sealed sleeper coaches and slipping through hotel freight elevators to avoid crowds, there was nothing Ryan and Cassidy loved more than rolling down to the corner of Progress Street after ten o'clock in the morning, watching a silver-and-blue Blue Ridge Transit low-floor bus kneel at the curb, and boarding up the front aluminum ramp alongside Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute undergraduates and retired townspeople who treated the guy in the manual wheelchair and his sister holding onto his push-handles like ordinary neighbors.

Respecting their ironclad post-ten-o'clock morning rule, Tyler had driven the five of them east from Blacksburg that morning in Unit 02 so they could join the New River Valley delegation for the afternoon half of Virginia Transit Legislative Day and lobby the General Assembly for state operating and capital grants for Blue Ridge Transit's articulated fleet.

As Ryan locked his wheel brakes behind the brass gallery rail and Cassidy eased down into the padded companion seat immediately to his right—resting her left hand on his right forearm so her leg muscles could uncoil—they exchanged warm nods with the other transit riders lined up along the accessible gallery row. Sitting right beside them were two other families from Christiansburg and CPI whom the five of them saw almost every week in the ninety-five-point-five-degree therapy water of The Cove at the Blue Ridge Regional Aquatic Center and out on the Huckleberry Trail: [Jordan in his power wheelchair alongside Aubrie Jean, Megan, Zoë, and Isabel](https://books.raggiesoft.com/aubrie/spring-semester-2016/off-precautions-megan-s-first-night/part-2-virginia-transit-legislative-day-house-bill-663-delegate-katie-prairie-in-room-518), and [Freddie in his matte-black titanium manual chair alongside his teenage sister Harper in her neon-purple wheelchair and their sisters Paige, Jessica, Sarah, and Jenna](https://books.raggiesoft.com/paige/system-integration/the-closed-circuit/part-3-virginia-transit-legislative-day-the-bathroom-police-bill-room-518), with two Virginia Dominion University students in royal-blue jackets—a young man in a blue manual wheelchair holding his sister's hand—just down the rail.

Down on the green carpet of the House Chamber, the Speaker called up the next bill on the calendar: House Bill 663, patroned by Delegate Martin Cobb of Spotsylvania.

Holly's jaw tightened beside Ryan's left wheel as she flipped open her legal portfolio to a printed copy of the bill. "Here it is," she murmured low enough for only the five of them to hear. "The bathroom police bill. Line 18 requires every restroom in a state building, public school, or public university—which includes every concourse restroom at The Crucible on the CPI campus—to be restricted by 'anatomical sex,' enforced by a fifty-dollar civil penalty for the Literary Fund."

Beside Ryan, Cassidy's fingers curled tighter around his jacket sleeve.

All five of them despised House Bill 663 with every fiber of their being. For twenty-five years since the black ice at Mile Marker 98 had severed Ryan's spinal cord at the tenth thoracic vertebra, Ryan's life outside their farmhouse on Progress Street had run on the strict four-hour intermittent catheterization clock of Protocol T-10—and Cassidy's mobility in crowded public buildings depended on holding onto Ryan's rear push-handles or leaning on Evan, Tyler, and Holly when cerebral palsy spasticity locked her calves. Whenever an older highway rest area, municipal building, or campus hall lacked a single-occupancy accessible family restroom, Cassidy walked behind Ryan's chair all the way to the restroom threshold, and Holly or Cassidy regularly had to step across a doorway to hand Ryan a sterile medical kit or help Evan and Tyler steady Cassidy on slick tile. The idea that politicians in Richmond wanted to station government officials or campus police outside public restrooms to interrogate people about their anatomy under threat of a fifty-dollar fine made Ryan's blood boil.

"Wait until they try enforcing that nonsense without violating the Fourth Amendment," Evan muttered under his breath, leaning his forearms on the gallery rail beside his twin brother.

"Watch the floor," Tyler whispered, pointing down toward the back row of the chamber. "Look who just raised her hand."

Down in the accessible floor bay beside the center aisle sat thirty-year-old [Delegate Katie Prairie of Virginia Beach](https://books.raggiesoft.com/katie/the-gentlewoman-from-virginia-beach-the-house-of-delegates/house-bill-663-live-on-meet-the-nation/part-1-line-18-seated-by-delegate-ronald-villa-on-the-floor-a-question-across-the-chamber)—the only member of the General Assembly who used a power wheelchair full-time, parked right beside her twin brother Nate in his matching black power wheelchair.

When the Speaker recognized the Gentlewoman from Virginia Beach and Delegate Cobb yielded for a question, Delegate Prairie held up the two printed pages of House Bill 663 in her right hand. Her voice rang out through the overhead chamber speakers with crystal-clear, literal precision:

"Why on earth should I—or any woman or girl in the Commonwealth of Virginia—have to pull my pants down for a government official or a police officer to prove I have female parts just to use the ladies' room?!"

Up in the third-floor gallery, Tyler and Evan let out a sharp bark of delighted laughter as Cassidy clapped both hands together and Holly broke into a fierce, triumphant grin. All along the accessible gallery rail, the Blue Ridge Transit riders from Christiansburg and Blacksburg and the VDU students from Hampton Roads burst into applause while the Speaker hammered his gavel for order.

Down on the House Floor, Delegate Cobb stood frozen behind his brass microphone, his face turning the color of a stop sign through four full seconds of dead silence until the House Majority Leader sprang to his feet and moved that House Bill 663 be passed by for the day.

"Oh, she just ended that bill on live camera," Holly whispered, her eyes shining as she looked down at the thirty-year-old Delegate in the black power wheelchair. "In ninety-five seconds flat, without a single legal Latin phrase, she just exposed the entire enforcement mechanism as a strip search."

## February 4, 2016 • 2:40 PM — Seven Wheelchairs in Room 518

At two-forty that afternoon—after spending an hour on the third and fourth floors of the General Assembly Building walking the New River Valley legislative offices through Blue Ridge Transit's student and town ridership numbers along Main Street, Commonwealth Mall, and Route 1—Ryan, Cassidy, Holly, Evan, and Tyler rode the fifth-floor elevator up alongside Jordan's family and Freddie's family to pay a visit to Room 518.

Right as the elevator doors slid open on the fifth floor, the two Virginia Dominion University students from the gallery—the young man in the blue manual wheelchair and his sister—rolled onto the elevator car heading down, trading warm smiles and waves with the New River Valley group as they passed in the corridor.

Even though none of the Blacksburg and Christiansburg riders lived in Virginia Beach's Eighty-Fifth House District, there was no way any of them were driving back up Interstate 81 without thanking the bus-riding Delegate in the wheelchair who had just demolished House Bill 663 on the House Floor.

Inside Room 518, Delegate Katie Prairie and her twin brother Nate rolled their two compact black SwiftChair power wheelchairs out from behind her mahogany desk as the three New River Valley groups entered the office together: Jordan in *S.S. Freedom* with Aubrie Jean, Megan, Zoë, and Isabel; Freddie in *The Rover* and Harper in her neon-purple wheelchair with Paige, Jessica, Sarah, and Jenna; and Ryan propelling his matte-black titanium manual wheelchair with Cassidy holding onto his rear push-handles right behind him and Holly, Evan, and Tyler at their sides.

Nate looked around the room and broke into a huge grin from beside his silver laptop. "Four powerchairs and three ultralight manual chairs in one legislative office! Seven wheelchairs in Room 518—now this is what the General Assembly Building is supposed to look like on Transit Legislative Day!"

After Aubrie Jean, Jordan, and Freddie thanked Katie for standing up for transit riders and shredding Line 18 of House Bill 663, Ryan rolled forward half a wheel-turn into the semi-circle while Cassidy kept her right hand resting steadyingly on his left push-handle.

"Delegate Prairie," Ryan said warmly, extending his right hand across their armrests to shake Katie's hand and then Nate's, "I'm Ryan O'Connell, these are my sisters Cassidy and Holly, and these are our twin cousins Evan and Tyler Wright. All five of us live on Progress Street NE in Blacksburg and ride Blue Ridge Transit around town and down to Christiansburg whenever we aren't on the road. Watching you ask that question on the floor today wasn't just entertaining—for people like my sister and me who rely on each other at every public restroom door across this state, you said out loud what every disabled family in Virginia needed someone with a microphone to say."

"Wait a second," Nate said, his eyes widening behind his glasses as he looked from Ryan's matte-black titanium chair to Cassidy holding the rear push-handles, and then to Evan, Tyler, and Holly. "Ryan and Cassidy O'Connell, Holly O'Connell, and Evan and Tyler Wright... from Blacksburg? Katie—that's The Stardust Engine!"

Katie's hands flew to her cheeks as her blue eyes lit up. "You wrote *Electric Color* and *Reignition*! Nate and I listen to *'Neon on the Huckleberry Trail'* on our headphones when we're compiling transit timetables at night! And look at the rear push-handles on your chair, Ryan—you have them set at the exact height for your sister to use as a rolling walker!"

"Every chair I've owned since 1991," Ryan smiled, glancing back at Cassidy, whose cheeks flushed a pleased pink behind her glasses. "I push the rims, and Cass steers from the handles."

"And as General Counsel for Engine Room Records," Holly added, tapping her MagnaPad Pro tablet as she stepped up beside Katie's desk, "I wanted to thank both of you personally. We work closely with Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute and Blue Ridge Transit in Blacksburg, and a statute like House Bill 663 turning university and public building restrooms into fifty-dollar police checkpoints would have been a constitutional disaster for every student, resident, and visitor in the Commonwealth. Nate, do you have that ninety-five-second captioned floor clip hosted on an open link?"

"Right here on our legislative archive server," Nate said proudly, turning his silver MagnaBook Pro screen so Holly could scan the URL onto her tablet while Zoë, Aubrie Jean, Paige, and Jenna pulled out their phones beside her. "Open captions burned right into the video track so anyone can watch it with the sound off."

"Consider it shared across every Engine Room Records and Blacksburg channel we have before we even reach the elevator," Holly grinned, tapping Share on her screen while Evan and Tyler exchanged high-fives with Nate. "By six o'clock tonight, Delegate Cobb is going to wish he had never learned how to spell 'anatomical.'"
