## 10:30 PM — Seated Copper Counters and Ninety-Six Inches of Cedar

Back in Virginia Beach, my own efficiency apartment had a galley counter thirty-six inches high designed exclusively for standing cooks, and Studio 205 in Norfolk squeezed Nolan's four-burner gas stove so tight against the bathroom wall that neither Nolan nor Gwen could turn a wheelchair beside a boiling pot.

Cooking our first real late supper together inside Keepers' Hall felt like stepping into a home designed by someone who had actually watched real human bodies live through a Tuesday evening.

Every flagstone counter around the copper hearth curved outward with smooth, open knee-clearance underneath at exact wheelchair height. Gwen rolled her freshly trued manual chair right under the herb-chopping block without bumping a shin, slicing golden orchard pears and mild cave-aged cheese effortlessly while Nolan rolled beside the low simmer-grate to ladle warm root-and-barley broth into earthenware bowls—zero wrist strain on forearm crutches, zero shifting weight from tired hip to tired hip. Sitting on a cushioned cedar stool right on Nolan's left so all three of our shoulders brushed whenever we passed the bread basket, I buttered thick slices of warm hearth-loaf and marveled at how quiet my own head felt when nobody in the room was exhausted from fighting their own floorplan.

After washing our three bowls in warm mountain spring water that flowed straight from a polished bronze spout at seated reach, Nolan wheeled over to the wide sleeping alcove beneath the crystal twin-moon skylight. First, he lifted Grandpa Walter's silver-framed photograph carefully out of his padded laptop sleeve and set it on the recessed cedar headboard shelf—angling the silver frame slightly inward toward the pillows where our eyes would rest rather than facing out into the room. Next, he and Gwen parked both of their manual wheelchairs side by side near the glowing hearthstones and slotted all four single-tube Lofstrand forearm crutches—his matte-silver pair and Gwen's matte-charcoal pair—into the felt-lined cedar notches carved straight into the bedpost within twelve inches of reach.

Then the three of us stood and sat together at the edge of the Twin-Moon Canopy Bed and just looked at the sheer, glorious width of it.

Where Studio 205's corner mattress measured fifty-four cramped inches across, the Keepers' bed spanned nearly eight full feet of seasoned mountain cedar, topped with a supportive wool-and-down mattress at exact wheelchair-transfer height and heaped with cloud-soft cream quilts. Together we spread Grandpa Walter's dark-green and navy-blue plaid wool blanket right across the center third—and without even needing to discuss the geometry, all three of us knew the exact permanent layout.

Nolan eased onto the mattress first and scooted right into the middle. Gwen transferred smoothly onto his right side, stretching all six feet and one inch of her frame out with a blissful groan because her ankles finally had six spare inches of mattress to spare instead of dangling into empty air. Slipping out of my forest-green cardigan down to my soft cream t-shirt and setting my thin silver wire-rimmed glasses and blue ballpoint pen neatly on the left-hand cedar shelf, I climbed in along Nolan's left side and pulled Grandpa Walter's plaid wool blanket warm across all three sets of shoulders.

"Zero elbows over the edge," Nolan murmured in quiet awe, turning to share his deep, unhurried ten-second goodnight hug with Gwen first—resting his chin beneath his sister's collarbone while Gwen squeezed his shoulders tight—before turning back to lace his left fingers through mine and rest his forehead gently against my temple. "Both safe people within zero inches. How is this actually real?"

"Verified across three independent observers," I whispered back, kissing his cheek as Gwen beamed warmly at both of us across the pillow in the silver-and-rose moonlight.

## 2:10 AM — One Eye Open and the Lock Tammy Cannot Cross

Even inside a fortress of warm cedar and mountain stone, trauma carved into a nervous system across three thousand miles doesn't erase itself on a single calendar night.

Hours after the hearth-coals settled to a soft amber glow—while the larger Ivory Moon hung high in the crystal skylight above our blankets—I woke without my glasses to feel the mattress vibrating with a silent, rigid tremor beside my right ribcage.

I reached immediately for my thin silver wire-rimmed glasses on the cedar shelf, slipped them onto my nose, and looked inward.

Right between Gwen and me, Nolan had bolted half-upright against the carved headboard, chest heaving in shallow, soundless gasps while cold sweat dampened the collar of his heather-navy t-shirt. Ever since Gwen and I brought him home from Puget Sound General Hospital after what Tammy did to him inside Pasco Intermodal Terminal, Nolan had slept with one metaphorical eye open—half-convinced every time a floorboard creaked that Tammy was going to force her way back into his room while he was out of his chair. Now, staring wide-eyed past the foot of our bed toward the shimmering golden curtain of the Glowing Hearth-Archway and the Star-Court beyond, his hands were twisted white-knuckled in Grandpa Walter's plaid wool blanket.

"Nolan?" Gwen's voice came instantly awake and infinitely gentle on his right, rising up onto one elbow before wrapping both of her long, steady arms securely around our favorite guy's trembling shoulders. "Hey, shortstop—I've got you. Look at me."

"The doorway back to Studio 205," Nolan whispered hoarsely, his voice shaking hard as he leaned into Gwen's collarbone while staring in dread toward the courtyard. "We left the sixteenth archway awake out in the Star-Court. What if—what if Tammy comes banging on door 205 in Norfolk, forces the lock open, and steps straight through the amber glass into here while we're asleep?"

My chest ached at how literal, logical, and terrifying that question was inside Nolan's mind. Vague reassurances like *don't worry about that* never helped an autistic brain running worst-case threat paths at two in the morning; what Nolan needed was both physical safety and verifiable engineering truth.

Scooting closer on his left under Grandpa Walter's blanket while Gwen held his right side snug against her heart, I gently uncurled Nolan's cold left fist and held both of his hands warmly between my palms.

"Listen to the literal architecture, Nolan," I said quietly, keeping my voice low, calm, and crystal-clear in the double moonlight. "One: Studio 205 is paused solid at nine-fifteen PM on Friday night. Nobody on Earth can walk down Harborview Towers' hallway, turn a doorknob, or climb stairs while Earth's clock is standing at zero delta. Two: remember the ternary state-tables etched into the middle bronze ring I turned with you and Gwen this evening?"

Nolan swallowed hard, blinking damp hazel eyes toward my glasses as his racing breath tried to match mine. "The... the three-heart lock?"

"Exactly," I nodded gently, rubbing slow, steady circles over his knuckles while Gwen's cheek rested firmly against his hair. "Archivist Thorne showed me the charter inscription on the pedestal rim before supper. Doorway Sixteen isn't an open hole in a wall—it's a keyed harmonic bridge bound exclusively to the three pulses that turned the Astrolabe. Even if Earth weren't paused, Tammy couldn't see that amber glass, touch it, or force it half a millimeter any more than a lone courier could budge the bronze rings. Only you, Gwen, and I hold the key."

For five quiet seconds Nolan processed every word—testing the logic from end to end the way he tested a workstation motherboard—and I literally felt the cold iron band around his ribs give way beneath my palms.

A long, shaky breath left his lungs. Turning into Gwen's deep, unhurried safe-person hug first, then pressing my joined hands warm against his sternum, Nolan eased slowly back down onto the pillows right in the middle between us.

"Paused clock... and keyed to us," Nolan whispered tiredly, eyelids drooping heavy again as Gwen pulled the cloud-wool quilt snug over his right shoulder and I tucked Grandpa Walter's plaid blanket up under his chin on his left. "Sorry I woke both of you up."

"Never apologize for needing your safe people, shortstop," Gwen murmured fiercely into his hair.

"Zero apologies permitted," I agreed softly, keeping my fingers laced securely through Nolan's hand on the pillow and staying awake beside Gwen until every muscle in Nolan's face smoothed out into real, guarded-from-both-sides sleep beneath the twin moons.

## 11:45 PM — Zero Rattling Compressors and Eight Feet of Peace

For twenty minutes after the hearth-coals banked down to a soft rose glow, none of the three of us wanted to close our eyes yet—not out of worry, simply because lying stretched out side by side with zero cramped knees and zero rattling refrigerator compressors felt too good to rush through.

Overhead through the crystal facets of the Moon-Glass skylight, the pale Ivory Moon and smaller Rose-Amber Moon drifted in serene, crystal-clear three-to-two harmony across a velvet field of stars, casting soft silver light over Grandpa Walter's framed smile on the cedar shelf above us.

"Think about it," Gwen whispered contentedly from Nolan's right side, her fingers loosely resting over her brother's forearm atop Grandpa's plaid blanket. "Right now back in Norfolk, that raindrop on our air-conditioner hasn't moved a hair past nine-fifteen. Nobody's counting sick days, nobody's yelling through thin drywall, and tomorrow morning we get to roll straight out that glowing front door in two smoothly working wheelchairs and explore an entire world."

"And I brought three blank eighty-sheet spiral pads," I murmured happily on Nolan's left, resting my head comfortably near his shoulder without my glasses pinching my nose. "Sixteen outward archways plus a whole valley town down below the cliffs means months of clean field notes before Saturday morning even starts on Earth."

Right in the middle between us, Nolan let out the longest, deepest, most unguarded sigh I had ever heard leave his chest since the afternoon we met in the Store Four-Twenty-One breakroom—every ounce of survival armor melting clean out of his shoulders into the cloud-wool mattress.

"Best fifty-four-inch upgrade in history," Nolan mumbled sleepily, his voice already warm and heavy at the edges as his right hand stayed tucked in Gwen's and his left stayed laced in mine.

Within three minutes, his breathing slowed into deep, rhythmic peace right between us. Across the soft rise and fall of Nolan's chest in the moonlight, Gwen caught my eye and smiled with pure sisterly warmth—and curling closer under Grandpa Walter's blanket on Nolan's other side, I closed my eyes knowing all three of us were finally home.
