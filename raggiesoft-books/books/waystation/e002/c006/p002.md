## 1:45 PM — Cloud-Wool Armrests and Silent Wheel Bearings

If Regional Mobility Supply back in Tidewater ever saw what Cloudbrook Hollow's craftspeople did to our two Earth wheelchairs over one pot of spiced pear tea, their entire billing department would dissolve into dust.

While Nolan, Mallory, and I ate warm savory leek hand-pies beside the turning cedar waterwheel, Master Doran knelt cheerfully beside Nolan's wheelchair with a tiny copper oilcan of refined mountain pine-seed lubricant and two hand-stitched wraps of thick, slate-blue Vaelorian cloud-wool brought over by his wife Tamsin from the weavers' guild. Within six minutes—without asking for a single coin or insurance code—Tamsin laced a plush cloud-wool cover cleanly over the cracked, black-taped vinyl armrest pad Nolan had endured for two years on Earth, while Doran oiled all eight wheel hubs until spinning a rear tire felt like floating on air.

"Fine moonsilver-light alloy your smiths used on those frames," Master Doran praised admiringly, wiping his hands on a linen cloth as he admired the matte-silver aluminum of Nolan's crutches tucked in his backrest pocket beside my matte-charcoal pair. "Shame they starved the bearings of oil and wrapped your elbows in split oilcloth! Those cloud-wool pads breathe cool in summer and warm in frost—come down the lift whenever a spoke wants tuning."

"Thank you, Master Doran—genuinely," Nolan said, running his left forearm over the soft, seam-free wool pad where rough electrical tape had chafed his elbow every day in Norfolk, before reaching his right hand across to squeeze mine and his left to hold Mallory's.

Even better were the two wrapped parcels waiting for Mallory and me from the village stationer's guild—sent down at Archivist Thorne's word to welcome the new Keepers. Untying mine across the cedar table revealed a leather tube of creamy, tear-proof star-vellum charts mapping three centuries of Vaeloria's northern and southern skies, complete with brass vernier protractors for plotting the seasonal tides of the Ivory and Rose-Amber moons. Untying Mallory's revealed six smooth dark-slate tablets inlaid with sliding brass ternary beads—reusable mechanical logic boards the Slow-Footed Order's engineers used to test portal state-tables before etching permanent runestones!

Mallory went utterly silent for ten seconds behind her thin silver wire-rimmed glasses, sliding three brass beads across a slate groove with a crisp tactile *click* that made her whole freckled face light up brighter than Christmas morning.

"Tactile non-volatile ternary memory," Mallory whispered in pure autistic bliss, looking up at Nolan and me with shining blue-gray eyes. "Zero battery drain, zero electromagnetic interference, and it clicks like an IBM mechanical keyboard."

Nolan burst out laughing so warmly that two crested orchard finches hopped closer along the stone rail, and I grinned so wide my cheeks ached.

## 3:10 PM — All the Unhurried Time Earth Doesn't Know About

As golden afternoon light slanted through the pear blossoms onto the murmuring brook, the three of us lingered around the cedar table with fresh mugs of warm honey-mint tea, spreading my new star-vellum map flat across the wood.

All across Vaeloria's painted parchment stretched places none of us had laid eyes on yet: the Mirror-Salt Flats to the south where twin-moon reflections doubled the night sky underfoot, the Singing Reed Towns along the eastern inland sea, the High Glass Libraries of Northwatch—all linked by the Slow-Footed Order's step-free causeways, canal barges, and water-balanced sky-lifts.

"You realize what Archivist Thorne's paused-clock law actually means for the three of us, right?" I said softly, tracing a smooth finger from Cloudbrook Hollow down toward the southern inland sea while looking from my brother to Mallory. "Back in Norfolk, it is still nine-fifteen on Friday night. We don't have to cram Vaeloria into two rushed days and sprint back to punch a timeclock."

Mallory rested her chin lightly on her knuckles, following the exact math with a quiet, fond smile. "We can spend weeks—or whole months—living in Keepers' Hall, mapping the sixteen archways, exploring every valley town on this chart at two hundred paces at a time, and sleeping every night in a bed that actually fits all three of us... and whenever we feel ready to step back into Studio 205 for Nolan's Saturday morning lab at CCHR and my opening shift at Four-Twenty-One, zero minutes will have passed on Earth and we'll still be twenty-four and nineteen."

"Wait a second," Nolan chuckled, looking from Mallory's linked hand to mine. "Think what's going to happen whenever we *do* eventually step back to Norfolk after living together in Keepers' Hall for six months of Vaeloria time. One week back in Studio 205's fifty-four-inch bed and we're going to want a bigger apartment on Earth where Mallory lives with us too—and everybody at CCHR and Store Four-Twenty-One is going to think Mal and I are rushing into moving in together after barely half a year of dating!"

Mallory let out a genuine, delighted laugh that crinkled the freckles across the bridge of her nose, while I nearly snorted honey-mint tea across my star-vellum chart.

"Let Earth's calendar think whatever it wants," Mallory said plainly, squeezing Nolan's hand tighter with unmistakable devotion. "We'll know the actual runtime."

"Best inside secret in two worlds," I beamed, unlocking my smooth wheel brakes beside Nolan's chair as Mallory rolled my star-vellum tube carefully back into its leather case. "Come on, Keepers—Archivist Thorne promised to show us how the sixteen outer portals talk to the Moon-Glass Observatory before sunset!"
