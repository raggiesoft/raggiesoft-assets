## 6:50 PM — Three Weeks of Twin-Moon Rhythm

Three weeks of Vaeloria sunrises had turned Keepers' Hall from an astonishing miracle into the warmest, most natural daily routine the three of us had ever known.

Every morning began at our own unhurried pace beneath the crystal skylight of the Twin-Moon Canopy Bed—me waking right in the middle between Gwen on my right and Mallory on my left under Grandpa Walter's green-and-navy plaid wool blanket, starting the day with Gwen's and my deep, unhurried safe-person hug before the three of us rolled and padded into the copper hearth kitchen for toasted orchard bread and hot spiced cider.

The night terrors hadn't vanished by magic—real healing never skips steps—yet by our third week beneath Vaeloria's twin moons, the nights I bolted awake with my heart racing over Pasco had dropped from every pre-dawn to once every four or five nights, and even then three steady sentences from Mallory's hand on my left and ten unhurried seconds tucked beneath Gwen's collarbone on my right brought my lungs smoothly back to peace. For the first time since August of last year, my shoulders no longer lived an inch below my ears during the day.

Three uninterrupted weeks without a single stalled nineteen-sixty-five elevator, six-lane crosswalk sprint, or cramped mattress had smoothed every pinched line around Gwen's eyes and mine, leaving all three of us brimming with quiet energy from morning till starlight.

Up inside the crystal-domed Moon-Glass Observatory at the top of Keepers' Hall's sweeping interior rose-quartz ramp, each of us had claimed a corner of the wide semicircular cedar workbench that fit our minds like custom gloves. On the right, Gwen's upgraded Quantum OS 2000 Professional laptop—charged cleanly off a small brass water-wheel dynamo Mallory and Master Doran had wired beside the snowmelt conduit!—ran side by side with her unfurled star-vellum charts as she mapped the subtle ninety-day nodal tide of the Ivory and Rose-Amber moons across Vaeloria's southern seas. On the left, Mallory sat cross-legged on a cushioned bench with her blue ballpoint pen moving briskly between her spiral notebook and Archivist Thorne's brass-beaded ternary slates, cataloging the exact harmonic signatures of all sixteen outward runestone archways encircling our courtyard below. And right in the middle, I maintained the bronze vernier linkages and inspected the Waystation's outward valley causeways—making sure every ramp slab, canal dock, and sky-lift landing stayed level and true for any traveler arriving through the rings.

"Archway Seven just finished its full ternary handshake," Mallory announced with quiet excitement just before sunset, sliding the final brass bead home on her fourth slate tablet and pushing her thin silver wire-rimmed glasses up her freckled nose. "Listen to what Thorne's oldest ledger says is on the other side of the inward Vaeloria road network, you two: three days south of Cloudbrook Hollow by cushioned barge and gentle coast-road lies Tidestone Harbor, where glass-bottomed boats glide out over sunken star-coral reefs at double moonrise."

Gwen looked up immediately from her brass vernier dividers, green-hazel eyes lighting up like twin lanterns. "Wait—that's the exact latitude where the Rose-Amber Moon passes directly through zenith at the solstice! We could bring both wheelchairs, Grandpa's blanket, and Mal's slates right onto the harbor quay!"

## 7:15 PM — Earth Waiting on Rain, Vaeloria Wide Open Ahead

Leaving our two smoothly oiled wheelchairs parked just inside the Glowing Hearth-Archway for the short evening stroll, Gwen and I clicked our forearms into our matte-charcoal and matte-silver Lofstrand crutches and walked three abreast with Mallory out onto the western parapet of the Star-Court as Vaeloria's twenty-first sunset blazed copper-gold and deep violet across fifty leagues of peaks and valleys.

At the center of the courtyard behind us, the Three-Ring Bronze Astrolabe turned in silent, effortless harmony—Gwen's celestial ring, Mallory's middle runic vernier ring, and my ground-way ring humming one low, steady chord you could feel right through the warm rose-quartz flagstones under your soles. Around the wide circle shone all sixteen outward portals like jewelled stained-glass windows across the cosmos—and tucked peacefully in the sixteenth arch glimmered the soft amber reflection of Studio 205 back in Norfolk, holding Friday night's rain at *9:15 PM* in patient, unbreakable trust until whichever week or season we felt ready to step back for a Saturday morning shift and Ghent lunch.

Gwen leaned gently on her left charcoal crutch so she could loop her right arm warmly around my shoulders, resting her cheek against the crown of my head. Right on my other side, Mallory laced her warm fingers through mine, leaning her copper-auburn head lightly against my left arm while the evening breeze carried up the distant chime of Cloudbrook Hollow's supper bells and the sweet scent of wild mountain thyme.

"Zero rush to turn the page," Gwen murmured happily, looking out past the sunlit crags toward the winding roads, shimmering canals, and distant southern coast waiting for our wheels and crutches. "We've got Tidestone Harbor to chart, fourteen more valley towns on that star-vellum map, and hot cinnamon-pear cider waiting on our own copper hearth tonight."

"Plus sixteen active portals and two whole notebooks left to fill," Mallory smiled behind her silver lenses, giving my hand a warm, steady squeeze. "Where do we point the compass tomorrow morning, Nolan?"

Standing balanced and unhurried right in the middle between my sister and my girlfriend under two rising moons—with a whole step-free fantasy world stretching open to the horizon and zero storms capable of splitting the three of us apart—I smiled out across the golden valley.

"Two hundred paces at a time," I said warmly, hugging Gwen close on my right and holding Mallory's hand fast on my left. "Let's go explore all of it."
