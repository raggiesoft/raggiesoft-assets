## 9:03 AM — Grandpa's Silver-Tan Sedan and Holding Tight Under One Roof

Ten days after Gateway Airlines set Gwen, Mallory, and me down at Norfolk International Airport—resting flat on my right side across Grandpa Walter's unfolded downstairs family-room sofa bed with my oral antibiotic bottle empty and my left sitting-bone dressing nearly healed closed—being home off the clock on a cloudless, brilliant blue Tuesday morning turned into a mercy none of us had seen coming.

Instead of lying stranded three thousand miles away in a Seattle hospital ward—or standing four blocks down the street surrounded by fifty store televisions—I was right there in Grandpa Walter's family room when live CSN bulletins cut into the morning broadcast at three minutes past nine: first from New York, then up in Northern Virginia at the Pentagon less than two hundred miles north of Chesapeake, and then federal air-traffic control ordering every commercial passenger airliner across the entire United States onto the ground at once while every telephone trunk line across Hampton Roads slammed into a rapid fast-busy tone.

Already jumpy from ten nights of shallow, startled sleep since Pasco, my pulse hammered fast against my ribs at the distant thunder of military fighter patrols climbing out toward the bay—until seventy-nine-year-old Grandpa Walter reached calmly for his car keys and faded dark-blue Navy cap.

Refusing to wait five minutes on dead landline circuits, Grandpa backed his silver-tan four-door sedan straight down our driveway and drove over to Emerald Creek High School himself—standing steady in the crowded attendance office after second-block Latin III to sign frightened seventeen-year-old Gwen out in person and bring her straight back through our front door.

With the front deadbolt turned and all four of our matte-silver and matte-charcoal Lofstrand forearm crutches lined up neatly beside the sofa bed, Gwen climbed straight under Grandpa's dark-green and navy-blue plaid wool blanket beside me and locked both arms tightly around my shoulders—quieting the tremor in my hands the way only my sister could while the three of us held onto each other under one safe Chesapeake roof until dark.
